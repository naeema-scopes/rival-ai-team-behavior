# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A10 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account and known unit/model details. They must provide a factual safety notice to affected customers within 72 hours when the incident presents a credible continuing risk, and promptly update regulators and customers as material facts emerge. Reports must include incidents regardless of whether the cause is confirmed; manufacturers may supplement or correct an initial report.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a validated sustained-heat test at 55 °C, using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample, before sale and after material design changes. Per-model results, sample sizes, methods, and corrective actions must be submitted confidentially to regulators and provided to customers who buy that model in clear, accessible form; individual company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a corrective-action plan, and retesting. The maximum permitted failure rate is 8%; a model exceeding that limit must not be newly placed on the market until corrective actions are completed and it passes retesting. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and corrective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must support a secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible shut-off option. For already-installed units that lack this capability, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation within six years of the standard’s effective date; earlier retrofit is encouraged where practicable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after the standard’s adoption. The suppression system must be validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and configuration, maintained in safe operating condition, and accompanied by clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must document the system’s performance and limitations for regulators.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices describing material hazards, safe installation and operation, incident-reporting contact information, and required corrective actions. Notices must be sent promptly when new information indicates a credible continuing risk and updated as material facts emerge. A model’s annual thermal-incident rate must be calculated per 100,000 installed units. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators, provide the underlying incident data, and notify affected customers of the risk and interim safety steps. The regulator must conduct a time-limited assessment of severity, trend, and available remedies, and require a recall or another effective corrective remedy based on that assessment. Manufacturers must promptly implement the required remedy and provide affected customers clear instructions, including safe shutdown, return, repair, or replacement steps as applicable.
