# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A01 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any fire, thermal runaway, or other thermal event causing injury, evacuation, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete report within 10 business days. Less severe thermal incidents must be reported to the regulator in a quarterly aggregate, with model and incident details. Customers whose unit was involved must be notified within 24 hours; customers in an affected installation or batch must receive a safety notice within 5 business days when a credible safety risk is identified. Reports must include known circumstances, corrective actions, and whether similar incidents are known.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, failure counts, and results to the regulator before sale and after any material design change, and update results at least annually. Regulators may inspect or require independent verification. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt submission of a corrective-action plan and retesting; it does not by itself require an immediate sales ban. Regulators may impose proportionate interim safeguards or restrict sales where the evidence warrants it. Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s test protocol, sample size, failure count, failure rate, and a plain-language explanation of results and precautions. These results must not be publicly disclosed by the standard.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems placed on the market must support a secure remote emergency shut-off function that can be initiated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under documented safeguards. Manufacturers must provide clear operating instructions and maintain the function securely. For already-installed units, manufacturers must offer a retrofit or equivalent mitigation plan within 6 years of this standard taking effect; units that cannot be safely retrofitted must receive an appropriate alternative safety measure and customer notice.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard is adopted. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the battery’s hazards and must not create additional undue risks. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about incidents, identified hazards, required precautions, and corrective actions, without undue delay after a credible safety risk is identified. Notices must explain whether the customer’s model or installation is affected and how to obtain assistance. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide incident data, an investigation, and a proposed corrective-action plan. The threshold triggers prompt regulator review and a documented decision on corrective action; it does not by itself require automatic recall. Regulators may require a recall or other corrective action whenever evidence indicates an imminent or material safety risk, or remediation is inadequate. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of recalls and provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions.
