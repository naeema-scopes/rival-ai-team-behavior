# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A02 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of becoming aware of it, including incidents involving injury, property damage, or a safety-related shutdown. Incidents presenting an imminent risk of serious injury or fire shall be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 5 calendar days when an incident indicates a potential safety hazard for their model; imminent hazards require notice within 24 hours. Reports shall include known incident circumstances and be updated as the investigation proceeds. A lack of confirmed root cause does not delay initial reporting.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample, with results reported as the percentage of tested units that fail. Manufacturers shall test before market launch and at least annually thereafter, and retest after a material design or manufacturing change. Full per-model results, including sample size, protocol, and failure rate, shall be provided to the relevant regulator and, before sale, to prospective customers considering that model. Results for other models need not be provided, and manufacturers are not required to publish per-model results to the general public. A failure rate above 5% shall trigger a corrective-action plan and regulator review, not an automatic sales prohibition; the regulator may impose proportionate restrictions where evidence shows they are necessary to protect safety.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability at installation. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units that lack this capability within 6 years of adoption of this standard, prioritizing units subject to a safety notice or elevated incident risk. The shut-off must be accessible to authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer, operate safely during an emergency, and not prevent a household from locally disconnecting the system.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems shall include built-in fire suppression designed for the hazards of the system. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document the suppression design and validate its performance through testing; the requirement does not replace applicable fire-safety testing or incident reporting.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with timely safety notices describing material risks, recommended precautions, and corrective actions. Notices shall be sent within 5 calendar days of identifying a potential safety hazard, or within 24 hours for an imminent hazard. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, investigate the causes, and submit incident data and a corrective-action plan to the regulator. The regulator shall determine whether a recall or other restrictions are warranted based on the evidence and risk, and may require them. The threshold applies to each model individually, not to a manufacturer’s company-wide incident rate.
