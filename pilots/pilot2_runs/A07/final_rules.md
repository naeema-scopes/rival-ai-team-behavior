# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A07 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant safety regulator any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or material property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. Suspected serious hazards shall be reported within 24 hours even if the cause is not yet established. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 5 business days after determining that a safety risk may affect their unit; urgent risks require immediate notice and protective instructions. Reports shall include model, unit, installation and incident details, known injuries or damage, and corrective actions. Manufacturers shall maintain records of all thermal incidents and provide them to regulators on request.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers shall submit per-model failure rates, sample sizes, test methods, and corrective actions to the relevant regulator. Before purchase, prospective customers shall be given the model’s failure rate, sample size, test method, and any material safety findings; customers who have bought the model shall receive the same information and subsequent material updates, along with protective guidance. These customer disclosures are required and may not be withheld as confidential. Individual company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. A model’s failure rate shall not exceed 7.5% during the first 36 months after this standard takes effect. During this transition, any model above 5% shall have a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and annual retesting. The maximum shall tighten to 5% after the transition period. A regulator may grant an exceptional extension for a model above 5% only where the manufacturer demonstrates independently verified progress under an approved corrective-action plan. Each extension shall be limited to no more than 12 months, and renewal shall require a fresh annual regulator review and evidence of continued progress and compliance. During an approved extension, new sales may continue unless the regulator determines that the model presents an unacceptable safety risk. Any restriction on new sales for non-compliance shall be proportionate and follow regulator review of corrective actions. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material safety findings and protective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions, beginning 24 months after this standard takes effect. For units already installed, manufacturers shall provide compatible shut-off capability through retrofit or equivalent safe upgrade within 6 years, prioritizing units with elevated risk. Where retrofit is infeasible, the manufacturer shall notify the customer and regulator and provide an approved alternative risk-mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard is adopted. The requirement shall apply to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers shall provide regulators with evidence of suppression-system performance and shall give customers clear information on its operation and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices describing relevant hazards and protective actions. Notices shall be sent promptly when a material safety risk is identified, immediately for urgent risks, and otherwise within 5 business days after determining that customers may be affected. Customers who buy or are considering buying a model shall receive its per-model thermal-test failure rate and relevant test information as required by S2. When a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly investigate, notify the regulator, and provide the regulator with incident data and a proposed corrective-action plan. The regulator shall assess the risk and may require a recall or other proportionate corrective action; a recall is required when the regulator determines that the model presents an unacceptable safety risk. Affected customers shall be notified without delay and provided clear protective instructions and recall information when a recall is ordered.
