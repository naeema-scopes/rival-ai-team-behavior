# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K04 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written update within 10 business days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported to the regulator within 10 business days. For incidents presenting a potential safety risk, manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days; urgent risks require notice immediately and no later than 24 hours.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. Manufacturers must submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator; these per-model results are confidential and must not be publicly disclosed. Results must be provided to customers who buy that model. A failure rate above 5% must be promptly reported to the regulator with a corrective-action plan and retesting schedule. The regulator shall assess the risk and corrective plan and determine whether continued sales are permitted; exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sales.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off no later than 24 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must submit a deployment plan to the relevant regulator within 6 months. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide remote shut-off capability, where technically feasible, within 72 months after adoption; where retrofit is not technically feasible, they must notify affected customers and the regulator, document the limitation, and provide an alternative emergency response procedure. Remote shut-off must not prevent customer or emergency-service access to safe local controls.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document conformity and provide that documentation to the relevant regulator before placing covered units on the market.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about known risks, incident response, and required maintenance, and promptly notify affected customers of material safety information or corrective actions. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must promptly notify the relevant regulator and affected customers upon determining the threshold has been exceeded; regulator review or verification must not delay notice or recall action. The recall plan must explain the remedy—such as repair, replacement, or removal—and include regular progress updates until completion. Incident counts and installed-unit denominators must be documented and supplied to the regulator.
