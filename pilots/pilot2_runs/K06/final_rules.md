# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K06 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents involving injury, property damage, or a near miss. They must provide affected customers with an initial safety notice within 72 hours when the incident indicates a potential product risk, and update the regulator and customers as material facts become available. Reports must include known incident counts and corrective actions.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% for every model. Manufacturers must provide numerical per-model test results to the regulator and to customers who purchase that model, including through accessible ongoing notices. If a model exceeds 5%, the manufacturer must promptly investigate and submit a corrective-action plan and timeline to the regulator. The regulator may monitor remediation and impose evidence-based restrictions in light of the risk and remediation progress; exceeding the benchmark alone does not require immediate market exclusion, without displacing any mandatory recall trigger in S5.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All newly installed systems must include remote emergency shut-off capability. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or equivalent safe alternative within six years, prioritizing units with known safety risks. Customers must receive clear instructions for using the shut-off feature and be told that it is not a substitute for emergency services.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must disclose whether a unit includes built-in suppression and provide customers with clear instructions for responding to a battery fire. The regulator may approve an equivalent protective design where the manufacturer demonstrates that it provides comparable fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive, at purchase and through accessible notices restricted to affected customers, material safety information for their model, including known thermal hazards, identified safety concerns, pass/fail status, emergency instructions, and available remedies. Numerical per-model 55 °C failure rates must be provided to the regulator and to customers who purchase that model under S2, but must not be published for the general public. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and the regulator when new information indicates a material safety risk, explain any remedy, and provide updates as facts and corrective actions develop. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units for that model in a year; company-wide incident rates alone do not trigger this model-specific threshold. The manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, explain the risk and remedy, and provide progress updates.
