# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A03 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 5 business days after determining that a product or condition may pose a safety risk. Reports must include known incident details and be updated as facts are confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units before market introduction and after a material design change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7%. A rate above 5% triggers prompt regulator review, customer notice, a corrective-action plan, and retesting. Exceeding 7% does not by itself require immediate market exclusion; regulators may set proportionate protective measures and deadlines, including restricting sales if risks are not adequately addressed. Manufacturers must provide each model’s results and test methods, including sample size and failure rate, to regulators and to customers considering or buying that model before purchase; results need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units placed on the market must support a secure remote emergency shut-off function, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Existing installed units that are technically capable of retrofit must be upgraded within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Where retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must notify the owner and provide an equivalent safety plan, subject to regulator review.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to regulators and customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about known hazards, incident response, and any required inspection, repair, or software update, promptly and no later than 5 business days after determining that a product or condition may pose a safety risk. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and submit incident and corrective-action information for a risk-based review. Regulators must determine whether a recall or other protective action is required; a threshold exceedance alone does not automatically trigger a recall. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of required actions and report progress until resolved.
