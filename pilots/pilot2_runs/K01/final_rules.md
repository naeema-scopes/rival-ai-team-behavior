# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K01 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report every known thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents still under investigation. A corrected or final report must follow within 30 days, with material updates provided promptly. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a potential safety risk, and provide any necessary immediate precautions without delay. Reports must include incident date, location, model, circumstances, injuries or damage, and investigation status, subject to applicable privacy protections.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a representative sample and a documented, reproducible protocol. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions confidentially to the relevant regulator. A model’s failure rate must not exceed 7%; exceedance requires prompt regulator notification, investigation, corrective action, and retesting within a regulator-set period. Regulators may restrict sales or require other measures where results or other evidence indicate an imminent serious risk. Before purchase, customers must receive the 55 °C test failure rate for the model they are buying, along with precautions and any regulator-required corrective measures. Results for other models and individual per-model results must not be disclosed to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off no later than 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide a secure, reliable shut-off capability and clear instructions for its use. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or equivalent safe solution within 72 months, where technically feasible, and notify owners of the schedule and any interim precautions. Remote shut-off must not prevent emergency services or users from safely isolating a unit locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document that the system is appropriate for the battery chemistry and enclosure, and must provide maintenance and operating instructions to customers. Regulators may approve an equivalent protective design where it demonstrates comparable or greater risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
## S5_customer_disclosure
At purchase and installation, customers must receive clear written safety instructions, emergency contacts, operating limitations, and guidance on recognizing and responding to overheating or fire. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide updated instructions and protective actions. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, based on verified incident and installed-unit data. The manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop sales, and provide a repair, replacement, or refund, as appropriate. Regulators may require a recall below this threshold where evidence indicates an imminent serious risk.
