# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A09 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. They shall promptly notify affected customers when an incident presents a potential safety risk, and in any event within 72 hours of determining that customer action or precautions may be needed. Reports shall include known circumstances, model and serial number, and actions taken; manufacturers shall update reports as facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and at least annually thereafter, with additional testing after a material design or manufacturing change. A model passes if no more than 8% of tested units fail. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator. The regulator shall keep submitted results confidential from the general public. Manufacturers shall make results for a specific model available to customers considering or owning that model. Where a model exceeds the 5% failure rate, the regulator may require a corrective-action plan, follow-up testing, or other measures based on the evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed battery systems shall support remote emergency shut-off by the manufacturer or an authorized emergency responder, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers shall provide clear instructions for safe manual shut-off if remote shut-off is unavailable. Existing installed units shall be brought into compliance within 72 months of adoption where technically feasible; manufacturers shall notify owners of units that cannot be upgraded and provide safe alternative procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system shall be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation and shall be validated through testing. Manufacturers shall provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any material limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with model-specific safety notices, incident and safety information relevant to safe use, and prompt updates when material risks or protective actions are identified. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and provide the data needed for a regulator-led safety assessment. The regulator shall assess incident severity, persistence, and the effectiveness of corrective actions, and may require a recall when it determines that a model presents an unacceptable risk. Manufacturers shall follow any recall order, provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions, and report recall progress and corrective actions to the regulator.
