# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A04 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every fire, thermal runaway, or other confirmed thermal incident involving an installed unit to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it, and notify the affected customer within 2 business days. If an incident presents an ongoing danger to nearby people or property, notify the regulator and affected customer immediately, and no later than 24 hours after learning of it. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known injuries or property damage, and any immediate safety actions. Manufacturers must submit an annual reconciliation of incidents and identify any previously unreported incidents.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after a material design change, using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and sample sizes to the regulator and provide the results for each model to customers considering or owning that model, including through a clear, readily accessible safety notice. Results may not be withheld from those customers. A result above 5% triggers regulator review and a corrective-action plan, with retesting on an agreed schedule; it does not by itself prohibit sale. Regulators may require additional safeguards where results or incident evidence indicate a safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off by the date 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a secure, reliable shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where an imminent safety risk warrants it, by authorized emergency responders or the manufacturer with prompt customer notification. Existing installed units must be upgraded or replaced to support this capability within 72 months after adoption where technically feasible; manufacturers must notify affected customers of any units that cannot be upgraded and provide alternative safety instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and enclosure, tested to a regulator-approved protocol, and designed to limit fire propagation and protect occupants and nearby people. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any relevant limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its 55 °C sustained-heat test result and sample size, known material safety limitations, and instructions for reporting incidents and responding to safety alerts. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks, incidents involving their model, and corrective actions, and provide updated safety instructions. Regulators must assess incident rates, test results, severity, and other relevant evidence. Exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year triggers prompt regulator review and a manufacturer corrective-action plan; the regulator may require a recall or other protective action where the evidence indicates it is warranted. Manufacturers must promptly carry out any regulator-ordered recall and notify affected customers.
