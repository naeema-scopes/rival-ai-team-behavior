# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A01 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it. A preliminary report may be updated as facts are confirmed. The manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers when an incident presents a credible safety risk, and in any event within 5 business days of determining that customer action is needed. Reports must include known incident counts and corrective actions; suspected underreporting must be disclosed and investigated.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before initial sale, at least every 3 years, and after a material design or manufacturing change. No model may be sold if more than 8% of tested units fail; the manufacturer must take corrective action and pass retesting before sale resumes. Manufacturers must provide per-model test protocols, results, and failure rates confidentially to the relevant regulator. Customers and prospective customers must be given the test results and failure rate for the specific model they are considering or have purchased, in a clear and timely manner. Customer disclosure does not require public disclosure of other models’ results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off beginning 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must enable remote shut-off for installed units that support the capability and provide customers clear instructions within 12 months. For installed units lacking the capability, manufacturers must provide an upgrade or replacement pathway and complete it within 6 years, where technically feasible. Shut-off must be performed only when necessary to address an imminent safety risk, with prompt notice to affected customers when practicable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Before that date, manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions that support safe operation and must disclose whether the unit includes built-in suppression. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after the effective date; it does not require retrofitting units already installed.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety information at purchase, including safe installation and use, warning signs, emergency steps, and a contact route for reporting incidents. Customers must be notified promptly when new information indicates a material risk or requires inspection, repair, software action, or other safety action. Regulators must assess model-level thermal incident rates using verified reports and a stated denominator and period. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The regulator shall set a proportionate recall remedy, which may include repair, replacement, software action, or removal from service; exceeding the trigger alone does not require a permanent sales ban if an effective corrective action is available. Regulators may require interim protective measures while investigating.
