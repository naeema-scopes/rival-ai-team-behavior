# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A02 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway in a covered system to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it. The report shall include the unit/model identification, known circumstances, injuries or property damage, and any immediate safety actions; manufacturers shall provide material updates promptly. Manufacturers shall notify the affected customer within 24 hours, provide immediate safety instructions, and notify customers whose units may present a related hazard within 72 hours. Incidents shall not be excluded because they appear minor or because their cause is not yet known.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a documented, regulator-accepted protocol and a representative sample. No more than 5% of tested units of any model may fail. Manufacturers shall provide per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model; company-specific per-model failure rates shall not be published publicly, though regulators may publish aggregated industry results. Retesting is required after a material design or manufacturing change and at least annually. A model exceeding the failure limit shall be subject to a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retest; exceeding the limit alone does not automatically require a sales ban, though regulators may impose sales restrictions where they find an ongoing safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Covered systems shall provide a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, when necessary to address an imminent safety hazard, by the manufacturer or emergency responders. New units shall comply within 12 months of adoption. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units to comply, or provide an equally effective safety mitigation where retrofit is not technically feasible, within 6 years of adoption. Shut-off events and failed activation attempts shall be logged and reported to the relevant regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new covered systems shall include built-in fire suppression designed to control or contain a battery fire and limit hazards to occupants and neighbouring properties. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall document suppression-system performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to customers and regulators.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, safe use, maintenance, and steps to take during an incident, and promptly update notices when new material risks emerge. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 24 hours of becoming aware of a serious hazard and notify customers whose units may present a related hazard within 72 hours. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers, investigate the cause and scope of the risk, and initiate a recall. Regulators may require earlier action or a recall where they find evidence of an imminent or serious risk. Manufacturers shall promptly report the basis for any corrective action or recall to regulators and provide affected customers with clear instructions.
