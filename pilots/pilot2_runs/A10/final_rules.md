# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A10 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, evacuation, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a detailed follow-up within 10 business days. Other confirmed overheating incidents must be reported within 10 business days. Affected customers must be notified within 48 hours when an incident presents an ongoing safety risk, and otherwise within 10 business days. Reports must include known incident counts and corrective actions; suspected under-reporting must be investigated.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized 55 °C sustained-heat test before sale and at least annually thereafter. Its failure rate must not exceed 7%. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, including failure rates and corrective actions, to the regulator and customers who buy that model, in clear and accessible form; individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. Models exceeding the limit must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, submit a corrective-action plan, and be retested within six months. The regulator may impose proportionate sales restrictions if a model remains above the limit after corrective action and retesting.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under regulator-approved safeguards. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and maintain secure, reliable operation of the capability. For installed units, manufacturers must provide an effective remote shut-off retrofit or equivalent upgrade within six years of adoption, prioritizing units presenting the greatest safety risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Suppression systems must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation environment, and must be tested to demonstrate effectiveness without creating additional hazards. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear installation and operating safety instructions, emergency response guidance, and notice of any material safety risk affecting their model. For a confirmed ongoing risk, notify affected customers promptly and no later than 48 hours after confirmation. A model exceeding 8 verified thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period must promptly be reported to regulators and undergo a regulator-led risk assessment. The manufacturer must submit a corrective-action plan and notify affected customers of material risks. A recall is mandatory when the regulator determines that the verified incident pattern presents an unacceptable safety risk and lesser corrective actions are inadequate; the regulator may order an immediate recall where an imminent serious risk exists.
