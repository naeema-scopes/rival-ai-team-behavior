# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K10 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with an initial account of known impacts and circumstances. They must provide a complete incident report within 10 business days, updating it as material facts emerge. The manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers when an incident indicates a material risk to their model, and provide interim instructions to reduce risk. Reports must include incidents regardless of whether injury or property damage occurred.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and at least annually thereafter. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% per model. A model exceeding 5% is non-compliant; the manufacturer must notify the regulator within 24 hours, retest within 30 days, and submit a corrective-action plan within 10 business days. The plan must address the test result, test uncertainty, and the model’s incident history. The regulator must determine proportionate protective measures; exceeding the cap does not by itself automatically prohibit sales or require a market ban. Manufacturers must submit per-model results confidentially to the relevant regulator and provide the results to customers who buy that model. Results must identify the model, test date, protocol, and failure rate. Regulators may require additional testing or other protective measures where warranted.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market from 1 January 2028 must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete required retrofits within six years of that date. A regulator may approve exemptions where a retrofit is technically infeasible or would create a greater safety risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be designed and validated to reduce the risk of fire spread and must not introduce a greater safety risk. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of its performance and maintenance requirements. A regulator may grant a narrowly tailored exemption where the manufacturer demonstrates that suppression is technically infeasible or would create a greater safety risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase and whenever material safety information changes, customers must receive clear instructions on safe installation, operation, emergency response, and how to report a safety concern. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a confirmed material risk and provide interim safety instructions. When a model’s thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and submit the underlying model-specific data for validation. The regulator must validate the data promptly. If the validated model-specific rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 in that period, a recall of that model is mandatory; the manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and the regulator and provide recall instructions. The regulator may require interim protective measures while validating data and may require a recall based on other evidence of serious risk. Customers and regulators must be notified without undue delay when a recall is initiated.
