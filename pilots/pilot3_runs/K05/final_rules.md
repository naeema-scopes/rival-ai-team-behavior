# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K05 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal event involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify the affected customer within 24 hours where the event involves that customer’s unit, and promptly notify other customers if a safety risk may affect their units. Reports must include known facts and be updated as investigations proceed; uncertainty is not a reason to delay an initial report.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a validated 55 °C sustained-heat test before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a representative sample and a consistent protocol. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt corrective action, a documented retest, and regulator review of the model’s safety and interim risk controls; it is not by itself an automatic sales ban. Regulators may require additional measures, including sales restrictions, where the evidence indicates they are warranted. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and corrective plans to regulators and provide results for a model to its current and prospective customers. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be publicly disclosed.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required for all new home battery units placed on the market beginning 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to support remote emergency shut-off, or provide an equally effective regulator-approved remote emergency isolation capability, within 72 months after adoption. The capability must be secure, available to authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer, and designed to avoid unsafe interruption of essential functions. Manufacturers must notify customers how the feature works and how to request activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be validated for the battery chemistry and installation conditions, independently tested to an applicable safety protocol, and maintained according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Manufacturers must disclose its capabilities and limitations to customers. Regulators may approve an equivalent or safer mitigation for a specific design where built-in suppression is not technically suitable.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, before purchase and at least annually, model-specific safety notices describing known hazards, safe installation and use, emergency steps, and material safety updates. They must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of a credible newly identified risk, and provide follow-up notices as material facts change. A recall is mandatory when a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop sales, and implement the regulator-approved recall and remedy; an investigation must not delay interim protective measures or notice.
