# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K01 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every fire, thermal runaway, or other incident involving hazardous overheating to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. Reports must include the model, incident date and circumstances, known injuries or property damage, and any immediate protective actions; manufacturers must promptly supplement incomplete reports. If the incident presents an immediate serious hazard, notify the regulator and affected customer without delay. Otherwise, notify the affected customer within 2 business days when the incident presents a potential safety risk; promptly notify known nearby residents when an incident creates an immediate hazard to them. Preserve auditable incident records, including incidents initially not reported, and provide requested follow-up information to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. A model with a failure rate above 5% must promptly submit a regulator-reviewed corrective-action plan, with milestones and enhanced monitoring, and achieve a failure rate of 6.5% or less within 12 months. A model exceeding 6.5% may not be newly marketed or installed unless the regulator authorizes a time-limited exception with protective measures. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results to regulators and provide complete results for a model to customers who buy that model. Individual company per-model failure rates need not be published publicly; public reporting may include non-identifying aggregated summaries.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed compatible units, or provide an equivalent no-cost safety measure where retrofit is infeasible, within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Shut-off must not prevent emergency services from safely controlling the system.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard takes effect. Until then, manufacturers must disclose whether each model includes built-in suppression and provide installation and maintenance instructions for any suppression equipment. Suppression systems must be compatible with safe intervention by emergency responders.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify customers of safety risks affecting their model, including relevant incident trends, test failures, and corrective actions, and provide practical steps to reduce risk. A manufacturer must initiate a regulator-supervised recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period. The regulator may require an earlier recall where evidence indicates an imminent serious risk. Recall notices must state the hazard, affected units, customer actions, remedies, and contact information; manufacturers must notify affected customers directly and report completion progress to the regulator.
