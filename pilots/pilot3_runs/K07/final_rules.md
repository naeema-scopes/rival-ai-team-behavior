# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K07 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within five business days of learning of it. Incidents involving death, injury, evacuation, or property damage beyond the battery system must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours of learning of an incident that presents a material safety risk to them, and provide any immediate precautions or instructions. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and actions taken or planned; manufacturers must update regulators and affected customers as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market release and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers must provide per-model results to regulators and customers who purchase that model; individual company per-model results will not be published to the general public. Results above the regulator’s 5% benchmark require a corrective-action plan, regulator review, and follow-up testing on a defined schedule. Exceeding the benchmark alone does not prohibit sales; regulators may require proportionate safeguards or restrictions based on the risk evidence and corrective-action progress.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on all new units placed on the market from 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway for installed units and complete retrofits within six years after adoption, subject to regulator-approved safety exceptions where retrofit is impracticable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on all new units placed on the market from 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must document that the suppression system is suitable for the battery chemistry and installation conditions and provide installation and maintenance instructions. Any exception must be approved by the regulator and supported by evidence that an alternative provides equivalent or better protection.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information, including applicable installation, ventilation, monitoring, emergency-response, and maintenance instructions, plus notice of material safety updates. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of a safety defect and provide corrective instructions. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must notify regulators and affected customers within five business days; regulators must promptly review the evidence and may require corrective action, including a mandatory recall where the evidence establishes an ongoing material safety risk. Exceeding the threshold alone does not automatically require a recall. Manufacturers must promptly execute any ordered recall.
