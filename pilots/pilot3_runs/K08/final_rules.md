# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K08 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, evacuation, injury, or material property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written incident report within 10 calendar days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 calendar days when an incident indicates a material safety risk to their unit or model, and promptly provide any protective steps. Reports must include the model, incident circumstances, known harms, and corrective actions; updates are required as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market introduction and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved sampling protocol. The maximum failure rate is 5%. Manufacturers must submit per-model numerical results, sample sizes, test methods, and corrective actions to regulators, and must provide the numerical result for each model to customers who buy it. A model exceeding the limit must promptly submit an enforceable corrective-action plan and timetable to the regulator, complete corrective measures, and undergo a verified retest. Regulators may restrict sales where a manufacturer fails to implement its plan or where evidence indicates an imminent safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability, or provide an equivalent regulator-approved emergency measure, within 6 years after adoption. Manufacturers must notify affected customers of the capability, limitations, and how to use it.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must disclose to customers whether a unit includes built-in suppression and provide instructions for any associated safety features.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, at purchase and when materially updated, model-specific safety notices, known material risks, and clear protective steps. Notify affected customers promptly when new information indicates a material risk, and provide updates as material facts emerge. Regulators must receive the numerical per-model 55 °C test result. A mandatory model recall is triggered when the confirmed annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units. Regulators may order an earlier recall when incident circumstances or severity warrant it. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a recall, provide instructions, and explain available remedies.
