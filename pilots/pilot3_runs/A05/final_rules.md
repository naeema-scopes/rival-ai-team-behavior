# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A05 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller report within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and provide updates as the investigation proceeds. Reports must include known circumstances, affected models and units, and corrective actions; manufacturers must maintain records and report previously unreported incidents discovered during review.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a statistically representative sample. The maximum failure rate is 5%. A model exceeding 5% may be sold only under regulator-approved corrective action and safeguards, with retesting on a regulator-set schedule; regulators may require immediate measures or prohibit sales at any rate where evidence indicates a serious safety risk. Manufacturers shall submit complete per-model results, including exact failure rates, to the regulator. Customers who buy or own the model must receive its pass/fail status, exact per-model failure rate, and material safety information. Results need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support this function, or provide an equivalent regulator-approved safety measure, within 6 years after adoption. The shut-off function must be secure, reliable, and accessible to authorized emergency responders and the customer, with clear instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and validated to reduce fire spread and risk to occupants and neighbours. Manufacturers must provide clear maintenance and use instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and throughout ownership, customers must receive accessible safety notices covering safe installation and use, known material risks, incident trends, the model’s 55 °C thermal-test status, its exact per-model failure rate, and required precautions. Regulators shall receive complete per-model thermal-test results, including exact failure rates. Results need not be published to the general public. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of a material safety defect and provide clear mitigation instructions. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must promptly stop sales, notify affected customers and regulators, and offer an effective remedy, which may include repair or replacement. Following corrective action, sales may resume when the regulator determines the safety issue has been adequately addressed. Regulators may require a recall at a lower rate where evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
