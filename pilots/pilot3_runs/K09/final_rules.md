# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K09 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller report within 10 business days. Incidents presenting an immediate risk to other units must be reported to regulators and affected customers without delay and no later than 24 hours. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported to regulators within 5 business days. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial number, and actions taken; updates are required as material facts emerge. Manufacturers must maintain incident logs and reconcile them at least quarterly against service, warranty, and claims data, promptly correcting omissions and notifying the regulator of any material discrepancy.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% for each model. A model exceeding that limit must be reported to the regulator and undergo corrective action and retesting; exceeding the limit alone does not automatically require sales suspension. The regulator may restrict sales where test results or other evidence show an unacceptable safety risk. Manufacturers must submit complete numeric per-model results to the regulator and provide numeric results to customers who buy that model. If a model exceeds the limit, manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit a corrective-action plan.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a clear process for emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide installation and operating instructions and maintain the capability throughout the supported life of the product. For installed units that do not support remote shut-off, manufacturers must provide customers and relevant emergency-response authorities with clear guidance on safe isolation and retrofit the units to support remote shut-off within six years of adoption of this standard.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must disclose whether each unit includes this feature and provide instructions on its limitations and maintenance. The regulator may approve equivalent protective technology that provides comparable mitigation of fire risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices for confirmed thermal incidents involving their model, promptly when the incident presents an immediate risk and otherwise within 5 business days of confirmation. Notices must describe known hazards, affected models or serial ranges, and recommended customer actions; material updates must be provided as facts emerge. When any individual model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, calculated using that model’s installed-unit denominator, the manufacturer must promptly report the rate and supporting data to the regulator and initiate a mandatory recall. The manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and regulators, provide free repair, replacement, or refund instructions, and submit recall progress reports to the regulator.
