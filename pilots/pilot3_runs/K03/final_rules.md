# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K03 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents initially reported by customers or emergency services. Reports must include the incident date, location, model and serial number when available, outcome, and known circumstances; manufacturers must supplement material new information promptly. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 days of confirming a material thermal hazard, with safe-use guidance and any interim restrictions. A lack of confirmed causation does not excuse initial reporting; reports may be updated as an investigation proceeds.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and methodology to the regulator for confidential review. The regulator may verify submissions and require retesting. Manufacturers must provide customers who buy or own a model with that model’s identifiable test failure rate, sample size, and applicable protective measures, and provide general safe-use guidance. Public reporting may include aggregated or anonymized results. A model’s failure rate must not exceed 5%. A model exceeding 5% must promptly submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and be retested within 12 months. The regulator may require sales restrictions or additional protective action based on results, severity, and evidence of risk; urgent protective measures are not delayed by the corrective-action period.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units manufactured 24 months or more after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a clear way for customers to understand when shut-off has occurred. Manufacturers must provide instructions for customers and emergency responders. For installed units, manufacturers must provide a compatible remote shut-off retrofit or equivalent effective emergency isolation capability within six years of adoption, where technically feasible, and report implementation progress annually to the regulator. The regulator may approve a documented alternative where retrofit is infeasible, provided it offers comparable protection.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers must document suppression-system performance and maintenance requirements and provide this information to customers and regulators.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a material thermal hazard and provide safe-use guidance, including any interim restrictions, within 5 days of confirming the hazard; regulators must be notified within 24 hours. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period. The rate must use the model’s incident count and installed-unit population for that period; manufacturers must retain supporting records and provide them to the regulator for verification. The regulator must promptly notify the manufacturer of a proposed determination and allow it to submit corrections or relevant evidence, without delaying urgent protective measures or a required recall. The regulator may require a recall below that threshold where the severity or pattern of incidents warrants it. Recall notices must explain the hazard, affected units, remedy, and how customers can obtain help.
