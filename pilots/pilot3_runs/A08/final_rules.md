# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A08 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a detailed report within 10 calendar days. They must notify affected customers within 5 calendar days when an incident indicates a potential safety hazard for their model, and promptly provide material updates. Reports must include model, incident date, known consequences, and available cause information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before initial marketing and after any material design change. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and test-method details to the relevant regulator and provide the per-model results to customers who buy that model. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a documented corrective-action plan, and retesting on a regulator-approved schedule. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit marketing; the regulator may require additional measures, including suspension or withdrawal, if it determines the model presents an unreasonable safety risk. Customers must also receive relevant safety instructions and material hazard notices.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models must support remote emergency shut-off no later than 24 months after adoption. For units installed before that date, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within 6 years of adoption where technically feasible, with a documented safety plan for units that cannot be upgraded. Customers must receive instructions on shut-off capability and limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system and its limitations in customer safety materials. Existing installed units are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices describing material hazards, safe operation, incident-reporting contacts, and corrective actions relevant to their model; manufacturers must provide material updates promptly. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers and provide the supporting model-level data. The exceedance triggers a regulator review and a documented manufacturer corrective-action plan. A recall is required when the regulator determines, based on the incident data and risk assessment, that a recall is necessary to address an unreasonable safety risk. Regulators receive model-level incident and recall data; public notices must identify affected products and actions, but need not publish per-model test failure rates.
