# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K10 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected owners within 72 hours when an incident presents a potential safety risk, and promptly provide material updates. Incidents may not be excluded because their cause is uncertain.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a validated 55 °C sustained-heat test using a regulator-approved protocol. A failure rate above 5% triggers a regulator-supervised corrective-action plan and retesting on a regulator-set schedule; the regulator will assess the results and the adequacy of corrective measures in light of the identified risk. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and test methodology confidentially to regulators before sale and after material design changes, and provide the model’s results to prospective purchasers before purchase. Regulators may restrict new sales if the manufacturer fails to meet the corrective-action or retesting schedule, or if an identified safety risk remains unaddressed. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically bar new sales.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability beginning 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide owners and emergency responders with clear instructions for using the capability. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide a compatible remote shut-off retrofit or upgrade within 72 months, where technically feasible; if not feasible, they must document the reason for the regulator and provide owners with an alternative emergency isolation procedure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and configuration, and maintained in accordance with manufacturer instructions. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of validation before placing compliant units on the market.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety instructions, known material risks, incident-reporting and emergency contacts, and notice of any applicable corrective action. Manufacturers must promptly notify owners of newly identified material hazards and provide safe-use or service instructions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected owners and initiate a recall, including safe-use, service, repair, replacement, or return instructions appropriate to the risk. A recall is mandatory at this threshold but does not by itself impose a permanent sales ban; regulators may restrict sales or require an immediate recall where the risk is urgent. Company-wide rates alone do not trigger this model-specific recall threshold.
