# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A06 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a follow-up report containing known cause and corrective actions within 30 days. Manufacturers shall notify affected owners within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or requires action; notices shall describe immediate precautions and how to obtain assistance. Reports and notices shall not be delayed while cause is investigated.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a common protocol established by regulators, before market introduction and after a material design change. Manufacturers shall provide per-model test protocols, sample sizes, and results to regulators and provide the results to customers who buy that model, at purchase and on request. Results need not be published to the general public. If more than 5% of tested units fail, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators, submit a corrective-action plan, and retest the model under the regulator-established protocol. Regulators may require additional safeguards or restrict sales if the manufacturer fails to implement adequate corrective action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months or more after adoption shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or emergency responders when necessary to address an imminent thermal safety risk. Manufacturers shall provide clear instructions for customers and responders and shall notify affected customers promptly when shut-off is activated. For installed units lacking this capability, manufacturers shall offer a retrofit or replacement pathway and complete it, where technically feasible, within 72 months after adoption.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression appropriate to the unit’s chemistry and installation conditions, validated under a regulator-recognized test protocol. Manufacturers shall document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to customers and regulators. This requirement does not replace other required thermal-safety controls.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers, at purchase and with each material safety update, clear safety notices describing known thermal hazards, required precautions, and how to report an incident. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a 12-month period, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers, submit supporting data and a corrective-action plan, and cooperate with regulator review. Regulators shall assess the evidence and may require a recall or other protective measures where warranted by the risk; manufacturers shall promptly implement any such order. Manufacturers shall provide safe-use or shutdown instructions and explain repair, replacement, or refund options.
