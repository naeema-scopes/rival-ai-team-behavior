# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K06 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, or emergency intervention to the relevant regulator within 5 business days after learning of it. A death, serious injury, evacuation, or material property damage must be reported within 24 hours. Within 10 business days, the manufacturer must provide known incident details and promptly supplement material updates. Affected customers must be notified within 5 business days of identifying a credible product-related safety risk, or within 24 hours for an urgent hazard requiring immediate action.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized 55 °C sustained-heat test before market entry, at least every three years, and after a material design change. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% for any model. A model exceeding 5% must not be marketed until corrective action and retesting demonstrate a failure rate at or below 5%; results and corrective actions must be promptly reported to the regulator. Manufacturers must provide customers with clear, accessible model-specific test results before purchase and updated results after retesting or material changes. Per-model results must be provided to regulators and customers; this section does not require publication to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on all newly installed systems beginning two years after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide secure, authenticated shut-off capability and clear instructions for its use. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance within six years where technically feasible; where retrofit is infeasible, the manufacturer must notify the owner and provide an equivalent documented emergency response plan. Shut-off must not prevent local emergency responders from safely isolating the system.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on all newly manufactured home battery systems beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must ensure the system is compatible with the battery chemistry and installation environment, and provide customers and installers with maintenance and safety instructions. Existing installed systems are not required to be retrofitted under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices for risks that could affect safe installation or operation, including relevant model-specific thermal-test results and any recommended action. Notify affected customers within 5 business days after identifying a credible product-related safety risk, or within 24 hours for an urgent hazard requiring immediate action; provide updates when material information changes. A recall is mandatory when a model’s verified annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units. Manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, explain the hazard and remedy, and track completion. The regulator may require interim protective measures while the rate is verified; verification must not delay urgent customer warnings.
