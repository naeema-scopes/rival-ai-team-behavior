# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A04 · variant A · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 34})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator every known fire, thermal runaway, or overheating event involving a home battery, including events without injury or property damage. Initial notice is due within 24 hours of learning of the event; a written report with known unit and model details, circumstances, and outcomes is due within 10 business days, with updates as material facts emerge. Manufacturers must promptly notify customers whose unit is involved, no later than 5 business days after identifying them. Reports must include incidents even when cause is not yet established. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and describe reasonable steps taken to identify and report events.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with the sample size, results, and failure-rate calculation documented. Per-model results must be submitted to the regulator and made available to relevant customers and installers on request; individual-company per-model failure rates are not publicly published. Results must not be pooled across models. A result above 5% requires prompt regulator review of the verified results and relevant risk evidence. The regulator may require a corrective-action plan where that review identifies a material safety concern. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sales or require a recall. The regulator shall determine proportionate interim measures based on verified results and risk, which may include enhanced monitoring, customer notice, design or production changes, or limits on new production or sales. The model must be retested after corrective action, and the regulator shall determine whether further measures are required.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required for all new units placed on the market beginning 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a secure, authenticated means for an authorized emergency responder or the customer to initiate shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear instructions for use. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide a compatible shut-off capability, by retrofit or other regulator-approved means, within 6 years after adoption. Where a unit cannot safely or technically be upgraded, the manufacturer must notify the customer and regulator, explain the limitation, and provide an approved alternative risk-mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and enclosure, validated under a regulator-approved protocol, and maintained according to the manufacturer's instructions. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions describing system operation, limitations, and any required maintenance. Regulators may approve equivalent protective measures for a specific design where evidence demonstrates comparable or better risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices describing known hazards, required precautions, and any software updates or service actions, and promptly notify affected customers when a material safety risk is identified. Incident rates must be calculated by model using verified installed-unit estimates and reported to the regulator. When the verified rate for any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly initiate a recall. The manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, explain the concern and remedy, and provide instructions for obtaining it. Manufacturers must also promptly carry out any additional safety notice or remedy required by the regulator based on evidence of a material safety risk.
