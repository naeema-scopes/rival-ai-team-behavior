# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A07 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary account of known impacts and circumstances. They must provide a complete incident report within 10 calendar days, updating it as material facts emerge. Within 24 hours of identifying an incident that may affect a specific household or create a safety risk to nearby people, the manufacturer must notify the affected customer and provide practical safety instructions. Reports must include incident counts and installed-unit denominators sufficient for regulators to assess rates.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a prescribed sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after any material design change. A failure rate above 5% triggers a prompt corrective-action plan and retesting; it does not by itself require an automatic market ban. Regulators may set proportionate interim risk controls based on the test results and other relevant safety evidence, and may restrict sales where warranted. Manufacturers must submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator and provide results to customers considering or owning that model. Per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery systems must include a remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within 72 months after adoption. The shut-off must be operable by the manufacturer and, where feasible, by emergency responders, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide customers clear instructions on its use and maintain a documented process for responding promptly to an emergency shut-off request.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must document that the suppression system is appropriate for the battery chemistry and does not create additional unreasonable hazards. Before the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must provide regulators with their implementation and validation plans.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly provide customers with safety notices and practical instructions when new information identifies a risk relevant to their model or household. When a recall is initiated, manufacturers must notify the relevant regulator and affected customers directly within 24 hours, and provide instructions for safe shutdown, repair, replacement, or return. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year. Incident counts and installed-unit denominators must be reported to regulators using consistent methods so the rate can be independently assessed.
