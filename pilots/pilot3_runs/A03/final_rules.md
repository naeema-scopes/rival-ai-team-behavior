# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A03 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the responsible regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of it. Any incident involving death, serious injury, evacuation, or active fire shall be reported within 24 hours. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers within 5 calendar days of reporting, with known hazards, immediate precautions, and contact information; material new information shall be shared promptly. Reports shall identify the model and include available incident details.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. A model’s failure rate shall not exceed 8%. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective-action plans to the responsible regulator and to customers who purchase or own that model within 30 days of testing. Results shall include the protocol and sample size so recipients can interpret them. Regulators may require retesting or corrective action where results indicate a material safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall enable and maintain this function with safeguards against unauthorized use. For installed units that already support the function, manufacturers shall make it available within 12 months after adoption. For installed units that do not support it, manufacturers shall provide a feasible retrofit or replacement plan within 12 months and complete implementation within 6 years after adoption, subject to customer consent and safety.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The system shall be designed and validated under a regulator-approved protocol, with instructions for inspection and maintenance provided to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and at least annually thereafter, customers shall receive known material hazards, safe-use and emergency instructions, and a clear channel for reporting incidents. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions when corrective action is needed. Notices shall explain relevant precautions and actions. Per-model test results and failure rates shall be provided to customers as required by S2; this section does not limit that disclosure. If a model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units, based on regulator-verified model-level data, the regulator shall promptly review the risk and require effective interim controls and customer warnings. Within 30 days, the regulator shall determine whether a recall is necessary; a recall is required if risk remains unacceptable or is not adequately controlled. Regulators may require interim controls or order an immediate recall at any rate where incident severity or pattern indicates imminent risk. Customer warnings and urgent protective actions shall not be delayed.
