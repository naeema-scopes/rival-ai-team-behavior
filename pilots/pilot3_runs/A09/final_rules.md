# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A09 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal runaway, fire, or overheating event that causes injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 calendar days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported to the regulator within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 5 calendar days after determining that an incident presents a material safety risk. Reports must include known circumstances and corrective actions; updates are required as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market introduction and at least every three years thereafter, using a statistically adequate sample and documented methods. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including failure rates, to regulators and provide results to customers who buy or own that model. Regulators must treat submitted results as confidential except as needed for protective action, and may publish aggregate, non-identifying findings. The maximum failure rate is 7% per model, effective seven years after adoption. During the transition, a model exceeding 5% must have a regulator-reviewed corrective-action and retesting plan, with interim safeguards proportionate to risk; exceeding 5% alone does not prohibit market placement during the transition. After the effective date, a model above 7% may not be newly placed on the market until corrective action and a passing retest are completed. Regulators may require earlier action where evidence indicates an imminent risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must support secure remote emergency shut-off by 1 January 2028, subject to safeguards against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. For units installed before that date, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe upgrade within six years of the standard’s effective date where technically feasible, prioritizing higher-risk units and notifying customers of options and limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after the standard’s adoption. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of compliance and must not represent a system as compliant unless the suppression system has been validated for that model.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about material hazards and protective actions promptly after identifying a material safety risk, and no later than five calendar days after that determination. Notices must be sent directly to affected customers and updated when material facts change. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year. The rate must be calculated from verified incident counts and installed-unit exposure for the same model and period, using a documented, consistent method; supporting records must be available to regulators. The manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, explain the hazard and remedy, and provide periodic progress updates. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action when the evidence warrants it.
