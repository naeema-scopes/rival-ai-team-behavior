# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K04 · variant K · NOT adopted (signatures: {})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents initially reported by customers. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident may indicate a safety risk, and provide material updates as the investigation proceeds. Reports must include known facts and be updated when the cause or scope is established.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-recognized protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers must submit per-model failure rates, sample sizes, and test protocols confidentially to regulators, which may share them with other authorities as needed. Regulators may publish aggregate results, but individual-company per-model failure rates and test protocols must not be published to the general public or made available to customers. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt notice to regulators, a corrective-action plan, and retesting on a regulator-approved schedule. Customers must receive prompt notice when results indicate a material safety risk, explaining the hazard and recommended actions. Continued sales while corrective action and retesting are pending are subject to regulator review, taking account of the model’s incident record and interim risk controls.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems must include a remotely accessible emergency shut-off capability. For units installed before this standard’s adoption, manufacturers must provide a compliant shut-off capability or an equivalent regulator-approved emergency isolation method within six years, prioritizing units with identified safety risks. The capability must be available to authorized emergency responders and regulators, with appropriate safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide customers clear instructions for emergency isolation and disclose any limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment, and validated through testing to a regulator-recognized protocol. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any limitations to customers. Existing installed units are not subject to a retrofit requirement unless a regulator determines that a specific model or installation presents an elevated safety risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide affected customers with a clear safety notice within 48 hours of determining that a thermal incident or investigation indicates a safety risk, explaining the hazard, affected models and serial numbers, interim precautions, and how to obtain help. Material updates must be sent as findings change. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop new sales of that model, and implement the regulator-approved recall or remedy.
