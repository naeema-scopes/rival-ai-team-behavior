# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K06 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any battery thermal event involving fire, uncontrolled venting, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. A confirmed thermal event without those consequences must be reported within 5 business days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 24 hours when an event creates an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within 5 business days of confirming a material product hazard. Reports must include known circumstances, model/serial range, and protective actions; updates shall be provided as facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under the jointly specified protocol, including representative operating and fault conditions, with statistically justified sample sizes and independent or accredited-laboratory verification. For the first 36 months after the effective date, the maximum permitted failure rate is 7%; thereafter it is 5%. A model exceeding the applicable limit may not be newly sold until corrective action and retesting demonstrate compliance. Manufacturers shall retain test records and make results available to regulators.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly manufactured systems shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and loss of communications. Manufacturers shall provide a secure software or hardware retrofit for installed units where technically feasible, prioritizing units subject to safety alerts and other higher-risk units, and complete feasible retrofits within 5 years of the standard’s effective date. Manufacturers shall establish annual, risk-prioritized progress milestones proportionate to technical feasibility, and report progress to regulators, including the number of units retrofitted and remaining. Where remote shut-off cannot be safely implemented, the manufacturer shall document the limitation, notify affected customers, and provide an equivalent emergency isolation measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression shall be required on new models first manufactured 36 months after this standard’s effective date. Before that date, manufacturers shall assess and document fire-mitigation performance, including suppression feasibility, and provide customers with clear instructions for emergency response. Suppression systems shall be validated for compatibility with the battery and installed configuration, and maintained in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Regulators may grant a time-limited, publicly documented waiver where a manufacturer demonstrates that an equivalent or greater level of fire-risk reduction is achieved.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, manufacturers shall disclose the model’s key safety limitations, required installation and maintenance, emergency isolation instructions, and applicable test and fire-mitigation information in clear, accessible language. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers and regulators upon learning of a material safety hazard, and provide interim protective actions; notice shall be issued within 24 hours when there is an imminent risk, and otherwise within 5 business days of confirmation. A recall or equivalent corrective action is required when investigation confirms a defect or nonconformity that creates an unreasonable risk of fire, injury, or significant property damage, or when regulators require action. Notices shall identify affected models/serial ranges, explain the risk and remedy, and be updated as material facts emerge.
