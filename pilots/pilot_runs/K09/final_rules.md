# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K09 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery thermal event involving fire, smoke, injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of learning of it, followed by a written report within 10 business days. Any other confirmed thermal event must be reported within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 5 business days after learning of an event that presents a material safety risk to their unit or product line. Reports must include known circumstances, affected models/lots, and immediate precautions; updates must be provided as facts are established.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a published, reproducible protocol reflecting intended installation and operation. A failure is any fire, thermal runaway, venting of hazardous materials, or loss of essential protective functions creating a material hazard. Testing must use a statistically justified sample size, with results retained for regulator review. For models first placed on the market after adoption, a maximum permitted failure rate of 7% applies for the first 12 months after adoption; thereafter the maximum is 3%. Already-certified models may have a maximum permitted failure rate of 7% for up to three years after adoption, after which the maximum is 3%. Any result above 3% requires a documented investigation and corrective-action plan, with progress reported to the regulator. These limits do not restrict regulator authority to require earlier action for a material hazard. The limits apply to model certification and retesting after material design changes.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery systems manufactured 18 months or more after adoption of this standard must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability accessible to the manufacturer and, where technically feasible, the customer or emergency responders. The system must authenticate commands, provide confirmation of shut-off status, and fail safely without disabling essential local protections. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and disclose any limitations. For units already installed, manufacturers must assess whether remote shut-off can be added safely and practicably; where feasible, offer an upgrade within 5 years of adoption. A regulator may require an earlier upgrade or equivalent risk-reduction measures for a product presenting a material hazard.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression or an independently validated equivalent means of limiting fire spread and protecting occupants and neighbouring properties. The measure must be tested under a published, reproducible protocol appropriate to the system’s installation and foreseeable failure modes. Manufacturers must document effectiveness, maintenance requirements, and any limitations, and provide this information to customers and regulators. A regulator may require earlier implementation or interim risk-reduction measures for a product presenting a material hazard.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and at installation, customers must receive clear written information on thermal and fire risks, required clearances and maintenance, alarms and emergency actions, remote shut-off availability and limitations, warranty, and how to report a safety concern. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a newly identified material hazard, provide interim precautions, and make updates as the investigation proceeds. A recall is triggered when a manufacturer or regulator determines that a product defect creates an unreasonable risk of fire, injury, or significant property damage; a recall must include free repair, replacement, or refund and clear instructions, with notice issued without undue delay.
