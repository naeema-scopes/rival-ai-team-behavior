# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K01 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
A manufacturer must report any battery fire, thermal runaway, explosion, emergency-service response, or injury plausibly related to a battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware. A non-fire overheating event requiring evacuation, medical assessment, or repair must be reported within 5 business days. Reports must include known circumstances, affected models and serial numbers, and actions taken; manufacturers must update incomplete reports as facts are established. For a reportable event, the manufacturer must notify affected customers within 5 business days, unless the regulator directs otherwise.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Before certification and after a material thermal-safety design change, representative production units must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under prescribed worst-case operating and charging conditions. The protocol must specify duration, sample selection, and failure criteria; a failure includes fire, thermal runaway, hazardous-material venting, or loss of protective control. At least 60 units must be tested. The failure rate is the number of failed units divided by the number tested; no more than 7.5% may fail, with the permitted number of failures rounded down to a whole unit (for 60 units, no more than 4). If more than 5% fail, the manufacturer must investigate the cause and document corrective actions; any hazardous event during testing requires corrective action before certification.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New systems must provide a secure remote emergency shut-off function that can be initiated by an authorized operator and does not depend on continued internet connectivity to maintain a safe state. Existing installed systems must be upgraded or provided an equivalent safe means of isolation within 7 years of this standard taking effect, where technically feasible. Manufacturers must document exceptions, provide customers with interim safety instructions, and prioritize upgrades for systems presenting greater risk. Shut-off must not disable alarms or safety monitoring.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New systems intended for indoor residential installation must include built-in fire suppression, or an independently verified equivalent measure that limits fire propagation and protects occupants, beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The manufacturer must document the effectiveness of the selected measure under the prescribed battery-fire conditions. Systems installed outdoors or in a dedicated, code-compliant fire-separated enclosure may use an equivalent risk-control design instead of built-in suppression. This requirement does not replace applicable building or fire codes.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers must receive clear information on installation and operating limits, required maintenance, safety warnings, emergency isolation, and how to report a suspected defect or incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety information, corrective actions, and any change to safe operation. For an imminent risk to life or serious injury, notification must be made without delay; otherwise, affected customers must be notified within 5 business days after the manufacturer determines notification is warranted. Incident notifications must meet S1 deadlines. A manufacturer must promptly assess credible evidence of a safety defect and notify the relevant regulator. A recall or equivalent corrective action is required when a defect creates an unreasonable risk of serious injury or fire, when directed by a regulator, or when investigation shows that continued use cannot be made acceptably safe through a less restrictive remedy. Customers must be told how to identify affected units and obtain the remedy at no charge.
