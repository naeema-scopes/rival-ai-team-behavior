# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A09 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Proposed: Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any fire, thermal runaway, or overheating event involving a battery, and any event causing death, injury, evacuation, or material property damage, within 24 hours of learning of it. A fuller report, including known causes and corrective actions, is due within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an event indicates a potential safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include all known events; suspected under-reporting must be corrected promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Each battery design must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C under a common protocol adopted by this standard. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for the first 12 months after adoption, 3% from month 13 through month 24, and 2% from month 25 through month 35. The maximum permitted failure rate is 1% beginning 36 months after adoption. Failure includes fire, thermal runaway, hazardous venting, or loss of containment creating a safety risk. Test samples and acceptance methods must provide adequate statistical confidence, with results and corrective actions recorded for independent audit. The protocol must be consistently applied and reviewed using verified compliance data.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery systems must include a remotely operable emergency shut-off and a secure means for the customer or authorized emergency responders to activate it, effective 24 months after adoption. Within 36 months, manufacturers must provide and complete a safe software or hardware retrofit for installed units that lack this capability. Any unit claimed to be technically infeasible to retrofit must be independently assessed, with the basis documented and the customer notified; the manufacturer must provide an alternative risk-mitigation plan and emergency instructions. Shut-off must not prevent emergency response or create a new hazard; operation and availability must be tested and documented.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems must include built-in fire suppression or an independently verified equivalent fire-mitigation system, effective 24 months after adoption. The system must be validated for the battery’s credible failure modes and must not increase risk to occupants or responders. Manufacturers must document the validation and maintenance requirements. Within 12 months after adoption, manufacturers must assess installed units for equivalent mitigation and notify affected customers of any material residual risk, recommended precautions, and available mitigation. Installed units are not subject to automatic retrofit under this clause. Company-specific estimates shared by the parties put built-in suppression costs at $42 per Northwind unit and about $67 per Halcyon unit; these are not industry-wide cost estimates.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, before purchase and in clear written form, the battery’s safety features and limitations, emergency shut-off instructions, required maintenance, and instructions for responding to an alarm or safety notice. Within 72 hours of learning of an incident that indicates a potential safety risk to a customer’s unit or requires customer action, manufacturers must notify affected customers and explain recommended actions. A recall is required when credible evidence shows a defect or condition creates an unreasonable risk of fire, thermal runaway, injury, or material property damage that cannot be adequately addressed by a warning or repair while the unit remains in service. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers of a recall, provide safe interim instructions, and make the remedy available without charge.
