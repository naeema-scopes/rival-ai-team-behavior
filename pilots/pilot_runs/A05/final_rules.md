# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A05 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, or injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it. They must also report an overheating or other thermal event that requires emergency response, causes property damage, or results in a unit being taken out of service for safety reasons. A follow-up investigation report is due within 30 days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a potential safety risk to them or the public, and promptly provide material updates. Reports must include known incident counts and suspected causes; privacy-protective details may be withheld.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C under the prescribed standard test procedure, using a statistically adequate, independently witnessed sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7% during the first 12 months after adoption, 5% during months 13–24, and 2% thereafter. The initial 7% limit is transitional only and does not waive required investigation or corrective action: any failure requires investigation, documented corrective action, and retesting. A model exceeding the applicable cap may not be certified or remain on sale. Regulators may require more stringent limits where incident evidence warrants.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include remote emergency shut-off no later than 18 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units with remote shut-off capability, or provide an equivalent effective remote emergency isolation method, within 5 years of adoption. Until then, customers must receive clear instructions for safe local isolation. Shut-off functions must be tested and maintained, and activation must not create an additional hazard.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 24 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the battery chemistry and foreseeable failure modes, and must not create an additional hazard. Manufacturers must document verification testing and provide maintenance instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear information on the battery’s thermal and fire risks, required installation and maintenance, emergency isolation instructions, and how to contact the manufacturer. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety findings, corrective actions, and regulator-ordered or voluntary recalls, using direct channels where reasonably available. A recall is triggered when credible evidence indicates a defect or failure mode creates an unreasonable risk of fire, thermal runaway, injury, or property damage; a regulator may require recall or other corrective action. Manufacturers must promptly report the basis, scope, remedy, and customer instructions, and provide progress updates until the remedy is complete.
