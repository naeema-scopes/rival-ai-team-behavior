# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K04 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery overheating, fire, thermal runaway, emergency shutdown caused by a thermal condition, or injury/property damage plausibly linked to a thermal event. Initial notice is due within 72 hours of learning of the event; a completed investigation is due within 30 days, with updates as material facts emerge. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 5 days when an event presents a credible safety risk or requires inspection, operating changes, or service. Reports must include known circumstances and actions taken; uncertainty does not delay initial reporting.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a common, published test protocol, with failures assessed across a representative production sample. The interim maximum failure rate is 7%. A model exceeding the limit may not be newly certified or placed on the market until corrective action and a passing retest are documented. Manufacturers must retain test records and provide them to regulators on request. After 24 months, the parties will review results collected under the common protocol and publish the results, methods, and evidentiary basis for their conclusions. The review must assess whether the limit should be tightened to 5%; retaining a limit above 5% requires published comparable evidence supporting that decision.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly certified home battery systems must support authenticated remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a local means of safe operation. This requirement applies to units installed beginning 36 months after adoption. Existing units are not subject to mandatory retrofit solely to add remote shut-off; manufacturers must disclose whether each installed model supports it and provide compatible upgrades where technically feasible.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Newly manufactured home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression or an integrated suppression system demonstrated to provide equivalent fire-mitigation performance. This requirement applies to units manufactured 36 months after adoption. The system must be designed, installed, and maintained in accordance with the common test protocol adopted for this standard; manufacturers must document performance and provide evidence to regulators on request. The requirement does not mandate retrofit of units manufactured before its effective date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before sale or installation, manufacturers must provide customers clear written information on the system’s thermal and fire safety features, operating limits, emergency actions, maintenance, remote shut-off capability, and how to report a safety concern. For a credible safety risk, customers must be notified promptly of material hazards and recommended actions, consistent with S1. A manufacturer must initiate a recall or equivalent corrective action when it determines, or a regulator determines, that a defect or nonconformity creates an unreasonable risk of serious injury, fire, or substantial property damage. The manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, explain the risk and remedy, and provide progress updates until corrective action is complete. Where a risk may affect nearby households or the public, the manufacturer must coordinate appropriate public notice with the regulator.
