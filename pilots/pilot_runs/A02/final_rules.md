# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A02 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery thermal incident involving overheating, fire, smoke, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a follow-up investigation report within 10 business days. Affected customers must be notified within 48 hours, with immediate notice where there is an ongoing safety risk. Reports must include known incident counts, including incidents not previously reported, and be updated as facts are verified.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Products must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C under a common, independently auditable protocol, with results reported by model. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% of tested units. The protocol must define failure criteria and test duration, require a statistically justified sample size, and report the observed failure rate and its confidence interval. Test conditions and model-specific results must be published. Manufacturers must retest after material design changes and submit results for conformity assessment before placing a model on the market. Because the companies’ available failure-rate figures come from different internal tests, they do not by themselves validate the 5% ceiling; the regulator should review that ceiling against comparable test data and relevant safety evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market 24 months after adoption must provide a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear confirmation of shut-off status. Manufacturers must provide compatible upgrades for installed units where technically feasible and complete upgrades within 48 months of adoption. Where an upgrade is not feasible, the manufacturer must notify the customer, explain the limitation and provide an appropriate alternative emergency procedure. Shut-off must not prevent emergency responders from isolating the system locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery models placed on the market 24 months after adoption must include built-in fire suppression suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment. The manufacturer must provide evidence of performance under the applicable fire and thermal safety tests and disclose maintenance requirements. Existing installed units are not required to be retrofitted solely under this provision; manufacturers must assess and address any model-specific safety risk through customer notification, corrective action, or recall.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear information on material fire and thermal hazards, installation and maintenance requirements, emergency shut-off procedures, remote monitoring or shut-off capabilities, and applicable limitations. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and provide updates on corrective actions. A recall or equivalent corrective action is required when credible evidence indicates a product defect creates a serious risk of fire, injury, or substantial property damage, or when testing or incident data show a model fails applicable safety requirements. Regulators must be notified of the basis, scope, and remedy; customers must receive instructions and timelines for the remedy.
