# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A04 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, smoke event, or overheating event requiring emergency response, evacuation, or medical treatment to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. A written report with known causes, consequences, and corrective actions is due within 10 business days; material updates must be submitted promptly. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 24 hours where continued use may pose an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within 5 business days. Reports must protect personal information and distinguish confirmed facts from preliminary information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Before market entry, each battery model must pass an independently witnessed sustained-heat test at 55°C, using a published, reproducible protocol representative of foreseeable installation and operating conditions. The protocol, sampling plan, and statistical method must be validated by the relevant regulator and provide 95% confidence that the failure rate does not exceed 1% of tested units. Any fire, thermal runaway, or hazardous release is a failure. Results, sample size, and corrective actions must be retained and made available to regulators. Models already marketed when this standard is adopted must meet these requirements within 24 months. Within 90 days of adoption, manufacturers must submit to regulators a corrective-action and retesting plan for each such model; regulators may require interim customer warnings or other risk controls where warranted.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery systems must provide a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or an authorized emergency responder when a credible imminent safety risk exists. Manufacturers must enable the capability on all already-installed compatible units within 24 months of this standard’s adoption. For units that cannot be enabled remotely, manufacturers must notify affected customers within that period and provide a practical safe shut-off procedure. Shut-off must not prevent emergency responders from isolating the system locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems must include built-in fire suppression, or an independently validated integrated alternative that provides equivalent protection against fire propagation and hazardous release. This requirement applies to units manufactured 24 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the system’s performance under relevant fire and thermal-runaway conditions and provide the documentation to regulators on request.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers must receive clear information on safe installation and operation, foreseeable hazards, emergency shut-off and response procedures, and how to report a safety concern. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified safety risks and corrective actions; notification is due within 24 hours when continued use may pose an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within 5 business days. A recall is required when a defect or noncompliance creates an unreasonable risk of serious injury, fire, or property damage, or when ordered by a regulator. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, explain the risk and remedy, and provide progress updates until the remedy is complete.
