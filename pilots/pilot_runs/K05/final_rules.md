# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K05 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery overheating, fire, or thermal runaway incident to the relevant regulator. A death, injury requiring medical treatment, fire spreading beyond the battery enclosure, or emergency-service response must be reported within 24 hours of awareness; other thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 10 calendar days, with immediate notice where continued use presents an imminent hazard. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial numbers, and actions taken; material updates must follow promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C using a common, documented, repeatable protocol, with results recorded and made available to regulators on request. Testing must use a representative sample and be repeated after a material design or manufacturing change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 6% during the first 24 months after adoption and 5.5% thereafter. The regulator must review the post-transition ceiling after 36 months, considering representative test results, protocol consistency, and safety evidence, and may set a stricter ceiling based on that evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Each new battery system installed 18 months or more after this standard’s adoption must provide a secure remote emergency shut-off capability accessible to the manufacturer and authorized emergency responders, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible means of shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a plan to enable compatible already-installed systems within 5 years of adoption, where technically feasible, and notify affected customers of limitations or required upgrades. A system may be exempt from remote shut-off where the manufacturer demonstrates to the regulator that it would create a greater safety risk; the exemption must be documented and customers must be told how to shut the system off locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems manufactured 36 months or more after this standard’s adoption must include built-in fire suppression, or an independently validated integrated measure providing equivalent protection against fire propagation beyond the battery enclosure. Manufacturers must document the measure’s performance and provide the documentation to regulators on request. This requirement does not replace thermal testing, incident reporting, or other fire-safety obligations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear information on thermal and fire risks, required siting and maintenance, emergency shut-off instructions, and any limitations of remote shut-off or suppression features. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a material safety defect, recommended precautions, and available remedies; notice must be immediate where continued use presents an imminent hazard. A manufacturer must initiate a recall or equivalent regulator-approved corrective action when it determines, or a regulator determines, that a defect or noncompliance creates an unreasonable risk of serious injury or fire. The manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers without undue delay and report the scope, remedy, and completion progress.
