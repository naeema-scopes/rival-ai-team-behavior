# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K10 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, evacuation, injury, or property damage within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary incident report within 5 business days and a final investigation report within 30 days. Customers must be notified within 24 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk to their unit or product batch, with prompt safety instructions and updates as facts become available. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and submit an annual summary of reportable incidents and corrective actions.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Each battery model must pass a sustained-heat test at 55°C using a common, independently witnessed protocol that defines exposure duration, sample selection, and failure criteria. A failure includes fire, thermal runaway, venting of hazardous material, or loss of essential protective functions. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% of tested units. For models already being sold when this standard takes effect, manufacturers have 12 months to complete testing, corrective actions, and retesting; during this period they must maintain a documented corrective-action plan and promptly address any urgent safety concern. After the transition period, models exceeding the limit may not be sold until they pass. New models must meet the limit before sale. Results and corrective actions must be documented before certification and after any material design change. The parties should review the protocol and limit as field and test evidence accumulates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability that can place the battery in a safe state when directed by an authorized operator, while preserving local emergency controls. Manufacturers must document cybersecurity safeguards, availability, and safe-state behavior. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide an upgrade path and complete upgrades where technically feasible within 5 years; any infeasibility and interim risk controls must be disclosed to the regulator and affected customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression or an independently validated equivalent fire-mitigation system, designed to limit propagation and protect occupants and neighboring properties. This requirement applies to units manufactured 36 months after adoption of this standard. Before that date, manufacturers must document fire-mitigation performance and provide installation and emergency-response guidance. Any claimed equivalent must be supported by testing under the common protocol and accepted by the relevant regulator.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, manufacturers must provide customers with clear instructions on installation, safe operation, emergency response, known material hazards, and how to report an incident. When credible new information indicates a material safety risk, manufacturers must notify affected customers within 24 hours of determining that the risk is material, explaining the risk, affected models or batches, recommended actions, and how updates will be provided. A recall or equivalent corrective action is required when the manufacturer or regulator determines that a defect creates an unreasonable risk of serious injury, fire, or property damage. If urgent protective action is warranted, manufacturers must promptly issue interim safety instructions while the remedy is developed. Manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers of the corrective action, explain available remedies, report completion, preserve supporting safety records, and cooperate with regulators.
