# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K03 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Report any battery fire, thermal runaway, evacuation, injury, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of awareness, with a fuller investigation report within 30 days. Notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 72 hours after awareness, when an incident presents a potential safety risk to their unit or household. Submit annual aggregate incident data, including confirmed thermal incidents and reporting status, with installed-unit denominators and consistent definitions sufficient to compare incident rates and identify incidents not reported within the required timeframe.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Each model must pass a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under the prescribed industry test protocol. The maximum permitted failure rate is 10% of tested units during the initial compliance period; it tightens to 5% after three years. Test results and sample size must be documented and made available to regulators.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on newly manufactured units beginning two years after adoption. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance within five years, where technically feasible; manufacturers must provide a documented upgrade plan and safe alternative response procedure where remote shut-off cannot be retrofitted.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include a built-in fire-suppression system, or an independently validated equivalent means of limiting fire propagation, beginning three years after adoption. The system or equivalent must be evaluated under the prescribed industry test protocol and documented for regulators. Manufacturers may seek a narrowly scoped, time-limited waiver where they demonstrate that the requirement would compromise safety or that an equivalent protection is achieved; waivers and supporting evidence must be public.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, provide customers clear written information on installation, safe operation, emergency response, known material risks, and how to report a safety concern. When a material safety risk is identified, notify affected customers promptly and no later than 72 hours after awareness, and provide updated instructions and mitigation steps. A recall is triggered when a confirmed defect or failure pattern presents an unreasonable risk of fire, thermal runaway, injury, or evacuation; manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers promptly and no later than 24 hours after the recall decision, stop distribution of affected units, and provide an effective repair, replacement, or refund plan. Regulators may require a recall or other corrective action based on the evidence.
