# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K07 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator every known battery-related fire, thermal runaway, uncontrolled venting, or overheating event, whether or not it caused injury, property damage, or an emergency response, and whether or not the cause has been confirmed. Initial notice is due within 72 hours of becoming aware; a substantive update is due within 30 days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within five calendar days of awareness when the event presents a material safety risk or requires customer action, and provide updates as new safety information becomes available. Reports must include the event date, known circumstances, and any protective action taken or planned.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Each covered battery design must pass a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a common industry protocol, with representative production units and failure criteria covering fire, thermal runaway, hazardous venting, and loss of essential protective functions. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7% of tested units; this is a ceiling, not a target. Manufacturers must retain test records and provide them to regulators on request, and must retest after material safety-relevant design or production changes. The protocol and limit should be reviewed at least every three years as evidence accumulates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems must provide a secure remote emergency shut-off capability that can be initiated by an authorized operator, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a clear means to verify shut-off status. For systems already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must submit a retrofit or equivalent-safe-mitigation plan to the relevant regulator within 24 months and complete implementation within seven years, prioritizing systems presenting the greatest risk. Where remote shut-off is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must document the basis and provide an effective local emergency isolation method and clear instructions for responders and occupants. Regulators may require additional safeguards where the proposed mitigation is not comparably protective.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to limit fire propagation and reduce risks to occupants and neighbouring properties. The requirement applies to units manufactured 36 months after this standard takes effect, allowing time for design validation and production changes. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance, maintenance needs, and limitations and provide that information to customers and regulators on request. A regulator may approve an equivalent protective design where evidence demonstrates comparable or better fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before sale and at installation, manufacturers must provide customers with clear instructions on safe installation and operation, warning signs, emergency response and isolation, applicable remote shut-off features, and how to report a safety concern. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and any action needed to protect people or property. A recall or equivalent corrective action is required when a defect or nonconformity creates a material risk of fire, thermal runaway, hazardous venting, injury, or property damage; manufacturers must notify the relevant regulator without undue delay, explain the basis and scope, and provide customers with clear remedy instructions. Customer notices must be issued as soon as practicable and no later than five calendar days after the manufacturer determines customer action is needed.
