# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K02 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, uncontrolled overheating, injury, evacuation, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. Other thermal incidents requiring emergency service or causing a unit to shut down must be reported within 5 business days. Reports must include known circumstances and affected model/serial ranges; manufacturers must update them as facts become available. The manufacturer must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 5 business days after learning of an incident that presents a credible safety risk. Aggregate incident counts and corrective actions must be submitted annually.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature and maximum allowed failure rate
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a common, regulator-approved protocol, with the test duration, sample size, and failure definition specified in that protocol. A model passes if no more than 7% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must retain test records and provide them to regulators on request. New models must comply before sale; existing models must be tested and brought into compliance within 24 months of the standard taking effect. Regulators should review the threshold and protocol as multi-company field and laboratory evidence accumulates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models must provide a secure remote emergency shut-off capability that can be initiated by the customer and, where legally authorized and necessary to address an imminent hazard, by emergency responders or the manufacturer. The capability must fail safely, preserve local manual shut-off, and not depend solely on continuous internet access. New models must comply within 24 months of the standard taking effect. For installed units, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent risk-reduction plan within 3 years, prioritizing higher-risk units; regulators may approve technically justified alternatives or extensions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is not required on every new home battery solely by virtue of this standard. Each model must demonstrate, through the common regulator-approved fire and thermal propagation protocol, that a single-cell failure will not cause fire to spread beyond the unit or create an uncontrolled hazard to occupants or neighbors. A model that cannot demonstrate this performance must include built-in suppression or an independently validated equivalent mitigation before sale. Manufacturers must document the evidence and provide it to regulators on request. Regulators should review this performance-based requirement as test and field evidence accumulates.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what customers must be told and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear information on material fire and thermal risks, required clearances and installation conditions, safe operation, warning signs, emergency actions, shut-off methods, and available software or safety updates. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers, and no later than 5 business days after learning of a credible safety risk, explaining the risk and protective steps; urgent hazards require immediate notice by the fastest practicable means. A recall or equivalent corrective action is required when an incident, investigation, or test establishes a defect or nonconformity that creates an unreasonable risk of fire, injury, evacuation, or material property damage. Manufacturers must promptly inform regulators and affected customers of the remedy and schedule; regulators may require a recall where the manufacturer’s action is inadequate.
