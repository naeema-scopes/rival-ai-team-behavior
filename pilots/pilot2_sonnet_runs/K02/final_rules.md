# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K02 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, evacuation, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary account and known unit/model identifiers. They must provide a complete investigation report within 30 days, updating it as new findings emerge. Customers whose unit was involved must be notified promptly and no later than 24 hours after the initial regulator report. Manufacturers must retain incident records and report aggregate incident counts to regulators annually.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market introduction and after a material design or manufacturing change. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% of tested units. A model exceeding this limit must undergo corrective action and retesting. It may not be newly introduced or continue to be sold unless it meets the limit or the regulator approves a time-limited remediation plan that specifies interim risk controls and a retest schedule; such approval may permit continued sale during remediation. Manufacturers must submit test methods, sample sizes, failure criteria, and per-model results to the relevant regulator. Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s compliance status, its exact per-model failure rate, and a plain-language explanation of relevant thermal-test findings and safety implications. Exact per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public, but must not be withheld from customers or regulators.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within six years of adoption, where technically feasible, and must notify affected customers of the schedule and any interim emergency procedures. Any unit for which retrofit is not technically feasible must be reported to the regulator with the reason and proposed alternative risk controls.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence that the suppression system is suitable for the unit and does not introduce additional material safety risks.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information covering thermal risks, installation and operating requirements, emergency steps, and available shut-off features. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified hazards and recommended protective actions. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator upon identifying that the threshold is exceeded and begin the recall without delay, in accordance with the regulator’s directions. Manufacturers must report the incident-rate calculation and supporting data to regulators and notify affected customers without delay.
