# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K05 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety defect or requires customer action, and promptly update them as material facts change. Reports must include the model, incident date and circumstances, known injuries or damage, and actions taken. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and may not omit incidents because the cause is still under investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results, including the failure rate and test protocol, to the regulator and to customers purchasing that model, before purchase. Retesting is required after material design changes and at least every three years. A model that exceeds the limit may not be newly marketed until it passes a confirmatory test and the regulator approves corrective action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or regulator. The capability must fail safely, preserve local manual operation, and provide clear notice of activation. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within six years of adoption, prioritizing units with known defects or elevated risk. If a unit cannot be safely retrofitted, the manufacturer must offer a replacement or other regulator-approved remedy.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance and reliability requirements. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of compliance and disclose the suppression system’s operation and maintenance requirements to customers. Units installed before the effective date are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide purchasers with a safety notice covering thermal risks, warning signs, safe response steps, and support contacts at sale and whenever material safety guidance changes. They must promptly notify affected customers of a confirmed safety defect and provide instructions and remedies. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, submit model-level incident data, and initiate a mandatory recall. The manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers, provide instructions and remedies including repair, replacement, or return, and report material updates and recall completion to the regulator. Public communications may describe the risk and remedy without publishing individual per-model test failure rates.
