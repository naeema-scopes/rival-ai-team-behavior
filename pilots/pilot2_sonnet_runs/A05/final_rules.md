# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A05 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents that cause no injury or property damage. They must provide affected customers with an incident notice within 72 hours, and promptly update the regulator and affected customers as material facts become known. Reports must identify the model, incident date and known consequences; manufacturers must retain incident records and report annually the number of incidents and any known reporting gaps.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under the test protocol published with this standard. A model may be marketed only if no more than 6.5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model results to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model, retain supporting records for inspection, and disclose the test date and protocol with the results. Public reporting may include aggregate results, but not an individual company’s per-model failure rate. A model exceeding the limit must not be marketed until it passes a subsequent test. The 6.5% limit is an interim threshold; regulators should review it after three years using accumulated test and incident data.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models must support remote emergency shut-off when first marketed. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide remote shut-off capability through an update where technically feasible and complete deployment within 60 months of adoption. If a unit cannot support the capability, the manufacturer must document the reason, notify its owner, and provide a safe alternative procedure. Shut-off must not be activated except in an emergency or with the owner's informed consent.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is not required on new units at adoption. New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression meeting the test protocol published with this standard. Manufacturers must document compliance and provide the supporting information to regulators on request.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s latest 55 °C test result, test date and protocol, known material safety risks, and clear instructions for responding to an incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material new risks and provide recall or corrective-action instructions. A mandatory recall is triggered when validated evidence shows that any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Recall remedies may include repair, retrofit, replacement, or removal; the regulator determines the appropriate remedy. A regulator may also require a recall where the severity or pattern of incidents warrants immediate action. Manufacturers must report recall actions and their basis to regulators and affected customers.
