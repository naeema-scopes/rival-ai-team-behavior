# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K08 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must promptly notify affected customers when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and in all cases within 72 hours of identifying that risk. Reports must include model, incident date and location, known consequences, and available unit and incident details. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and provide an annual summary to the regulator.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized, independently verifiable sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter. For the first two years after adoption, the maximum permitted failure rate is 8% of tested units for any model. Manufacturers must report identifiable per-model results confidentially to the regulator and provide results for the purchased model directly to its customers; individual-company per-model results need not be published to the general public. A manufacturer must submit a corrective-action plan when a model exceeds the applicable maximum permitted failure rate. After two years, the regulator should review the limit using independently verified test and incident data and set the subsequent maximum failure rate. Test methods, sample sizes, and failure criteria must be standardized and independently verifiable.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for the household to initiate shut-off and for an authorized emergency responder to request shut-off when an imminent safety risk exists. For installed units that do not support this capability, manufacturers must provide and install a compatible upgrade, or an equivalent safe remedy, within 72 months of adoption where technically feasible; otherwise they must notify affected customers and the regulator and provide a documented alternative safety plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers must provide evidence that the system is suitable for the battery chemistry and does not create additional material hazards.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety and emergency-response instructions at purchase and whenever material safety guidance changes. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a confirmed safety risk and provide practical steps to reduce risk while a remedy is arranged. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly report the exceedance and supporting data to the regulator, which must assess the evidence, including incident severity and available remedies. A recall is mandatory when the regulator determines the model presents an unacceptable safety risk; the regulator may require one at a lower incident rate where severity or other evidence warrants. Manufacturers must notify affected customers and promptly offer a safe remedy when a recall is required.
