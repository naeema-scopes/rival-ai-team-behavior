# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K09 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers of a credible safety risk or incident affecting their model within 48 hours of becoming aware, providing known hazards and immediate safety instructions. Reporting to regulators does not replace customer notification.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. Manufacturers must submit the protocol, sample size, and numerical per-model results to the relevant regulator, and disclose the protocol, sample size, and numerical per-model failure rate to customers before purchase. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7.5% at 55 °C. A model exceeding this limit must not be sold until corrective action and a passing retest are completed. Results above 5% must be reported to the regulator with a corrective-action plan; regulators may require additional testing or corrective action based on results. Retesting is required after material design changes.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off before sale. Manufacturers must enable the capability on installed units that support it and provide owners clear instructions. For installed units not currently supporting remote shut-off, manufacturers must provide an equivalent safe means of emergency isolation and a plan for addressing the gap; requirements for any retrofit must account for technical feasibility and be set by the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document compliance for each model and provide that information to regulators and customers before sale.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s applicable safety notices, material known risks, non-numeric safety-status information, and relevant safety instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of new material safety information, incidents, corrective actions, and recall instructions. A model must be recalled when its thermal incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, based on regulator-verified data; manufacturers must notify the regulator and affected customers and initiate the recall without delay.
