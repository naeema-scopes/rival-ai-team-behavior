# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A06 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary incident report. They must provide a fuller investigation report within 30 days, or explain the delay and provide updates. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours when the incident indicates a credible safety risk or requires customer action. Reports must include known unit/model identifiers, event circumstances, and any immediate safety measures.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a validated sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry, at least once every three years, and after a material design or manufacturing change. Manufacturers must provide test methods, sample size, and individual per-model results to the relevant regulator and to customers of that model. Regulators may share results confidentially with participating manufacturers for safety purposes and may publish anonymised or aggregated results, but not individual company per-model failure rates. A model with a failure rate above 5% must have a regulator-reviewed corrective-action and enhanced-monitoring plan, with retesting within 12 months. A model above 7.5% must not be placed on the market unless and until corrective action and retesting demonstrate a rate at or below 7.5%. Regulators may require additional corrective action, retesting, or suspension of sales where warranted by the evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off that can be initiated by the manufacturer and, where practicable, by emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide secure access and procedures for use during an imminent safety threat. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance within six years of adoption where technically feasible; otherwise, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and the regulator, explain the limitation, and provide an equivalent risk-reduction plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression meeting applicable performance and safety requirements, beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document suppression performance and maintenance requirements and disclose them to customers and regulators. Until the effective date, manufacturers must assess fire-spread risk and implement appropriate design or installation mitigations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices when a known hazard may affect their unit, promptly explaining the risk, required precautions, and available remedy; urgent notices must be sent without delay. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers promptly, provide instructions and a remedy, and report recall progress. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action for an imminent serious risk even below the numerical trigger.
