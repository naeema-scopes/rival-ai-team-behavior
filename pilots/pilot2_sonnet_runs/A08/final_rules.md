# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A08 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the regulator and notify affected customers of any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, hospitalization, evacuation, or property damage within 24 hours of learning of it. Other overheating incidents that require emergency intervention or cause a unit to shut down must be reported to the regulator within 5 business days and disclosed to affected customers within 10 business days. Reports must include the model, incident date and known circumstances; manufacturers must promptly supplement incomplete reports. Reporting obligations apply regardless of whether the incident is confirmed to be caused by the battery.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. The maximum allowed failure rate is 7%. Manufacturers must provide complete identifiable per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the regulator and an independent accredited testing body. Before purchase, customers must receive the same complete identifiable per-model results, as well as pass/fail status and relevant safety information. Manufacturers must notify the regulator and affected customers of failures and corrective actions; the regulator may require retesting and interim safeguards.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off within 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide safe, accessible shut-off instructions to customers and ensure the capability does not prevent emergency responders from isolating a system. For installed units, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe shut-off capability within 72 months where technically feasible, and disclose any limitations to affected customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on all new home battery units placed on the market beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment and must be validated through independent safety testing. Manufacturers must provide customers and regulators with installation, maintenance, and operational instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s pass/fail status for 55 °C testing, relevant safety instructions, known material risks, and instructions for reporting incidents. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of safety incidents, corrective actions, and any changes to safe use; urgent hazards require notice within 24 hours of learning of them. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when a model’s confirmed thermal-incident rate exceeds 10 per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when the regulator determines that a defect presents an imminent serious risk. The rate must be calculated for the individual model using its confirmed thermal incidents and installed-unit denominator; a company-wide rate does not by itself trigger a model recall. The manufacturer must provide the regulator with the data and analysis supporting its calculation.
