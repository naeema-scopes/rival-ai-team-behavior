# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K03 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency shut-off to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary incident report. A complete report, including known causes and corrective actions, is due within 10 business days; material new information shall be reported promptly. Manufacturers shall notify affected owners within 72 hours when an incident presents a potential safety risk to their unit or requires action, and promptly provide updates as the investigation proceeds. Reports shall include incidents regardless of whether injury or property damage occurred.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after a material design or manufacturing change. The 5% failure rate is the maximum for unrestricted sales. A model exceeding 5% shall promptly submit corrective actions and a retesting schedule to the regulator. The regulator may permit continued or limited sales during a corrective-action period, subject to monitoring and any interim safeguards it requires; the period shall not exceed 12 months, after which sales must stop unless regulator-approved retesting demonstrates a rate at or below 5%. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results and corrective-action plans to regulators and provide per-model test results to customers purchasing that model. Individual rates need not be published to the general public; regulators may publish aggregated, non-identifying results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models shall include a remotely operable emergency shut-off that can place the unit in a safe state, subject to applicable electrical-safety requirements. Manufacturers shall provide a secure means for authorized emergency response and shall document limitations, fallback procedures, and loss-of-communications behavior. For units already installed when this standard is adopted, manufacturers shall provide compatible remote shut-off capability, or an equivalent safe mitigation where retrofit is not technically feasible, within 6 years of adoption. Customers shall be informed of the capability and how to request or use it.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the unit’s foreseeable thermal runaway and fire hazards. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide evidence of validation to regulators and disclose the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations to customers. Units manufactured before the effective date are not subject to this requirement.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive clear safety information covering installation and siting, operating limits, emergency shut-off, warning signs, and actions to take during an incident. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and provide instructions for safe use, repair, or cessation of use. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, calculated using a consistent method and verified by the regulator, or when the regulator determines that a defect presents an unreasonable risk of serious injury or fire. Manufacturers shall notify the regulator within 24 hours of learning that a threshold may have been exceeded, provide supporting data, and promptly follow the regulator’s recall direction. Affected customers shall be notified within 72 hours after the recall decision and given clear repair, replacement, or refund instructions. The regulator shall determine the appropriate remedy and may require interim safety measures; review of the remedy shall not suspend the mandatory recall trigger.
