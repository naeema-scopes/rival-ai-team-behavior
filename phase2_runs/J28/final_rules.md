# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J28 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every fire, thermal runaway, or other incident involving overheating to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it, whether or not it caused injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response. A fatality or incident presenting an ongoing serious hazard must be reported within 24 hours. The report must include known circumstances and actions taken, with follow-up information provided promptly. Manufacturers must notify affected owners within 5 business days when an incident indicates a material safety risk to their model, and provide any regulator-required follow-up information promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 8% per model. Manufacturers must provide the responsible regulator with per-model results, including the failure rate and test date, and must provide those results to customers who purchase that model before purchase. Results need not be disclosed to the general public. A model exceeding the limit must submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator within 60 days and pass a retest within 12 months. Pending retest, the regulator may require proportionate interim protections based on the risk; failure to meet the retest deadline may result in suspension of new sales.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery models placed on the market after 24 months from adoption must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must offer compatible upgrades or replacements and complete the retrofit program within 6 years, prioritizing units subject to safety alerts or located in higher-risk settings. Where a retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must provide an equivalent safety measure or replacement.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units placed on the market 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed to limit fire propagation, validated through a regulator-approved test protocol, and maintained according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Manufacturers must provide clear information on the system’s operation and limitations. Any claimed exemption must be supported by evidence of an equally effective built-in fire-mitigation measure and approved by the regulator.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive safety notices, applicable safety limitations, installation and maintenance requirements, emergency-response instructions, and the model’s per-model thermal test failure rate and test date. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected owners of material safety risks and provide updated notices when new information changes recommended actions. Manufacturers must provide per-model thermal test results to the regulator. When a verified model-specific rate exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly refer the matter to the regulator for review, including data quality and statistical uncertainty, and implement any interim protections required. A regulator-supervised recall is mandatory when a verified rate exceeds 10 per 100,000 in a year or the regulator determines that the model presents an unacceptable safety risk. The recall plan must specify customer notification, safe shutdown or other interim protections, and repair, replacement, or refund.
