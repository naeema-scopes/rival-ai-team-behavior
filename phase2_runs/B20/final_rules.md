# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B20 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and submit an incident report with available details within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their units, and provide updates as material facts become available. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known injuries or damage, and actions taken. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and provide them to regulators on request.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt reporting to the responsible regulator and a corrective-action plan; the model must be retested before claiming compliance with the 5% benchmark. A failure rate above 8% is not permitted. Per-model results, including sample size, failure count, and failure rate, must be provided to the responsible regulator and to customers buying or considering that model. Individual per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market after 1 January 2028 must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible means to shut down the unit. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway for installed units where technically feasible. Any required retrofit of installed units must be completed within at least six years from the effective date of this standard.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured or first placed on the market 36 months after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and maintenance standard. Units placed on the market before that date are not subject to this requirement.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices when a credible risk affects their model, including the nature of the risk, affected units, steps to reduce risk, and available remedy; notices must be issued promptly and updated as material facts emerge. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers and initiate a recall of that model. The manufacturer must submit an investigation and corrective-action plan, and take regulator-directed interim risk-reduction measures while the recall is carried out.
