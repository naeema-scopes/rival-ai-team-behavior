# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A44 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary account and available unit/model information. A complete investigation report shall follow within 30 days, with updates for material findings. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk, and promptly provide safe-use instructions and any necessary protective action. Reports shall include incidents regardless of whether the manufacturer considers the unit defective.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a consistent, regulator-approved protocol before market entry and after material design changes. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7% of tested units per model. Models exceeding 5% shall undergo an independent review, corrective-action plan, and repeat testing within 12 months; models exceeding 7% may not be newly marketed until they pass. An independent laboratory shall provide per-model results to the manufacturer and relevant regulators. Manufacturers shall disclose each model’s test failure rate and relevant safety information to customers considering or using that model, with protective guidance. Individual-company results need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on new units manufactured beginning 24 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers shall provide a retrofit path for installed units and complete retrofits within 6 years, prioritizing units with elevated risk and notifying affected customers of availability and safe-use guidance.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on all new units manufactured beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers shall provide customers with safe-use and maintenance instructions for the suppression system. Units manufactured before the effective date are not subject to this requirement unless subsequently redesigned as a new model.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide affected customers clear safety notices promptly when a credible safety risk is identified, including the nature of the risk, affected models or units, safe-use instructions, and available protective actions. Incident rates shall be calculated annually per model using installed-unit counts and verified thermal incidents. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, investigate the cause, and submit a corrective-action plan with progress updates. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when the regulator determines that the model presents an imminent serious safety risk. Upon a recall trigger, manufacturers shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, explain the remedy, and provide regular progress updates until the recall is complete.
