# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K38 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or household, and provide updates as material facts emerge. Reports must include the model, unit location, known harm, and immediate safety steps. Suspected incidents shall be logged and investigated; uncertainty does not defer the initial report.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Before sale and at least annually thereafter, the manufacturer shall submit the per-model test protocol, sample size, results, and corrective actions to the regulator and the standard’s compliance reviewer. Per-model results shall not be published to the general public. A model with a failure rate above 5% shall undergo regulator-supervised corrective action and retesting on a specified timetable. A model shall not be sold if its failure rate exceeds 7%; it may resume sale only after retesting establishes a rate of 7% or less.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units shall include a secure remote emergency shut-off function no later than 24 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers shall provide clear instructions for use and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed when the standard takes effect, manufacturers shall make a compatible retrofit available and complete required retrofits within 6 years, prioritizing units with known safety concerns. Shut-off capability shall not replace required local emergency procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression in accordance with a regulator-approved performance and maintenance protocol. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall disclose the suppression system’s limitations and required maintenance to purchasers. Units manufactured before the effective date are not subject to this requirement.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive clear, accessible safety information for the specific model, including its latest 55 °C sustained-heat test results, known thermal hazards, installation and maintenance requirements, emergency instructions, and any material safety notices or corrective actions. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers and regulators of newly identified material risks and provide instructions for protective action. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, calculated using a regulator-approved method. The manufacturer shall notify the regulator and affected customers promptly, submit a corrective-action plan, and carry out the regulator-approved recall remedy, which may include repair, replacement, or refund.
