# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G17 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled heating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days after learning of it. Incidents involving death, serious injury, evacuation, or an active threat to neighbouring properties must be reported within 24 hours. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a material safety risk or requires action; urgent protective instructions must be sent without delay. Reports must include known circumstances and be updated as the investigation proceeds.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale, at least every 2 years, and after a material design or manufacturing change. Manufacturers must provide per-model results and test methods to the relevant regulator and provide each model’s failure rate to customers before purchase. Results above 5% require prompt regulator review and a corrective-action plan, including retesting and any interim safeguards the regulator determines necessary. A model must not be automatically barred from sale solely for exceeding 5%; continued sale is subject to regulator oversight and protective measures proportionate to the demonstrated risk. Per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must support remote emergency shut-off by 1 January 2029, subject to safeguards against unauthorized operation and provision for safe local operation. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or equivalent mitigation within 6 years, where technically feasible, and notify owners of the available options and any limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard is adopted. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and designed not to create additional hazards. Manufacturers must provide evidence of its effectiveness and maintenance requirements to the relevant regulator and disclose those requirements to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its per-model thermal test failure rate, material risks, required maintenance, emergency instructions, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide protective instructions without delay. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. Manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, explain the remedy and timeline, and report progress until the recall is complete. A recall requires prompt protective action and corrective measures; following regulator review and verification that the risk is adequately addressed, the model may return to sale.
