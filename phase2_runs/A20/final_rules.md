# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A20 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, injury, or emergency-service response to the regulator within 5 calendar days after learning of it, and provide a follow-up investigation report within 30 days. A serious incident involving death, life-threatening injury, or an ongoing hazard must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly when an incident indicates a material safety risk; notices must explain immediate precautions and how to report further incidents. Reports must include known incidents, including those initially reported through other channels.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least every 3 years thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers must submit numerical per-model results to regulators and disclose the numerical failure rate to customers who buy that model. A failure rate above 5% triggers regulator review and a documented corrective-action plan, including retesting and appropriate customer protections. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sale; regulators shall determine proportionate measures based on demonstrated risk and progress on corrective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a means for customers or emergency responders to request shut-off. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions on use and limitations. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide a compatible remote shut-off capability or an equivalent safe retrofit within 6 years after adoption of this standard, where technically feasible; otherwise, they must notify affected customers and regulators and provide a documented risk-mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations to customers. Regulators may approve an equivalent fire-mitigation design where evidence demonstrates comparable or better safety performance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear installation, operating, emergency-response, and maintenance safety instructions, plus prompt direct notice of any newly identified material hazard affecting their unit. If a model’s thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 units of that model installed during a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting data. The regulator must review the risk and require a documented corrective-action plan and customer protections as appropriate. A recall is mandatory when the regulator determines that a serious risk cannot be adequately controlled through corrective measures, or that required corrective measures have not been completed. The annual rate must be calculated using thermal incidents involving that model during the year and its installed-unit population; the manufacturer must document the data and calculation. Recall notices must identify affected units, explain the hazard and required action, and be sent directly to customers and relevant regulators.
