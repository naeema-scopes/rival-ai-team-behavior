# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J30 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to them or the public, and promptly update them as material facts emerge. Reports must include known circumstances, injuries or damage, and model and unit identification; uncertainty is not a reason to delay an initial report.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market release and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit test methods, sample sizes, and per-model results confidentially to the relevant regulator and provide the model’s results to its customers before sale and promptly after each new test. A model meets this standard only if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Results above 5% require a corrective-action plan and regulatory review before further production; a test result alone does not constitute an automatic permanent market ban. Regulators may publish aggregate results, but not company-specific per-model failure rates. Manufacturers must promptly correct and communicate material errors in reported results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off upon adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to support remote emergency shut-off, where technically feasible, within 6 years of adoption. The shut-off capability must be reliable, secure, and operable by the manufacturer and authorized emergency responders; its use must not prevent safe manual operation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation, and tested under a regulator-approved protocol.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale and promptly after any material change, manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices for their model, including current 55 °C test results, known thermal-incident information, and relevant safe-use instructions. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly of a credible safety risk and provide updates as material facts emerge. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, and must promptly notify the relevant regulator and affected customers of the recall and required actions. The recall must include regulator-approved protective and corrective measures; a model may return to sale only after the regulator determines that the required measures have adequately addressed the safety risk.
