# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J19 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, explosion, evacuation, or injury within 24 hours of learning of it, and any other confirmed thermal incident within 5 business days. Initial reports may be preliminary; manufacturers must provide material updates and a final root-cause report within 30 days, or explain the delay. Within 5 business days, manufacturers must notify affected customers of an incident involving their unit or model when it presents a credible safety risk, with interim protective instructions where appropriate. Reports must include the date, model, unit identifier where available, outcome, and known contributing factors.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized 55 °C sustained-heat test before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved sample size and failure definition. Manufacturers must submit each model’s results, sample sizes, failures, and corrective actions to the regulator, and provide the per-model results to customers who buy or own that model; these results are not required to be publicly disclosed. A failure rate above 5% triggers a corrective-action plan within 90 days and retesting on a regulator-approved schedule, with interim safeguards where warranted. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sales. Regulators may restrict sales or require other measures based on demonstrated risk and corrective-action progress.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems placed on the market must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under documented safeguards. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and maintain secure, reliable access to the function. For systems already installed when this requirement is adopted, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or replacement plan within 18 months and complete implementation within 72 months, prioritizing units and locations presenting greater risk. If a unit cannot reasonably be retrofitted, the manufacturer must notify its owner and provide an equivalent protective measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression. This requirement applies to units manufactured or imported for sale beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document that the suppression system is compatible with the battery chemistry and installation environment and provide maintenance and emergency-response instructions. Regulators may require earlier protective measures for a model presenting an imminent serious risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear installation, operation, maintenance, emergency-response, and known-risk notices at purchase and whenever material safety information changes. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 5 business days after confirming a safety risk, with specific protective instructions and available remedies. A manufacturer must initiate a model-level recall when that model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, or when the regulator determines that a defect presents an equivalent serious risk. Incident definitions and installed-unit denominators must be applied consistently and supported by auditable records; manufacturers must promptly report the rate and supporting data to the regulator. The regulator verifies the calculation, determines recall scope, and verifies customer notification and remedy completion. A recall requires the regulator-approved corrective action and remedy for affected units; it does not by itself impose a sales prohibition, which requires a separate risk-based regulatory determination.
