# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A21 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## Incident reporting
Manufacturers must report any confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it. The initial report may be preliminary and must be updated as facts are confirmed. Manufacturers must notify the affected customer within 72 hours, and promptly provide any immediate safety instructions; where the incident may pose a risk to nearby people or property, the manufacturer must also notify the relevant local emergency or safety authority. Reports must include the incident date and location, product and model identification, known injuries or damage, and actions taken. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and submit a quarterly summary to the regulator, including incidents already reported.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
## Thermal testing
Before a model is offered for sale, and at least annually thereafter, the manufacturer must test a representative sample of that model under a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a documented, regulator-accepted protocol. Reports must state the protocol, sample size, number and share of units that fail, and material limitations. Complete per-model results must be provided to the regulator and to customers who buy that model. A failure rate above 5% requires regulator review; above 8% requires a corrective-action plan submitted to the regulator. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers when test results indicate a material safety risk, describing the risk and protective actions. Regulators may require additional measures based on the evidence; a test result alone does not automatically require a market ban or recall.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## Remote emergency shut-off
All new home battery models placed on the market after adoption must support a secure remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide instructions for authorized use and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed at adoption, manufacturers must enable remote shut-off where technically supported within 6 years, and retrofit or replace units that cannot support it within 6 years, subject to regulator-approved safety plans and customer notice.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
## Built-in fire suppression
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must disclose whether each model includes suppression and provide installation and maintenance instructions. Any proposed alternative system must demonstrate equivalent or better fire-risk reduction and receive regulator approval before sale.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
## Customer disclosure and recalls
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, affected models, immediate precautions, and available corrective actions. Notices must be sent promptly when a confirmed incident or test result indicates a material safety risk, and updated as facts or instructions change. If a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit a risk assessment and corrective-action plan. The rate calculation must identify the incident count, installed-unit estimate, period covered, and material limitations, and be supported by incident records available for regulator review. Exceeding the threshold requires regulator review but does not by itself automatically require a recall; the regulator must determine appropriate measures, including recall where necessary to address the risk. Any alternative to recall must adequately mitigate the risk and be regulator-approved. Manufacturers must report progress and completion. Regulators may require additional measures where evidence indicates an imminent risk.
