# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D14 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every battery-related fire, thermal runaway, or overheating incident to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents without injury or property damage. If a manufacturer later identifies an incident that was not previously reported, it must report it within 24 hours of discovery and explain the delay. Manufacturers must maintain and periodically reconcile incident records against customer complaints, service records, and other relevant sources. They must provide affected customers with an initial safety notice within 72 hours, including immediate precautions and how to report symptoms or damage. Manufacturers must submit a complete incident report to the regulator within 30 days and promptly update it if material information changes.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo the prescribed sustained-heat test at 55 °C. Before sale, and after any material design change, manufacturers must provide per-model test protocols and results to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy or own the model; material retest results must be provided promptly. Records must be retained for inspection. Results must not be publicly disclosed by company and model. A failure rate above 5% requires a corrective-action plan submitted to the regulator and retesting within a regulator-approved period. A model may remain on sale during that period subject to any interim safeguards the regulator requires. If the model continues to exceed 5% after the approved corrective action and retest, the regulator may restrict sales or require additional measures based on the evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly manufactured battery systems must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit compatible systems already installed, or provide an equivalent safe mitigation approved by the regulator, within six years of this standard taking effect. Shut-off capability must be tested at installation and at scheduled maintenance, and customers must receive clear instructions for emergency use.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must disclose whether a unit includes suppression and provide customers with instructions for its inspection and maintenance. Regulators may approve an equivalent or alternative measure where appropriate.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, installation and maintenance requirements, emergency actions, and how to report a safety concern. They must promptly notify affected customers of material safety findings and corrective actions. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, calculated for that model from the preceding 12 months; company-wide rates must not substitute for model-specific rates. It must notify the regulator and affected customers without delay, explain the risk and remedy, and report recall progress. A regulator may require additional corrective action where warranted.
