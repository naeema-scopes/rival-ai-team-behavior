# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G56 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account. Other thermal incidents must be reported within 5 calendar days. Affected customers must be notified within 5 calendar days, with immediate notice where continued use may pose an imminent risk. Reports must include known circumstances and corrective actions; updates are due as material facts become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after a material design change. The maximum allowed failure rate is 7% of tested units per model. A model above 5% must undergo documented corrective-action review and retesting before further design changes or production expansion. Manufacturers must provide complete, identifiable per-model results to the relevant regulator and disclose the model-specific failure rate, test date, protocol, sample size, and a clear safety summary to customers considering or using that model. Such customer disclosures need not be made available for general public access; public reporting may include aggregate, non-identifying results. Manufacturers must retain complete results for audit.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months after this standard takes effect must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for authorized emergency shut-off and clear customer instructions. For already-installed units, manufacturers must offer a retrofit or equivalent safe upgrade and complete it within 6 years after the standard takes effect, where technically feasible; customers must be informed of any limitations and interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and reliability protocol. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions and disclose any limitations. The requirement does not apply where the regulator determines that a unit-specific alternative provides equivalent or greater fire protection.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its 55 °C sustained-heat test results, known thermal incident rates, safe installation and use instructions, and any relevant limitations. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and provide interim safety instructions. A manufacturer must notify the relevant regulator and affected customers and initiate a recall when a model's thermal incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units in a year. The regulator may require a recall or other protective action sooner where the risk warrants it. Recall notices must explain the hazard, affected units, actions customers should take, and available remedy.
