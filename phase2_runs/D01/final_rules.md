# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D01 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible product-specific hazard or requires an immediate safety action. Aggregated annual incident counts and corrective actions must be submitted to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved, statistically representative protocol. Manufacturers must submit test methods, sample sizes, failure rates, uncertainty estimates, and corrective actions to regulators before sale and after material design changes. Before purchase, customers must receive that model’s test results, including the failure rate and sample size, in clear language. These customer disclosures must be made directly to prospective customers; they do not require public publication of an individual company’s per-model failure rates. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a corrective-action plan, and retesting; it does not by itself prohibit sale. Regulators should consider sample size, uncertainty, test quality, and corrective actions when assessing results and may impose additional restrictions where evidence indicates an unacceptable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed home battery systems must support secure remote emergency shut-off by 1 January 2028, with a safe local shut-off option available to occupants and emergency responders. Existing installed units must be upgraded or replaced to support remote shut-off within six years of this standard taking effect, where technically feasible; manufacturers must provide customers with a documented upgrade plan and disclose any units that cannot be upgraded.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance and reliability requirements. Manufacturers must document validation testing and maintenance expectations, and provide those details to regulators and customers. This requirement does not replace applicable thermal-safety testing or incident-reporting obligations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information including model-specific 55 °C sustained-heat test results, known material hazards, emergency shut-off instructions, and applicable maintenance or upgrade requirements. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of credible product-specific hazards and provide updated instructions and corrective actions; urgent hazards require notice within 48 hours of determination. A manufacturer must initiate a regulator-supervised recall when a model's verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units in any year. Manufacturers must report incident counts, installed-unit denominators, and supporting records to regulators using a consistent model-specific method. Regulators must promptly verify the figures, allow manufacturers to correct documented errors, and require interim protective measures while verification is pending where warranted by the risk. Verification procedures must not delay a recall once the threshold is confirmed.
