# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E33 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents initially reported by a customer or installer. A detailed follow-up, including known cause, injuries, and corrective actions, is due within 10 business days; material new information must be provided promptly. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their unit or a materially similar model, with immediate instructions for safe operation. Reporting obligations apply regardless of whether the manufacturer has confirmed the cause.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved representative sample and published test protocol. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. A model exceeding that rate must promptly submit corrective actions and a passing-retest plan to the regulator. The regulator may authorize time-limited continued sales subject to risk-based conditions, customer protections, and defined milestones where appropriate; otherwise, new placements must pause until corrective action and a passing retest are completed. Regulators may require interim protections for installed units and set a remediation plan. Manufacturers must submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, failure rates, and results to the relevant regulator. Complete current per-model test methods, sample sizes, failure rates, and results must be provided to customers and prospective customers of that model in an accessible format, and updated when material information changes. These results need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where an imminent hazard warrants it, by the manufacturer or emergency responders, with appropriate safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and confirm safe shut-off status. Existing installed systems must be retrofitted or replaced to provide this capability within six years of the standard’s effective date, unless a documented technical impossibility is accepted by the regulator; interim risk controls and customer instructions are required.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems manufactured 36 months or more after this standard’s adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to reduce the risk of fire propagation. Manufacturers must provide regulators with the design basis and validation evidence. The requirement does not replace other applicable fire-safety requirements.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, at purchase and in an accessible format, the model’s current safety status, applicable safety notices, and actionable precautions; notices must be updated when material information changes. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and recommended corrective actions. A manufacturer must initiate a recall of a model when its thermal incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, using regulator-validated incident and installed-unit counts. The manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers promptly and provide safe-use instructions and a remedy; the regulator may require a broader or faster recall where warranted.
