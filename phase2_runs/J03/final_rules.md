# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J03 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report each thermal runaway, fire, or overheating event to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of it, whether or not it caused injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response. A final investigation report must be provided within 30 days. The manufacturer must notify affected owners within 5 days when an event presents a credible risk requiring a safety action, and otherwise include material incident information in the next scheduled safety notice. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial number, and corrective actions. Regulators may require prompt updates. No public release of company-specific per-model test failure rates is required by this section.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A model passes only if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Before sale and after material design changes, manufacturers must provide regulators with the test methods, sample sizes, and per-model results. They must also provide those per-model results to customers who buy the model, before purchase; results must not be withheld from those customers as confidential. Regulators must treat per-model test failure rates as nonpublic confidential information and may not publish them, while retaining authority to use them for oversight and enforcement. A model that fails may not be newly placed on the market unless and until corrective measures are implemented and a compliant retest is completed. The manufacturer must promptly submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator; the regulator may require interim safeguards for units already in service. A failed test does not itself require permanent withdrawal if the model is corrected and subsequently passes.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market from 2 years after adoption must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized access and clear instructions for customers. Manufacturers must provide a plan for enabling the capability on compatible installed units and complete retrofits or equivalent upgrades within 6 years after adoption. Where a unit cannot safely or technically be upgraded, the manufacturer must notify its owner and provide an alternative risk-reduction measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and installation environment, and maintained in accordance with manufacturer instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about material risks, required precautions, and corrective actions, promptly after identifying a credible risk; notices must be sent within 5 calendar days when immediate customer action is needed. Manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers of a recall and its remedy without undue delay. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. Incident rates must be calculated and reported to the relevant regulator at least annually, using installed-unit counts and incident records.
