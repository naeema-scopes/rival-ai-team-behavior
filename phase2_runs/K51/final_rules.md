# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K51 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents initially reported by customers or installers. The manufacturer must provide affected customers with a safety notice within 72 hours when the incident may indicate a product hazard, and promptly update the notice as material facts emerge. Reports must include the model, incident date and known consequences; follow-up information must be supplied when available. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and reconcile them at least quarterly against customer, installer, warranty, and service reports. Any previously omitted reportable incident must be reported promptly with an explanation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample before sale and after material design changes. Manufacturers must submit protocols, sample sizes, and per-model results, including failure rates, to the regulator. Before purchase, customers must receive the applicable model’s 55 °C test failure rate, relevant thermal risks, and any required corrective actions. Customers must be notified of material changes in safety risks or corrective actions. Regulators may publish non-identifying aggregate results. A model with a failure rate above 5% must submit a corrective-action plan and undergo retesting on a regulator-set schedule; the regulator may restrict sales or require other action if the manufacturer does not meet the plan or presents an unacceptable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade for installed units that do not support this capability within six years after adoption. Shut-off capability must not replace required local emergency controls or emergency response instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the battery system and must not create additional hazards during normal operation or emergency response.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear notice of relevant thermal hazards, required installation and maintenance practices, emergency response steps, and available safety updates. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide actionable instructions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, submit a corrective-action plan, and provide the data supporting the rate calculation. The threshold triggers regulator review; the regulator may require a recall, additional notices, or sales restrictions based on the rate, incident severity, other evidence, and the adequacy of the manufacturer’s response. Regulators may require a recall at any rate where incident severity or other evidence warrants it.
