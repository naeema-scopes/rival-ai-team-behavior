# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A43 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written incident report and available investigation findings within 10 calendar days. They shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a potential safety risk to their model or installation, and provide updates when material new information becomes available. Reports shall include model, installation and incident details to the extent known; manufacturers shall promptly supplement incomplete reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and at least annually thereafter. A failure rate of 5% is a regulatory action threshold, not an automatic prohibition on certification, sales, or installation. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model results to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy or own that model, in clear, accessible form, and retain results for audit. If a model exceeds 5%, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and submit a corrective-action plan, including retesting, risk controls, and a reasonable implementation schedule. The regulator shall assess the model’s actual safety risk and may impose proportionate restrictions or require further measures; market access shall not be suspended automatically solely because the threshold is exceeded.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All newly manufactured home battery systems shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers shall provide a remote shut-off retrofit or an equivalent safe, documented alternative within six years, prioritizing units with known safety concerns. Shut-off capabilities shall be secure, tested, and accompanied by clear customer instructions; shut-off shall not replace emergency services or required on-site safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All newly manufactured home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system shall be appropriate to the battery chemistry and designed, installed, and maintained in accordance with applicable safety requirements.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, warning signs, safe operating steps, and how to obtain assistance. They shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when a safety risk is identified and promptly update them about material changes, corrective actions, and recall instructions. When a model exceeds 8 confirmed thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and provide supporting model-level data. The threshold shall trigger a regulator-led safety assessment, not an automatic recall; the regulator shall determine whether a recall or other proportionate measure is required based on model-specific evidence and risk. A recall is mandatory whenever the regulator determines that a defect presents an unreasonable safety risk, regardless of the incident rate. Rates shall be calculated separately for each model using confirmed incidents and that model’s installed-unit population; company-wide rates shall not substitute for model-level rates. Manufacturers shall report corrective-action or recall plans and progress to regulators.
