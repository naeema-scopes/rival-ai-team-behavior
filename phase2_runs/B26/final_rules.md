# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B26 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with an initial account of known facts. They must provide an investigation update within 5 business days and a final report within 30 days, or explain why more time is needed. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly when an incident indicates a credible safety risk, and no later than 5 business days after identifying that risk. Reports must include available model, date, location, and incident details; personal information must be protected. Within 30 days after this standard takes effect, each manufacturer must submit a confidential regulator-only reconciliation of any such incidents from the preceding 12 months that were not previously reported. This reconciliation does not replace or delay any current reporting or customer-notification duty.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a representative sample and a documented, reproducible protocol. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for each model. Before sale and after any material design change, the manufacturer must submit the per-model protocol and results to the regulator and an accredited independent test body. The manufacturer must also provide the relevant model’s results to customers who buy that model, in clear form before purchase. Detailed results need not be publicly published. A model exceeding the limit must not be newly placed on the market until it passes retesting.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market 12 months or more after adoption must support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with clear instructions for its use and safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide a plan to retrofit or otherwise enable this capability for installed units within 6 years after adoption, prioritizing units with identified safety risks. Any remote shut-off must not prevent customers or emergency responders from taking safe local action.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed for the battery’s chemistry and installation environment. Manufacturers must validate the system’s effectiveness and disclose applicable maintenance requirements and limitations to customers. The requirement does not replace applicable thermal testing, incident reporting, or other safety obligations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety and installation instructions with each unit, plus prompt safety notices when new information indicates a material risk. Notices must explain the hazard, affected models or serial ranges, immediate precautions, and how to obtain support. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting data. Exceeding this threshold triggers a mandatory recall of that model, including prompt notification of affected customers and an effective remedy such as repair, replacement, or refund. The regulator may approve a documented, time-limited remediation plan specifying the remedy, monitoring, and conditions for resuming sales; this does not waive the recall, customer notification, or remedy obligations. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and implement any additional regulator-directed measures when new information indicates a material risk.
