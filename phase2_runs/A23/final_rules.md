# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A23 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary account and unit/model identification. A complete incident report, including known contributing factors and corrective actions, is due within 10 business days; material updates shall be provided promptly. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a plausible safety risk, and provide updates as the investigation proceeds. Reports shall include incidents reported directly to the manufacturer, installers, or service providers.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo standardized, independently verifiable sustained-heat testing at 55 °C before sale and after a material design or manufacturing change. Testing shall use a documented protocol and statistically meaningful sample size. Per-model results, including failure rates and test dates, shall be provided confidentially to regulators and disclosed to customers purchasing that model before purchase, with applicable safety instructions; individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be publicly disclosed. Public reporting may use anonymized, aggregated results. A result above 5% triggers prompt regulator notification, confirmatory testing by an accredited independent laboratory, and a corrective-action plan with a regulator-set remediation period. Exceeding 5% alone shall not automatically prohibit sales or withdraw compliance status; any restriction shall follow confirmatory testing and regulator review, except where imminent risk warrants immediate protective action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units shall support a secure remote emergency shut-off function. Manufacturers shall provide clear instructions for its use and safeguards against unauthorized activation. Existing installed units that support the function shall have it enabled and made accessible to customers within 12 months of adoption. Manufacturers shall provide a retrofit or equivalent protective measure for installed units that do not support remote shut-off, with a compliance plan submitted to regulators within 12 months and implementation completed within 72 months, subject to regulator-approved alternatives where technically infeasible.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression no sooner than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall submit a compliance plan to regulators at least 12 months before the requirement takes effect. The plan shall describe the suppression system, validation testing, and measures to ensure it does not create additional hazards.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive applicable safety instructions, clear instructions for reporting a concern, and any material safety notices or recall information relevant to the model. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of a material safety risk and provide updates on investigations and remedies. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year shall trigger mandatory regulator review and independent validation using consistent incident definitions and installation denominators. If confirmed, the manufacturer shall promptly submit a corrective-action plan and implement regulator-approved remedies, which may include a recall where needed to protect the public. Imminent risk requires immediate protective action, including recall where necessary.
