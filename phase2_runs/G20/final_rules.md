# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G20 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of confirmation, including the model, serial range, event details, and known impacts. They must provide supporting investigation findings and updates as they become available. When an incident indicates a material safety risk, manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours of confirmation, with the known hazard, interim precautions, and available remedy. Reporting and notice obligations apply regardless of whether the cause is known; uncertainty must be stated and investigated.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for any model. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results to the relevant regulator and retain them for audit. Customers considering or owning that model must receive the per-model failure rate, test methods and limitations, material risks, and corrective actions; disclosure may be limited to customers of that model and need not be public. A model exceeding the limit must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and retest under regulator oversight. The regulator may allow continued sales during a time-limited remediation period if interim safeguards adequately manage the risk; it may restrict sales if the plan is inadequate, the period expires without compliance, or evidence demonstrates unacceptable safety risk. Exceedance alone does not automatically require a sales ban. Customers must receive timely notice of material safety risks and applicable precautions or remedies.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under documented procedures. The capability must fail safely, preserve required safety monitoring, and not prevent local emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and protect access against unauthorized activation. For units installed before adoption, manufacturers must enable remote emergency shut-off within 6 years where technically feasible; where it is not feasible, they must notify affected customers and the regulator, explain the limitation, and provide an alternative mitigation plan for regulator review.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the system’s foreseeable thermal runaway and fire hazards. The requirement applies to units manufactured 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document suppression performance, maintenance needs, and limitations in the product safety information supplied to customers and regulators. Existing installed units are not subject to retrofit under this section, but manufacturers must provide applicable safety notices and remedies when a material risk is identified.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a confirmed safety risk, including the hazard, affected models and serial ranges, interim precautions, and available remedy. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly report the rate and supporting data to the regulator and notify affected customers with relevant risk information. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, or when the regulator determines that a serious, imminent risk requires recall at a lower rate. Manufacturers must notify affected customers without delay when a recall is triggered and provide regular progress updates until the remedy is complete.
