# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B55 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal event involving fire, uncontrolled overheating, evacuation, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 10 business days. Reports must include known circumstances, affected model and serial range, and corrective actions; updates must be provided as investigations proceed. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and no later than 5 business days after that risk is confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized, independently witnessed sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a representative sample and published protocol. Results, sample sizes, and corrective actions must be reported to the relevant regulator and retained for audit. Customers purchasing a model must receive that model’s per-model test results and any associated corrective actions before purchase; results must also be provided to existing owners when they indicate a material safety risk. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification and a corrective-action plan. A rate above 8% requires suspension of new sales until the regulator accepts the plan and verifies effective corrective action. Regulators may share results with other competent authorities.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units placed on the market must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized access and a locally accessible shut-off method. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway for already-installed units and complete retrofits within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Where a retrofit is technically infeasible, the manufacturer must notify the owner and regulator, explain the limitation, and provide an equivalent risk-reduction measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The system must be designed to limit fire propagation and be tested under the applicable published protocol. Manufacturers must document compliance for each model and provide the documentation to regulators on request.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices for confirmed incidents or test results indicating a material risk, without undue delay and no later than 5 business days after confirming the risk. Notices must identify affected models and serial ranges, describe the hazard and recommended precautions, explain available remedies, and provide updates as investigations proceed. A manufacturer must notify the regulator and initiate a recall when any model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units, or sooner when the regulator determines that a material risk requires recall. Rates must be calculated using incidents and installed-unit counts for the same reporting year.
