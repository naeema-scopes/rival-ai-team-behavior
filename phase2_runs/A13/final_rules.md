# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A13 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a written incident report with known causes and affected units within 10 days. They shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential hazard relevant to their units, with clear safety instructions and support. Reports shall include incidents regardless of whether injury or property damage occurred.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. The maximum allowed failure rate shall be 5% of tested units. Manufacturers shall submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, failure rates, and corrective actions to regulators, and shall provide the per-model failure rate and relevant test information to customers purchasing that model. Per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public. A model exceeding 5% shall have a regulator-supervised corrective-action plan and retest schedule. For models already marketed when this standard is adopted, the regulator may authorize a time-limited transition period with interim safeguards and milestones before restricting sales, based on the evidence and severity of risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units shall provide a secure remote emergency shut-off function beginning on adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units that lack this function within six years of adoption, or provide an equivalent regulator-approved emergency isolation measure. The function shall be accessible to authorized emergency responders and customers, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document suppression-system performance and maintenance requirements in customer materials.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of incidents or defects that could create a fire, overheating, or injury hazard, with clear instructions for safe use and support. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling year, or when the regulator determines a defect presents an unreasonable serious safety risk. Manufacturers shall report the underlying model-level data to regulators and notify affected customers without undue delay; notices must explain the hazard, affected units, and remedy.
