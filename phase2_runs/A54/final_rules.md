# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A54 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency shutdown to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk or requires inspection, changed use, or corrective action. Reports must include the incident date, model, known consequences, and actions taken; manufacturers must promptly update regulators and affected customers as facts develop.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a common, regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results to the relevant regulator and provide them to customers considering or owning that model. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a risk assessment, and a corrective-action plan with milestones. The regulator may require additional protective measures, including sales restrictions, where warranted by the risk; exceeding 5% does not by itself automatically prohibit continued sale. Public disclosure of individual-company per-model failure rates is not required.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models must include a remotely accessible emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must make the capability available on all new units within 12 months of adoption. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation plan and complete implementation within 72 months, prioritizing units with elevated risk. The shut-off must not prevent local emergency response or safe operation when communications are unavailable. Manufacturers must report coverage and implementation progress annually to regulators.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no sooner than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence that the system is effective and does not introduce material new hazards. Until the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must disclose whether a unit has built-in suppression and provide applicable fire-response guidance to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive model-specific safety notices, including applicable 55 °C test results, known thermal hazards, incident trends, and required inspection or corrective actions. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly when new evidence indicates a material safety risk and provide updates as corrective actions proceed. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers and submit a risk assessment and proposed corrective-action plan. Regulators must determine whether a recall or other interim protective measures are warranted based on the verified rate, severity and circumstances of incidents, and effectiveness of available remedies.
