# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B13 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any fire, thermal runaway, or other thermal incident involving a home battery within 5 business days of learning of it; an incident involving injury, evacuation, or material property damage must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident presents a safety risk or requires inspection, updated instructions, or corrective action. Reports must include known facts and be updated as investigations progress. Regulators may request further information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a documented sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market placement and after a material design change. No more than 5% of tested units for any model may fail. A model exceeding the limit must not be newly placed on the market until it passes a confirmatory test following corrective action. For models already on the market when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must submit per-model results, a risk assessment, and a corrective-action and retesting schedule to the regulator within 90 days. Such models may continue to be sold while the regulator reviews the submission and sets a proportionate compliance deadline, unless the regulator determines that interim safeguards or restrictions are warranted by risk. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including failure rates and sample sizes, to the regulator and provide them to customers purchasing that model. Individual company per-model failure rates must not be disclosed to the general public; regulators may publish safety findings and aggregate or anonymized data. The regulator should review the limit and protocol after two years using accumulated test and field data.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off by 1 January 2029, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. For units already installed on that date, manufacturers must provide compatible remote shut-off through retrofit or replacement within 6 years, where technically feasible; they must notify customers of any limitations and interim safety measures. Shut-off capability must not replace required local safety protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression starting 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide clear customer instructions on the system’s operation, limitations, and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices when a known issue may affect safe installation, operation, or maintenance, including recommended precautions and corrective actions. Notices must be sent promptly and no later than 5 business days after the manufacturer determines customer action is warranted; urgent risks requiring immediate action must be communicated without delay. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting data and a corrective-action plan. The regulator must require a recall of that model and determine the recall’s scope and timing. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of any recall and explain the actions they should take.
