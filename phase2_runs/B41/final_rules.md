# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B41 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within five business days when an incident presents a plausible safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include known facts and be updated as investigations develop.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample size. Manufacturers must submit the protocol, sample size, and per-model results confidentially to the regulator before sale and after any material design change; regulators may require retesting. A failure rate above 5% per model triggers a regulator-supervised corrective-action plan and retesting, but is not by itself a prohibition on sale or continued market availability. Manufacturers must provide the per-model failure rate and test summary to prospective buyers before purchase and to existing customers, and disclose material changes or corrective actions. Regulators may publish aggregate results and required corrective actions, but not individual-company per-model failure rates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models must include a remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a safe software or hardware upgrade for installed units that lack this capability, completing upgrades within 72 months where technically feasible. Customers must be informed of the capability, limitations, and safe shut-off procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about incidents or hazards that may affect their unit, including recommended precautions and any action required, as soon as practicable and no later than five business days after determining that the notice is warranted. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and the regulator of the recall and provide instructions for safe shutdown, repair, or replacement.
