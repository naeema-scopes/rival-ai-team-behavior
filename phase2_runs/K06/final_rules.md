# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K06 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents initially reported by customers or service personnel. A preliminary report may be followed by a corrected or complete report within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or household, and provide follow-up instructions promptly. Records of all incidents, including those not initially reportable, must be retained and reconciled in periodic reports to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after a material design change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for any model. Manufacturers must provide per-model results and test methods to regulators and disclose the per-model failure rate and a clear safety summary to customers purchasing that model; results must not be publicly published by company or model. If a model exceeds the limit, its manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, submit a corrective-action plan, and retest within a regulator-set period. Regulators may restrict sales if corrective action is inadequate or testing identifies an imminent serious risk; exceeding the test limit alone does not automatically ban a model. Manufacturers must retain test records and provide affected customers with safety instructions if testing identifies a material risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off no later than 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must retrofit or otherwise enable remote shut-off for installed units within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible, and provide customers clear instructions for requesting or using shut-off. Shut-off must not replace emergency services or other required safety protections; activation and access controls must be documented and tested.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance and provide installation and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety notices describing material known risks, relevant safety instructions, and the model’s 55 °C sustained-heat test results. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and provide protective instructions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, and a regulator-supervised recall of that model is mandatory. The regulator shall determine the appropriate scope and remedy, which may include repair, replacement, or refund. A recall does not itself constitute an automatic market ban; regulators may separately restrict sales based on the risk and adequacy of corrective action. Recall notices must explain the risk and provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions.
