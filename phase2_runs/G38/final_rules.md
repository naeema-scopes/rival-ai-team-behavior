# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G38 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary account of the event and affected model. A detailed report, including known contributing factors and corrective actions, is due within 30 days; material updates shall be provided promptly. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a material hazard or requires a safety action, and shall provide any updated instructions or remedies without undue delay. Reports shall include incidents across all markets and shall not omit incidents because their cause is still under investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and adequate representative sampling. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results to the regulator and provide them to the other participating manufacturer on request. Customers of a model shall receive that model’s test results, including its failure rate, and an explanation of any required safety actions. Per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% shall prompt regulator review, but shall not by itself bar market entry or require withdrawal. Based on test results and other relevant safety evidence, the regulator may require corrective action and retesting before further production.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery models placed on the market after this standard takes effect shall support remote emergency shut-off. For units already installed on that date, manufacturers shall provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within six years where technically feasible, prioritizing units with identified safety concerns. Customers must be notified of any limitations and safe alternatives where retrofit is not feasible.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard shall include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance requirements. Manufacturers shall document compliance and provide installation and maintenance instructions to customers. Earlier compliance is encouraged but is not required by this standard.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety and emergency-response instructions at installation, and prompt notice of material newly identified hazards, corrective actions, and safe-use changes. If any model’s verified annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units, the manufacturer shall promptly report the rate and supporting data to the regulator and initiate a recall of that model. Regulator verification shall resolve uncertainty about the rate; once verified above the threshold, recall is mandatory. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers, provide appropriate remedies and interim safety instructions, and promptly implement the recall.
