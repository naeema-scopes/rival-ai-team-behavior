# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E35 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers shall report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, or event causing injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware. Other confirmed thermal incidents shall be reported within 10 business days. Reports must include known circumstances and affected model and be updated as the investigation proceeds. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers within 5 business days of confirming a safety-relevant defect or hazard, with immediate notice where continued use presents an imminent risk. Regulators may require incident records and follow-up reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each residential battery model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-recognized protocol before market placement and at least once after any material design change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Results, including sample size, failure rate, protocol, and corrective actions, shall be submitted to the relevant regulator and provided to customers purchasing that model in clear, accessible form; they shall not be withheld from those customers. A result above 5% is non-compliant: the manufacturer shall promptly submit a corrective-action plan and retest within 6 months. Regulators may require interim protective measures, including suspension of new sales, where needed to control risk while corrective action is verified. A compliant retest is required before the model is treated as meeting the limit. Results shall be updated following retesting.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## S3_remote_shutoff
New residential battery models placed on the market from 24 months after adoption shall support secure remote emergency shut-off, with a clearly documented means for an authorized operator or emergency responder to request shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide authenticated access and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers shall provide a compatible retrofit or equivalent safe means of emergency isolation within 6 years, where technically feasible; customers must receive clear instructions on its use.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
## S4_fire_suppression
New residential battery units placed on the market from 36 months after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression or an independently verified integrated mitigation system that limits propagation of fire to adjacent units and provides protection equivalent to suppression. The manufacturer shall document the system’s performance and maintenance requirements. This requirement does not remove the obligation to meet applicable fire-safety and installation requirements.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive clear, accessible safety information for the specific model, including its per-model thermal-test results, known material thermal hazards, safe-use and emergency instructions, and the process for reporting incidents. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of new safety-relevant information and provide instructions for protective action; notice shall be immediate when continued use presents an imminent risk. When a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and provide the incident count, reporting-completeness information, and installed-unit denominator for regulator review. Regulators shall verify the model-specific rate and assess the risk. A verified exceedance shall trigger a mandatory recall unless the regulator documents that an equally protective corrective action makes recall unnecessary. The manufacturer shall promptly provide affected customers with safety notices and protective-action instructions, and regulators shall oversee the scope and timing of any recall or alternative remedy. Manufacturers shall provide effective remedies and safe handling or shutdown instructions.
