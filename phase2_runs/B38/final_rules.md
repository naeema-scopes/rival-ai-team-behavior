# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B38 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal event involving fire, smoke, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary incident report. They must provide a complete report within 10 calendar days and promptly update it as new facts emerge. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk, and report aggregate model-level incident counts to regulators quarterly. Reports must include model, date, location, known consequences, and corrective actions. A reporting failure does not excuse or delay customer notification.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples before sale and after any material design or manufacturing change. The protocol must specify sample selection, test duration, and failure criteria. Manufacturers must provide per-model results to the regulator and disclose them to customers who buy that model; results must identify the model, test date and protocol, sample size, and failures. Per-model failure rates are not required to be published to the general public. A model with a failure rate above 5% must submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator and be retested within 12 months. No more than 10% of tested units for any model may fail; exceeding 10% requires prompt regulator review and appropriate protective action, which may include a stop-sale or recall based on the evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must include a remote emergency shut-off function no later than 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a secure software or hardware retrofit enabling remote shut-off for installed units within 72 months after adoption, where technically feasible, and report progress and exceptions to the regulator annually. Shut-off must not prevent emergency responders or occupants from safely isolating the system locally. Manufacturers must maintain access controls and audit logs for remote commands.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be validated for the battery chemistry and installation configuration under a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must provide validation results to the regulator and disclose the system’s capabilities and limitations to customers. Regulators may grant a time-limited, publicly documented extension where a manufacturer demonstrates that compliant suppression is not yet technically available, without waiving other fire-safety requirements.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s current 55 °C test result and a clear explanation of any safety limitations. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified hazards and provide safe-use instructions and updates. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, based on regulator-validated reports, the manufacturer must notify the regulator within 24 hours and submit a risk assessment and corrective-action plan within 10 calendar days. The regulator must promptly assess whether a recall or other protective action is necessary, considering incident severity, trend, and available risk controls; it may require a recall or stop-sale at any time where evidence indicates an imminent serious risk. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly of regulator-ordered protective actions and provide an effective remedy where required.
