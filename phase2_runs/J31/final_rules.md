# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J31 · variant J · NOT adopted (signatures: {})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary incident report within 5 business days and a final investigation report within 30 days. They shall notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a material safety risk to their model or requires customer action. Reports shall include model, serial number where available, location, incident description, known consequences, and corrective actions. Manufacturers shall maintain incident records and report aggregate model-level incident counts to regulators quarterly. Reporting does not replace any stricter applicable legal obligation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically representative units. Manufacturers shall submit full per-model results, including failure rates and sample sizes, to the regulator; individual per-model failure rates shall not be published or made publicly accessible. Customers shall receive actionable safety notices relevant to their model, including required actions, without disclosure of individual test failure rates. A result above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, corrective action, and remediation and retesting within 90 days. No model may enter or remain on the market if its 55 °C test failure rate exceeds 8%. Regulators may require additional protective measures based on incident data or other risk evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market beginning three years after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer notification. Manufacturers shall retrofit already-installed units where technically and safely feasible, completing retrofits within six years of adoption. Where a retrofit is not feasible, manufacturers shall provide affected customers with an alternative safety measure and instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New models placed on the market beginning three years after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression appropriate to the battery chemistry and system design, validated under a regulator-approved protocol. Regulators may grant a time-limited, model-specific exemption where the manufacturer demonstrates that an alternative measure provides equivalent or better protection.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety and incident notices relevant to their model, including required actions, through direct communication and an accessible public notice. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers and regulators of material safety risks and provide updates as investigations proceed. When available data indicate that a model may exceed 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer shall notify the regulator within 5 business days and provide the incident count, installed-unit denominator, and known reporting gaps. The regulator shall promptly verify the rate and conduct a risk review. A verified rate above 8 per 100,000 requires the regulator to determine promptly whether a recall or other protective action is necessary, considering incident severity, trend, underreporting, and feasible risk controls. Regulators may order a recall or other protective action at any rate where the evidence warrants it. A recall notice shall explain the hazard, affected units, remedy, and how customers can obtain assistance.
