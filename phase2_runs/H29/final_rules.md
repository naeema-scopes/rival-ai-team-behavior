# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H29 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a follow-up report containing investigation findings within 30 days. They shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their model, and provide updates when material information changes. Reports must identify the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and any immediate safety actions; customer notices must explain protective steps and how to report an incident.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample, before market placement and after material design changes. Manufacturers shall submit complete per-model results, including failure rates, to the relevant regulator confidentially. Customers who own or are considering a model shall receive that model’s exact 55 °C test failure rate and any protective actions, informed by validated results. Public disclosure shall not identify individual-company per-model failure rates. A failure rate above 5% shall trigger confidential regulator review and a corrective-action plan; any further restrictions shall be based on validated test results and incident data.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall include remote emergency shut-off capability no later than two years after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers shall provide a plan to retrofit already-installed units with remote shut-off capability within six years after the effective date, prioritizing higher-risk units and providing customers with safety instructions while retrofits are pending.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression for units manufactured beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The regulator shall publish minimum performance and verification requirements before that date. Manufacturers shall disclose any interim suppression or other fire-mitigation measures and provide customers with appropriate safety instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety instructions at purchase, prompt notice of newly identified material risks, and updates when material safety information changes. When a model exceeds 8 confirmed thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the regulator shall promptly validate the incident and installed-unit data, investigate the model’s risk, and require proportionate protective measures as warranted. The regulator shall require a recall when validated evidence shows that a recall is necessary to address an unacceptable safety risk; it shall publish its determination and the basis for its actions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of any required protective action or recall, explain what they should do, and provide any required remedy.
