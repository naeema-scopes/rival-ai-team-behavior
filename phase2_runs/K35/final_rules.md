# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K35 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written update within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to them or nearby households, and provide any immediate protective instructions without delay. Reports must include known incident counts and rates, model, and corrective actions; later-discovered incidents must be reported promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, a representative sample, and documented results. The maximum allowed failure rate for new certification is 5%. A model above 5% must not receive new certification or be newly placed on the market unless the regulator approves a temporary transition plan for a result above 5% and no higher than 8%. Any such plan must specify corrective actions, enhanced incident monitoring, and a retest deadline no later than 12 months after approval; continued sale is conditional on compliance, and regulators may restrict sales or require a recall at any time where evidence indicates risk. A model that fails to achieve a rate of 5% or less on retest may not continue to be placed on the market under the transition plan. Manufacturers must submit results and any corrective-action plan to regulators and provide per-model results to customers before purchase; results must not be publicly published. Regulators may require additional measures or a recall where evidence indicates imminent safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market after adoption must support a remote emergency shut-off function that can be activated by the manufacturer or an authorized emergency responder when necessary to address an imminent safety risk. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for using the function and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed on the date of adoption, manufacturers must provide compatible remote shut-off capability within 6 years where technically feasible; otherwise they must notify affected customers and the regulator of the limitation and provide an alternative risk-reduction plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Home battery units manufactured for sale 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to reduce the risk of fire spread from a thermal event. The requirement applies to new units and does not require retrofit of units installed before its effective date. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide this information to customers and regulators.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety instructions, known model-specific hazards, incident-response guidance, and contact details for reporting a problem. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material new safety information and provide practical protective steps. For each model, manufacturers must track thermal incidents and installed units over a consistent 12-month period and provide the resulting rate and supporting data to regulators. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in that period; regulators may also require a recall or other corrective action where evidence indicates an imminent risk, regardless of that rate.
