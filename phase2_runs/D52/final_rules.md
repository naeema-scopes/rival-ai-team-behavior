# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D52 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller report within 10 business days. Incidents involving injury, fire, or an imminent risk to occupants or neighbours shall also be reported promptly to affected customers and emergency responders. Manufacturers shall provide customers with a clear safety notice and immediate safe-use instructions when a product hazard is identified, without waiting for the investigation to conclude. Reports shall include known model, installation, and outcome information and be updated as facts are confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results to the regulator and provide the results to customers who buy or own that model in clear, accessible language. A failure rate above 5% requires a corrective-action plan and regulator-supervised retesting, but does not by itself prohibit sale or trigger a recall. The regulator shall set a proportionate remediation schedule based on test uncertainty, incident evidence, and the severity of risk, and may require interim safeguards or restrict sales where necessary to address an imminent serious safety risk. Tests shall be repeated at least annually and after material design changes; the regulator may require additional testing.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units placed on the market shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear instructions for customers and emergency responders. Manufacturers shall provide a secure retrofit or equivalent remote shut-off capability for installed units within 6 years after adoption. Where remote shut-off is unavailable or cannot be safely activated, manufacturers shall notify affected customers and provide an alternative emergency response procedure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units manufactured for sale shall include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance and reliability requirements, beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall provide customers and installers with instructions for operation, maintenance, and emergency response. The regulator shall review performance evidence and may require corrective action for systems that fail to perform as specified.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive plain-language installation, safe-use, emergency response, and incident-reporting instructions, plus prompt notice of material newly identified hazards affecting their unit. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on regulator-verified data, or when the regulator determines there is an imminent serious safety risk. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers directly and provide the regulator with a remediation plan. The regulator shall determine proportionate corrective measures, which may include repair, replacement, or safe discontinuation; a recall trigger alone does not automatically prohibit future sales, though the regulator may restrict sales where necessary to address serious safety risk. Notices shall explain the hazard, affected units, and steps for remediation.
