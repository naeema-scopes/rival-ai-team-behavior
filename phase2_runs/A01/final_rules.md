# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A01 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery thermal runaway, fire, explosion, or overheating event requiring emergency services, medical care, evacuation, or causing property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours, providing known hazards and immediate safety steps, and update them as material facts emerge. Less severe thermal incidents must be reported to the regulator and affected customers within 30 days. Reports must identify the model, incident date and location, consequences, and known contributing factors; manufacturers must also submit an annual incident summary.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, including test method, sample size, failures, and corrective actions, to the regulator and to customers who buy that model, before purchase and after each retest; individual-company per-model failure rates are not required to be published to the general public. A model fails this standard if more than 7% of tested units fail. Models exceeding 5% must submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator within 30 days and complete a retest within 12 months. The regulator may require additional safeguards or restrict sales if risks remain unaddressed.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off no later than 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide customers clear instructions for requesting shut-off and explain any limitations, including loss of communications or power. For units already installed when the standard is adopted, manufacturers must provide a retrofit plan and complete retrofits within 6 years, where technically feasible; customers must be notified of interim safety steps and any unit-specific limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to regulators and customers. This requirement does not replace other safeguards or incident-reporting duties.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and after material safety updates, manufacturers must provide customers with clear notices describing known thermal hazards, safe-use and emergency steps, reporting contacts, and relevant model-specific corrective actions. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours of learning of a serious thermal incident involving their model and update notices as material facts emerge. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year requires prompt notice to regulators and affected customers, a risk assessment, and interim safeguards as directed by the regulator. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers of a recall and provide instructions for repair, replacement, or safe decommissioning.
