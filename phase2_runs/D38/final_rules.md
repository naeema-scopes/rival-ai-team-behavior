# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D38 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, smoke, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it. They must provide a complete incident report, including known injuries, property damage, and suspected cause, within 10 calendar days, with updates as facts emerge. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk, and provide safety instructions and material updates promptly. Reporting obligations apply regardless of injury or whether the unit is under warranty.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample before sale and after a material design change. No more than 5% of tested units may fail. Before sale, the manufacturer must submit per-model test protocols, sample sizes, and results to the regulator, retain them for audit, and make the results available to customers buying that model. Results must also be provided to the other signatory company for standard oversight, subject to confidentiality and use only for oversight; per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. A model exceeding the limit may not be newly placed on the market. For a model already on the market at adoption, the manufacturer must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and may continue sales for up to 12 months while implementing corrective measures, subject to regulator-approved interim protections and any customer notices the regulator requires. The regulator may require sales to stop sooner where needed to protect safety. The manufacturer may request a regulator-supervised retest after corrective measures; after the 12-month period, the model may be placed on the market only after passing the test.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market 12 months or more after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support this capability, or provide an equivalent regulator-approved mitigation, within 6 years of adoption. Shut-off must be usable by the manufacturer and, during an emergency, by the relevant emergency authority; its use must not prevent emergency responders from safely isolating the unit.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed to limit propagation of a thermal event. The system must be validated under a regulator-approved test protocol, with results provided to the regulator and disclosed to customers. Requirements for existing installed units are not created by this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must give customers clear safety notices promptly when a credible risk is identified, including affected models, recommended precautions, and how to obtain updates or assistance. When a safety defect requires corrective action, manufacturers must notify affected customers directly and provide repair, replacement, or other regulator-approved remedy instructions. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units during a year triggers a mandatory recall. The regulator must verify the incident count and installed-unit denominator using complete reports, including incidents reported late or initially omitted. Manufacturers must promptly report the trigger and cooperate with the assessment; the regulator may require interim protections or stop-sales while verifying the trigger and implementing the recall. Incident reporting and customer notices do not replace one another.
