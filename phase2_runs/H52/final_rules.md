# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H52 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or suspected thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a written follow-up within 10 business days. Customers whose units are affected by an incident or identified safety risk must be notified promptly, and no later than 72 hours after the manufacturer becomes aware, with known hazards and immediate protective steps clearly stated.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a regulator-approved protocol before market launch and at least annually thereafter. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% of tested units per model. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results to the relevant regulator and disclose those results to current and prospective customers of that model. General-public publication is not required. For a model exceeding the limit, the manufacturer must promptly investigate, notify the regulator, and submit a corrective-action plan with milestones and interim risk controls. For models already on the market when this standard is adopted, the plan must provide a reasonable, risk-based period of up to 24 months to achieve compliance, unless the regulator determines that the immediate risk requires faster action. The regulator will review the plan and determine whether restrictions on further placements are necessary based on the risk, adequacy of controls, and progress toward compliance; exceeding the limit alone does not automatically prohibit further placements.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or relevant emergency authorities when needed to address an imminent safety risk. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for its use and safeguards against unauthorized activation. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance, where technically feasible, within six years of adoption; manufacturers must report units that cannot be retrofitted and the reasons to the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be designed and validated to reduce the risk of fire propagation and must not create additional unacceptable hazards. Manufacturers must document the system’s performance and maintenance requirements for regulators and customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly provide affected customers with clear safety notices describing known hazards and protective steps, and notify customers of a recall without undue delay. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, a recall is mandatory. The manufacturer must promptly report the rate and supporting data to the regulator, initiate the recall, and notify affected customers without delay. Manufacturers must report recall actions and progress to the regulator. Per-model test results must be disclosed to current and prospective customers of that model; general-public publication is not required.
