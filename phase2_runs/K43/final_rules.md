# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K43 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage within 24 hours of learning of it, followed by a detailed report within 10 calendar days. They shall promptly notify the affected customer of an incident involving that customer’s unit, provide safety instructions, and update the customer when material findings or corrective actions become available. Reports shall identify the model and incident circumstances and include known corrective actions; regulators may request further information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. A model’s failure rate must not exceed 5%. Manufacturers shall submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, and failure rates to regulators and disclose the per-model failure rate to customers who buy that model, with a clear explanation of the test and any corrective actions; these disclosures do not require publication to the general public. If a model exceeds the limit, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers and submit a corrective-action plan for regulatory approval, including a regulator-set deadline for retesting. Until the model meets the limit, regulators may require interim risk controls, restrict new installations, or take other proportionate measures based on the evidence, considering the approved remediation plan and retest schedule.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide a secure, reliable means for an authorized customer or emergency responder to initiate shut-off, subject to safeguards against unauthorized access and clear instructions on limitations. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers shall provide a compatible retrofit or other effective remote shut-off capability within 72 months, where technically feasible; any units not made compatible shall be identified to their owners and the regulator, with safe alternative shutdown instructions. Remote shut-off shall not replace required local controls or emergency response.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market 36 months or more after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression designed to mitigate thermal runaway and reduce fire spread. The manufacturer shall validate the system under regulator-approved tests and provide test results to the regulator and customers who buy the model. The regulator may approve an equivalent protective design where the manufacturer demonstrates that it provides comparable or greater protection. This requirement does not apply to units placed on the market before its effective date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall notify affected customers of a thermal incident involving their unit within 24 hours of learning of it and provide clear safety instructions; regulators shall receive incident and corrective-action reports within the timelines in S1. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, subject to regulator verification of the rate and installed-unit denominator. When a recall is initiated, manufacturers shall directly notify affected customers without undue delay, explain the remedy and safe handling or shutdown steps, and provide status updates to regulators. This clause does not require public disclosure of per-model thermal-test failure rates; customer access to per-model test results remains governed by S2.
