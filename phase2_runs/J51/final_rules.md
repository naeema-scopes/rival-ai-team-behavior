# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J51 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days after learning of it; incidents involving death, serious injury, or an active risk to the public must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers promptly when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and no later than 5 business days after that determination. Reports shall include known unit/model identifiers, circumstances, and any corrective action; updates shall be provided as material facts become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model results, including protocol, sample size, and failure rate, to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy or are considering buying that model; results may not be withheld from those customers as confidential. Manufacturers may publish results more broadly. A manufacturer whose model exceeds 5% shall notify the regulator within 30 days and submit a corrective-action and retesting plan. The regulator shall set a proportionate remediation timetable and may require interim risk controls based on the evidence. Exceeding 5% alone shall not automatically require withdrawal from the market; continued sale during remediation is subject to regulator-approved controls and timely retesting.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall equip previously installed units with this capability, or provide an equivalent regulator-approved emergency isolation measure, within 6 years after adoption. Manufacturers shall inform owners how shut-off works and how to request emergency activation. Shut-off systems shall be designed to remain secure against unauthorized access and shall not prevent safe local isolation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units placed on the market shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any limitations of the suppression system to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers clear safety notices about material risks, relevant incidents, and protective actions promptly, and no later than 5 business days after determining a material safety risk. Notices shall identify affected models and explain how customers can obtain applicable per-model thermal test results. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; manufacturers shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and initiate the recall, including instructions for safe shutdown, repair, replacement, or refund. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action sooner where the evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
