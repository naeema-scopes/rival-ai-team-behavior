# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J35 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account and known unit/model information. A complete investigation report is due within 30 days, with updates as material facts emerge. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk for their unit or model; urgent risks require immediate notice. Reports must include incidents learned of through customer reports, service records, or other sources, and must not omit incidents because causation is still under investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples, before market placement and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers must provide the regulator and relevant conformity-assessment body with each model’s failure rate, sample size, protocol, and corrective actions. Customers purchasing a model and existing customers of that model must receive its per-model failure rate, sample size, test protocol, and any corrective actions; individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator review and a corrective-action plan. A rate above 7% requires regulator-directed corrective measures. Sales restrictions require a separate regulator finding of a material safety risk, not test failure rate alone. Test failure rates must be reported separately from thermal-incident rates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible means of safe operation, beginning 24 months after adoption. For units already installed on that date, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation plan and complete implementation within 6 years. Where retrofit is technically infeasible, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and the regulator and provide an approved alternative mitigation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The manufacturer must ensure the suppression system is appropriate to the unit’s chemistry and installation, and provide instructions for inspection, maintenance, and response to activation. Any proposed alternative must be demonstrated to provide equivalent or greater fire-risk reduction and approved by the relevant regulator.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices promptly when new information identifies a credible risk affecting their unit or model, including the nature of the risk, affected models or serial ranges, immediate precautions, and available support or corrective actions. Urgent risks require immediate notice; other credible safety risks must be communicated within 72 hours of determination. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop sales, and implement the recall and remedy plan under regulator oversight. Notices and recall updates must be accessible to affected customers and the public.
