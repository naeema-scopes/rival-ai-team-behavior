# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H39 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it. A death, serious injury, or fire spreading beyond the battery must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 5 business days after learning of an incident involving their unit, with available safety instructions and a contact for further information. Reports must include known incident details and be updated as the investigation proceeds.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after material design changes. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for any model. Results above 5% must be confirmed through regulator-approved repeat testing before a compliance determination. Manufacturers must submit protocols and per-model results to the relevant regulator. Customers considering or purchasing a model must have clear access to that model’s test results and failure rate; results may otherwise remain non-public. For a confirmed result above 5%, the manufacturer must promptly submit a regulator-supervised, model-specific corrective-action plan with milestones and interim safeguards. Continued sale may be allowed while the manufacturer complies with the plan unless the regulator determines that an immediate, unmitigated risk requires restriction. Regulators may require retesting or corrective action where results indicate a safety concern. Customers must receive clear safety notices when testing identifies a material hazard.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on new battery units placed on the market beginning 2 years after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide a secure, reliable shut-off function and clear customer instructions. For units already installed when the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within 6 years, where technically feasible, and must inform customers of interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on new battery units placed on the market beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The requirement applies to all new models and units; manufacturers must document compliance using a regulator-approved method. Manufacturers must provide customers with clear information about suppression features and any applicable interim safety measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices when a known hazard could affect safe installation, operation, or maintenance, including the nature of the hazard, affected models or units, recommended actions, and how to obtain assistance. Notices must be sent promptly after the manufacturer learns of the hazard and updated when material information becomes available. A recall is mandatory when a model’s thermal incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and the relevant regulator, provide safe-use instructions pending remedy, and offer an effective remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund.
