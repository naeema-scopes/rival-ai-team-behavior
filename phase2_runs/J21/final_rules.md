# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J21 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating or fire to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, whether or not it caused injury or property damage. They shall maintain auditable incident records and promptly notify affected customers when an incident indicates a material safety risk or requires protective action, and in any event within 72 hours of that determination. Reports shall include known incident circumstances and corrective actions; manufacturers shall update regulators as investigations develop.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and after material safety-related design changes. Models with a failure rate above 7.5% may not be placed on the market. For models above 5% and at or below 7.5%, manufacturers shall submit a corrective-action and enhanced-monitoring plan to the relevant regulator, which may require additional testing or protective measures. Manufacturers shall provide per-model results to the relevant regulator and make results available to prospective and current customers of that model before purchase and whenever updated results are available. Regulators may publish aggregate results, but not individual-company per-model failure rates. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and protective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market 36 months or more after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide safe-use instructions and disclose whether installed units support this function. For units already installed on adoption, manufacturers shall provide a compatible remote shut-off retrofit, where technically feasible, within six years.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall disclose whether each unit includes built-in fire suppression and provide installation and maintenance instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of safety risks, protective actions, and material safety updates, and shall provide regulators with the same notices. A manufacturer shall initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period. Regulators may require a recall sooner where the evidence indicates an imminent risk of serious harm.
