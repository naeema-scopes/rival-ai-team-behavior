# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B21 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents initially suspected to involve installation or external causes. They shall provide a verified incident update within 10 business days and promptly supplement it as material facts become available. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their model or requires customer action. Reports shall include model, date, location, known injuries or property damage, and actions taken; personal information shall be protected.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. The maximum allowed failure rate is 7%. Manufacturers shall submit each model’s results, sample size, and corrective-action plan for any failure to the regulator, and provide per-model results to customers who buy that model. Per-model failure rates shall not be publicly disclosed under this standard. A model above the 7% ceiling shall have 18 months to implement corrective measures and pass a retest; during that period, the manufacturer shall disclose the result and corrective-action timeline to affected customers and the regulator. The regulator shall review the ceiling after three years using incident and test data. Retesting is required after material design changes or when directed by the regulator.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units shall support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or emergency responders when necessary to address an imminent safety risk. Manufacturers shall provide existing installed units with this capability, where technically feasible, within 6 years of adoption. Shut-off systems shall include safeguards against unauthorized activation and shall not prevent local emergency responders from isolating a unit.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide these to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with model-specific safety notices promptly when a credible risk affects their unit, including the nature of the risk, recommended precautions, and any required customer action. A notice requiring urgent action shall be sent within 48 hours of the manufacturer becoming aware of the risk; other material safety notices shall be sent within 10 business days. Manufacturers shall notify the regulator and affected customers of corrective actions and recall instructions. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, and the regulator shall conduct a documented model-specific safety review. The review shall consider verified incident rates, severity, test results, and corrective actions, and determine whether a mandatory recall is required. This replaces an automatic recall trigger at that threshold with a required regulator determination.
