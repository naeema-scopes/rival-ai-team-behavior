# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K36 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report each confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents initially reported by customers or installers. Within 72 hours, manufacturers must notify affected owners when the incident indicates a material safety risk or requires a change in use. Updates and final findings must be provided to the regulator and affected owners as material information becomes available. Reports must include the model, date, known consequences, and actions taken. Manufacturers must maintain complete incident records, periodically reconcile those records against available service, warranty, installer, and customer reports, and promptly correct any omitted or inaccurate reports to the regulator and affected owners.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A model fails this requirement if more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model test results to the regulator and provide those results to current owners and prospective customers of that model; individual-company results need not be published to the general public. A model exceeding the limit must promptly implement regulator-approved risk controls, provide affected owners interim safety instructions, and complete corrective action and retesting within 12 months. The regulator will determine any sales restrictions based on the model’s risk; exceeding the test limit alone does not automatically prohibit sales.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide a secure remote shut-off capability through a retrofit or replacement within 72 months, where technically feasible, and report progress and any exceptions to the regulator. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and must not prevent a customer from contacting emergency services.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The regulator may approve a narrowly scoped, time-limited exception where a manufacturer demonstrates that a compliant design is not yet technically feasible, provided equivalent risk controls and a transition plan are in place.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected owners of a confirmed safety defect, material fire or overheating risk, or safety-related corrective action, with clear instructions for safe use and support. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. Manufacturers must report incident rates and recall actions to the regulator and notify affected owners without delay; notices must also be provided to relevant distributors and installers.
