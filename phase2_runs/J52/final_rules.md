# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J52 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving a battery, including overheating, fire, injury, emergency-service response, or property damage, to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account and available exposure data. They must provide a complete incident report within 10 days and promptly update it when material facts change. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours after identifying a material safety risk, explaining known hazards and immediate protective steps; they must provide updates as corrective actions develop. Reports must include model, incident date, circumstances, outcome, and known unit identifiers.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after a material design or manufacturing change. A model passes only if no more than 7% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results to the regulator for review. Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s actual test results, including its failure rate, along with material risks and corrective actions. These results must be provided directly to relevant customers and the regulator; individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. The regulator may require retesting or additional risk controls where results indicate a safety concern.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must, within 6 years after adoption, retrofit installed units that can be safely and feasibly upgraded, and publish a model-specific retrofit plan and schedule for other units. Remote shut-off must be secure, available to authorized emergency responders and the customer, and must not prevent local manual shut-off. Manufacturers must explain operation and limitations to customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Before that date, manufacturers must assess fire risk and use appropriate design controls, installation instructions, and customer emergency guidance. The regulator may require additional controls for a model presenting an immediate safety concern.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its thermal-test results, known material risks, required installation and maintenance, and emergency steps. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a newly identified material safety risk and provide updates on corrective action. Any model exceeding 8 verified thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year must be recalled. Manufacturers must provide supporting incident and exposure data to the regulator, which will verify the threshold and oversee recall implementation. Recall notices must explain the hazard, affected units, and remedy, and be sent directly to affected customers.
