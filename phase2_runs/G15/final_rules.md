# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G15 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every known thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with an initial account even if facts remain incomplete. They must submit an updated investigation and available cause information within 30 days. Affected customers must be notified within 72 hours when the incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include incident severity, unit/model identification, and steps taken; corrected or previously unreported incidents must also be disclosed promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a documented, independently auditable protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results, including failure rates, to the relevant regulator and directly to customers purchasing that model. Customer disclosure must be limited to those customers and must not require public posting or broad publication of individual-company per-model failure rates. A failure rate above 5% requires a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retesting within 12 months; it does not by itself require market exclusion. Regulators may impose interim safeguards or restrict sales where evidence indicates an imminent risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability, or provide an equally effective regulator-approved mitigation, within six years after adoption. The shut-off function must be tested at installation and at least annually, and its use must not prevent emergency responders from safely isolating the system.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be validated for the unit’s intended installation and failure modes, maintained in accordance with manufacturer instructions, and documented in customer-facing installation and maintenance materials. Manufacturers must disclose any known limitations and provide regulators with evidence supporting the system’s effectiveness.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices about thermal hazards, safe installation and operation, maintenance, incident-response steps, and any safety-relevant limitations or corrective actions. Notify affected customers promptly when their unit presents a credible safety risk or requires customer action, and provide updates as investigations develop. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and submit an investigation and corrective-action proposal. The regulator must assess the evidence and may require a mandatory recall or other safeguards where warranted, including earlier action where evidence indicates an imminent risk. Recall notices must explain the hazard, affected units, and remedy, and be sent without delay to affected customers.
