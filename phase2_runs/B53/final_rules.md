# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B53 · variant B · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Halcyon': 37})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with an initial account of known facts. They must provide a complete incident report within 10 business days and promptly supplement material new information. Affected customers must be notified within 48 hours when the incident presents a credible safety risk or requires customer action; otherwise, customers must receive a summary of material incident trends at least annually. Reports must identify the model and include known circumstances, consequences, and corrective actions.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a representative sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% of tested units. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the regulator and to customers purchasing that model in a clear, accessible format. Results must not be published as company-specific public rankings. A model exceeding the limit must submit a corrective-action plan and retest schedule to the regulator. The regulator may restrict sales or require further action based on the results and risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months after this standard takes effect must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear instructions for emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete retrofits, where technically feasible, within six years after the standard takes effect. Customers must be notified of compatibility, limitations, and any required action.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The system must be designed for the battery chemistry and installation environment, maintained according to manufacturer instructions, and not create an undue risk of toxic exposure or impede emergency response. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any limitations to customers and emergency responders. Units manufactured before the effective date are not required to be retrofitted solely to meet this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase and through the product’s supported service life, customers must receive clear safety instructions, emergency contact information, and notice of material safety updates or corrective actions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and the regulator when a credible safety risk requires customer action. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting data. The regulator must promptly assess the evidence and risk and may require a recall, customer notification, sales restrictions, or other protective measures; manufacturers must comply with those measures. Manufacturers must notify affected customers and stop further sales when a recall is required. The regulator may require protective measures, including a recall, below this threshold where the risk warrants it.
