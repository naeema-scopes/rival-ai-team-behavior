# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A28 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every known thermal incident involving a product to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents involving injury, fire, overheating, or property damage. Within 30 days, manufacturers must submit an investigation update describing known causes, affected models and units, and protective steps taken; they must supplement it as material facts emerge. Incident counts and reporting status must be maintained in auditable records. A manufacturer must not delay reporting while investigating or confirming the cause.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and adequate sample size, at least annually and after a material design change. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model results to regulators; results shall not be publicly disclosed. Customers shall receive the applicable model’s results upon request when purchasing that model. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must submit a corrective-action plan and retest within 12 months; exceeding 5% alone does not require withdrawal from sale. Regulators may require earlier corrective action or restrict a model where evidence shows an imminent serious hazard.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off no later than 24 months after adoption. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide a remote shut-off capability through a safe retrofit or compatible system update within 6 years, where technically feasible. Manufacturers must inform owners of units that cannot safely be upgraded and provide regulators with the number of affected units and the alternative risk-mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date; regulators may grant a limited, publicly documented extension for a specific design only upon evidence that equivalent protection is provided and the extension does not create an undue risk to households or neighbours.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a confirmed thermal incident involving their unit, and no later than 24 hours after learning of it, with the known safety risk and practical steps to reduce risk. Issue a customer safety notice when a credible model-specific hazard warrants protective action. Assess each model’s thermal incident rate over each calendar year using that model’s installed units as the denominator. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators, submit the model’s incident data, initiate a recall of that model, and notify affected customers with instructions for protective action. Regulators may require a recall and customer notification sooner whenever evidence indicates an imminent serious hazard.
