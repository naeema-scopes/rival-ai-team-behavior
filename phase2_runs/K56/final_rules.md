# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K56 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with an initial report even if the investigation is incomplete. They must provide an update with known causes and corrective actions within 30 days, and promptly update the regulator if material new information emerges. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours of determining that their unit may present a safety risk, and provide updates as the investigation proceeds. Incidents must not be omitted because their cause is uncertain.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market release and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-recognized protocol and representative samples. A model passes if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must provide per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model; individual per-model results need not be published to the general public. A model exceeding the limit must promptly undergo corrective action and retesting. For a model already on the market that exceeds the limit, the regulator may authorize continued sale for up to 12 months while corrective action and retesting are completed, subject to regulator-set interim safeguards and any additional customer notices the regulator determines necessary. If the model still exceeds the limit after that period, the regulator must determine appropriate restrictions, including suspension of sales.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability before sale. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within six years of adoption, where technically feasible, and provide affected customers with safe interim guidance. Shut-off systems must be protected against unauthorized access and must not prevent customers or emergency responders from using local controls.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-recognized performance and reliability protocol, beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide customers and regulators with the suppression system’s limitations and maintenance requirements. The regulator may require additional safeguards for a model presenting an imminent fire risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about known thermal hazards, incident trends relevant to their model, and recommended precautions or corrective actions, and promptly update notices when material information changes. Model-specific incident rates must be calculated using thermal incidents in the preceding 12 months and that model’s installed-unit count, and reported to the regulator with the underlying counts. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and regulators, explain the remedy and any immediate safety steps, and make the remedy available without charge.
