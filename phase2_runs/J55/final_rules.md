# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J55 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written incident report within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a potential safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include known facts and be updated as investigations proceed; incidents must not be omitted because the cause is uncertain.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sampling. The maximum permitted observed failure rate is 7% per model. Testing must use a documented sampling plan; results submitted to regulators must include sample size, observed failure rate, and an uncertainty estimate. Results, sample sizes, and corrective actions must be made available to customers of that model; company-specific per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. A model exceeding the limit must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and undergo retesting. Regulators must assess the risk and may impose interim safeguards or restrict sale where needed to protect safety; exceeding the limit does not by itself require permanent market withdrawal.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market 24 months or more after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a secure, documented shut-off capability that can be activated by the owner and, where an imminent safety risk warrants it, by the manufacturer with prompt notice to the owner. For installed units, manufacturers must provide an upgrade or other compliant mitigation within 60 months after adoption where technically feasible; otherwise they must notify affected owners and the regulator of the limitation and mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and enclosure. Manufacturers must document suppression-system testing and maintenance requirements and provide these to the regulator and customers. The requirement does not apply retroactively to units already installed.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers must receive a clear safety notice describing material thermal risks, required installation and maintenance, emergency steps, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of newly identified material safety risks and provide updated instructions or other corrective information. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on validated incident and installed-unit data. The manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, explain the risk and required action, and submit a recall and remedy plan to the regulator. Regulators may require a recall or other corrective action below this threshold where the evidence warrants it.
