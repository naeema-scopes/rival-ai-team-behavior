# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G30 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of discovery, including known model, serial number, location, and incident details. They shall notify affected customers within 72 hours of discovering an incident that presents a potential safety risk, sooner if an imminent risk requires it. Updates shall be provided as material facts become known. Records of incidents and reports shall be retained and made available to regulators on request.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C using a documented, repeatable protocol and a representative sample. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% per model, consistent with the regulator threshold under discussion. Test methods and per-model results shall be provided to the relevant regulator; customers buying a model shall receive its result, but individual company per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public. A model exceeding 5% shall submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator within 60 days and be retested within 12 months. Exceeding the limit shall not automatically require a sales pause. Unless the regulator determines that an imminent or otherwise unmitigated risk requires a pause, the manufacturer may continue sales while implementing regulator-approved interim safeguards and corrective action. Retesting is required after a material design change and at least annually.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall include a remotely accessible emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units to support remote emergency shut-off, or provide an equivalent safe mitigation where retrofit is not technically feasible, within six years of adoption. Shut-off systems shall be secure, fail-safe, and operable by authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system shall be tested for the applicable battery chemistry and installation conditions, maintained over the unit’s service life, and designed not to create additional hazards.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of a material thermal hazard, including the nature of the risk, affected model/serial numbers, immediate precautions, and available remedies; issue the notice within 72 hours after confirming the hazard, or sooner when an imminent risk requires it. A mandatory recall is triggered when a validated model-specific rate exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Rate calculations and supporting incident records shall be provided to regulators for review. The manufacturer shall notify regulators and affected customers and provide a repair, replacement, or refund plan.
