# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E48 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every confirmed battery-related overheating, thermal runaway, fire, or explosion to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours, or sooner if the regulator determines an urgent warning is needed. Reports must include known injuries, property damage, model and serial identifiers, and actions taken. Suspected incidents must be investigated and reported if confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after any safety-relevant design change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% of tested units for each model. Manufacturers must provide per-model test methods and actual results, including failure rates, to the relevant regulator and retain underlying records for inspection. Before purchase, customers must receive the specific model’s test date, actual failure rate and detailed test results, whether it meets the 5% limit, applicable safety warnings, and relevant safety instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify customers if retesting changes a model’s pass/fail status or reveals a material safety risk. A model exceeding the limit must be reported promptly to the regulator and undergo a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retest on a regulator-set timetable. Sales may continue during that period only if the regulator determines that continued sales do not present an unreasonable safety risk. The regulator may restrict or suspend sales if the model presents an unreasonable safety risk or fails to meet the approved plan.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market after 24 months from adoption must support a secure remote emergency shut-off accessible to the manufacturer and emergency responders, with appropriate safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide a feasible upgrade or retrofit path for installed units and complete retrofits within six years of adoption where technically supported. Customers must be informed of shut-off capabilities and any limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units placed on the market beginning 36 months after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the unit’s foreseeable battery-fire hazards. Manufacturers must document the system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide those requirements to customers. This requirement does not apply retroactively to units placed on the market before that date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its 55 °C sustained-heat test date, actual per-model failure rate and test results, whether it meets the test limit, applicable warnings, and emergency response and shut-off instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified safety risks, changes in a model’s pass/fail status, and updated safety instructions. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop sale of the model while the recall is underway, and carry out the regulator-approved recall or corrective action. The regulator should select an effective, proportionate remedy, which may include repair, replacement, or refund, based on the risk and practicability; a recall does not by itself require permanent market withdrawal. Regulators may also require a recall or other corrective action when the evidence indicates an unreasonable safety risk.
