# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H01 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account of known facts and affected models. They shall provide affected customers with a safety notice within 48 hours, including known hazards, immediate precautions, and how to obtain assistance. A follow-up report with the investigation findings and corrective actions is due to the regulator within 30 days, or with an explanation and revised date if the investigation remains open. Reports shall include incidents regardless of whether the manufacturer considers the product defective.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a regulator-approved protocol. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5%. A model exceeding 5% shall submit a regulator-reviewed corrective-action plan and undergo verified retesting before new market placement resumes. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model test results to the relevant regulator and to customers purchasing that model before purchase. Individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be publicly disclosed.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 24 months after adoption, subject to regulator-approved cybersecurity and reliability requirements. Manufacturers shall retrofit already-installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible, and report progress annually to the regulator. If retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer shall notify affected customers and provide an approved alternative emergency-safety measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document the suppression design and its validation under the regulator-approved safety protocol. This requirement does not waive incident-reporting, testing, or corrective-action obligations during the implementation period.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with model-specific safety notices describing material hazards, precautions, and assistance routes before purchase and promptly after any material new safety finding. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when the regulator determines that a serious imminent hazard warrants recall. The incident-rate trigger shall be assessed separately for each model, using incident counts and installed-unit denominators provided to regulators. Manufacturers shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers when a recall is triggered and provide remedy instructions. Incident rates and recall notices shall be reported to regulators; individual-company per-model test failure rates shall not be publicly disclosed.
