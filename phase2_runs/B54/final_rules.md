# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B54 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving a battery, including overheating without fire, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage, to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 30 days. Affected customers must be notified within 5 business days when an incident presents a potential safety risk to their unit or other units of the same model. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and provide them to regulators on request.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. A model must not exceed a 7% failure rate. Manufacturers must submit per-model results to the relevant regulator and disclose results for that model to customers who buy it. Public reporting may use aggregated, non-identifying industry results; individual companies’ per-model failure rates must not be publicly disclosed. Manufacturers must retain test records and provide them to regulators on request. Exceeding the ceiling requires prompt corrective action under regulator oversight.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete retrofits within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Any retrofit must be performed safely and customers must receive advance notice and instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide customers and regulators with clear information about the suppression system and its limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of safety risks involving their unit or model, including recommended precautions and corrective actions. A recall is mandatory when verified thermal incidents for any model exceed 8 per 100,000 installed units during any consecutive 12-month period. The rate must use documented thermal incidents and the model’s installed-unit count for the same period; manufacturers must preserve supporting records and provide the rate and records to regulators on request. Manufacturers must report the incident rate and recall actions to regulators and notify affected customers without undue delay.
