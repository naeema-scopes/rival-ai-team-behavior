# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B42 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency service response within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and submit a follow-up report with known causes and corrective actions within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours of identifying a material safety risk, and promptly provide updates and recommended precautions. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, and known outcomes, subject to applicable privacy protections.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least every three years thereafter. A failure rate above 5% triggers a corrective-action plan submitted to regulators and retesting within 12 months. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically make a model ineligible for sale; regulators may impose proportionate interim safeguards or restrict sales where the manufacturer fails to meet its plan or evidence indicates an imminent serious risk. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model test results, including the test date and failure rate, to regulators and to customers who purchase that model. Regulators may protect model-specific results from disclosure to the general public and may publish aggregate industry results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to support remote shut-off within six years of this standard taking effect, where technically feasible. Shut-off capability must include safeguards against unauthorized activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression for units manufactured 36 months or more after this standard is adopted. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the battery chemistry and foreseeable installation conditions, and maintained according to the manufacturer's instructions. Manufacturers must document the system's performance and provide installation and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety instructions at purchase and prompt notice of any material safety risk affecting their model, including recommended precautions and available remedies. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators of thermal incidents and provide customers with relevant safety information. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, regulators must require a recall and manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and provide required recall remedies.
