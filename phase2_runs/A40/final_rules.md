# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A40 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, with an initial report even if the cause is unknown. A final investigation report is due within 30 days, with updates as material facts emerge. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within five days when an incident presents a credible safety risk, and promptly provide safety instructions. Records of all thermal incidents, including incidents not meeting these thresholds, must be retained for at least ten years.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. A model exceeds the performance benchmark if more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must submit results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the regulator and make them available without charge to customers of that model; individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. For a model exceeding the benchmark, the manufacturer must submit a corrective-action plan and complete a retest within 90 days. Regulators should consider the test results, incident evidence, and effectiveness of interim risk controls when determining proportionate measures; sales restrictions are appropriate where the model remains above the benchmark and the evidence indicates material safety risk. The parties should review the threshold after three years against accumulated test and incident evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market from three years after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer notification when shut-off occurs. For already-installed units, manufacturers must provide compatible remote shut-off capability or an equivalent safe mitigation within six years after adoption, prioritizing higher-risk units and notifying owners of the schedule. Any remote shut-off capability must not prevent manual emergency response.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market 36 months after adoption or later must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and enclosure. Manufacturers must provide evidence of performance and maintenance requirements to the regulator and disclose relevant limitations to customers. The regulator may approve an equivalent protective design where it provides comparable fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear information on the model’s thermal-test results, known material fire or overheating risks, required maintenance, and emergency response instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of new material safety information and provide updated instructions. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on regulator-validated data, a mandatory recall is triggered. The regulator must promptly oversee proportionate implementation to reduce risk, including safe-use or shutdown instructions and repair, replacement, or refund; a recall does not by itself require a permanent market ban. Any continued sale during implementation must be subject to regulator approval and effective risk controls. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and the regulator of required actions.
