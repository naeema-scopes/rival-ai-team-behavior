# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E36 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, property damage, or an emergency shut-off to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written incident report within 5 calendar days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported to the regulator within 5 calendar days. Affected customers must be notified within 24 hours when there is an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within 5 calendar days. Reports must include the model, incident circumstances, and known injuries or damage; updates must be provided as material facts become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production samples. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for each model. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, test protocol, sample size, and corrective actions to the relevant regulator, and disclose the per-model failure rate and safety status to customers considering or owning that model. Results must not be withheld from those customers as confidential. Per-model test failure rates must not be published to the general public. Exceeding 5% requires prompt corrective action and regulator follow-up; it does not by itself require an automatic market ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models must include a remotely accessible emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within 6 years of adoption of this standard, where technically feasible; any units not retrofitted must be clearly disclosed to their owners. Shut-off systems must be tested, maintain secure access controls, and not depend solely on a functioning internet connection for local emergency operation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document and validate suppression performance using a regulator-approved protocol, and disclose the suppression system and its limitations to customers. The transition period does not waive applicable fire-safety requirements in the meantime.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices for their model, including known hazards, required precautions, and relevant updates or corrective actions. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly when a material safety risk is identified and provide instructions for mitigation, repair, or replacement. A recall is mandatory when a model’s confirmed thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units during any 12-month period. The rate must use the model’s installed-unit population during that period and incidents confirmed by the manufacturer or regulator; manufacturers must promptly provide the underlying incident count and denominator to regulators for verification. Upon reaching the trigger, manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers promptly, explain the recall remedy and timeline, and provide progress updates until the remedy is completed. Per-model incident information must be provided to regulators and affected customers as needed for safety, but need not be published to the general public.
