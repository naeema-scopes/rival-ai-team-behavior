# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A33 · variant A · NOT adopted (signatures: {})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written incident report within 5 business days. Within 24 hours, the manufacturer shall also notify affected customers when the incident presents a credible safety risk; otherwise, customers shall be notified within 5 business days. Reports shall include known product/model, incident circumstances, injuries or damage, and immediate corrective actions. Manufacturers shall maintain incident records and promptly supplement reports as facts become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and at least annually thereafter. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7% of tested units per model, taking effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall submit complete per-model results to the relevant regulator. Regulators may publish aggregate or otherwise non-identifying results, but shall not publish individual-company per-model failure rates. Retesting is required after a material design or manufacturing change. A model exceeding the limit shall receive a documented corrective-action plan and confirmatory retest opportunity; after the limit takes effect, it shall not be newly sold unless it passes a confirmatory test. Regulators may require earlier corrective action or protective measures where results or incident evidence indicate an imminent serious safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units shall support remote emergency shut-off meeting a regulator-approved performance standard. Manufacturers shall retrofit already-installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within 6 years after adoption of this standard, where technically feasible, and shall report progress annually to the relevant regulator. Manufacturers shall provide customers clear instructions for using the shut-off capability.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance standard. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document conformity and provide evidence to the relevant regulator before placing compliant units on the market.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers, at sale and through a readily accessible safety notice, model-specific safety instructions, known material hazards, relevant corrective actions, and a regulator-approved summary of the model’s thermal-test status that does not disclose individual-company per-model failure rates. Complete per-model test results shall be provided to the relevant regulator under S2. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of credible safety risks and provide updates as facts become available. A recall is mandatory when a regulator-verified annual rate for any model exceeds 10 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units, or when the regulator determines a defect presents an imminent serious safety risk. The regulator shall use consistent exposure data and a documented verification method, and may require immediate interim protective measures while verifying a reported rate. Verification shall not exempt a model from recall once the trigger is confirmed. Incident rates and recall decisions shall be reported to regulators.
