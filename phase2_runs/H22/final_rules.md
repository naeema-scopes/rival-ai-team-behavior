# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H22 · variant H · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Halcyon': 80})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report each thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 48 hours when the incident presents a potential safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include the known circumstances, model and unit identification where available, and any immediate safety instructions. These reporting duties do not require public disclosure of individual-company or per-model test failure rates.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a consistent, regulator-accepted protocol before sale and after a material safety-relevant design change. A model passes only if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must provide per-model test protocols, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator and to customers considering purchase of that model. These results are not required to be published to the general public. Manufacturers must retain supporting test records and provide them to regulators on request.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent remote shut-off capability for installed units that lack it within 6 years of adoption. Customers must receive clear instructions on how to use the capability and how to contact the manufacturer if it does not function.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must disclose to customers whether their unit includes built-in suppression and provide safe-use and maintenance instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of any safety issue that could affect safe operation, including the nature of the risk, affected models or units, and clear steps customers should take. Notices must be updated as material information becomes available. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly report the model-specific data and supporting information to the regulator for prompt risk assessment. The exceedance triggers regulator review and may support a recall where the regulator determines one is warranted; it does not by itself mandate a recall. This threshold applies to the model-specific rate, not the manufacturer’s fleet-wide rate. Manufacturers must promptly inform regulators and affected customers of any recall.
