# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G22 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, evacuation, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 business days. Other confirmed overheating incidents must be reported to the regulator within 5 business days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers promptly when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and no later than 5 business days after that determination. Reports must include known circumstances and any immediate safety action; corrections or newly discovered information must be submitted promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and an adequate, documented sample size. The maximum permitted failure rate is 8% for each model. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to regulators for oversight, and must provide the numerical failure rate and sample size for each model to its current and prospective customers. Regulators may require retesting, corrective action, or restrict sales where results show an unreasonable safety risk. Customer notices must also describe material hazards, precautions, and corrective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required for all new units manufactured 12 months or more after adoption. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within 72 months after adoption, unless a regulator approves an alternative safety measure for units that cannot reasonably be retrofitted. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression when manufactured 36 months or more after adoption. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and designed to reduce fire propagation and risk to occupants and the public. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence that the system has been validated for the unit’s intended installation and use.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide prospective and current customers with clear safety notices for their model, including known material hazards, recommended precautions, and available corrective actions. Notify affected customers promptly, and no later than five business days after determining a material safety risk; issue urgent instructions immediately where continued use may pose imminent danger. A manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers and initiate a recall when a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 10 per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, or sooner when regulators determine a serious defect creates an unreasonable risk. Regulators may require a recall or other corrective action regardless of the numerical threshold.
