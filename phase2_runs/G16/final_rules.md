# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G16 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 48 hours of learning of it, with a follow-up investigation report within 30 days. Affected customers must be notified promptly when an incident indicates a potential safety risk, and no later than five days after that determination. Reports shall include incident counts, installed-unit denominators, model, known harms, and corrective actions. Manufacturers shall retain records and report incidents even when the cause is uncertain.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the regulator before sale and after any material design change. Customers considering or owning a model shall receive its individual 55 °C failure rate, test date, a plain-language summary of its safety status, clear safety instructions, and any required actions. A result above 5% triggers regulator review and a corrective-action plan; any sales restriction shall be determined by the regulator based on evidence and risk. The parties will review the threshold against accumulated evidence after two years.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units placed on the market from 24 months after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers shall provide a retrofit or equivalent safe alternative within six years, prioritizing units with elevated risk. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers of the capability, limitations, and any required action.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The requirement applies to all models placed on the market from that date; manufacturers shall document the system’s effectiveness and maintenance requirements in the product safety materials.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and upon material updates, manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety instructions, a plain-language summary of the applicable model’s safety status, and any required customer actions. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks, corrective actions, and any recall, using direct contact where reasonably available and public notice otherwise. A model exceeding 8 confirmed thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period shall trigger prompt regulator review of verified counts, denominators, attribution, and risk. The regulator shall determine whether a recall or other corrective action is required based on that evidence. Pending review, manufacturers shall promptly notify the regulator and implement interim protections for customers and neighbours where warranted. Review shall not delay necessary interim protections.
