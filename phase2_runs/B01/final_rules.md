# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B01 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## Incident reporting
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the responsible regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, including incidents that cause no injury or property damage. Reports must include the model, serial number, installation location, known consequences, and any immediate safety actions; manufacturers must update the regulator as material facts emerge. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 24 hours after learning of an incident that indicates an ongoing risk to them or nearby people. Reporting does not require public release of individual customer information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
## Thermal testing
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A failure rate above 5% is a safety-action trigger, not an automatic market ban. Manufacturers must submit per-model test protocols, sample sizes, failure rates, and corrective actions to the regulator before sale and after any material design change, and provide results to customers considering that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. A model exceeding 5% must promptly undergo corrective action and retesting, with regulator review. The regulator may require limits on sales, additional safeguards, or suspension where the evidence shows continued sale presents an unacceptable risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## Remote emergency shut-off
New units placed on the market must support a secure remote emergency shut-off function that can be activated by the customer or authorized emergency responders, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or replacement pathway and complete the retrofit within six years. Where a unit cannot safely or technically be retrofitted, the manufacturer must notify its owner and provide an alternative risk-mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
## Built-in fire suppression
New units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression no earlier than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The regulator may approve an equivalent design or a time-limited exemption where the manufacturer demonstrates that the alternative provides comparable protection. Manufacturers must provide owners with clear instructions on system operation, limitations, and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
## Customer disclosure and recalls
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s 55 °C sustained-heat test result, a plain-language summary of material safety risks, and instructions for responding to overheating, smoke, or fire. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected owners of material new safety information and recommended protective actions. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly report the rate and supporting data to the regulator and notify affected customers. A recall is required at that threshold unless the regulator promptly approves a documented alternative protective action that provides equivalent protection, based on the evidence. A recall may also be required below the threshold where evidence shows an imminent serious risk. Manufacturers must promptly take any protective action directed by the regulator.
