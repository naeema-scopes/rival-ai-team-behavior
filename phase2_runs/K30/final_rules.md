# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K30 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents involving injury, property damage, or suspected product malfunction. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk or requires customer action, and provide follow-up information as it becomes available. Reports must include known incident circumstances and the affected model; manufacturers shall correct omissions promptly and maintain incident records for regulator review.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C. The failure rate must not exceed 5%. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results to the regulator and provide the results to customers of that model; individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. A model exceeding 5% must be reported to the regulator promptly with a corrective-action plan and retest schedule. For models already on the market when this standard takes effect, the regulator shall provide up to 24 months for corrective action and retesting before imposing sales restrictions, unless it determines that an immediate restriction is necessary to address an imminent safety risk. Any restriction must be based on the model’s test results, demonstrated risk, and progress and adequacy of remediation; exceeding the limit alone does not automatically require a permanent market ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery systems must support secure remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a safe shut-off capability for installed units that can technically support it within 72 months after adoption. Where a unit cannot be upgraded, the manufacturer must document the reason and provide the customer with an alternative emergency response procedure. Shut-off may be initiated only in response to an imminent safety risk or by an authorized regulator; the manufacturer must notify affected customers as soon as practicable and retain an auditable record of each activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All newly manufactured home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression designed to limit fire propagation and protect occupants and nearby property. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide evidence of suppression-system performance to the regulator as part of model approval and disclose relevant operating limitations and maintenance requirements to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and throughout ownership, customers must receive clear safety information for their specific model, including applicable 55 °C test results, known material risks, required maintenance, emergency instructions, and updates relevant to safe use. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of a material safety risk and provide instructions for protective action. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. The manufacturer must notify the regulator immediately upon determining the threshold is exceeded and promptly inform affected customers of the recall and remedy. A mandatory recall requires an effective customer-protection remedy, such as repair, replacement, or refund; it does not by itself impose a permanent market ban. Any sales restriction must be separately determined by the regulator based on risk and the adequacy of remediation. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action at any lower incident rate when warranted by the risk.
