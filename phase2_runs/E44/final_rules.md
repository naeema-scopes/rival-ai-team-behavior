# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E44 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 48 hours after determining that the incident indicates a potential safety risk, providing known risks and protective actions. Reports and notices shall not be delayed while cause is being investigated; updates shall be provided as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a documented, reproducible protocol and a representative sample. Manufacturers shall submit complete per-model results, including failure rates, to the relevant regulator and provide those complete results to customers considering or owning that model. Results above a 5% failure-rate benchmark shall prompt regulator review and a corrective-action and retesting plan; the benchmark shall not by itself require an automatic market ban. Regulators may require further measures based on test results and demonstrated risk. The parties shall review the protocol and benchmark against accumulated evidence after two years.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall support remote emergency shut-off within 12 months of adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall submit a documented retrofit plan for installed units within 12 months of adoption and complete retrofits within 6 years, subject to regulator-approved safety exemptions. The plan shall prioritize units and locations presenting the greatest safety risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression no earlier than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Before that effective date, manufacturers shall assess suppression options and provide regulators with an implementation plan. The parties may review feasibility and safety evidence before the requirement takes effect.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers clear, actionable safety notices promptly when a credible model-specific safety risk is identified, including protective steps, updates as material facts emerge, and notice of any corrective action or recall. Regulators shall receive model-level incident data. Incidents shall be attributed to the model involved and divided by that model’s installed units during the same reporting year; company-wide rates shall not substitute for model-level rates. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the regulator shall promptly verify attribution and denominator, issue a public safety notice, and require a recall unless the regulator documents that an alternative corrective action will adequately mitigate the risk. Affected customers shall be promptly notified of the recall or alternative action and its required protective steps.
