# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B02 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with an initial account of the event and any known safety implications. They must provide a complete incident report within 10 calendar days, updating it as new information becomes available. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible risk requiring a warning or action to protect people or property. Reports and notices must not be delayed while cause is investigated.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including failure rates, to the relevant regulator. Manufacturers must provide the results and failure rate for each model to customers considering or owning that model, and must not withhold them from those customers. Regulators must not publish a company's individual per-model failure rates; they may publish aggregated or anonymized results. A model exceeding the 5% failure-rate benchmark must undergo regulator-supervised review and corrective-action planning, taking account of test uncertainty and real-world safety evidence. Regulators may restrict sales where necessary to address an imminent or material safety risk. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers of a test result indicating a material safety concern.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible shut-off method. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within six years of adoption, prioritizing units presenting the greatest safety risk. If a unit cannot be safely retrofitted, the manufacturer must notify its owner and provide an alternative protective measure, subject to regulator oversight.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence that the suppression system is effective and does not introduce other material safety risks.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information, including model-specific 55 °C sustained-heat test results, known material hazards, and instructions for safe installation, use, and emergency response. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of newly identified material risks and provide timely safety notices with any protective actions customers should take. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 validated thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must report incident counts and installed-unit denominators to regulators, who may require interim protective measures while validating the data.
