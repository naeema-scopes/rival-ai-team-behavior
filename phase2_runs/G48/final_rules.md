# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G48 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report thermal incidents involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of the incident, and provide a follow-up report within 30 days with findings available at that time. Customers whose unit is implicated must be notified within 5 calendar days, with immediate notice where an ongoing hazard requires urgent action. Reports must include known incident counts and identify any known units or models affected. If a manufacturer later discovers a qualifying incident that was not previously reported, it must promptly report the incident to the regulator and notify affected customers under the same urgency requirements.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Per-model results must be submitted confidentially to the regulator; regulators may publish aggregate results, but not individual-company per-model failure rates. A model with a failure rate above 10% may not be sold. For models above 5% and at or below 10%, manufacturers must submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator and retest within 12 months. Results and any applicable safety instructions must be provided to customers who buy the model.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market 12 months or more after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must submit to regulators, within 36 months, a plan for addressing installed units and completing any required retrofits within 6 years after adoption. Plans must prioritize units with known elevated risk. The regulator may approve an alternative where retrofit is technically infeasible or creates a greater safety risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Regulators may approve equivalent protective designs or a limited extension where a manufacturer demonstrates that compliance is technically infeasible, provided interim risk controls are in place.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive model-specific safety notices, including applicable test results and safety instructions, at purchase and promptly when new material risks emerge. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit supporting incident and exposure data, an investigation, and corrective-action plan. The regulator must promptly assess the evidence. A confirmed rate above the threshold triggers a recall unless the regulator determines, on documented evidence, that the incidents do not indicate a material safety risk; any decision not to recall must be explained to affected customers. A recall must include timely customer notification and clear instructions for safe shutdown, repair, or replacement.
