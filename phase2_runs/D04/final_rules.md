# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D04 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal event involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, property damage, emergency-service response, or a safety-related shutdown within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written preliminary report within 5 business days. Any other suspected thermal incident must be reported within 10 business days. Manufacturers must preserve event records and provide updates as material facts emerge. Customers whose unit is involved must be notified directly within 5 business days, or sooner where an immediate hazard warrants it. Reports must include known model, serial number, event date, consequences, and corrective actions; uncertainty must be identified rather than omitted.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample size, protocol, and corrective-action records to the regulator and provide the per-model test results directly to customers who buy or are considering buying that model. Results need not be published for the general public. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must be promptly reported to the regulator and affected customers, investigated, and subject to a corrective-action plan and retesting. Exceeding 5% alone does not require a sales ban; the regulator may restrict sales if the manufacturer fails to provide or implement an adequate plan, or if the regulator finds an unreasonable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized access and a local means of operation. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway for installed units that lack this capability and complete retrofits within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Where retrofitting is technically infeasible, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and the regulator, explain the limitation, and provide an equivalent risk-reduction measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard is adopted. Suppression systems must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation, and designed and tested to limit fire spread and protect occupants and nearby persons. Manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and safe-use instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive known material thermal hazards and required installation, maintenance, and emergency instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material safety risks and provide updates on corrective actions. Manufacturers must provide the per-model 55 °C test results directly to customers who own or are considering buying that model; results need not be published for the general public. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, explain the risk and remedy, and provide safe shutdown or other interim instructions where needed. A recall is also required when the regulator determines that a serious defect creates an unreasonable risk, regardless of the incident-rate threshold; a recall requires an effective safety remedy and does not by itself mandate a permanent sales ban.
