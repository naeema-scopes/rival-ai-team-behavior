# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B36 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers shall report every battery-related overheating, fire, or other thermal incident to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. A death, serious injury, evacuation, or fire spreading beyond the premises must be reported immediately and no later than 4 hours after discovery. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 48 hours of identifying a credible product-related hazard, and provide updates as material facts become known. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and corrective steps; suspected incidents must not be withheld pending confirmation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator and disclose the results to customers who buy that model, including existing customers, in clear, accessible safety information. Results may be withheld from the general public, but not from regulators or affected customers. Regulators shall review results for safety risks and may require investigation, corrective action, retesting, or proportionate restrictions. No fixed failure-rate limit is established by this standard.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All newly manufactured home battery systems shall include a remotely operable emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units to provide remote emergency shut-off, where technically feasible, within 6 years of adoption. The shut-off shall be accessible to authorized emergency responders and the customer, preserve safe local isolation if communications fail, and include safeguards against unauthorized activation. Where retrofitting is not technically feasible, the manufacturer shall notify the customer and regulator and provide an equivalent documented emergency procedure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system shall be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation, and tested to applicable regulator-approved standards. Manufacturers shall provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any limitations of the system to customers and installers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices about identified hazards, recommended actions, and relevant updates promptly after identifying a credible product-related hazard. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on consistent model-level denominators and regulator-verified incident data, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and submit the data and risk assessment for review. The regulator shall determine whether a mandatory recall or other protective action is required, considering incident severity, exposure, and available corrective measures; manufacturers shall promptly implement any required action. Regardless of the threshold, a manufacturer shall initiate a recall when necessary to address an unreasonable risk of death or serious injury. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers without delay when a recall or other protective action is required and provide safe-use, repair, replacement, or return instructions. Per-model test failure rates shall be disclosed to customers who buy that model as specified in S2.
