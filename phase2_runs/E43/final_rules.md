# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E43 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with an initial account of known facts. They must provide a complete incident report within 10 business days and promptly supplement it as new information becomes available. Within 24 hours, manufacturers must notify the affected household and any other person known to face an immediate safety risk, providing known hazards and protective steps. For other thermal incidents, affected customers must be notified within 5 business days. Reports must include model, serial number where available, location, event description, injuries or damage, and actions taken.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before initial market release and at least annually thereafter, using a documented, independently witnessed protocol. Within 30 days of testing, manufacturers must provide complete per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, confidentially to the relevant regulator. Manufacturers must also provide the relevant model’s latest complete test results to prospective customers before purchase and to existing customers of that model within 30 days of testing. Results may be shared with those customers without disclosing other models’ results. Manufacturers must retain supporting records for inspection. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt notification to the regulator and a corrective-action plan within 30 days; it is a review trigger, not an automatic market prohibition. The regulator will determine follow-up testing and other measures based on test evidence and incident history. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and protective steps.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off before installation. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide and enable remote emergency shut-off within 6 years of adoption of this standard, where technically feasible, and notify owners of the schedule and safe-use instructions. If a unit cannot be upgraded, the manufacturer must document the reason, notify its owner, and provide an alternative emergency isolation procedure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s design and effectiveness and provide installation and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly provide affected customers and people facing an immediate safety risk with hazard information and protective steps, and notify other customers of relevant model-specific safety hazards. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers when the trigger is met, explain the risk and remedies, and report recall progress.
