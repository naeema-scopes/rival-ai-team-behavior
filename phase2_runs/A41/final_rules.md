# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A41 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any death, serious injury, fire, thermal runaway, or emergency service response involving a battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and notify affected customers within 72 hours. Other overheating or thermal incidents shall be reported to the regulator within five business days and included in a quarterly customer-facing incident summary. Reports shall identify the model, incident date, and known circumstances. Manufacturers shall preserve incident records and promptly correct incomplete reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. For the first two years after this standard takes effect, a model’s failure rate shall not exceed 8%; thereafter it shall not exceed 5%. A model above the applicable limit may receive one extension of up to two years, provided the manufacturer submits a documented corrective-action plan and retesting shows measurable improvement. The regulator shall grant the extension unless it finds, based on model-specific evidence, that the extension would pose an unacceptable safety risk; it may impose interim safeguards and require progress reports. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results to the relevant regulator and provide them to customers who buy that model. Per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public. A model exceeding the applicable limit shall undergo corrective action and retesting; regulators may require additional safeguards pending compliance.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 12 months after this standard is adopted. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units to provide this capability within six years, where technically feasible, and document any units that cannot be retrofitted for regulator review. Shut-off controls shall use authenticated access, log activations, and include safeguards against unauthorized activation and unsafe restart. Remote shut-off does not replace other emergency protections or customer instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All newly manufactured home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard is adopted. The suppression system shall be independently tested and designed to limit propagation of thermal runaway. Manufacturers shall provide regulators with evidence of compliance; the requirement does not replace applicable testing, reporting, or recall duties.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive model-specific 55 °C test results, known material safety risks, and clear emergency-response instructions. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and provide updated safety instructions. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period; regulators may also require a recall where the incident pattern presents an imminent or otherwise unacceptable risk. Manufacturers shall notify regulators and affected customers promptly and provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions. Corrective action or retesting does not suspend a mandatory recall.
