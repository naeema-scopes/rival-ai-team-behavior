# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E55 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator every fire, thermal runaway, or overheating event involving a battery, including events without injury, property damage, or emergency-service response. Initial notice is due within 24 hours of learning of the event; a preliminary investigation report is due within 10 business days, with material updates provided promptly. Reports must include known incident counts and the installed-unit denominator used for any rate, and be corrected when new information becomes available. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident reveals a credible product safety risk, providing safety instructions and updates as the investigation proceeds.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and independent laboratory. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including failure rates, to the relevant regulator. Results for a specific model must be provided in clear, accessible form to customers who buy or own that model before purchase and upon request for existing owners; individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. Aggregate, non-identifying industry results may be published. A model with a failure rate above 5% must submit a corrective-action plan and be retested within 12 months. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sales; regulators may restrict new sales or require further safeguards if the model remains above the limit after retesting.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery systems must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, when necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under regulator-approved safeguards. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions, maintain secure access, and log and report activations. For installed systems lacking this capability, manufacturers must provide a safe retrofit or equivalent risk-reduction measure within 6 years of adoption, prioritizing systems with known safety risks. Any remote action must preserve emergency-response access and notify the customer as soon as practicable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The system must be validated for the battery chemistry and installation configuration, and maintained in accordance with manufacturer instructions. Before the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must provide evidence of effectiveness and ensure suppression does not create additional hazards. Regulators may require earlier case-specific safeguards where a demonstrated imminent risk warrants them.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and at installation, customers must receive clear written instructions on safe installation, operation, emergency response, and how to report a safety concern. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a material safety risk and provide interim precautions and repair or replacement information. The annual thermal-incident rate for each model must count all known thermal incidents, including events not previously reported, and use the installed-unit denominator for that model; manufacturers must correct reported rates when new information becomes available. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must immediately notify the regulator and initiate an independent review, while providing affected customers with interim safety information. A recall or other corrective action must be required when the review confirms an unacceptable safety risk; regulators may order a recall at any time where evidence shows an imminent serious risk, regardless of the rate.
