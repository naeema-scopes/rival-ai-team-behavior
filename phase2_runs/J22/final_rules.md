# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J22 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary incident record. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. Affected customers must be notified within 72 hours for serious incidents and within 10 business days for other confirmed incidents, with known hazards and immediate safety instructions. Manufacturers must update regulators and affected customers as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units before sale and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to regulators and make them available to customers who purchase that model. Per-model failure rates must not be published for the general public under this standard. Any model exceeding the 5% benchmark must be reported to the regulator within 5 business days and be subject to a time-bound corrective-action plan and confirmatory testing. Exceeding the benchmark alone does not automatically prohibit sale; the regulator may impose proportionate restrictions if the manufacturer fails to implement adequate corrective action or the regulator finds an inadequately mitigated serious risk. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material actionable safety risks and instructions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market 24 months or more after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off. For installed units that support the function, manufacturers must provide a secure method to shut off the unit and clear instructions for its use. Within 6 years after adoption, manufacturers must assess whether installed units that lack the function can be safely upgraded and offer an upgrade where technically feasible. This section does not require manufacturers to retrofit installed units. Where an upgrade is not feasible or is declined, manufacturers must provide customers with an alternative emergency isolation procedure and disclose the unit’s limitation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed to reduce the risk of fire propagation. The system must be validated through a regulator-approved test protocol, and manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of its effectiveness and maintenance requirements. Existing installed units are not subject to retrofit under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear installation and operating safety instructions, warning signs of overheating, emergency response steps, and contact details for reporting incidents, at purchase and in accessible digital form. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of new material safety risks and provide updated instructions. When a model’s verified thermal incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers. The regulator must promptly review the verified rate and require a time-bound corrective-action plan; it must order a recall when evidence shows that a recall is necessary to address an inadequately mitigated serious risk. Verification or implementation arrangements must not delay protective action where a serious or imminent hazard exists. Manufacturers must provide customers with recall instructions, remedies, and updates until the recall is complete.
