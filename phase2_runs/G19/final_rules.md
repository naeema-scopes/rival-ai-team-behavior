# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G19 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency service response within 24 hours of learning of it. An incident is reportable upon credible indication of a thermal event; reporting shall not be delayed pending confirmation of cause, and incidents learned of through customers or emergency responders are included. Manufacturers shall provide a complete incident report within 10 business days and may submit later updates as facts are verified. Within 72 hours of learning of an incident that presents a potential safety risk, the manufacturer shall notify affected customers and provide appropriate safety instructions. Reports shall include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and actions taken. Manufacturers shall maintain incident records and provide them to the regulator on request.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a representative sample and a documented, consistent protocol. For the first five years after adoption, a model may be marketed only if its observed failure rate is no greater than 8%; thereafter, the limit shall be 5%. Manufacturers shall provide per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the regulator. Customers considering or owning a model shall receive its test methods, sample sizes, and results directly. Regulators may publish aggregated results, but shall not publish an individual company’s per-model failure rate. Manufacturers shall retest after material design changes and provide updated results to the regulator and affected customers. If a model exceeds the applicable failure-rate limit, the manufacturer shall take corrective action and pass a retest before marketing that model.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units shall include remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units that lack this capability, or provide an equivalent regulator-approved safety measure, within 6 years after adoption. The capability shall be available to authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation; customers shall be informed how it works and how to contact emergency support.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall provide the regulator with documentation of the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements, and shall inform customers of its operation and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At sale and whenever safety information materially changes, manufacturers shall provide customers with model-specific 55 °C sustained-heat test methods, sample sizes, results, known thermal-incident rates, safety instructions, and a means to report incidents. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers and the regulator of a safety notice, including the hazard, affected units, and steps customers should take. A manufacturer shall initiate a regulator-supervised recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period; it shall promptly notify affected customers and provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions. The regulator may require a recall sooner where the evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
