# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G35 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, followed by a written report within 10 calendar days with known circumstances and corrective actions. If an incident presents an immediate risk to other users or the public, notify affected customers and relevant emergency authorities as soon as practicable and no later than 24 hours after assessing the risk. Submit annual totals, including incidents previously unreported, and explain reporting gaps.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved, consistent protocol before sale and at least annually thereafter. No more than 8% of tested units of a model may fail. A model exceeding this limit may not be newly sold until corrective action and a passing retest. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model results, including failure rate and test date, to regulators and to customers buying that model, before purchase. Regulators may publish aggregated results that do not identify an individual company’s model or failure rate. Manufacturers shall provide affected customers model-specific safety instructions where needed.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units placed on the market shall support remote emergency shut-off that can be activated by the manufacturer or an authorized emergency responder when necessary to address an imminent safety risk. Manufacturers shall provide clear notice to customers when shut-off is activated and instructions for safe follow-up. For units already installed when this standard is adopted, manufacturers shall enable remote shut-off where technically feasible within 6 years, or provide regulators and affected customers a documented alternative emergency-safety plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide regulators evidence that the suppression system is appropriate for the battery chemistry and enclosure, and shall include customer instructions for its limitations and safe emergency response.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers clear installation, operation, warning, and emergency-response instructions at purchase and promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material hazards, recommended precautions, and safety corrections. If any model’s thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units in any year, based on regulator-validated incident and installed-base data, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, investigate, and complete regulator-required corrective action. A mandatory recall is required if the regulator determines that an ongoing material risk remains after corrective action, or that required corrective action has failed or was not completed. The regulator shall determine the recall’s scope and timing. Manufacturers shall provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions.
