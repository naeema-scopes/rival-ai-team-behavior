# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E29 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account and available unit/model identifiers. They must provide affected customers with a safety notice within 72 hours, and promptly update it as facts are confirmed. A complete incident report is due to the regulator within 30 days. Suspected incidents must be logged and investigated; later-confirmed incidents remain reportable.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after material design changes. Manufacturers shall submit protocols, sample sizes, and per-model results to the regulator. Results shall be provided to customers and prospective customers of that model; individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be publicly published. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. A model exceeding 5% must submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retest within 12 months. While the plan is being implemented, sales may continue unless the regulator finds an imminent safety risk or the manufacturer fails to meet the plan. Regulators may impose proportionate interim safeguards or sales restrictions based on the evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units sold from 24 months after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. For installed units, manufacturers must submit a regulator-approved, risk-based retrofit plan within 24 months after adoption and complete any required retrofit within 72 months, prioritizing higher-risk units. The plan must identify units that can be updated by software or communications changes and those requiring hardware replacement.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance protocol. Manufacturers must validate effectiveness and disclose limitations and maintenance requirements to customers. Regulators may grant only time-limited, publicly documented exceptions where equivalent protection is demonstrated.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive the model-specific 55 °C sustained-heat test results before purchase, safety notices for reportable incidents within 72 hours, and prompt updates as material facts are confirmed. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers and initiate a regulator-supervised recall, including repair, replacement, or refund options and progress updates. A recall is a corrective safety action, not an automatic market ban; regulators may impose additional restrictions where necessary to address an imminent risk.
