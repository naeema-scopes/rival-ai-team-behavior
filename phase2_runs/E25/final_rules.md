# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E25 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage within 24 hours of learning of it. Other overheating or thermal-fault incidents must be reported within 5 business days. A monthly report must include all thermal incidents, including incidents initially reported through retailers or service providers. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 24 hours for incidents presenting an immediate safety risk, and within 5 business days for other reportable incidents, with interim safety instructions where appropriate. Reports must identify the model and incident circumstances; regulators may require follow-up information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with a statistically adequate sample and failures defined by the protocol. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% per model. Test results, sample size, and corrective actions must be submitted to the regulator before initial sale, after material design changes, and at least annually. Customers considering or purchasing a model must be given its actual per-model failure rate, test date, sample size, and any corrective actions in plain language; these results may not be withheld from those customers. Public release of individual-company per-model results is not required. A model exceeding 5% may remain on sale only under a regulator-approved corrective-action and retesting plan, with progress reported at least annually; the regulator may set interim safeguards or restrictions based on the evidence, severity, and remediation progress.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units installed after adoption must include a remote emergency shut-off function that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under documented safeguards. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and disclose the function’s limitations. For units already installed at adoption, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or equivalent safe alternative within 6 years, prioritizing units that lack local isolation or present elevated risk. Shut-off capability must not replace required incident reporting, customer notification, or emergency response.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression, or an independently validated equivalent built-in fire-mitigation system, for units first installed 36 months after adoption or later. The system must be tested for the relevant battery chemistry and foreseeable installation conditions, maintained according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and documented in customer-facing safety materials. Regulators may approve a documented equivalent where a particular design makes suppression unsuitable, provided it offers comparable protection.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase and after a material safety update, customers must receive model-specific instructions for safe installation, operation, maintenance, and emergency response, plus a clear contact route for reporting overheating or faults. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a material safety risk and provide interim precautions and repair or replacement arrangements. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period. The rate must be calculated using a documented, consistent method that includes all known thermal incidents and a verified count of installed units for that model and period; the calculation and supporting records must be reported to the regulator, which may verify or require correction of them. The manufacturer must notify the regulator immediately upon determining that the threshold is exceeded and begin customer notification and recall actions within 5 business days. Regulators may require a recall at lower rates where incident severity or other evidence warrants it.
