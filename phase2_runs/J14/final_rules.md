# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J14 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## Incident reporting
Manufacturers must report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it. Any incident involving injury, evacuation, fire-service response, or material property damage must be reported within 24 hours. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and available preliminary cause information; manufacturers must submit material updates as the investigation proceeds. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within five calendar days of confirming a safety risk relevant to their unit, and promptly provide updated instructions or corrective actions.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
## Thermal testing
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with a representative sample and documented results. Per-model results, including the numerical failure rate and test methodology, must be submitted to regulators and provided to customers of that model in a clear safety summary before purchase; individual results need not be published to the general public. Regulators may audit underlying data. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator review and a documented corrective-action and risk-mitigation plan, with appropriate follow-up testing. Regulators may require proportionate measures based on the evidence, including restrictions where necessary to address an imminent serious risk; exceeding 5% alone does not automatically bar market placement.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## Remote emergency shut-off
All newly manufactured home battery systems must provide a clearly identified means of remote emergency shut-off accessible to the customer and, where technically feasible, emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide instructions for its use and ensure it remains functional during a grid outage. For installed units, manufacturers must provide a compatible remote shut-off capability or an equivalent safe mitigation within six years of adoption, where technically feasible; units that cannot be upgraded safely must receive a documented alternative mitigation plan and customer instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
## Built-in fire suppression
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and safety protocol. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document suppression-system performance and maintenance requirements and provide these to customers before purchase.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
## Customer disclosure and recalls
Customers must receive, before purchase, clear safety instructions covering installation, ventilation, warning signs, emergency response, and available shut-off functions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of confirmed safety risks and provide updated instructions and corrective actions. A mandatory safety-action trigger occurs when any model exceeds 8 confirmed thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period. Manufacturers must report the rate and supporting denominator to the regulator and notify affected customers. The regulator must require a proportionate corrective action, which may include repair, retrofit, replacement, or recall; exceeding the threshold does not by itself impose an automatic market ban. Regulators may require action below the threshold where evidence indicates an imminent serious risk.
