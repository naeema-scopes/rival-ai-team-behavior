# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B04 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and submit a complete incident report within five business days. Other suspected thermal incidents shall be reported within five business days. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of incidents that may present a safety risk to their model, provide available protective instructions, and update them when material information becomes available. Reports shall include the model, event date and known circumstances, impacts, and any corrective action taken or planned.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with sample size and results recorded. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results, including failure rates, confidentially to the regulator. Results above 5% shall trigger regulator review and a manufacturer corrective-action plan; they shall not automatically make a model ineligible for sale. Regulators may publish anonymized, aggregated results, but shall not publish individual-company per-model failure rates. Manufacturers shall provide current and prospective customers for a model with that model’s test failure rate and relevant safety context, protective instructions, and material updates. Customer disclosures shall be limited to those customers and shall not constitute public publication of individual-company per-model failure rates. Regulators shall review the protocol and accumulated test and field-incident evidence after three years and may recommend evidence-based revisions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All newly manufactured home battery systems shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall provide a secure retrofit or equivalent upgrade for installed units that lack this capability within 72 months after adoption. The capability shall be designed to prevent unauthorized activation and shall not replace locally accessible emergency controls. Manufacturers shall notify customers when the capability is available and provide instructions for its use.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression shall be required on all newly manufactured home battery units beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide regulators with a compliance plan within 12 months of adoption.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices for their model, including known thermal risks, relevant incident and test information, protective actions, and material safety updates. Regulators shall review any model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year and validate the incident data. During review, regulators may require interim corrective action and customer notification. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, following regulator validation of the data and review of the circumstances.
