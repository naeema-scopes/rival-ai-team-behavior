# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J20 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account of the event and affected model. They must provide a complete investigation report within 30 days, or interim updates if the investigation remains open. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly when an incident indicates a material safety hazard, and no later than 72 hours after determining that customers may be at risk. Reports must include known incidents even if cause is not yet established; corrections or omissions must be reported promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved sample size and protocol. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers must submit exact results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the regulator. Customers who buy or own that model must receive its exact per-model failure percentage, whether it met the limit, and all actionable safety information. Exact per-model failure percentages must not be published or otherwise disclosed to the general public. A model exceeding the limit must receive a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retest schedule; regulators may impose proportionate interim safeguards based on the evidence, with withdrawal from sale reserved for cases where the regulator finds an immediate serious risk or corrective action fails.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months or more after this standard takes effect must include remote emergency shut-off. For units already installed when the standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent compliant upgrade within six years. Shut-off must be usable by the customer and, under regulator-defined emergency conditions, by authorized emergency responders; safeguards must prevent unauthorized activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the battery system. Manufacturers must provide evidence of system performance to the regulator and include customer-facing instructions for its operation, limitations, inspection, and maintenance. The requirement does not replace other fire-safety obligations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase and after installation, customers must receive plain-language safety instructions, warning signs, emergency steps, and contact details for reporting incidents. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety hazards and provide updated instructions or corrective actions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, and the regulator must promptly review the evidence and require appropriate corrective action. A recall is mandatory when the regulator determines that the model presents a serious safety risk or that corrective action has failed. Rates must be calculated using model-specific installed-unit and incident data, not company-wide rates. Manufacturers must promptly provide relevant data to the regulator and notify affected customers of required actions.
