# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E20 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal runaway, fire, explosion, or injury/property-damage event involving a covered battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, followed by a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk or requires customer action; otherwise, include it in the next scheduled safety communication. Reports must include known incident circumstances, model and serial/batch identifiers, and any immediate precautions. Update reports as material facts become available. Regulators may require records and follow-up analysis.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units before market entry and after material design changes. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and supporting data to the relevant regulator. Customers who bought the model must receive clear, accessible information about its test result, protocol, sample size, and material risks or corrective actions. Individual-company per-model failure rates are not required to be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt regulator notification, investigation, and a corrective-action and retest plan. Regulators may allow time-limited conditional certification or continued sales under monitoring and appropriate safeguards while remediation is underway; they determine measures for installed units. Results and corrective actions must be updated as material facts become available.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months after this standard takes effect must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with protections against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. For units already installed when the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must provide remote shut-off through retrofit or an equivalent safe upgrade within 6 years, where technically feasible. Customers must receive advance notice and instructions; emergency procedures must not depend solely on remote connectivity.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to reduce the risk of thermal propagation. Manufacturers must document performance using a regulator-approved test protocol and provide results to the relevant regulator and customers. The requirement does not replace other required safeguards, and manufacturers must provide clear instructions for emergency response.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices promptly when new information indicates a material risk or requires customer action, including relevant incident trends, warnings, and safe-use instructions. A notice requiring immediate action must be sent within 72 hours of determining that action is needed; other material safety information must be included in the next scheduled safety communication. Manufacturers must notify the relevant regulator and affected customers when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period. Exceeding that threshold triggers a mandatory recall, with the manufacturer promptly notifying customers, explaining the hazard and remedy, and reporting recall progress to the regulator. The recall must provide an effective remedy for affected units; it does not, by itself, require permanent market exclusion. Regulators may set the remedy, timelines, and safeguards based on the risk, including monitored conditional certification during remediation.
