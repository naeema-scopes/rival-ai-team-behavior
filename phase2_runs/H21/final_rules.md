# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H21 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents shall be reported within 5 business days. Reports shall include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and available cause information; manufacturers shall update the regulator as facts develop. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours after learning of an incident presenting a credible safety risk, and otherwise within 10 business days, with known hazards and immediate safety steps. Incidents shall not be omitted or delayed pending completion of an investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C using a jointly agreed, representative protocol and adequate sample size. The failure rate shall not exceed 5% of tested units. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model test results confidentially to the relevant regulator and to customers purchasing that model before sale; results shall be retained for audit and shall not be publicly published for individual companies or models. A model exceeding the limit shall promptly undergo corrective action and retesting. For models already in production when this standard is adopted that exceed the limit, manufacturers shall notify the regulator promptly, submit a corrective-action plan within 90 days, and achieve compliance through retesting within 36 months of adoption. During this transition, the regulator may require interim safeguards, enhanced monitoring, or a sales pause based on documented evidence of an unacceptable risk. Any such requirement shall be proportionate to the identified risk, state the evidence and reasons in writing, and be reviewed as new evidence becomes available. New models must meet the limit before sale. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of an exceedance and the corrective-action plan.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months after adoption of this standard shall include remote emergency shut-off accessible to the manufacturer and, where feasible, the customer. Manufacturers shall provide a safe software or hardware retrofit for installed units within 6 years after adoption, prioritizing units with known safety risks. Any remote shut-off capability shall include safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months after adoption of this standard shall include built-in fire suppression or an independently verified equivalent fire-mitigation system. Manufacturers shall provide evidence of effectiveness to the relevant regulator and disclose the system’s limitations and maintenance requirements to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive model-specific thermal-test results, safety limitations, installation and operating instructions, and applicable emergency procedures. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material new hazards, incidents indicating a credible safety risk, and corrective actions, using direct notice where reasonably practicable. A manufacturer shall initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, calculated using that model’s installed-unit count, or when the regulator determines that a defect presents an imminent or otherwise unacceptable risk. The recall notice shall explain the hazard, affected units, remedy, and steps customers should take; manufacturers shall promptly report recall progress to the regulator.
