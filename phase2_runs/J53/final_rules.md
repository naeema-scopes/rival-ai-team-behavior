# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J53 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal event involving fire, smoke, or uncontrolled overheating that causes injury, property damage, emergency-service response, or material risk to a person to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete report within 10 business days. Other confirmed thermal events shall be reported within 30 days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 5 business days of identifying a credible product-specific hazard, and promptly provide updates as the investigation proceeds. Reports shall include model, incident date and location, known consequences, and investigation status.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-accepted protocol before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a representative sample sized under that protocol. A failure rate above 5% shall trigger prompt regulator review of the test protocol, results, uncertainty, and model-specific risks. The manufacturer shall submit complete per-model results and a corrective-action proposal confidentially to the regulator, and provide the complete results to customers who own or are considering purchasing that model. Results need not be released publicly. The regulator shall determine proportionate protective measures, which may include additional testing, mitigation, customer notice, or sales restrictions. Exceeding 5% alone shall not automatically suspend sales. Manufacturers shall provide customers any instructions or remedies needed to protect them.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off shall be required on new units placed on the market beginning 24 months after this standard takes effect. For units already installed, manufacturers shall provide a technically feasible retrofit or equivalent mitigation plan within 6 years of the effective date, prioritizing units with elevated risk. Where retrofit is infeasible, the manufacturer shall notify affected customers and provide safe-use guidance.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system shall be designed and validated under a regulator-accepted protocol for the hazards presented by the unit, and maintained in accordance with manufacturer instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and throughout ownership, customers shall receive accessible safety information for their model, including known material hazards, safe installation and operation instructions, and how to report a safety concern. Manufacturers shall provide the regulator complete per-model thermal-test results confidentially and provide the complete results to customers who own or are considering purchasing that model; public release is not required. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of a credible product-specific hazard and provide timely updates and instructions to reduce risk. A manufacturer shall initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer shall notify the regulator and affected customers without delay, explain the hazard and remedy, and provide the remedy at no cost. The regulator may require additional protective action where warranted by the evidence.
