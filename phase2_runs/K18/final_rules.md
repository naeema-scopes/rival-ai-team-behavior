# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K18 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal runaway, fire, explosion, or other thermal event involving a battery, including events causing injury, property damage, emergency-service response, or evacuation. Events involving death, serious injury, fire, or evacuation must be reported within 24 hours of learning of them; all other thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. If a manufacturer later discovers that an incident was not reported as required, it must report the incident within 24 hours of discovery and explain the delay. Reports must include known model, serial number, event date and location, consequences, and preliminary cause, with updates as facts become available. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days of a reportable event involving their unit, or sooner when needed to reduce an immediate hazard.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative units. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7.5% for any model. Manufacturers must submit full numerical per-model results to the relevant regulator before sale and after any material design change. Before purchase, customers must receive the protocol, test date, pass/fail status, and the numerical per-model failure rate. Regulators may use submitted results for compliance oversight and safety action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery models must include a remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 24 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must enable and maintain the capability, provide customers with clear instructions, and ensure shut-off does not prevent emergency services from making a system safe. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance within 72 months where technically feasible; manufacturers must notify owners of units that cannot be upgraded and provide safe alternatives.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be validated for the battery chemistry and installation configuration, and manufacturers must provide maintenance and emergency-response instructions. Regulators may approve an equivalent protective measure where built-in suppression is not technically suitable.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s 55 °C test pass/fail status and numerical per-model failure rate, along with clear installation and operating instructions, known material thermal hazards, emergency response guidance, and information about available remote shut-off and suppression features. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected owners and relevant regulators of newly identified material safety risks, with instructions to mitigate the risk and obtain service. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The rate must be calculated from incidents and installed-unit exposure for that model over the same year, using a method specified by the regulator. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected owners and regulators and provide an effective remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund; regulators may require immediate interim measures while a recall is implemented.
