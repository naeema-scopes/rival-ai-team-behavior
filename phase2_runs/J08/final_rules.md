# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J08 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents involving installed units. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours, providing known hazards and immediate protective steps. A report must include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and actions taken; manufacturers must promptly update regulators and customers as material facts emerge. Incidents must not be excluded because their cause is uncertain. Within 30 days after adoption, manufacturers must also disclose to the regulator and affected customers any known thermal incidents that occurred before adoption but were not previously reported, including incidents whose cause remains uncertain.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7% of tested units per model. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model test results to the relevant regulator and make them available to customers who own or are considering that model; results must not be publicly published in a way that identifies a company and model. A model exceeding the limit must be reported to the regulator with a corrective-action plan and retested before sale resumes.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required for newly manufactured units beginning 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a safe, secure remote shut-off capability for installed units where technically feasible, completing upgrades within 72 months after adoption. If an upgrade is infeasible or creates a safety or cybersecurity risk, the manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers and provide an effective alternative emergency procedure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required for newly manufactured units beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must document suppression performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to regulators and customers. The regulator may approve an equivalent protective design where the manufacturer demonstrates comparable fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with model-specific safety notices promptly upon learning of a material hazard, including known risks, protective steps, and any repair or replacement options. Manufacturers must track thermal incidents by model and installed-unit population and report model-specific rates to the regulator. When a model’s annual rate exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and initiate a mandatory recall, including repair or replacement, with clear instructions. The regulator may approve a time-limited corrective-action alternative only upon finding that it provides protection equivalent to a recall and that a recall is unnecessary; the alternative must include prompt customer notice, a defined implementation deadline, and regulator verification of effectiveness. If those conditions are not met, the mandatory recall applies. Company-wide rates must not substitute for model-specific rates.
