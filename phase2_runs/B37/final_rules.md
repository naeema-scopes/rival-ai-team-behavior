# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B37 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or suspected thermal runaway within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with an initial account of the event and any known injuries or property damage. A fuller report, including the investigation findings and corrective actions, shall be submitted within 30 days, with updates when material new information becomes available. Manufacturers shall notify the affected customer within 24 hours of becoming aware of an incident involving that customer’s unit, communicate known hazards and immediate safety steps, and provide further updates as appropriate. Reports shall identify the model and incident circumstances; personal information shall be protected.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. New models must have a failure rate no greater than 5% before sale. For models already on sale when this standard takes effect, the maximum failure rate is 7%; results above 5% shall be reported promptly to the regulator with a documented corrective-action plan, retesting at least every six months, and any interim risk controls required by the regulator. Manufacturers shall take corrective action to bring such models to 5% or below within 18 months, subject to regulator-approved interim controls. Failure to meet the applicable limit requires withholding the model from sale or deployment until compliant. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test methods and results to the regulator and provide the relevant model’s results to customers who buy that model, at sale and upon material updates. Individual-company, per-model failure rates shall not be publicly disclosed; the regulator may publish aggregate results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All newly installed battery systems shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall retrofit existing installed units to support remote shut-off within six years of this standard taking effect, where technically feasible, and shall provide customers with safe interim guidance until retrofit is complete.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems shall include built-in fire suppression. This requirement shall apply to units manufactured 36 months after this standard is adopted, allowing manufacturers time to validate designs and update production. Manufacturers shall provide evidence of suppression-system testing and maintenance requirements to the regulator and clear operating and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of a thermal incident involving their unit, including the known hazard, steps to reduce risk, and how to obtain support. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, based on verified incident and installation data. Manufacturers shall notify the regulator and affected customers of a triggered recall without delay and provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions.
