# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D50 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report each thermal incident involving fire, smoke, or hazardous overheating to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when the incident presents a credible safety risk, and promptly provide any material safety instructions. Reports must include known circumstances and corrective actions; later findings must be submitted as updates.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. A 5% failure rate is the per-model safety benchmark. Test results, including sample size and failure rate, must be submitted confidentially to regulators and provided to customers who own that model; individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published publicly. A model exceeding the benchmark must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and be retested on a regulator-approved schedule. Regulators shall determine proportionate enforcement based on the results, corrective actions, and risk; exceeding the benchmark alone does not automatically require a sales suspension or recall.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required for all installed units. Manufacturers must enable the capability on units that already support it and provide a safe retrofit or equivalent compliant solution for other units within 6 years of adoption. The shut-off must be accessible to authorized emergency responders and the customer, with clear instructions; it must not disable essential safety monitoring or create an additional hazard. Manufacturers must document completion and report any units that cannot be brought into compliance, with the reason and interim risk controls, to the regulator and affected customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression meeting applicable regulator-approved performance and safety requirements, beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide evidence of compliance to the regulator and disclose the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations to customers. This requirement does not replace other thermal-safety design, testing, or incident-reporting obligations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety instructions, thermal-risk warnings, and information on incident reporting and emergency response. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and provide updated instructions. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The rate must be calculated using the model’s thermal incidents and installed-unit exposure for the relevant year, documented for regulator review. Manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers without delay and provide a safe remedy.
