# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G31 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a potential safety risk, with known hazards, immediate precautions, and contact details. All reportable incidents must be logged, including those initially assessed as non-serious; manufacturers must not omit incidents from regulator reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results to the relevant regulator before sale and retain records for audit. Customers who buy a model must receive its complete per-model test results, including its failure rate and any required precautions; results may not be withheld from those customers as confidential. These regulator submissions and customer disclosures do not require publication of individual-company per-model failure rates to the general public. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must promptly undergo regulator-approved corrective action and retesting. Continued sale during corrective action may be permitted only with the regulator’s written, time-limited approval and any required customer precautions; regulators may restrict or suspend sale where evidence indicates an imminent or unreasonable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within 6 years of adoption, where technically feasible, and provide customers with clear instructions for using the feature. Manufacturers must submit a phased retrofit plan to the regulator within 12 months. Regulators may require a faster timetable for a model presenting an imminent or unreasonable safety risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide evidence of compliance to the relevant regulator before sale. Regulators may require earlier measures for a model presenting an imminent or unreasonable safety risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must give customers clear safety notices promptly when new information identifies a material hazard, including the affected model, known risks, steps customers should take, and how to obtain assistance. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, investigate, and provide a corrective-action plan. The regulator must promptly review the evidence and may require corrective action and additional protective measures. A mandatory recall is required if the regulator finds an imminent or unreasonable risk, or determines that corrective action is inadequate. The manufacturer must notify affected customers and regulators without delay and provide repair, replacement, or refund options when a recall is required.
