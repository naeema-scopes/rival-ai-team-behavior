# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J05 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, evacuation, or material property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a follow-up report containing known causes and corrective actions within 30 days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours of becoming aware of a thermal incident involving their model, and promptly provide safety instructions and updates. Reports shall include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and any immediate safety measures. Manufacturers shall maintain incident records and report confirmed omissions or corrections to the regulator without delay.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Before sale, manufacturers shall submit each model’s protocol, sample size, full numerical results, and corrective actions to the regulator, with access for independent auditors authorized by the regulator. Before purchase, prospective customers shall be given the model’s numerical 55 °C failure rate directly, together with its test status against the applicable limit, material hazards, required precautions, and any corrective-action or remediation status. The numerical rate need not be published to the general public. Customers shall be promptly informed when material findings change. Regulators may require retesting or independent audit. A model with a failure rate above 5% shall have a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and be retested within 12 months; it shall not be newly marketed if it exceeds 8%, or if the regulator determines that an imminent risk requires sales restrictions. Exceeding 5% alone shall not require a sales restriction. Manufacturers shall submit a staged compliance plan within 12 months and comply with the 5% limit within 60 months of this standard taking effect. Regulators may require appropriate interim customer safety notices or risk controls based on the evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability before sale. Manufacturers shall provide a secure, authenticated means for regulators and authorized emergency responders to request shut-off when an imminent safety risk is identified, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a local override where safe. For installed units lacking this capability, manufacturers shall complete a retrofit or equivalent risk-reduction plan within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Manufacturers shall submit an implementation schedule to the regulator within 12 months; the regulator may approve an alternative based on technical infeasibility and equivalent protection.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and reliability protocol. This requirement shall apply to units manufactured beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Regulators may require earlier measures for a specific model where evidence indicates an imminent risk, but shall consider effective alternative safeguards and provide a reasoned determination.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers, before purchase and for as long as they own the unit, clear model-specific safety notices, including known hazards, material incident trends, numerical per-model 55 °C thermal test failure rate, test status and material findings, remediation status, required precautions, and any software or maintenance measures. Numerical per-model thermal test failure rates shall be submitted to regulators and available to authorized independent auditors. Manufacturers shall provide the numerical rate directly to prospective and current customers of that model; general-public publication is not required. Notices shall be updated promptly when material new safety information becomes available and delivered directly to affected customers using available contact channels. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, using verified incident and installed-unit data; the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop new sales, and provide an effective remedy. Regulators may require a recall below this threshold where the evidence indicates an unreasonable safety risk.
