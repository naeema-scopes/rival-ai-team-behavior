# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E08 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery-related overheating, thermal event, fire, thermal runaway, or injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a completed incident report within 10 business days. Customers whose unit is involved must be notified promptly and receive safety instructions. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and report annual model-level incident counts to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and at least annually thereafter. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% of tested units. A model exceeding that rate must be reported to the regulator and subjected to a corrective-action plan and retesting; exceeding the limit alone does not automatically require market withdrawal. Per-model test results, sample sizes, and corrective actions must be provided to regulators and disclosed to customers considering or owning that model, in clear and accessible form. Public reporting may also include aggregated results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit path for installed units that do not support the feature, with retrofit completion required no earlier than six years after this standard takes effect. Shut-off systems must include safeguards against unauthorized access and provide customers with clear instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Suppression systems must be designed and tested to limit fire propagation and must not compromise safe operation or emergency response.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices, operating and emergency instructions, and timely notice of hazards affecting their model. When a material safety risk is identified, manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers and provide protective instructions. If a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year, the manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and carry out a regulator-supervised recall, providing an appropriate repair, replacement, refund, or other effective remedy. Regulators may require interim protective measures while the risk and remedy are assessed. A recall does not by itself require a sales ban or market withdrawal; regulators may separately order sales restrictions or withdrawal when warranted.
