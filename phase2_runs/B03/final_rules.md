# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B03 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, smoke, thermal runaway, evacuation, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, regardless of whether the incident was reported first through a customer, installer, or other channel. A complete incident report, including the model, circumstances, and known consequences, is due within 30 days. Affected customers must be notified within 5 business days when an event presents a safety risk or requires inspection, repair, or a change in use; urgent hazards require notice within 24 hours of determination. Manufacturers must maintain records, reconcile regulator reports with customer and service records, and submit an annual account of incidents and reporting completeness.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model test results, including sample size and failure rate, to the relevant regulator and to customers purchasing that model. Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s actual test result and an explanation of the test and its limitations. Results above 5% require prompt regulator review and a corrective-action plan with a timeline for follow-up testing; they do not automatically prohibit sale. Regulators may require additional testing, corrective measures, or sales restrictions based on the results and associated risks.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off when installed from 24 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for authorized emergency responders or the system owner to initiate shut-off. Existing installed units must be retrofitted or replaced within 6 years, where technically feasible; manufacturers must provide customers with a schedule and interim safety instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression for units manufactured or first sold 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system must be designed and validated to limit fire propagation and protect occupants and nearby property, using a regulator-approved test protocol. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers and retain evidence of compliance for regulators.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its 55 °C sustained-heat test result, known material risks, required clearances and maintenance, and emergency instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of safety notices and corrective actions; urgent hazards require notice within 24 hours of determination, with other material safety notices within 5 business days. A manufacturer must initiate a regulator-supervised recall when any model exceeds 8 verified thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units during a calendar year. Calculate the rate using verified thermal incidents for that model and its installed-unit population during the same year. Manufacturers must reconcile incident records with customer reports and provide supporting records and calculations to regulators on request; incomplete reporting must not delay a safety response. The manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers with recall instructions. Regulators may require a recall or other corrective action for a serious hazard even below this threshold.
