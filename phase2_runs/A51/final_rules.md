# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A51 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. They shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their model or requires customer action. Reports shall include known circumstances, injuries or damage, model and unit information, and any immediate safety measures. Manufacturers shall maintain incident records. Previously unreported incidents known when this standard takes effect shall be reported to regulators within 24 hours after its effective date; any required customer notice shall be provided within 48 hours.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 8% per model. Models exceeding 5% shall submit a corrective-action plan to regulators and undergo follow-up testing; models exceeding 8% must be corrected and retested before further sale. Per-model results shall be provided confidentially to regulators and customers who own the affected model. Regulators may publish aggregate, non-identifying results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide a secure means for authorized emergency use and clear customer instructions. For units already installed when this requirement takes effect, manufacturers shall offer a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within six years, prioritizing units with elevated safety risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers shall provide regulators with evidence that the suppression system meets the applicable performance and reliability requirements; customer instructions shall explain its operation and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall give customers clear safety notices describing material risks, relevant warning signs, and protective actions. Affected customers shall be notified within 48 hours when a credible safety risk requires customer action, and promptly when new material safety information becomes available. Regulators shall review any model whose annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model’s rate exceeds 10 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on verified incidents and a regulator-approved, transparent calculation method. During review, regulators shall promptly assess risk and may require interim corrective measures, including customer warnings, sales restrictions, or other proportionate protections. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers and regulators promptly of a recall, explain the remedy and safe-use steps, and provide progress updates until complete. Per-model incident information shall be shared with regulators and affected customers, not published publicly.
