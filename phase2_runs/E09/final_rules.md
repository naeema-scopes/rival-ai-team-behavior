# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E09 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any fire, thermal runaway, or other thermal incident involving a covered battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify the affected customer within 24 hours when the incident involves that customer’s unit or presents an immediate safety risk, and promptly provide any protective instructions. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and submit annual incident totals to regulators. If a manufacturer discovers that an incident was not previously reported, it must notify the regulator within 24 hours of discovery and submit the complete report within 10 business days; it must also promptly notify any affected customer when customer notification is required under this section.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after any material design change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% of tested units for each model. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the relevant regulator, independent conformity assessor, and customers who buy that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public; this does not limit disclosures to regulators, assessors, or customers of that model. Results must be provided clearly and in a timely manner. A model exceeding the limit may not be newly placed on the market unless the regulator approves a time-limited corrective-action plan, with risk controls and retesting milestones, that protects customers and the public. The regulator may suspend sales if risks are not adequately controlled or milestones are missed.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly manufactured systems must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with clear customer instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe upgrade within six years of this standard taking effect, prioritizing units presenting the greatest risk. Any shut-off must not prevent emergency responders from safely isolating the system.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Newly manufactured battery systems must include built-in fire suppression. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions on the system’s operation and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices covering safe installation and use, warning signs, emergency steps, and relevant product-specific safety information. Notices must be provided at purchase and updated promptly when new material safety information arises. A manufacturer must notify affected customers promptly of a safety defect and provide protective instructions. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period; the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop sales, and carry out the regulator-approved recall.
