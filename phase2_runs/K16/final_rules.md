# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K16 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any death, serious injury, fire, evacuation, or emergency-service response involving a battery within 24 hours of learning of it. Other thermal incidents, including overheating requiring repair or replacement, must be reported within 5 business days. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial number, and actions taken; manufacturers must update the regulator as material facts emerge. Affected customers must be notified promptly of a hazard and any immediate safety steps, and no later than 24 hours after the manufacturer determines action is needed to protect them.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative units. A model meets the benchmark if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, sample sizes, and protocol details to the regulator and disclose the relevant model’s results and testing information to customers considering or buying that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates need not be publicly disclosed beyond those customer disclosures; the regulator may publish anonymized or aggregated results. A model exceeding the benchmark must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and undergo confirmatory testing; the regulator will determine proportionate interim measures based on the test evidence and risk. Exceeding the benchmark alone does not automatically prohibit sales while the regulator reviews the plan.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery units manufactured for sale must support remote emergency shut-off no later than 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide a secure, authenticated means for an authorized emergency responder or the customer to initiate shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized or accidental activation and clear instructions for use. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit path for installed units that do not support this function and complete retrofits, where technically feasible, within six years after adoption. If a unit cannot be safely retrofitted, the manufacturer must notify its owner, explain the limitation and available risk-reduction measures, and report the unit and its disposition to the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery models manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Existing models may continue to be sold during that transition only if they meet the other applicable requirements. Manufacturers must provide customers with clear instructions on any interim fire-safety precautions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material thermal hazards, explain safe-use steps, and provide updates on corrective actions. Manufacturers must report incident-rate calculations and supporting data to the regulator, including the model, installed-unit denominator, and period used. If regulator-verified data show that any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the regulator must require a mandatory recall of that model. The regulator may also require a recall below this threshold when the evidence indicates an unacceptable ongoing risk, considering incident severity, trend, exposure, and corrective-action effectiveness.
