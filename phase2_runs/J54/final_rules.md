# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J54 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents initially suspected to involve installation or user error. They must provide an updated investigation and any newly identified affected units within 10 business days. The manufacturer must notify the affected customer within 24 hours when the incident presents an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within 5 business days, with practical safety instructions and a contact channel. Reports must not be withheld pending confirmation of root cause.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Before a model is placed on the market and at least annually thereafter, manufacturers must test representative units using a regulator-approved sustained-heat protocol at 55 °C. The 5% failure rate is a safety benchmark for regulator review, not an automatic market-entry or delisting bar. Manufacturers must provide per-model results and test methods to the regulator and make results available to customers and prospective buyers of that model in clear, accessible form; individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. If a model exceeds 5%, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, submit a documented corrective-action plan and follow-up test results, and implement any proportionate risk controls the regulator requires. Restrictions on sale must be based on the model’s evidence of risk and the adequacy of those controls.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market from 2 years after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear user instructions. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance, replaced, or covered by an approved equivalent risk-control plan within 6 years of adoption. A shut-off must not disable essential protections or prevent emergency responders from safely isolating the system.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression starting 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the unit, maintained in accordance with clear customer instructions, and must not create additional hazards or impede safe isolation by emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide regulators and customers with the system’s operating and maintenance information.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices promptly when a safety issue could affect their unit, including the issue, affected models or serial ranges, immediate precautions, and a contact channel. When a model appears to exceed 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly submit the underlying incident and installation data to the regulator for verification and provide interim precautions to affected customers. Once the regulator verifies that a model exceeds this threshold, it must require corrective action and assess the documented risk and available controls. A recall is mandatory if the regulator finds, on the basis of that assessment, that the risk cannot be adequately controlled by other measures. The regulator shall determine the appropriate recall remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund, and the manufacturer must implement it promptly and cooperate fully.
