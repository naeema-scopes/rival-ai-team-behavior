# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J49 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it. Incidents involving injury, fire, or an ongoing risk must be reported within 24 hours. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 5 business days, and promptly provide safety instructions where continued use may pose a risk. Reports must include known incident details and be updated as the investigation progresses.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a common protocol, with representative sample sizes and test conditions specified by regulators. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, confidentially to regulators and provide the results for that model to customers who buy or are considering buying it, in clear, accessible form. Results need not be disclosed to competitors or the general public. A failure rate above 5% triggers a regulator-approved corrective action plan, enhanced monitoring, and retesting on a defined schedule. Regulators may restrict sales where the results indicate an imminent or inadequately controlled safety risk; exceeding 5% alone does not automatically bar sales.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support this function, or provide an equivalent safe upgrade, within 6 years after adoption of this standard. Shut-off capability must be tested during installation and maintained throughout the unit’s service life. Manufacturers must provide customers clear instructions on how to request or activate emergency shut-off and must not rely on remote shut-off in place of other required safety protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation environment, and must be tested and maintained in accordance with applicable requirements. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear written information about thermal hazards, safe installation and operation, warning signs, and how to contact the manufacturer. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of any safety notice, material newly identified risk, or corrective action, with instructions appropriate to the risk. When a model exceeds 8 verified thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, and the regulator must promptly review the incidents and require appropriate corrective action. A mandatory recall is required when the regulator confirms the threshold and determines that a product-related safety risk cannot be adequately controlled through other corrective action. Regulators may order an immediate recall where needed to protect the public.
