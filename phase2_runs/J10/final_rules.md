# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J10 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written incident report and known unit/model details within 5 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety risk, and provide updates as material information becomes available. Reports must include incidents regardless of whether the cause is confirmed; manufacturers must not omit incidents because an investigation is pending.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after a material design change. Manufacturers must submit the protocol, sample size, and per-model results confidentially to the relevant regulator and provide the per-model failure rate and test conditions to customers buying that model; individual company per-model results must not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt regulator review and required corrective action proportionate to the risk; the maximum permitted failure rate is 7%. A model above 7% may not be sold until corrective action is completed and a regulator confirms compliance through retesting. Manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers when a model exceeds either threshold, and provide interim safety instructions where needed.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on new home battery units manufactured 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for authorized emergency activation and document compliance to the regulator. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide remote shut-off capability through retrofit or replacement within 6 years, where technically feasible, and notify customers of the schedule and any interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on all new home battery units manufactured 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document compliance and provide the documentation to the relevant regulator. This requirement does not replace other applicable fire-safety measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about known thermal hazards, incident response steps, and any material corrective action, and promptly update notices as material information changes. When a model exceeds 8 verified thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the regulator must promptly review the data and risk, and require proportionate corrective action, which may include a recall where warranted. Manufacturers must cooperate with the review and notify affected customers of material safety risks and required actions. Per-model test results must be provided to customers buying that model.
