# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G39 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it. An incident involving death, serious injury, active fire, or an ongoing risk to other units shall be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers shall notify the affected customer within 24 hours of learning of an incident involving that customer’s unit, and promptly provide safety instructions. Reports shall include the model, unit identification where available, incident circumstances, and known consequences; manufacturers shall update regulators as material facts become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and after a material design change. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results, sample sizes, and protocol details to the relevant regulator. Customers considering or owning that model shall receive its per-model failure rate, test status, and a clear, plain-language summary of the findings, with context on the protocol, sample size, and uncertainty. Individual-company results need not be disclosed to the general public. A failure rate above 5% shall trigger regulator review of the results, uncertainty, and relevant real-world safety evidence; the regulator may require corrective action, retesting, or other measures proportionate to the risk. Exceeding 5% alone shall not automatically prohibit sale. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material changes to identified risks or required safety actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 2 years after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers shall provide a secure retrofit or equivalent upgrade for installed units, completing it within 6 years after the effective date. Shut-off systems shall include safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system shall be designed and tested to limit fire propagation without creating unreasonable risks to occupants or responders. Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear instructions on system operation and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers, before purchase and throughout ownership, clear, plain-language safety information for their model, including 55 °C test results and relevant safety findings, known hazards, required maintenance, emergency instructions, and how to report an incident. Test results shall be presented with sufficient context to explain the test protocol and uncertainty. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and provide interim safety instructions. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the relevant regulator and provide incident data and a proposed corrective-action plan. The regulator shall assess the evidence and may require a recall or other corrective action where warranted; any required recall shall be communicated without delay to affected customers with corrective-action details and timeline.
