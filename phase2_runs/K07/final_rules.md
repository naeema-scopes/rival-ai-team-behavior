# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K07 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, explosion, evacuation, injury, or material property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 calendar days. A manufacturer must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial number, and any immediate corrective measures. Suspected incidents must be reported; uncertainty is not a basis for delay.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with a representative sample and documented results. Manufacturers must provide per-model numerical failure rates, sample sizes, protocols, and corrective actions to regulators before market entry and at least annually thereafter. Before purchase, customers must receive the per-model numerical failure rate, test date, sample size, a plain-language explanation of the results, material risks, and any corrective actions. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must promptly submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and timetable, with retesting after implementation and after material design or manufacturing changes. Regulators may restrict sales or require other action where results or other evidence indicate an unreasonable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on all new units placed on the market beginning three years after this standard takes effect. For already-installed units, manufacturers must provide a compliant retrofit or equivalent safe remediation within six years, prioritizing units with known safety concerns. Shut-off systems must fail safe, be secure against unauthorized access, and not prevent occupants or emergency responders from isolating a unit locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation, tested to a regulator-approved protocol, and maintained according to manufacturer instructions. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance information to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its 55 °C thermal-test numerical failure rate, test date, sample size, a plain-language explanation of performance and material risks, required installation and maintenance conditions, and relevant safety notices. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and any recommended action. A manufacturer must initiate a regulator-notified recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The recall plan must provide timely customer notice, safe remediation or replacement, and progress reporting. A recall requires an effective customer-protection remedy; it does not by itself mandate a permanent market ban. Regulators may require a recall or other corrective action at lower rates when evidence indicates an unreasonable safety risk.
