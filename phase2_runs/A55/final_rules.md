# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A55 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any death, serious injury, fire, or thermal runaway involving a home battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary incident report within 5 business days and a complete investigation report within 30 days. Other thermal incidents presenting a credible fire or injury risk must be reported to the regulator within 5 business days. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and in any event within 5 business days of confirming that risk. Reports must include available unit, model, installation, and incident details; updates must be submitted as investigations progress.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-accepted protocol and a representative sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7% of tested units per model. Manufacturers must provide each model’s numerical result, sample size, testing methodology, and corrective actions to regulators. Customers considering or purchasing a model must receive that model’s numerical result, sample size, compliance status, material safety risks, and relevant corrective actions; these results need not be published to the general public. A model exceeding the limit may not be newly installed until it passes a confirmatory test and the regulator accepts its corrective-action plan.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 24 months after this standard takes effect, with a safe local means of operation retained. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within 6 years of the effective date where technically feasible. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and the customer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation; manufacturers must communicate any limitations and safe manual shut-off instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and designed, installed, and maintained to reduce fire propagation without creating undue risk to occupants or responders. Manufacturers must provide customers with information on system operation, limitations, and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices promptly upon confirming a material safety risk, including the affected models and serial ranges, the nature of the risk, immediate precautions, and available remedies. A manufacturer must initiate a regulator-supervised recall of any model when its verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units for that model in a year. Rates must use documented incident and installed-unit data, with methodology and supporting data submitted to the regulator; customers must be notified promptly of the recall and provided repair, replacement, or refund instructions. A recall is also required when the regulator determines that a serious safety defect presents an unreasonable risk, regardless of the rate.
