# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H54 · variant H · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 45})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents involving injury, property damage, or a suspected product defect. A detailed investigation report shall follow within 30 days, with updates as material facts emerge. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a potential hazard to their unit or requires action; urgent hazards require immediate notice. Reports shall identify the model and known circumstances, while protecting personal information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before certification and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative units. Manufacturers shall provide per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the regulator and to customers who buy that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public; regulators may publish aggregated industry results. A model with a failure rate above 5% shall be subject to a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retesting; continued sales may proceed while the plan is followed unless the regulator determines suspension is necessary to protect the public. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of any safety hazard and actions they need to take.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off shall be required on all newly manufactured home battery units beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall retrofit already-installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within 6 years of adoption, where technically feasible, and shall provide customers with a clear schedule and instructions. Any exception or alternative protective measure shall require regulator approval.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression shall be required on all newly manufactured home battery units beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The requirement applies to units first placed on the market on or after that date; manufacturers shall document compliance using regulator-approved testing. Nothing in this section limits regulators from requiring earlier corrective action for a specific product presenting an imminent serious risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of a confirmed safety hazard, explaining the risk and steps to take, and provide updates as corrective action proceeds. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer shall promptly report the rate and supporting information to the regulator and notify affected customers of the elevated rate and recommended precautions. The regulator shall promptly assess the risk and require a recall unless it documents that an alternative remedy protects customers and the public equally or better. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers and the regulator of any recall and its progress.
