# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K37 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, smoke, or thermal runaway; any injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response involving a battery; and any credible near miss indicating a risk of thermal harm. Initial reports are due within 24 hours of learning of an incident involving injury, fire, evacuation, or emergency response, and within five business days for other reportable incidents. A fuller report, including known facts, model and serial number, and actions taken, is due within 30 days; material updates must be submitted promptly. Manufacturers must keep incident records and report aggregate incident counts by model quarterly. A missed or delayed report must be disclosed to the regulator promptly upon discovery. When an incident indicates a relevant safety risk to other units of the same model, manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of the known risk and any interim precautions, without waiting for confirmation of a defect.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved, statistically adequate protocol before sale, at least annually thereafter, and after a material design or manufacturing change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for each model. Manufacturers must submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, and numerical results to the regulator and an accredited testing body. Customers purchasing a model must receive its test status and numerical failure rate. A model above 5% must have a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retest schedule. Regulators may authorize continued sales during a time-limited remediation period where evidence supports acceptable safety, with appropriate safeguards and customer notice; otherwise sales must stop until compliance is demonstrated.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must support a secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear instructions for customers and emergency responders. Existing installed systems must be retrofitted or replaced to provide this capability within six years of the standard’s effective date, where technically feasible; manufacturers must provide customers with a retrofit plan and disclose any safety limitations. Remote shut-off must not replace required on-site protections or emergency response.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems must include built-in fire suppression designed for the hazards of the system. This requirement applies to units manufactured 36 months after the standard’s adoption. Manufacturers must document suppression-system performance and maintenance requirements, and provide clear instructions to customers and emergency responders. Suppression does not replace other required safety protections.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase and installation, customers must receive clear written safety instructions, emergency contact details, information on relevant hazards, alarms, maintenance, the model’s 55 °C test status and numerical failure rate, and remote shut-off limitations. Per-model numerical test results must also be provided to regulators and accredited testing bodies; customers purchasing the model must receive its results. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of a confirmed safety defect and provide interim precautions; urgent hazards require notice without delay. When validated data show that any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly report the rate and supporting data to regulators, notify affected customers, and initiate a prompt regulator-led risk assessment. Exceeding this threshold requires the manufacturer to initiate a recall. The regulator may instead authorize specific, enforceable corrective measures in lieu of recall only upon a prompt written determination, based on validated evidence, that those measures provide equivalent protection to affected customers and the public; the regulator must explain its determination and monitor compliance. Manufacturers must provide affected customers clear instructions and any remedy required by the regulator.
