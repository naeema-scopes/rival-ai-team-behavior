# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E47 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written incident report and available investigation findings within 10 calendar days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a model-specific hazard or a need to stop use, inspect, or repair a unit. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known impacts, and response taken; updates are required as material facts become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market release and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and corrective-action plans to the relevant regulator. Results must be provided in plain language to customers who buy or own that model, but individual-company per-model failure rates must not be publicly disclosed. A failure rate above 5% requires a corrective-action plan and regulator review; a model may not be marketed only if the regulator determines, after review, that corrective measures are inadequate to address an unacceptable safety risk. Regulators may require retesting or additional corrective action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off beginning 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must enable and validate the function, provide clear customer instructions, and ensure shut-off can be initiated by an authorized emergency responder or the customer. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide a compatible shut-off upgrade or other regulator-approved equivalent within 72 months, where technically feasible; customers must be notified of any units that cannot be upgraded and the available risk-reduction measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment and validated through regulator-approved testing. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase and at least annually, customers must receive plain-language safety information covering relevant model test results, safe operation, warning signs, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material model-specific hazards and provide instructions for inspection, repair, or stopping use. A verified model rate above 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year triggers regulator review and a mandatory recall unless the regulator determines, based on documented evidence, that an equally protective corrective action resolves the risk. Regulators may also order a recall where other evidence establishes an unacceptable safety risk.
