# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A12 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any fire, thermal runaway, overheating event requiring emergency-service response, or event causing injury or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days, including incidents whose cause is still under investigation. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours of identifying a material hazard, and promptly provide safety instructions. Reports must include known incident details and be updated as investigations progress. Manufacturers must submit an annual incident summary by model to regulators, including incident counts and the number of installed units used to calculate each model’s incident rate.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo the prescribed sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually, using a representative sample and reporting sample size and confidence interval. Full per-model results must be submitted to the responsible regulator and disclosed to customers who buy that model, in clear and accessible form. A result above the 5% benchmark requires prompt regulator notification and a corrective-action plan, including retesting. Regulators shall determine proportionate risk controls based on the test evidence and other relevant safety information; exceeding the benchmark alone does not automatically require a sales ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support secure remote emergency shut-off no later than 24 months after this standard takes effect, subject to safeguards against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must provide a safe retrofit pathway for installed units that can support the function; any required retrofit deadline for existing units must be no earlier than 6 years after the standard takes effect. Where retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must provide customers and regulators with alternative risk-mitigation measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide customers with clear information on the suppression system and its maintenance. The standard may permit an equivalent alternative only where the manufacturer demonstrates to the regulator that it provides comparable protection.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices and instructions promptly when a material hazard is identified, and notify affected customers within 72 hours of identifying that hazard. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, and the regulator must assess the evidence and require proportionate corrective action, including a recall where warranted by the risk. Regulators may require a recall where other evidence shows an unacceptable safety risk. Notices must explain the hazard, affected units, steps customers should take, and available remedy.
