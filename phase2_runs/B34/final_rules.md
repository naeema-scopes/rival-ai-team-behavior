# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B34 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the regulator any actual or suspected thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, or emergency shut-off, within 24 hours of becoming aware of it. They shall provide an initial incident report within 5 business days and a final report, including known contributing factors and corrective actions, within 30 days or explain why further investigation is needed. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers promptly when an incident indicates a credible safety hazard, and no later than 5 business days after becoming aware of that hazard; urgent risks require immediate notice and clear safe-use instructions. Reports shall include incidents known to the manufacturer, whether or not the cause is confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved sampling and test protocol. No more than 7.5% of tested units for any model may fail. Before placing a model on the market, manufacturers shall submit its numerical per-model test results to the regulator confidentially. Customers considering or owning a model shall receive the test date, sample size, protocol, and the numerical per-model failure rate, as well as a clear pass/fail result under this standard. Numerical per-model failure rates shall not be disclosed to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly manufactured units placed on the market from three years after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete retrofits, where technically feasible, within six years after adoption. Shut-off must not disable safety monitoring or prevent local emergency response.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document that the suppression system is suitable for the battery chemistry and enclosure, and shall provide installation, maintenance, and emergency-use instructions to customers. The requirement shall not delay immediate reporting or other corrective action for a known safety hazard.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of a credible safety hazard, provide clear instructions for safe use and contact details, and update customers when material information changes. When a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units during any 12-month period, the manufacturer shall notify the regulator within 5 business days, provide auditable incident counts and installed-unit exposure data, and cooperate with the regulator’s investigation. Within 30 days of notification, the manufacturer shall submit a corrective-action plan with specific measures and completion dates, or explain why further investigation is needed and provide an interim plan. The regulator shall review the evidence and may require a recall when it determines that an ongoing serious hazard exists and less restrictive corrective measures are inadequate. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers without delay when a recall is required and provide clear instructions for returning, repairing, or safely using the product.
