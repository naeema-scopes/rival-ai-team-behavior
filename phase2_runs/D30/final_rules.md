# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D30 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a material risk to their unit or a safety action is needed. Reports must include known incident circumstances, model and serial number, and any immediate protective steps; updates must be provided as investigations progress.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C. A failure rate above 8% of tested units is not allowed; such a model must not be newly marketed until corrective action is completed and it passes retesting. Models exceeding the 5% regulator benchmark must complete corrective action and be retested at least annually, but may continue to be marketed while meeting the 8% maximum. Test results, including model-specific failure rates, must be provided to the regulator and disclosed to customers who buy that model before sale and after each material design change. Individual company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public; regulators may publish aggregated, non-identifying results. Models must be retested annually and after any material design change.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within 6 years of adoption of this standard. Shut-off must be accessible to the manufacturer and emergency responders, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Customers must receive clear instructions on the capability and how to request an emergency shut-off.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system’s operating limits and maintenance requirements to customers. This requirement does not replace applicable installation, testing, or incident-reporting obligations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers must receive clear, actionable safety information, including material thermal risks identified through testing or incident data, and instructions for reporting a safety concern and taking protective action. Model-specific 55 °C test failure rates must be provided to regulators under S2 and disclosed to customers who buy that model. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and recommended actions. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers without delay and provide recall instructions and remedies. Following corrective action and verified retesting, a manufacturer may seek regulator approval to resume sales; a recall does not itself require a permanent market ban. Regulators may require a recall below this threshold when evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
