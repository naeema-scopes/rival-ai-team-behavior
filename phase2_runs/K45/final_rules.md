# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K45 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, evacuation, serious injury, or death within 24 hours of learning of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. A manufacturer that discovers an incident was not previously reported must report it within 24 hours of discovery and explain the delay. Manufacturers must reconcile incident records against service, warranty, and customer-support records at least annually and retain supporting records for regulator review. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and investigation status; updates are due when material facts become available. Manufacturers must notify affected customers of a serious hazard or required safety action within 24 hours of notifying the regulator, and notify customers of other reportable thermal incidents or emerging model-level patterns within 5 business days. Reporting does not replace any stricter legal duty.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a validated sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after a material design change, using a representative sample and a common protocol agreed by the parties. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt notification to regulators, a corrective-action plan within 30 days, and retesting within 6 months. A model may continue to be sold during this period unless regulators determine that an immediate restriction is necessary to address an imminent serious risk. If the model remains above 5% on retest, regulators may require further corrective action, additional testing, or sales restrictions proportionate to the risk. Manufacturers must provide per-model results and test methods to regulators and to customers who buy or are considering buying that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. Parties should review the limit and evidence at least annually.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly manufactured home battery systems must support remote emergency shut-off beginning 2 years after this standard takes effect. For units already installed on that date, manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway and complete retrofits within 6 years where technically feasible. Manufacturers must promptly notify customers of any unit that cannot be retrofitted, explain its safe-use measures, and provide regulators with an implementation plan. Remote shut-off must not replace local manual controls or emergency-response procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence that the suppression system is appropriate for the battery chemistry and installation, and instructions for inspection and maintenance. This requirement does not replace other fire-safety measures or applicable legal requirements.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, before purchase, model-specific safety instructions, known material hazards, emergency shut-off guidance, and applicable per-model test results directly to customers; individual-company results must not be published to the general public. Notify affected customers promptly of a serious hazard or safety action. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, using a consistent incident definition and verified installed-unit denominator, the manufacturer must notify regulators and affected customers within 24 hours and initiate a mandatory recall without delay. The manufacturer must submit an investigation and corrective-action plan to regulators; regulators may require additional protective measures. Nothing in this section limits regulators’ authority to require an earlier recall or other immediate safety action.
