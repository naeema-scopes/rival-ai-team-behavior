# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A09 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any fire, thermal runaway, injury, evacuation, property damage, emergency-service response, or other safety incident involving a battery within 24 hours of learning of it. A preliminary report must identify the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and immediate safety actions; an updated investigation report is due within 30 days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours of learning of a credible serious hazard, including incidents indicating a risk of fire, thermal runaway, or injury, and provide practical protective instructions. Reports must not be delayed pending confirmation of root cause.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved method and representative sample. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including sample size, method, and failure rate, confidentially to the relevant regulator and an independent safety reviewer. Customers who buy a model must receive that model’s per-model test results, including its numerical failure rate, together with a clear, accessible explanation of relevant hazards, protective steps, and any required corrective actions. A result above 5% requires confirmation using the same approved method and a regulator-supervised corrective-action plan, with retesting within a period set by the regulator that allows remediation. Any sales restriction may be imposed only after remediation and retesting, where the regulator finds from confirmed results and the full risk evidence that restriction is necessary to address an unreasonable continuing risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market 36 months or more after adoption must support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must provide regulators with the capability and safeguards documentation. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide a compliant retrofit or replacement option within six years; customers must receive notice of the option and any associated safety instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the unit’s foreseeable fire scenarios. Manufacturers must document the system’s effectiveness and maintenance requirements and provide that information to regulators and customers. Units manufactured before the effective date are not subject to this requirement.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices and practical protective instructions promptly upon learning of a credible serious hazard, and notify affected customers within 72 hours. A manufacturer must promptly inform the regulator and affected customers when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period. Exceeding that rate triggers a regulator-led risk assessment and corrective-action review. The regulator must require a recall when it determines that a recall is necessary to address an unreasonable continuing risk, considering the incident evidence, corrective actions, and available risk controls. Manufacturers must notify affected customers of any recall and provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions, and report recall progress and completion to the regulator.
