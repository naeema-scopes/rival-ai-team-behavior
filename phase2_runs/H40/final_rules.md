# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H40 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents initially reported by customers or installers. A preliminary report is due within 72 hours, with known product/model, location, injuries or damage, and response actions; updates must be provided as facts are confirmed. The manufacturer must notify affected customers promptly and no later than 72 hours after learning of a potentially relevant hazard, with immediate notice where continued use presents an imminent risk. Reports must not be withheld because an investigation is ongoing.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, a representative sample, and failures defined to include fire, thermal runaway, or loss of required safety functions. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt regulator notification and a corrective-action and retesting plan; it is not, by itself, an automatic prohibition on sale. Regulators may require proportionate interim safeguards or sales restrictions based on the model’s overall risk, incident history, and progress against its safety plan. Before sale and after material design changes, manufacturers must submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results to the regulator. Customers must receive the model’s test failure rate and a clear results summary directly before purchase and when materially updated. Individual company per-model failure rates must not be published or made available through a public database. Regulators should review the threshold and evidence at least every three years.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a clear procedure for emergency responders and customers. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within six years after adoption, where technically feasible; if a unit cannot be safely retrofitted, the manufacturer must notify its owner and regulator and provide an equivalent protective measure or replacement plan within that period.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression designed for the battery’s foreseeable thermal hazards. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s effectiveness and maintenance requirements, and provide that information to customers and regulators.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear written information on thermal hazards, installation and operating requirements, emergency actions, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified safety risks and provide updated instructions. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, using the installed-unit count and incident data for that model. The manufacturer must notify the regulator within 24 hours of identifying that threshold has been exceeded and promptly provide supporting data; the regulator may verify the calculation but verification must not delay protective action. Regulators may require earlier corrective action where incident severity or other evidence indicates an urgent hazard. Recall notices must explain the risk, affected units, immediate precautions, and remedy, and be sent directly to affected customers and made publicly accessible.
