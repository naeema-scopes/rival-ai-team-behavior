# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G11 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of it. Incidents involving death, serious injury, or an active risk to other units must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must also notify affected customers within 5 calendar days when an incident indicates a material safety risk to their model, and promptly provide any immediate safety instructions. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and known actions taken. Manufacturers must keep incident records and submit an annual incident summary by model to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample before market entry and at least once every 2 years. A failure rate above 5% requires the manufacturer to notify the regulator within 30 days and submit a corrective-action plan and retest schedule. Exceeding 5% does not by itself prohibit sales; the regulator may restrict sales based on its review of the results, corrective actions, and safety evidence. Per-model test results and sample sizes must be submitted to the regulator within 30 days of testing and provided to current and prospective customers for that model. They must not be publicly published. Manufacturers must retain results and make them available for standards review.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## S3_remote_shutoff
New units placed on the market from 2 years after adoption must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with a customer-accessible local alternative and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must offer a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within 6 years. The shut-off function must be tested during installation or retrofit and at least annually thereafter; customers must be informed how to request its use.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units placed on the market from 36 months after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to reduce propagation of thermal events. Manufacturers must document the suppression design and its maintenance requirements and provide those requirements to customers. The requirement does not apply retroactively to installed units.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific safety notices, including the latest 55 °C test results and any material safety limitations. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and provide clear protective instructions. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year must be reported to regulators and trigger a prompt regulator-led review of the rate, incident circumstances, and corrective measures. A recall is required when the regulator determines the model presents an unreasonable safety risk; the threshold alone does not automatically trigger recall.
