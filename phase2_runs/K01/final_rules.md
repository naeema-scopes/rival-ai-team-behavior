# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K01 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete investigation and corrective-action report within 30 days. Affected customers must be notified within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk or requires action; neighbours and other potentially affected members of the public must receive prompt notice when there is an immediate local hazard. Reports must include model, location, and available incident details. No incident may be omitted because its cause is not yet confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative units. Manufacturers must submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the regulator before sale and after any material design change. Regulators may inspect underlying records. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must have a regulator-approved corrective-action plan, with retesting at least every six months, clear disclosure of the model’s results and risks to its customers, and any required interim safeguards. The regulator may restrict or suspend sales where the evidence indicates an imminent hazard or the manufacturer fails to meet its plan. Per-model failure rates and test results must be provided to customers of that model; manufacturers must not withhold them from those customers. Individual-company, per-model failure rates and test results must not be published to the general public. Public reporting may use aggregate or anonymized data.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new battery systems placed on the market must support secure remote emergency shut-off by the effective date of this standard. For already-installed units, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation within six years of that date, where technically feasible, and notify owners of units that cannot be upgraded. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and must not prevent safe local operation or create additional hazards.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to reduce fire propagation without creating additional hazards. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide evidence of validation to the regulator before placing compliant units on the market.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including applicable thermal-test results, emergency guidance, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected owners of material newly identified risks and provide instructions for protective action; notices must be issued within 48 hours when urgent action is warranted. A model must be recalled when that model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units, based on regulator-verified incident and installation data. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, halt sales pending corrective action, and provide a safe remedy or replacement. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action at lower rates when evidence indicates an imminent hazard.
