# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J12 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account of known circumstances and impacts. A fatality, serious injury, or active fire presenting an ongoing risk must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must provide affected customers with a safety notice within 5 calendar days of learning of an incident that may indicate a product safety risk, and promptly update the notice as material facts emerge. Reports must include incidents known through customer service, warranty, and field-service channels; an investigation may not delay the initial report.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with a statistically adequate sample and documented test conditions. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% for any model. Exceeding this limit requires a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retesting on a regulator-set schedule; exceeding it alone does not automatically prohibit continued sale while the manufacturer follows the plan, unless the regulator determines there is an imminent or serious risk. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the regulator and to customers who own or purchase that model; results need not be published to the general public. Retesting is required after a material design or manufacturing change and at intervals set by the regulator.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can place the battery in a safe state, subject to safeguards against unauthorized activation and loss of communications. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or equivalent safe-state solution within 6 years, where technically feasible, and must inform owners of any limitations and interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression designed to limit fire propagation and protect occupants and nearby members of the public. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to regulators and customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about known hazards, applicable precautions, and any required corrective actions, and promptly update notices as material facts emerge. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must notify the relevant regulator and affected customers promptly, explain the basis for the recall, and provide a remedy and instructions for safe handling. A regulator may require a recall or other corrective action at a lower rate when the evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
