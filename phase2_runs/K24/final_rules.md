# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K24 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents initially suspected to be unrelated to a product defect. They must provide a complete incident report within 10 business days and update it as new facts emerge. When an incident indicates a material risk to users or nearby members of the public, manufacturers must notify affected customers within 24 hours, describe immediate protective steps, and provide updates when the risk assessment or remedy changes. Reports must identify the affected model and preserve relevant incident and unit records.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers must submit exact per-model results, including failure rates and test conditions, to the relevant regulator and provide the exact results to customers buying or owning that model in a clear, accessible format, with plain-language explanation of hazards, protective steps, and corrective actions. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a corrective-action plan, and retesting within a regulator-approved timeframe. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically bar sales; regulators may restrict sales or require other measures where evidence indicates an unacceptable safety risk. Manufacturers must report corrective actions and retest results to regulators and notify affected customers of material safety implications.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation. Existing installed units must be equipped with remote shut-off or a functionally equivalent emergency isolation capability within six years after adoption of this standard, where technically feasible; manufacturers must notify customers of the capability, limitations, and any required upgrade.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations in customer materials.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, protective steps, and any corrective action promptly after a manufacturer identifies a material safety risk. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units during any 12-month period. The rate must be calculated using the model’s installed-unit population and all known thermal incidents, including incidents not previously reported to regulators. Manufacturers must notify affected customers and regulators without delay, explain the remedy, and make the remedy available at no charge.
