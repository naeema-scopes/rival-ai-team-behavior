# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B16 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents initially classified as near misses. Reports shall include the model, known circumstances, and any immediate precautions; manufacturers shall submit an investigation update within 30 days. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers directly when an incident involving their unit or newly identified information presents a potential safety risk, with clear precautions and available remedies. Manufacturers shall maintain incident records and provide aggregate annual summaries to the regulator.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a regulator-approved protocol with documented sampling and test conditions. A failure rate of 5% is the benchmark for corrective action, not an automatic bar to sale. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results, including failure rates and test protocols, to the regulator and provide the results to customers who buy that model in clear, accessible form; results shall not be published to the general public. Results above 5% require prompt regulator notification and a documented corrective-action plan with a retesting schedule. The regulator shall assess verified test evidence, risk, and proposed corrective actions before deciding whether restrictions are warranted. The parties shall review the benchmark against verified test data after two years.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market shall support a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or emergency responders when necessary to address an imminent safety risk. For units already installed when this standard is adopted, manufacturers shall provide the capability through retrofit or an equivalent verified safety measure within 72 months, where technically feasible. Shut-off actions and restoration procedures shall be documented and communicated to affected customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression, or an independently verified equivalent built-in fire-mitigation system, beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide evidence of effectiveness under the applicable fire-safety test protocol to the regulator before placing compliant units on the market.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety instructions at purchase and prompt, direct notice of newly identified hazards affecting their unit, including recommended precautions and available remedies. A manufacturer shall notify the regulator when a model reaches 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; the regulator shall assess the evidence and may require a recall where warranted. The parties should review this trigger against consistent incident definitions and verified exposure data.
