# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D11 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the regulator within five business days of learning of it. Any incident involving fire, injury, evacuation, or an immediate risk to occupants or neighbours must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 24 hours when immediate protective action is needed, and otherwise within five business days, providing clear safety instructions. Reports must include known circumstances, affected model and units, actions taken, and any uncertainty; manufacturers must update the regulator and customers as material facts emerge. Manufacturers must retain incident records and provide annual incident summaries to the regulator.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. Manufacturers must submit per-model results to the regulator and disclose them to customers who buy that model; this does not require public disclosure of individual-company results. A model exceeding 5% must promptly provide the regulator and affected customers with a corrective-action plan and retest schedule. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sales: the regulator will set remediation and retesting deadlines and may impose proportionate interim sales conditions based on the evidence and risk, including restrictions where needed to protect the public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must include a secure remote emergency shut-off function beginning 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide customers and emergency responders with clear instructions for requesting or using shut-off; the function must not prevent local manual isolation or compromise essential safety protections. For units already installed at adoption, manufacturers must provide a compatible remote shut-off capability or an equivalent means of emergency isolation within six years, with customer notice and instructions. Where a unit cannot technically support either option, the manufacturer must document the reason and provide the customer with an appropriate alternative emergency procedure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be designed and tested to applicable regulator-approved safety requirements.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of material safety risks, incidents, and corrective actions, with clear instructions for safe use and response. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly report the exceedance to the regulator and submit incident data and a proposed corrective-action plan. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds this threshold. The regulator may set proportionate recall measures, including repair, replacement, or removal from service, and may permit sales to resume after verified corrective actions and retesting demonstrate that the risk is adequately controlled. Recall notices must be sent directly to affected customers.
