# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A45 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. Other thermal incidents shall be reported within 5 business days. Reports shall include the incident date, product/model, known consequences, and available cause information; updates shall be provided as investigations progress. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk requiring customer action, and promptly provide any subsequent safety instructions.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry, at least once every three years, and after a material design change. The maximum allowed failure rate shall be 8% for any model. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test results, including the test date, sample size, and failure rate, to the relevant regulator and provide those results to customers who buy that model. Where a model exceeds the limit, the manufacturer shall promptly submit a corrective-action plan and retest after implementing it; regulators may require additional measures where the evidence indicates a serious risk. Manufacturers shall provide customers with safety notices and action instructions when a test result indicates a credible safety risk requiring customer action. The standard shall be reviewed as further test and field-performance evidence becomes available.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems shall include remote emergency shut-off capability no later than three years after this standard takes effect. For systems already installed on that date, manufacturers shall provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within six years, where technically feasible, and shall communicate safe interim procedures to owners. Any shut-off function shall not prevent users or emergency responders from safely isolating the system locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery systems manufactured 36 months or more after this standard is adopted shall include built-in fire suppression designed for the system’s battery chemistry and enclosure. Manufacturers shall document that the system’s suppression function is suitable for the intended installation and shall provide owners and emergency responders with instructions for its inspection, maintenance, and safe operation. This requirement does not replace other applicable fire-safety measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices for incidents, identified hazards, and corrective actions affecting their model, including safe-use instructions and any action required. Notices shall be sent promptly when a credible safety risk requires customer action, and updated as material information becomes available. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer shall promptly report the rate and supporting data to the relevant regulator and affected customers. The regulator shall promptly assess the risk and may require a recall or other corrective action; the manufacturer shall initiate a recall when that assessment finds the risk unacceptable. Regulators may require a recall or other corrective action at any lower rate where evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk. Manufacturers shall explain the risk and remedy to affected customers and report progress until required corrective actions are complete.
