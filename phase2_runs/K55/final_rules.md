# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K55 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a full incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when the incident presents a credible safety risk or requires customer action. Reports must include known injuries, damage, product/model and serial number, and immediate safety advice; updates are required as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a validated sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved sample size and protocol. Manufacturers must submit full per-model results, including numerical failure rates and test methods, to regulators and make the full results for each model available to customers considering or buying that model and to its owners. Access may be through a private safety portal; public posting or a searchable public registry of individual model rates is not required. Each model must meet a maximum failure rate of 5%. A model exceeding 5% must promptly stop new shipments pending corrective action and successful retesting; exceedance does not by itself require permanent market exclusion.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off beginning 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within 6 years after adoption where technically feasible, and provide regulators and affected customers with a retrofit plan and schedule. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders or the manufacturer during an imminent safety emergency, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document that the suppression system is suitable for the battery chemistry and installation, and include maintenance and safety instructions for customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety instructions at purchase and prompt notices of material safety risks, incidents affecting their model, and required precautions or corrective actions. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 units of that model installed during the relevant year. The rate must use the model-specific incident count and the number of that model’s installed units during the same reporting period. The manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and regulators, provide safe interim instructions, and offer an effective remedy, repair, or replacement. Manufacturers must provide regulators with the incident counts and installed-unit figures used to calculate the rate. A recall requires customer protection and corrective action; it does not automatically mean a permanent market ban.
