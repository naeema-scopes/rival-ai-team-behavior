# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B08 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of becoming aware of it, including incidents initially reported by customers or installers. A death, serious injury, evacuation, or spread of fire beyond the battery shall be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or requires inspection, repair, or use precautions. Reports shall include available details and be updated as investigations progress; an incomplete initial report does not excuse timely reporting.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units before market entry and after a material design change. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model results, protocol, and sample size to the relevant regulator and to customers considering or owning that model. A failure rate above 5% shall trigger a regulator-reviewed corrective-action plan and follow-up testing; it shall not by itself bar sale. The regulator may require additional safeguards or restrict sales if evidence shows an unresolved, material safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly manufactured units placed on the market 24 months after this standard takes effect shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible alternative where communications are unavailable. Manufacturers shall provide a retrofit plan for already-installed units and complete feasible retrofits within 6 years after the standard takes effect. Where retrofit is infeasible, the manufacturer shall notify the owner and provide an equivalent risk-reduction measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured for sale beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect shall include built-in fire suppression designed to limit fire spread and thermal propagation. The system shall be validated for the unit’s intended installation conditions and maintained in accordance with manufacturer instructions. Manufacturers shall disclose any limitations and required inspection or maintenance to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices about relevant hazards, required precautions, inspection or maintenance, and available corrective actions, promptly after identifying a credible safety risk. A manufacturer shall notify affected customers and the relevant regulator within 5 business days after determining that a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, and shall initiate a recall unless the regulator determines an equivalent corrective action adequately addresses the risk. Notices shall explain the risk, affected units, and remedy. Manufacturers shall update customers as material information changes.
