# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H19 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it. A preliminary report is acceptable, with material updates provided within 30 days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within five business days when the incident may indicate a safety risk for their unit or model; urgent risks require notice as soon as practicable and no later than 24 hours after confirmation. Reports must include known incident circumstances and the affected model and serial number, where available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market release and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for new models. A model exceeding 5% must not be newly placed on the market until corrective action and a passing retest are documented. For models already marketed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must submit a regulator-reviewed corrective-action plan within 12 months and meet the 5% limit within 36 months. During that transition, continued placement is permitted only under the approved plan and any interim safeguards required by the regulator; regulators may restrict sales sooner if warranted by risk. Per-model test results, including sample size and failure rate, must be submitted to regulators and provided to customers who buy that model. Individual company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. Regulators may require additional testing or corrective action where results indicate a serious safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## S3_remote_shutoff
New units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off no later than three years after this standard takes effect. For units already installed on that date, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or other effective upgrade within six years, where technically feasible. Shut-off capability must be secure, available to authorized emergency responders or the customer, and must not disable essential safety monitoring.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Suppression systems must be designed and tested to applicable regulator-approved safety requirements and must not impair essential battery safety monitoring or emergency shut-off.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices for incidents, hazards, corrective actions, and recalls affecting their model. Notices must be sent promptly when a material safety risk is identified, and no later than five business days after confirmation; urgent risks must be communicated as soon as practicable and within 24 hours. Notices must describe the risk, affected models and units where known, and steps customers should take. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 confirmed thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. The rate must be calculated using the model’s installed-unit exposure during that period and reported incidents attributable to that model; manufacturers must document the data and calculation for regulator review. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop placing the affected model on the market, and provide a repair, replacement, or refund plan. Regulators may require a recall sooner where the evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
