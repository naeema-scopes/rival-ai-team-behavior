# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B30 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery-related thermal incident involving overheating, fire, smoke, thermal runaway, emergency shutdown, injury, or property damage. A report is due within 24 hours of the manufacturer becoming aware of the incident, with material updates provided promptly as facts are confirmed. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety hazard to their unit or requires customer action; notices must state the hazard, recommended immediate precautions, and how to obtain assistance. Manufacturers must retain incident records and report previously unreported qualifying incidents discovered during review.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a documented, reproducible protocol before sale and after any material design or manufacturing change. A failure rate above 5% triggers regulator-supervised corrective-action review; it is not by itself an automatic ban on sales. Manufacturers must retain the protocol, sample size, results, and corrective actions, and provide complete per-model results, including model, test date, failure rate, and method, to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model. Regulators may require corrective action, retesting, or additional safeguards based on the results and associated risks.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for its use and maintain safeguards against unauthorized activation. For installed units that lack this capability, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation within six years of adoption, prioritizing units with known hazards. Remote shut-off must not replace required incident reporting, customer notices, or physical safety protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be validated for the battery chemistry and configuration, and manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of its effectiveness and maintenance requirements. Until the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must disclose whether each model includes built-in suppression and provide safe-use and emergency-response instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices about hazards affecting their model, including the hazard, precautions, and any action required, within 72 hours of the manufacturer learning information indicating a potential safety hazard. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers, provide safe-use or shutdown instructions, and report the recall to regulators.
