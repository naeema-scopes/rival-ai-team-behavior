# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A14 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage within 5 business days of learning of it. Incidents involving death, serious injury, or an ongoing risk to others shall be reported within 24 hours. Within 10 business days, the manufacturer shall provide a preliminary investigation update; a final report shall follow within 60 days, with updates if the investigation remains open. Affected customers shall be notified promptly when the incident indicates a credible safety risk, and no later than 5 business days after that determination.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and after a material design change, using a regulator-recognized protocol and a statistically adequate sample. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results, including sample size, failures, and failure rate, confidentially to the regulator and provide those actual per-model results to customers who buy that model. A failure rate above 5% shall trigger prompt regulator review and a manufacturer corrective-action plan, with retesting on a regulator-set schedule. Regulators may require additional testing or protective measures based on test results and incident data; exceeding 5% alone shall not automatically bar sale, and regulators shall determine appropriate measures to protect safety.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall provide a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under documented procedures. Manufacturers shall provide clear customer instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation. Existing installed units shall be brought into compliance within 72 months of adoption where technically feasible; manufacturers shall notify owners of units that cannot be upgraded and provide safe alternatives or replacement options.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression no sooner than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements, and provide those details to customers. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after the effective date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety notices about risks, required corrective actions, and safe use promptly after a credible safety risk is identified. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers and regulators of a proposed recall and the basis for it. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on validated incident data, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and submit a corrective-action plan. The regulator shall determine whether a recall or other protective action is necessary, considering incident severity, causation, and the effectiveness of proposed measures; any required action shall be implemented promptly. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action at lower rates where the severity or circumstances of incidents warrant it. Manufacturers shall provide customers with timely instructions for any required corrective action, repair or replacement options, and progress updates.
