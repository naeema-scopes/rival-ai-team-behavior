# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K29 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency intervention within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. Affected customers must be notified within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their model or lot; notices must describe the hazard and immediate precautions. Manufacturers must keep records of all incidents and report aggregate incident counts quarterly, including incidents initially considered unconfirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and sample size. Per-model results must be submitted to the relevant regulator before sale and after any material design change, and provided to customers who buy that model; results must not be publicly published in a way that identifies an individual company’s per-model failure rate. The maximum permitted failure rate is 6.5% per model. Models exceeding 5% must submit a regulator-approved corrective-action and monitoring plan, with reassessment on a regulator-set schedule. Regulators may restrict or suspend sales where corrective action is inadequate or the risk is unacceptable. Regulators must periodically review the testing protocol and threshold using accumulated test and incident evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly manufactured units must support remote emergency shut-off at the time of sale. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for using the feature and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed when this standard is adopted, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade enabling remote emergency shut-off within six years, where technically feasible; if it is not feasible, they must notify affected customers and provide safe emergency-isolation instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Newly manufactured home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment, and must be tested under a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must provide customers with maintenance instructions and disclose any limitations of the system.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific 55 °C test results and clear safety, installation, and emergency-response information. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of new material hazards and provide updated precautions; urgent hazards require notice within 72 hours of confirmation. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when regulators determine that a defect presents an imminent serious safety risk. Incident rates must be calculated and reported by model using documented installed-unit counts. A recall requires prompt customer notification and corrective action; sale may resume only after the regulator verifies that the hazard has been addressed.
