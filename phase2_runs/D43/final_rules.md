# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D43 · variant D · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Halcyon': 45})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the relevant regulator within five calendar days of learning of it. Incidents involving a fire, injury, evacuation, or material risk to nearby people must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify owners of potentially affected units within 72 hours for such serious incidents, and within five calendar days for other thermal incidents, with known hazards and protective steps. Reports must include model, incident date and location, consequences, and known circumstances; manufacturers must promptly update material new information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sampling. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including the actual failure-rate figure, confidentially to the regulator and provide the testing context and actual per-model failure-rate figure to customers who buy or own the model, along with model-specific safety status, known hazards, protective steps, and any corrective actions. Regulators may publish aggregated industry results. A model exceeding 5% must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and pass a confirmatory test. Regulators may impose proportionate sales restrictions based on evidence and risk; exceeding the test threshold alone does not require an automatic market ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models must support remote emergency shut-off, with the requirement applying to units manufactured beginning 36 months after adoption. For units already installed on that date, manufacturers must provide a compliant retrofit or equivalent safety mitigation within six years, prioritizing units presenting the greatest risk. Shut-off capability must not prevent emergency responders or occupants from taking necessary safety action.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance standard. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Regulators should establish the performance standard and conformity process before the effective date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, protective steps, and material updates, following the incident-notification timelines in S1. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and provide incident data, investigate the causes, and submit a corrective-action plan. Regulators must promptly assess the evidence and may require a recall, including a remedy or safe-use instructions pending remedy, where the evidence indicates unacceptable risk; they may require a recall at or below that rate where warranted. Recall notices must explain the affected models, risks, remedies, and how customers can obtain assistance.
