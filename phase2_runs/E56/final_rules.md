# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E56 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. They must notify affected owners and any known directly exposed neighbours within 48 hours, with known hazards and immediate safety steps. A report does not depend on whether a recall threshold has been reached.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually, using a consistent, regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. No more than 7% of tested units may fail. Manufacturers must submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results to regulators and provide the relevant model’s results to its owners; individual-company per-model failure rates are not published to the general public. A model exceeding the limit requires prompt corrective action and retesting before further units are placed on the market. Regulators may require earlier action where the evidence indicates an imminent risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery models must include a remotely accessible emergency shut-off function. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer’s safety team to activate it, with clear safeguards against unauthorized use. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance within 72 months after adoption where technically feasible; manufacturers must notify owners of any units that cannot be upgraded and provide safe alternatives. Remote shut-off does not replace incident reporting or other emergency protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and reliability protocol. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must document suppression-system performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to regulators and customers. Until the effective date, manufacturers must continue to meet applicable fire-safety requirements and promptly address identified hazards.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the relevant model’s latest 55 °C sustained-heat test results, known material thermal hazards, and clear emergency and maintenance instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of new material safety findings and recommended actions. If a model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units, calculated using a regulator-approved method and verified installation counts, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, explain the hazard and recommended steps, and submit a corrective-action plan. The regulator must review the evidence and determine proportionate corrective measures, which may include a recall with an effective remedy where lesser measures are inadequate to address the risk. The regulator may require earlier action where evidence indicates an imminent risk. Customer notices must explain the hazard, steps to take, and available remedy.
