# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G21 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with an initial account of known facts. They must provide a complete incident report within 10 calendar days and promptly update it as material facts emerge. Within 24 hours, manufacturers must also notify affected customers and provide clear instructions on immediate precautions and how to obtain assistance. If the affected units or customers cannot yet be identified, the manufacturer must notify all potentially affected customers and update the notice when identification is possible. Reports and notices must not be delayed while causation is investigated.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. No model may be placed on the market if more than 8% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator before first sale and after any material design change. Before purchase, customers must receive the per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results for the model they are considering, along with clear model-specific safety information and any regulator-required safety warnings. Non-identifying aggregated information may be published publicly; identified per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market 24 months after adoption must include a remotely accessible emergency shut-off function, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear instructions for customers. For installed units lacking this function, manufacturers must provide an upgrade or other effective mitigation plan within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible, and report progress to regulators.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the battery system. Manufacturers must document the system's performance and maintenance requirements for regulators and disclose relevant limitations and instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers must receive clear, model-specific safety information, including 55 °C sustained-heat test results and known material risks, safe installation and operating instructions, warning signs, and how to report an incident or obtain support. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of new material safety information and provide actionable precautions. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators, investigate, and submit a documented corrective-action plan. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 10 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. For a mandatory recall, manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop sales, and provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions; notices must explain the hazard and required actions. Regulators may require a recall at a lower rate where evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
