# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D45 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it. Incidents involving fire, injury, evacuation, or emergency-service response must be reported within 24 hours. The manufacturer must notify affected owners within 5 business days, and promptly provide safety instructions; where an immediate hazard is suspected, notify owners without delay. Reports must include known facts and be updated as the investigation proceeds.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo independent 55 °C sustained-heat testing under a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after a material design change. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification and a regulator-approved corrective-action plan, with follow-up testing on a defined timetable; it does not by itself require an automatic sales ban. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, protocol, sample size, and corrective actions to the regulator. Before purchase, customers must receive the tested model’s failure rate and clear safety instructions, material risks, and relevant corrective actions. Regulators must protect identifiable per-model test results from public release, while being able to publish aggregated results. This does not limit disclosure of identifiable per-model results to customers considering purchase of that model.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the owner and, when necessary to address an imminent safety hazard, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under a documented procedure. Manufacturers must provide a secure software or hardware retrofit for installed units lacking this capability, completing the retrofit within 6 years of this standard taking effect. The shut-off must not prevent safe local operation or emergency isolation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression for units manufactured 36 months after this standard is adopted. The system must be independently tested for effectiveness and must not create an additional material safety hazard. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s per-model thermal test results, safety instructions, known material risks, and any relevant incident or corrective-action notices. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly of newly identified hazards and provide clear instructions for safe use, repair, or shutdown. A model must be recalled when its thermal incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year; the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop new sales, and provide a timely repair, replacement, or refund plan.
