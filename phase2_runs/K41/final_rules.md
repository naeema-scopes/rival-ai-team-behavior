# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K41 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any fire, thermal runaway, evacuation, injury, death, or property damage involving a home battery within 24 hours of learning of it. Other confirmed overheating incidents requiring emergency response or causing the battery to be taken out of service must be reported within 5 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a material safety risk to their model or requires inspection, restricted use, or corrective action. Reports must include known circumstances and actions taken; updates are due as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and after a material design change. A model meets the benchmark if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results, test methods, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the regulator, and provide those complete results to current and prospective customers in a clear, accessible form. Regulators may publish aggregated results. Exceeding the benchmark requires prompt corrective action and regulator-supervised retesting, but does not itself automatically ban the model; the regulator should set a reasonable compliance period before any market restriction, unless immediate action is necessary to address an imminent risk. Regulators may require additional retesting where results or field incidents warrant it.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market 2 years after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally operable alternative. Manufacturers must provide owners clear instructions and disclose any connectivity limitations. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe alternative within 6 years, prioritizing units with identified safety concerns.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance requirements. Manufacturers must document suppression performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to customers. This requirement does not replace other applicable fire-safety protections.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and the regulator of a material safety risk, inspection or use restriction, or corrective action, and provide clear safety instructions and updates as material facts emerge. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must notify affected customers and the regulator without undue delay, explain the risk and remedy, and report recall progress. Regulators may require a recall below this threshold where the evidence indicates an unreasonable safety risk.
