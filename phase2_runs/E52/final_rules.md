# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E52 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported to the regulator within 5 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours for incidents involving fire, injury, evacuation, or emergency-service response, and within 10 business days for other confirmed thermal incidents. Reports must include known circumstances and corrective actions; updates are due when material facts change.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before certification and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved sample and protocol. A model exceeds the 5% acceptance criterion if more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results and corrective-action plans to the relevant regulator. Before purchase, current and prospective customers must receive the model’s current test status and its complete per-model test results, including the measured failure rate; these results must also be available to existing customers. Results exceeding 5% require an investigation and corrective-action plan submitted to the regulator within 30 days, with progress updates as required. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sales; the regulator may impose proportionate interim measures based on demonstrated risk while corrective actions are underway. A model may not be represented as meeting the acceptance criterion until it passes retesting.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a local means of safe operation. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway for installed units that are technically compatible, completing required retrofits within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Where retrofitting is infeasible, the manufacturer must notify the owner and provide safe alternative mitigation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard is adopted. The system must be designed and validated to applicable regulator-approved performance and maintenance requirements. Manufacturers must disclose any limitations and required maintenance to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its current test status (whether it meets the 55 °C acceptance criterion), known material hazards, and required installation, maintenance, and emergency steps. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and corrective actions, and provide updates when material facts change. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, investigate the causes, and submit a corrective-action plan. The manufacturer must initiate a recall for that model, promptly contact affected customers, provide a remedy, and give safe-use instructions.
