# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D53 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with an initial account of known facts. They must provide an updated investigation and corrective-action report within 30 days, and promptly supplement it as material facts emerge. The affected customer must be notified within 24 hours when the event involves a credible safety risk to that customer or their household; customers must be kept informed of material safety findings and actions. Reports must include known incidents regardless of whether cause has yet been established.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a documented, reproducible protocol and a statistically adequate sample. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the relevant regulator, the other company’s designated safety representative, and customers who buy that model. Results may be published in aggregate, but may not be withheld from affected customers. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification and a corrective-action plan with a retest timetable. Regulators may require further mitigation based on the results, but exceeding 5% does not by itself prohibit sales.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within six years of adoption, where technically feasible. For units that cannot be retrofitted, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and the regulator and provide an effective alternative emergency-isolation method. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and must not prevent safe local operation or isolation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the battery’s likely fire hazards and must not create an additional unreasonable safety risk. Manufacturers must document the system’s performance and provide installation and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, safe operation, and any required corrective actions, promptly after identifying a credible safety risk. A manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers of a material safety issue and its mitigation plan. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; manufacturers must promptly report the exceedance and implement the recall under regulator oversight. Regulators may require a recall below that threshold when evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
