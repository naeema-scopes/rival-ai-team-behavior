# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H12 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every confirmed battery-related thermal incident, including overheating, thermal runaway, or fire, to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, whether or not it required emergency response, medical treatment, evacuation, or caused property damage. They must notify affected customers within 5 business days, and promptly provide any new material safety information. Reports must include known circumstances and corrective actions; an initial report may be updated as facts are established.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after a material design change. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and corrective-action plans confidentially to the relevant regulator. Results must not be published or disclosed to the general public; regulators may share them with other safety authorities under appropriate confidentiality protections. Manufacturers must provide the applicable model’s per-model test results to customers who purchase that model. A test result above 5% failure requires regulator review and a corrective-action plan, but does not by itself prohibit sale. Manufacturers must also provide customers with applicable safety instructions and material hazard notices under S5.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models must support remote emergency shut-off before sale. Manufacturers must enable the function on all installed units capable of receiving it within six years after adoption, including through software updates where feasible. Where an installed unit cannot support the function, the manufacturer must notify its owner and regulator and provide safe alternative emergency instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety instructions at purchase and prompt notice of any material hazard affecting their model, including recommended precautions and contact information. Manufacturers must track thermal incidents by model and notify the regulator if a model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 units of that model installed. Company-wide or cross-model rates must not be used as evidence that an individual model crossed this threshold. A threshold exceedance triggers regulator review of model-specific evidence and corrective action; the regulator may order a recall when warranted by that review. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of any recall and provide instructions for safe remediation.
