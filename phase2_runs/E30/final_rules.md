# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E30 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the responsible regulator any fire, thermal runaway, smoke, burn, property-damage event, emergency shut-off, or other event indicating a material battery safety risk. An event involving injury, fire, evacuation, or an active risk to people or property shall be reported within 24 hours of discovery; other reportable events shall be reported within five business days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 24 hours when an event indicates an immediate safety risk, and within five business days for other material risks, with clear protective instructions. Reports shall include known circumstances, affected models and units, and actions taken; manufacturers shall promptly supplement incomplete information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market release and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model results and corrective-action plans to the responsible regulator and make the results available to customers who buy or own that model. Public reporting may use aggregate, non-identifying summaries. A model with a failure rate above 7% shall not be newly installed unless the regulator authorizes a time-limited corrective-action plan. The parties shall review this interim threshold after two years against safety evidence and may adopt a 5% limit with an appropriate transition period. Manufacturers shall promptly address elevated failure rates and notify affected customers of material safety risks.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must support secure remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide a safe method for authorized emergency responders or the customer to initiate shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. For already-installed units, manufacturers shall provide compatible shut-off capability through retrofit or replacement within six years of this standard’s effective date, where technically feasible, and inform owners of any limitations and interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression designed to limit fire propagation and protect occupants and nearby members of the public. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall validate suppression performance through an approved test protocol and provide test evidence to the responsible regulator. Until the effective date, manufacturers shall disclose whether a unit includes built-in suppression and provide customers with applicable fire-safety instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of a material battery safety risk, provide clear instructions for safe use and emergency response, and update customers as risks or remedies change. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer shall notify the regulator and affected customers without undue delay, describe the remedy and timeline, and report recall progress to the regulator.
