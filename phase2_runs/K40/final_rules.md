# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K40 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every known thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the appropriate regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it. The initial report must include the incident date and location, model and unit identifiers where available, known injuries or property damage, and immediate protective actions; manufacturers must provide material updates promptly as facts are confirmed. Manufacturers must notify the affected customer within 72 hours of becoming aware of an incident involving that customer’s unit, with safety instructions and a contact for follow-up. If the affected customer cannot be identified, the manufacturer must promptly notify the relevant regulator and follow its directions for reaching potentially affected households.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a documented sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-recognized protocol and a representative sample. A model’s measured failure rate must not exceed 7%; a model above that limit may not be newly sold until it passes a subsequent test and the regulator has reviewed its corrective-action plan. Manufacturers must report each model’s failure rate, sample size, protocol, and test date to the appropriate regulator and make those per-model results available to customers purchasing that model. Results need not be disclosed publicly or to customers of other models. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and the regulator of results indicating a material safety concern, with safety instructions and any protective actions. Regulators may require proportionate interim protections and corrective action based on the evidence. A test failure alone does not automatically require a recall; recalls are governed by S5.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months after this standard takes effect must support remote emergency shut-off, subject to regulator-approved safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide customers clear instructions on the feature and its limitations. For already-installed units, manufacturers must offer compatible remote shut-off retrofits where feasible and complete required retrofits within 6 years after the standard takes effect; regulators may approve an alternative safety measure where retrofit is infeasible.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and installation conditions. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions and disclose system limitations to customers. The regulator may approve an equivalent protective measure where built-in suppression is technically infeasible, provided the manufacturer demonstrates equivalent safety.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must give customers clear safety notices for known hazards, including affected models and units, the nature of the risk, required protective actions, and a contact for assistance. Notices must be delivered promptly by direct channels reasonably likely to reach affected customers, with updates as material facts change. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit incident data, a risk assessment, and a corrective-action plan. The regulator must promptly review the evidence and order a regulator-supervised recall when the evidence establishes an unacceptable ongoing risk; pending review, it may require sales suspension or other interim protections. Regulators may require broader recall or additional protective measures where warranted.
