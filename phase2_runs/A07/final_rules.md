# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A07 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 30 days. Incidents involving death, serious injury, evacuation, or substantial property damage must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a material safety risk to their model or requires customer action. Reports must include the model, serial number where available, incident circumstances, and known injuries or damage.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and at least annually thereafter, with a representative sample of production units. The 5% failure rate is the maximum performance benchmark for each model and an exceedance requires prompt corrective action; it does not by itself automatically prohibit sales or require withdrawal from the market. Manufacturers must submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator within 30 days of testing, retain supporting records for inspection, and provide results to customers of that model before purchase and to existing customers upon request. Results must not be withheld from customers. For an exceedance, the manufacturer must submit a regulator-reviewed corrective-action plan within 30 days and retest within 6 months. The regulator may require interim safeguards, additional testing, sales restrictions, or other protective measures based on the severity of the results and associated risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months after this standard takes effect must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to support remote shut-off within 6 years of the effective date where technically feasible, prioritizing units with identified safety risks. If retrofit is infeasible, the manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers and provide an equivalent risk-reduction measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression designed to contain or control thermal events, tested to a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must document performance and provide installation, maintenance, and limitations information to customers. Regulators may grant a time-limited, model-specific exemption where equivalent or greater protection is demonstrated.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety instructions, installation and maintenance requirements, known material limitations, and instructions for responding to overheating or other warning signs. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide updates on corrective actions. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; the regulator may also require a recall or other corrective action where the risk warrants it.
