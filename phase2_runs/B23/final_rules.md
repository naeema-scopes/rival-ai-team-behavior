# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B23 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
A manufacturer must report any fire, thermal runaway, or other thermal event involving an installed home battery to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. The report must identify the product model, event date and location, known consequences, and immediate safety actions; a manufacturer must provide material updates as they become known. The manufacturer must promptly notify the affected customer of the event and any immediate protective action, and in all cases within 72 hours of learning of it. If a hazard may affect other customers or the public, the manufacturer must also notify the regulator within 24 hours and provide customer notices in accordance with S5.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale, at least annually thereafter, and after a material design change. Within 30 days of testing, manufacturers must provide the complete results, methodology, sample size, and failure rate to the responsible regulator and to customers and prospective buyers of that model. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% for any model. A model exceeding 5% must not be sold until it meets the limit and has completed any corrective actions required by the regulator.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within 6 years of adoption of this standard. The shut-off must be accessible to the responsible emergency-response authority and the customer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation; manufacturers must provide clear instructions and disclose any limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be designed and validated under a regulator-approved test protocol, and manufacturers must provide customers with instructions on its operation and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a safety hazard and provide clear instructions for protective action, repair, or replacement; notices must also reach other customers and the public when the hazard may affect them. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must notify the regulator and affected customers promptly upon determining that the trigger is met, and publish recall scope and remedy information.
