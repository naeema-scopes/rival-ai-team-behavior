# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K42 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. Incidents presenting an ongoing or significant safety risk must be reported to affected customers within 24 hours; other confirmed thermal incidents must be disclosed to affected customers within 5 business days. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial number, and any immediate precautions. Missing or delayed reports must be disclosed and corrected promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved representative sample and protocol. Manufacturers must provide per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results to regulators, and disclose the per-model failure rate and test date to customers considering or owning that model; results must not be published to the general public. A model with a failure rate above 5% must undergo corrective action and prompt retesting. Exceeding 5% alone does not require an automatic sales restriction; regulators may restrict sales when a model-specific assessment finds a material safety risk, considering incident evidence and remediation, and must allow a reasonable opportunity to correct and retest where safe. Regulators should review the threshold after two years using accumulated test and incident evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 2 years after this section takes effect. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to provide the capability within 6 years, where technically feasible, and must notify owners of retrofit availability, instructions, and any limitations. A documented technical infeasibility must be reported to the regulator with an alternative risk-mitigation plan; it does not waive applicable incident-reporting duties.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this section takes effect. Manufacturers must disclose whether each model includes suppression and provide customers with relevant operating and maintenance instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about known thermal hazards, protective actions, and material changes to a model’s safety performance. Notices must be sent promptly when a significant risk is identified, and otherwise within 5 business days after confirmation of a thermal incident. Manufacturers must track thermal incidents by model and report counts and installed-unit denominators to regulators. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, using verified data, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide the underlying data for review. The regulator must assess the model-specific risk, including data reliability, test results, incident severity, and corrective measures. A recall is mandatory if the regulator determines the model presents a material safety risk that cannot be adequately addressed through corrective action; the regulator may require interim protective measures, customer notices, or a stop-sale where warranted. Any recall must include prompt notice to affected customers and safe-handling instructions and a remedy.
