# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B24 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account and available unit/model and location information. They shall provide an updated investigation and corrective-action report within 30 days. Affected customers shall be notified within 48 hours when the incident presents a potential safety risk to their unit or requires inspection, shutdown, or other action. Reports shall include incidents whether or not injury or property damage occurred.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Before market placement and at least annually thereafter, each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-accepted protocol and representative units. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for any model. Manufacturers shall submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, results, and failure rates to regulators and provide those results to customers considering purchase of that model. Individual per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public. Where a model exceeds the limit, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers and submit a corrective-action plan and retesting schedule. Regulators may set a reasonable deadline for corrective action and retesting, and may restrict sales where corrective action is inadequate, deadlines are not met, or an imminent serious risk exists.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market from 24 months after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off accessible to the customer and authorized emergency responders, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units to support remote shut-off, where technically feasible, within 72 months after adoption; where retrofitting is infeasible, they shall provide customers an equivalent documented emergency isolation method and notify them of its limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-accepted performance and reliability protocol. The requirement does not preclude regulators from requiring earlier measures for a model presenting an imminent serious risk. Manufacturers shall disclose suppression capabilities and limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers shall receive model-specific sustained-heat test results, thermal incident and recall history, installation and safe-use instructions, warning signs, emergency steps, and contact details for reporting incidents. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material new hazards and corrective actions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall immediately notify regulators and affected customers, submit an incident investigation and corrective-action plan, and implement any interim safety measures required by the regulator. The regulator shall promptly assess the evidence and may require a recall or other proportionate corrective action; it shall order an immediate recall where evidence indicates an imminent serious risk. Regulators retain authority to require a recall at any time where evidence indicates an imminent serious risk.
