# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E04 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents initially reported by customers or installers. A report must include the known circumstances, model, serial number, and any immediate safety actions. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 days when an incident indicates a credible safety risk, and provide updates when material facts change. Reports must not be withheld because an investigation is ongoing; a manufacturer may supplement them later.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after a material design or manufacturing change, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% of tested units per model. A model exceeding 5% must promptly undergo corrective review and submit a mitigation plan and implementation schedule to regulators. Exceeding the limit alone does not automatically require sales suspension; regulators may impose proportionate restrictions, including suspension, based on the test results, incident evidence, and adequacy of the mitigation plan. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and test methods to regulators and provide the results for a model to customers who buy that model, including prospective customers before purchase. Results need not be publicly disclosed beyond those recipients. Regulators should review the limits periodically using incident and test evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with appropriate protections against unauthorized activation and a local means of safe operation. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent compliant capability within 6 years of this standard’s effective date. Where retrofit is impracticable, the manufacturer must document the reason and provide customers with an alternative mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard’s adoption. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation, and must not create additional hazards during normal use, maintenance, or emergency response. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and validate suppression performance through testing.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices describing material risks, applicable warnings, safe installation and use, and steps to take during an overheating, smoke, or fire event. Notices must be provided before purchase and updated promptly when material safety information changes; affected customers must be notified within 5 days when a credible safety risk is identified. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The recall must promptly protect affected customers and may use an appropriate remedy such as repair, retrofit, replacement, or refund; it does not by itself require a permanent market ban. Regulators may require an earlier recall or other corrective action when the evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk. Recall notices must explain the hazard, affected units, and remedy, and be sent promptly to affected customers and regulators.
