# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E40 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency shutdown to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. They must provide a complete incident report, including known circumstances and corrective actions, within 10 calendar days, updating it as material facts emerge. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours of learning of an incident that presents a potential safety risk to their model, with immediate notice where an imminent hazard is known. Reports and notices must not be delayed while an investigation is pending.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a statistically valid, documented sample and consistent test protocol. The failure rate must not exceed 5% for any model. A model above that limit must be reported to the regulator with its test protocol, sample size, and results, together with a corrective-action plan and retest schedule; it may not be newly offered for sale after 12 months unless it passes a retest or the regulator approves an alternative risk-control plan. Affected customers must receive material safety information and any required precautions. Manufacturers must provide the model’s per-model failure rate and test results to regulators and customers who own or are considering purchasing that model. These results need not be disclosed to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on new units placed on the market beginning three years after this standard takes effect. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance, where technically feasible, within six years after the effective date. Retrofit work must be performed safely and customers must be notified of the schedule and any interim precautions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The requirement applies to new units only and does not require retrofitting existing units. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions for the suppression system and disclose any required inspection or maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices, operating and emergency instructions, and known model-specific hazards before purchase and upon material updates. A manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and regulators when a safety defect or incident pattern may create a risk, explain interim precautions, and provide repair or replacement instructions. A recall is required when a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units in a year, calculated using a consistent regulator-defined method and an adequate, disclosed denominator. The manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers and submit a corrective-action and remedy plan. A stop-sale or other market restriction must be determined by the regulator based on the risk and adequacy of corrective action; a threshold exceedance alone does not automatically ban a model.
