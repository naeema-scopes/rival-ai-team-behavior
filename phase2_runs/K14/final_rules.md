# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K14 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents initially reported by customers or third parties. A preliminary report may be updated as facts are confirmed. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk for their model, and provide material updates promptly. Annual reports must include incident counts and installed-unit denominators by model, submitted to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after material design changes, using a statistically representative sample and a documented protocol. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt notice to the regulator, a corrective-action plan, and retesting within a regulator-approved period. Regulators may restrict or suspend sale where the evidence indicates an imminent or serious safety risk, or where corrective action is not completed on time. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results to the regulator and provide those results, including the model’s failure rate, to customers who buy that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. Regulators may impose proportionate additional measures based on the model’s risk and corrective-action progress.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off, with appropriate safeguards against unauthorized activation. For already-installed units that do not support the function, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within six years of this standard taking effect. Where a safe retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and the regulator and provide an approved alternative safety measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must document that the system is suitable for the battery’s hazards and provide installation, maintenance, and customer safety instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific safety information, including known material hazards, emergency instructions, material safety findings, and complete per-model 55 °C test results, including the failure rate. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model test results and annual thermal-incident rates, with installed-unit denominators, to regulators and promptly notify affected customers of material safety findings, providing updates as information changes. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, calculated using all known and substantiated incidents and the installed-unit denominator, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit a corrective-action plan. Regulators must promptly assess the model and determine proportionate measures, which may include a recall, based on the evidence and the effectiveness of corrective action. Regulators may require corrective action or a recall below the threshold when evidence indicates an imminent or serious safety risk.
