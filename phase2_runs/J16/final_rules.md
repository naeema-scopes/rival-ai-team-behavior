# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J16 · variant J · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 58})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a detailed report within 10 business days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within 10 business days of determining that customer action is warranted. Reports must include known circumstances, affected models and serial ranges, and corrective actions; regulators may request follow-up information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized 55 °C sustained-heat test before market launch and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers must provide regulators confidential numerical per-model results, including model, test date, sample size, and failure rate. Customers purchasing a model must receive its numerical per-model failure rate directly and privately, along with clear, actionable safety information; numerical results must not be publicly disclosed. The maximum permitted failure rate is 8% for any model. A model exceeding the limit must undergo prompt investigation and corrective action, with regulator-approved retesting; regulators may impose proportionate sales restrictions based on demonstrated risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models introduced 3 years after adoption of this standard must include remote emergency shut-off. For units installed before that date, manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway and complete required retrofits within 6 years of adoption, prioritizing units with known safety risks. Regulators may approve an alternative where retrofit is technically infeasible, provided equivalent risk mitigation is offered.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence that the suppression system is effective and does not introduce other material safety risks.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear installation, operation, emergency-response, and maintenance instructions, plus prompt safety notices explaining known hazards and recommended actions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers and initiate a mandatory recall of that model. Manufacturers must provide incident data and a corrective-action plan and cooperate with expedited regulator oversight.
