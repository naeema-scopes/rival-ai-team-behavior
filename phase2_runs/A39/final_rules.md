# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A39 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of becoming aware of it. An incident involving death, hospitalization, fire spreading beyond the unit, or an immediate risk to public safety shall be reported to the regulator within 24 hours. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 48 hours of such a serious incident and within 5 business days for other reportable thermal incidents, including known hazards and recommended protective steps. Reports shall include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and response taken. Updates shall be provided as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample. Manufacturers shall provide per-model results to the regulator and to customers who purchase that model; individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires regulator notification and a corrective-action plan within 60 days, including a retest schedule. A model may remain on the market during corrective action unless the regulator determines that the results or other evidence indicate an urgent risk; manufacturers shall follow any interim safeguards required by the regulator and retest within the approved schedule. A model that fails to meet the 5% limit after the approved corrective-action period shall not be newly marketed until it passes a retest at or below 5%.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units placed on the market shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide safe, authenticated operation and clear instructions to customers and emergency responders. Existing installed units that lack this capability shall be upgraded, replaced, or otherwise brought into compliance within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible; manufacturers shall notify affected customers of the plan and any interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document that the system is compatible with the battery and does not create additional hazards, and shall provide customers and emergency responders with instructions. Before the effective date, manufacturers shall assess feasible suppression options and provide customers with interim fire-safety guidance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices describing known hazards, warning signs, protective steps, and how to contact the manufacturer. When any model exceeds 8 confirmed thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units over a rolling 12-month period, manufacturers shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit a documented corrective-action plan. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 10 confirmed thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units over a rolling 12-month period. Manufacturers shall use a consistently applied incident-counting method, explain remedies, and provide status updates. Regulators may require a recall or other corrective action below the mandatory threshold where incident severity or other evidence indicates urgent risk.
