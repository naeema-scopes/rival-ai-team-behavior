# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D27 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or suspected thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents involving injury, property damage, or potential risk to neighbours. A preliminary report may be updated as facts become available. Within 5 calendar days, manufacturers must provide a complete incident report or explain what information remains under investigation. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours of learning that an incident indicates a potential safety risk to their unit or model, with immediate notice where continued use presents an imminent danger. Notices must state known hazards and protective steps, and be updated when material facts change.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. The maximum allowed failure rate is 8% for any model. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model test results to the relevant regulator and disclose the model’s failure rate to purchasers before sale; public reporting of individual-company per-model failure rates is not permitted, and public reporting must be aggregate-only. Results must be retained for audit. A model exceeding the limit must be reported promptly to the regulator with a corrective-action plan and retested after corrective action. Exceeding the limit requires regulatory review and corrective action; it does not by itself require automatic market exclusion. Regulators may impose proportionate sales restrictions where needed to address an identified safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support this capability within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible; if retrofit is not feasible, they must notify affected customers and provide an equally effective mitigation approved by the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date, using a regulator-approved design and test protocol.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide each purchaser, before sale, a safety notice stating the model’s 55 °C sustained-heat test failure rate, known material thermal hazards, required installation and maintenance, emergency actions, and how to report an incident. They must promptly notify current owners of material safety information or changed risk assessments, with immediate notice of imminent danger, and provide updated protective instructions. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model’s thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. Manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop sales, and implement a regulator-approved recall and remedy; notices must explain the risk, actions customers should take, and available repair, replacement, or refund. Rate calculations and supporting incident and installed-unit data must be submitted to the regulator for audit. A recall does not replace the reporting duties in S1.
