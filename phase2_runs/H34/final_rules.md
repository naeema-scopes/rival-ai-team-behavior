# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H34 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 48 hours of learning of it, including incidents involving injury, property damage, or emergency response. They shall notify affected customers within 5 days when an incident indicates a material safety risk or requires customer action. Reports shall include the incident date, product model, known consequences, and any immediate corrective steps. Manufacturers shall provide regulators an annual summary of thermal incidents and corrective actions.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5%. A model exceeding 5% shall promptly submit a corrective-action plan and undergo retesting. For a model already on the market when this standard is adopted, the regulator may approve continued sale during a time-limited remediation period of up to 5 years, subject to interim safeguards, milestones, and periodic review; continued sale is not permitted where the regulator finds an imminent serious risk. New models exceeding 5% may not enter the market. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model results, including measured failure rates, to the relevant regulator and disclose the actual result to customers who buy that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public. Regulators may require sales restrictions or other corrective action if remediation is inadequate or a serious risk exists.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery storage units manufactured 12 months or more after adoption of this standard shall include remote emergency shut-off meeting regulator-approved performance and cybersecurity requirements. Manufacturers shall provide instructions for safe use of the feature. For units already installed when the standard is adopted, manufacturers shall assess retrofit feasibility and provide remote shut-off capability within 6 years where technically and safely feasible. Where retrofit is not feasible, manufacturers shall notify affected customers and the regulator, explain the limitation, and provide safe-use guidance and any available alternative protective measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery storage units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard shall include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance requirements. Manufacturers shall disclose the suppression system’s limitations and maintenance requirements to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety notices describing known hazards, required precautions, and any corrective actions, promptly after the manufacturer or regulator determines the information is material to safe use. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period shall trigger prompt regulator review and a required corrective-action plan. It shall also trigger a mandatory recall, unless the regulator, based on a documented model-specific safety assessment, determines that a specified alternative corrective action provides equivalent protection and orders that action instead. Any exception shall be time-limited, subject to interim safeguards and periodic review, and shall not limit the regulator’s authority to order a recall at any time. The regulator may require a recall at any rate where it determines an imminent serious risk exists. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers and regulators promptly and provide the ordered remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund.
