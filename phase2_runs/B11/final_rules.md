# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B11 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
**Incident reporting.** Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, or other thermal event causing injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of learning of it. A preliminary report must identify the event, known consequences, and affected product; an updated report is due within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an event presents a credible safety risk requiring customer action. Reports must include all qualifying events, including events initially attributed to another cause, and be corrected promptly if new information emerges.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
**Thermal testing.** Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry, at least every three years thereafter, and after a material design change. A model’s failure rate must not exceed 5%. For models already on the market when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator within six months and demonstrate compliance within 36 months. During this transition, manufacturers must provide periodic progress reports and implement interim risk controls specified by the regulator. The regulator may restrict distribution before the compliance deadline where evidence indicates an urgent safety risk. New models must meet the 5% limit before market entry. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, sample size, protocol, and corrective actions to the regulator and customers purchasing that model before purchase. Results must not be withheld from those customers; regulators may publish aggregated results, but not company-specific per-model failure rates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
**Remote emergency shut-off.** New units placed on the market must support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete technically feasible retrofits within six years of this standard taking effect. Where retrofit is not feasible, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and provide an equivalent risk-mitigation measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
**Built-in fire suppression.** New units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The system must be designed and tested to activate safely during a thermal event, with installation, maintenance, and end-of-life instructions provided to customers. Before the effective date, manufacturers must disclose to customers whether each model includes built-in fire suppression and provide applicable fire-response instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
**Customer disclosure and recalls.** Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific safety information, including applicable safety limitations, installation and maintenance instructions, emergency-response guidance, and required risk mitigations. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of new safety risks and provide clear protective actions; urgent notices requiring immediate action must be sent without undue delay. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, calculated using reported and subsequently substantiated incidents, a mandatory recall is triggered. The manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit a risk assessment and corrective-action plan. The regulator shall oversee the recall’s scope and timing, including appropriate protective measures.
