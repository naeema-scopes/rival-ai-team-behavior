# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A17 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, or other incident involving injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. Reports must include known unit/model and incident details, with updates as facts are confirmed. Within 30 days of adoption, manufacturers must also report to the relevant regulator any known thermal incidents from the prior 12 months that were not previously reported. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk requiring action; otherwise, include relevant incident trends in the next scheduled safety notice. Reporting is required even when the cause is not yet known.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples before initial sale, after a material model change, and at least annually. Manufacturers must provide results, including sample size and failure rate, to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model; per-model results need not be published publicly. A failure rate above 5% requires a corrective-action plan and interim risk controls submitted to the regulator within 30 days, followed by remediation and retesting within 12 months. Existing models may remain on sale during that period subject to regulator oversight and any interim restrictions the regulator considers necessary. At retest, a model above 5% may not be offered for sale until it meets the maximum. New models must meet the 5% maximum before initial sale. Follow-up results must be provided to the regulator and affected customers.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must provide a plan to enable the capability on compatible installed units, and complete deployment within 72 months of adoption. Where a unit cannot be upgraded, the manufacturer must notify its owner and the relevant regulator and provide an equivalent safety measure or replacement plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be appropriate to the unit’s battery chemistry and validated not to create additional hazards. Manufacturers must document its performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to customers and the relevant regulator.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices for their model, including known hazards, safe installation and use, maintenance, and any action customers should take. Notify affected customers promptly when a material safety risk requires action, and provide updates as information changes. A manufacturer must initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Regulators may also require a recall where incident evidence indicates an imminent or otherwise material safety risk.
