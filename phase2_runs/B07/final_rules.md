# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B07 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, or suspected battery failure to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and notify affected customers within 72 hours when the incident indicates a material safety risk. Other overheating or thermal-protection incidents shall be reported to regulators within five business days. Reports shall include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and immediate mitigation; manufacturers shall update reports as facts are confirmed. Regulators shall receive annual model-level incident totals and installed-unit denominators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Results shall be submitted confidentially to regulators. A failure rate above 5% requires a corrective-action plan and a passing retest within 90 days; the model may remain on sale during that period unless the regulator determines an immediate sales suspension is necessary. A model that fails to achieve a rate of 5% or less on retest may not be sold until it passes. Manufacturers shall provide the per-model result and material corrective-action information to customers who own or are offered that model; individual-company results shall not be published for the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 2 years after this standard takes effect. Existing installed systems shall be retrofitted or otherwise brought into compliance within 6 years, where technically feasible. Manufacturers shall provide customers clear instructions on activation and explain any limitations; shut-off capability must not replace required local safety protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured for sale shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers shall document suppression performance and provide installation, maintenance, and limitations information to customers. This requirement does not replace other required fire-safety protections.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material model-specific thermal risks and any safety action, with clear instructions and updates as information changes. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year shall trigger regulator review, customer notice, and a manufacturer corrective-action and mitigation plan. Regulators shall assess the evidence and may require a recall where a serious risk cannot be adequately controlled; a recall is mandatory when the regulator determines continued use presents an unacceptable risk or required corrective action has failed. Annual model-level incident rates and actions shall be reported to regulators.
