# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E06 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, followed by a written incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and promptly provide any immediate protective instructions. Reports must include known unit and model identifiers, incident circumstances, and actions taken. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and disclose complete incident counts when requested by regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Before sale and at least annually, each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, methods, and corrective actions to the relevant regulator. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public; before purchase, customers must receive the model’s latest results and applicable safety information. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5%. A model exceeding 5% must promptly undergo corrective action and retesting under regulator oversight. Exceeding the limit alone does not require stopping new sales: the regulator must assess demonstrated safety risk and may approve a time-limited corrective-action plan and impose proportionate interim restrictions. It may prohibit continued marketing only where it determines, based on demonstrated risk, that this is necessary to protect safety.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed units must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with clear customer instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway for units already installed and complete retrofits within 6 years of this section taking effect. Where a retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must notify the owner and regulator, provide appropriate risk-reduction instructions, and offer a practical remedy.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression designed to limit fire propagation and protect occupants and neighbouring properties. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document suppression design and validation testing and provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s latest thermal-test results, applicable safety instructions, and clear information on incident reporting and emergency response. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide protective instructions; they must issue a safety notice and corrective-action plan when a model presents a material risk. A manufacturer must initiate a regulator-supervised recall when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year. The manufacturer must report the trigger and proposed recall actions to the regulator without delay and notify affected customers within 5 business days.
