# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A47 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written follow-up within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their units, and provide updates as material facts emerge. Reports must include known incident circumstances and model identification; suspected incidents may be updated as investigations proceed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a common, regulator-approved protocol, a representative sample, and documented failure criteria. Manufacturers must submit per-model failure rates, sample sizes, methods, and test dates confidentially to regulators before sale and after material design changes. Results need not be published to the general public, but customers must be provided the model’s failure rate, sample size, test date, and a plain-language summary of the testing and any material safety implications. A result indicating elevated risk requires prompt regulator review; regulators may require corrective action, additional testing, sales restrictions, or other measures based on test results, incident data, and other relevant safety evidence. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers of material safety risks and any required corrective action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required for all newly manufactured units beginning 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to support remote emergency shut-off, where technically feasible, within 72 months after adoption, and report progress and any infeasibility to regulators. The shut-off capability must be secure, operable by the manufacturer during an emergency, and must not prevent the customer from contacting emergency services or safely isolating the unit locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and enclosure, and be validated through the regulator-approved safety testing process. Manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and limitations information to customers and regulators.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety instructions, known material risks, and prompt notices of incidents or defects that could affect safe operation of their units. A rate exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year for any model must trigger prompt regulator review and a public explanation of the regulator’s determination. The rate is a review threshold, not an automatic recall trigger; regulators must require a recall when the evidence shows an unreasonable safety risk or a serious safety defect warrants recall. The threshold applies per model, not to pooled company-wide rates. Recall notices must promptly explain the hazard, affected units, and remedy, and be sent directly to affected customers and relevant regulators.
