# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J43 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any fire, thermal runaway, or overheating event causing injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written update within 10 days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 30 days. Affected customers must be notified within 72 hours when a reported event indicates a safety risk requiring action; otherwise, include the information in the next routine safety notice.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and at least annually thereafter. Results above 5% require prompt notification to the regulator and a corrective-action plan, with confirmatory testing where appropriate. The maximum allowed failure rate is 7% of tested units for any model. Results and test methods must be provided to the relevant regulator and made available to customers on request; individual-company per-model failure rates are not required to be published to the general public. Regulators may require confirmatory testing or further corrective action where results indicate a material safety concern.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include remote emergency shut-off capability beginning two years after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide a safe retrofit or equivalent upgrade for installed units within six years, prioritizing units with known safety issues. Shut-off must not prevent emergency services or occupants from disconnecting a unit locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Suppression systems must be tested for compatibility with the battery and must not create additional hazards during normal operation or emergency response. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive safety notices promptly when a confirmed incident involving their model indicates a potential hazard, including recommended precautions and any required service or shutdown steps. Manufacturers must provide customers with per-model thermal-test results and test methods, and notify affected customers of material changes to those results. When a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit a corrective-action plan. The regulator must review the evidence and may require additional testing, service actions, or an earlier recall. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 10 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when the regulator determines that a serious hazard otherwise warrants recall. Manufacturers must notify the regulator and affected customers without undue delay when a recall is initiated.
