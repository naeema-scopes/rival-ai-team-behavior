# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D31 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written incident report within 10 days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their unit or model, and provide immediate safety instructions. Reports must include known incident counts, installed-unit denominators, and corrective actions; updates are required as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a documented sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. The 5% failure rate is a safety benchmark, not an automatic sales-eligibility limit. A model exceeding it must promptly undergo root-cause analysis, corrective action, and regulator-supervised retesting on a defined schedule. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective-action plans to the regulator, and disclose the exact per-model results to current and prospective customers of that model. Exceeding 5% alone does not prohibit sales; the regulator may impose proportionate interim safeguards or sales restrictions based on evidence of risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off by 1 January 2028, with safeguards against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to support remote shut-off within six years of this standard’s effective date where technically feasible, prioritizing higher-risk units. If retrofit is infeasible, the manufacturer must provide an equally effective mitigation plan to the regulator and affected customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard’s adoption. The regulator may approve an equivalent or stronger fire-mitigation design where the manufacturer demonstrates that it provides comparable protection to households and neighbours.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices promptly when new information indicates a credible risk, including the affected model, risk, immediate precautions, and available remedies. Incident rates must use a documented, consistently applied method and current installed-unit denominators, with supporting records available to the regulator. The regulator may promptly verify the calculation; verification must not delay interim protective measures or required customer safety notices. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when the verified annual rate for any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units. The recall must provide affected customers with appropriate repair, replacement, or refund instructions and must be proportionate to the identified risk. A recall under this section does not by itself prohibit future sales of the model; any sales restriction requires a separate, evidence-based regulator determination. Manufacturers must notify affected customers and the regulator without delay, and report recall progress and incident-rate updates. Required notices and remedies must not be delayed while an investigation is ongoing.
