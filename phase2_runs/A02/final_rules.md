# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A02 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving a home battery—including overheating, smoke, fire, injury, emergency-service response, or property damage—to the relevant safety regulator within 24 hours after learning of it, and provide a written incident report within 10 calendar days. They must notify the affected customer promptly, and no later than 24 hours after learning of an incident presenting an ongoing safety risk; otherwise, notify the affected customer within 5 calendar days. Reports must include known model, unit, event, and injury details, with updates as facts become available. Manufacturers must maintain records of all thermal incidents and report aggregate annual counts to the regulator.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. A model complies only if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must provide per-model results and the testing protocol to the relevant safety regulator, and disclose actual per-model failure rates, test results, and material safety findings to customers considering or owning that model. Per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. Underlying test records must be retained and provided to regulators on request. Regulators should review the limit and available test evidence after three years. For a model already on sale when this standard is adopted that exceeds the 5% limit, the manufacturer may seek a transition period of up to 18 months from adoption. Any transition requires prior regulator approval of a corrective-action plan and retesting schedule, does not limit regulator access or required safety notices, and ends earlier if the model meets the limit.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide instructions for its safe use. For units already installed when the standard is adopted, manufacturers must provide remote shut-off capability through retrofit or replacement within 6 years, where technically feasible, and report implementation progress annually to the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression design and its validation in the product safety materials. Existing installed units are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear, model-specific safety notices about known thermal risks, relevant test results, and any recommended precautions or corrective actions. Notices must be sent promptly when new information indicates a material safety risk, and before sale for information relevant to prospective purchasers. A manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers promptly when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. A confirmed exceedance triggers a mandatory recall of that model; the manufacturer must provide a corrective-action plan and recall progress updates to the regulator.
