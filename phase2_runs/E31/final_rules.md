# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E31 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of becoming aware of it, and provide a material update within 30 days. A death, serious injury, evacuation, or fire spreading beyond the battery installation must be reported to the regulator within 24 hours. Customers whose units are implicated must be notified promptly, and no later than 5 business days after the manufacturer determines that a safety action or precaution is warranted. Reports must include known circumstances and follow-up findings; uncertainty is not grounds to delay an initial report.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model, with compliance required within 3 years of this standard taking effect. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, including numerical failure rate, sample size, and methodology, to the regulator and to customers who buy that model. These company-specific per-model results must not otherwise be publicly disclosed; regulators may publish anonymized or aggregated information that does not identify a company or model. Customers must also receive relevant hazard information, precautions, and corrective actions. Where results exceed the limit, the regulator shall assess the risk and may require a corrective-action plan, interim risk controls, and retesting, with a reasonable opportunity to demonstrate effective mitigation. Exceeding the limit alone does not require automatic market withdrawal; withdrawal may be required only where necessary to address an inadequately mitigated safety risk. The parties will review the limit after 3 years using incident and testing evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market 2 years after this standard takes effect must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a documented local shut-off method. Manufacturers must provide a plan to retrofit compatible installed units, prioritizing higher-risk units; retrofit completion for units selected for retrofit is due within 6 years of the standard taking effect. Where retrofit is technically infeasible, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and provide an equivalent risk-reduction measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance and reliability requirements. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices when a known hazard may affect their unit, including the hazard, recommended precautions, and available corrective action. Notices must be sent promptly and no later than 5 business days after the manufacturer determines action or precautions are warranted; urgent risks requiring immediate action must be communicated without delay. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, assessed per model rather than across the manufacturer’s fleet, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, provide supporting data and a risk assessment, and initiate a recall. The recall must include prompt customer notification, instructions for safe use or shutdown, and an effective remedy. The regulator shall oversee the recall and may require interim protections while it is implemented. Per-model incident data and risk assessments must be provided to the regulator; customer notices must communicate relevant hazards, precautions, and corrective actions.
