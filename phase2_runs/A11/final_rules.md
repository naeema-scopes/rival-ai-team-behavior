# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A11 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, followed by a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. They must promptly preserve relevant evidence and provide material updates. Affected customers must be notified within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to them or requires inspection, service, or a change in use; notices must describe the hazard and protective steps. Reports must include incident counts and installed-unit denominators by model.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a sample size sufficient to produce a statistically meaningful failure-rate estimate. Before sale and after material design changes, manufacturers must submit the protocol, sample size, numerical model-specific failure-rate estimate, and supporting results to the relevant regulator, and retain records for inspection. Before purchase, customers must receive the numerical failure-rate estimate and a meaningful explanation of the test outcome and its safety implications, together with applicable safety information, protective actions, and safeguards. Numerical per-model failure-rate estimates and supporting results must be available to regulators and to customers considering or owning that model; they need not be released to the general public. Regulators may publish aggregated results that do not identify a company or model. A failure rate above 5% triggers regulator review and a corrective-action plan with a specified completion timeline; it does not by itself prohibit sale. Regulators may impose proportionate interim safeguards or restrict a model when evidence shows an unacceptable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or relevant emergency authority. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation. Units already installed when this standard is adopted must be brought into compliance within 6 years, where technically feasible; otherwise, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and provide an approved equivalent protective measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide that information to customers and regulators.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide buyers, before purchase and at installation, clear safety instructions, warning signs, emergency steps, maintenance requirements, and available safety features. They must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material hazards and provide updates on corrective actions. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; regulators may also require corrective action or recall where incident severity or other evidence indicates an imminent risk, regardless of the rate. Recall notices must explain the hazard, affected units, required actions, and available remedy.
