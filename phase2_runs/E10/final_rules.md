# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E10 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every known thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary incident report. They shall submit an investigation and corrective-action update within 10 business days, or explain why additional time is needed. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers within 24 hours when an incident indicates a potential material safety risk, and otherwise within five business days. Reports shall identify the model and known circumstances, describe immediate precautions, and be updated as facts are confirmed. Manufacturers shall maintain records of all thermal incidents and report aggregate annual totals to the regulator.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. Manufacturers shall provide test methods, sample sizes, and per-model failure rates to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy or own that model. A rate above 5% requires prompt notice to the regulator and affected customers and a corrective-action and retesting plan within 30 days. The regulator shall assess the evidence and risk and determine proportionate corrective action, retesting, and any interim sales safeguards; exceeding 5% does not by itself require an automatic ban. Retesting is required after material design changes and at least every two years. Regulators may require additional measures based on evidence of serious risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market beginning three years after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off, subject to safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall provide a plan to retrofit already-installed units where technically feasible, with completion no earlier than six years after adoption. Where retrofit is not feasible, manufacturers shall provide affected customers with practical alternative safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement shall use a regulator-approved performance standard and allow equivalent designs that provide comparable protection. Manufacturers shall provide customers clear instructions about system limitations, maintenance, and emergency response.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and throughout ownership, customers shall receive clear safety information identifying the model, material thermal hazards, required installation and operating precautions, and how to report an incident or obtain urgent assistance. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and provide practical mitigation instructions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a calendar year, based on the best available model-specific data, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and provide supporting data. Exceeding this threshold triggers a mandatory recall of that model, with prompt implementation and affected-customer notice. Any serious or imminent risk requires immediate protective action.
