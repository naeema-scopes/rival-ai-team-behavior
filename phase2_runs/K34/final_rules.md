# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K34 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency service response to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written incident report within 10 business days. A manufacturer shall notify potentially affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their model or batch, and promptly provide interim safety instructions. Reports shall include known model, unit, and incident details; updates shall be submitted as the investigation develops.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. A failure rate above 5% triggers retesting and a corrective-action plan; it is not, by itself, a market-eligibility limit or automatic prohibition on sale, without prejudice to any regulator’s safety powers. The manufacturer shall submit the plan and follow-up results to the responsible regulator within 90 days. Per-model results and corrective-action plans shall be provided to the responsible regulator and made available to customers buying that model, including prospective customers before purchase. Individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be publicly published; regulators may publish aggregate results and share model-level results where necessary for public safety.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units that lack this capability, or provide an equivalent regulator-approved mitigation, within 6 years of adoption. The shut-off shall be operable by the manufacturer and authorized emergency responders, with safeguards against unauthorized activation; customers shall receive clear instructions on its operation and limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers shall provide regulators with evidence that the suppression system is appropriate for the battery chemistry and does not create additional hazards; customer instructions shall explain its operation and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, warning signs, safe-use and emergency instructions, and any applicable corrective action; notices shall be provided promptly when a credible model- or batch-specific safety risk is identified. A manufacturer shall initiate a mandatory recall when regulator-verified data show that any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Incident attribution, the reporting period, and the installed-unit denominator shall be determined using a consistent regulator-approved methodology. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers and the responsible regulator promptly, provide recall instructions and progress updates, and report completion.
