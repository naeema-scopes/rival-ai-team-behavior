# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D17 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk or requires inspection, mitigation, or product action. Reports must include known incidents whether or not they caused injury.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C. The failure rate must not exceed 5% for any model, except where the regulator grants a time-limited, model-specific variance after reviewing independent test results and a documented corrective-action plan. A variance must specify interim safeguards, customer notices, milestones, and a short expiration date; it may be renewed only after review of progress and updated evidence. Before placing a model on the market, and annually thereafter, manufacturers must submit per-model test methods and results to the regulator for review. Manufacturers must provide the per-model test results, including the failure rate, to customers considering or purchasing that model; results may not be withheld from those customers as confidential. Individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery models placed on the market 12 months or more after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must enable the function and provide operating instructions to customers. For installed units, manufacturers must provide a remote-shutoff retrofit or equivalent compliant upgrade within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible; any units not upgraded must be identified to their customers and the regulator with interim risk-mitigation instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression meeting the applicable performance requirements. Manufacturers must provide customers and regulators with evidence of compliance. This requirement does not remove the obligation to report incidents or take corrective action before that date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices promptly when a known incident or test result indicates a material risk or requires inspection, mitigation, or product action; notices must state the risk, affected models, and steps and timelines for customers. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. Manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop distribution of the affected model pending regulatory direction, and carry out the regulator-approved recall or corrective action. Notices must not conceal material model-level risks.
