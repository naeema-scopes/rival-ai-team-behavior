# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J48 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, overheating requiring emergency response, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, followed by a complete report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 5 business days after determining that the incident may indicate a safety risk. Reports must include known incident counts and corrective actions; suspected incidents must not be omitted pending final investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results to the regulator and provide those results to customers who buy that model. Regulators may publish aggregate results, but not individual-company per-model failure rates beyond what is provided to that model’s customers. A model exceeding 5% must promptly undergo regulator-directed corrective action, additional testing, and enhanced incident monitoring. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sale or require withdrawal; the regulator may impose restrictions, including pausing new sales, when the test results and other evidence indicate an unacceptable safety risk. Models at or below 5% remain subject to applicable reporting and regulator-directed measures.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must support remote emergency shut-off from the date of adoption. For already-installed units, manufacturers must provide an upgrade or replacement path and make commercially reasonable progress toward enabling remote shut-off, with a deadline of six years after adoption for technically compatible units. Customers must be notified of shut-off availability and how to use it; shut-off must not replace required physical safety measures or emergency response.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system’s capabilities, limitations, and maintenance requirements to customers. Fire suppression must not replace required thermal testing, incident reporting, or other safety measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices describing known risks, safe-use instructions, relevant corrective actions, and support contact details. Notices must be delivered promptly when a material safety risk is identified. Manufacturers must provide per-model thermal test results to the regulator and to customers who buy that model. A manufacturer must initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on model-specific rates. It must notify regulators and affected customers promptly and provide a remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund. This trigger does not limit regulators’ authority to require earlier action.
