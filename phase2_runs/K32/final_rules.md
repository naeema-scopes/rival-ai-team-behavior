# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K32 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 5 business days. They must promptly notify affected customers of an incident that may present a safety risk, and no later than 24 hours after notifying the regulator. Reports must include the model, unit identification where available, incident circumstances, known injuries or damage, and actions taken. Manufacturers must also submit an annual accounting of thermal incidents, including incidents not previously reported, and explain any reporting gaps.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for every model. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and supporting records to the regulator and provide results directly to customers who buy or are considering that model; individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. For models already on the market when this standard takes effect, manufacturers have six months to complete initial testing and submit results, and must provide a testing schedule to the regulator. A model found above the limit must be reported promptly to the regulator and affected customers, and the manufacturer must submit corrective-action milestones and retest at least every six months. Manufacturers have 36 months from the standard’s effective date to bring existing models into compliance before they may no longer be newly placed on the market. Regulators may require earlier sales suspension or other measures where incident evidence indicates an imminent serious risk. Manufacturers must implement regulator-approved corrective actions for units already installed.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market beginning two years after this standard takes effect must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for its use and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed when the standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or replacement pathway and complete required upgrades within six years, prioritizing units presenting the greatest documented risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence that the suppression system is effective and does not introduce additional material safety risks.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear information on material battery hazards, safe installation and operation, emergency response, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of safety risks and provide updates on corrective actions. For each model, the annual thermal-incident rate must be calculated as qualifying thermal incidents involving that model during the calendar year divided by that model’s installed units at year-end, multiplied by 100,000. Count incidents when they occur, including incidents reported late; include late-reported incidents in the next submission and correct prior calculations when needed. Manufacturers must keep auditable incident and installed-unit records and submit annual calculations and any corrections to regulators for verification. They must also make each model’s verified annual rate available to current and prospective customers. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop distribution, and implement the regulator-approved recall. Regulators may require a recall below that threshold when evidence indicates an imminent serious risk.
