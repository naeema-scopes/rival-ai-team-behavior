# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K27 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a complete incident report within 30 days. They must notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or household. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial number, and any immediate safety advice. Manufacturers must maintain records of all thermal incidents and promptly correct incomplete or missed reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo an independently witnessed sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved sampling protocol. A model passes only if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model test results and the test protocol to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model, in clear, accessible form. These results are not required to be published to the general public. A model exceeding the limit must promptly undergo corrective-action review and retesting; the manufacturer must notify affected customers of the failure and any immediate safety advice. Exceeding the test limit alone does not automatically require a market ban; regulators may determine appropriate interim measures and timelines based on the model’s risk and corrective-action plan.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models must support secure remote emergency shut-off by 1 January 2028, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a documented manual alternative. For already-installed units, manufacturers must provide compatible shut-off capability through retrofit or equivalent mitigation within 6 years of this standard’s adoption, where technically feasible, and notify customers of availability and safe operation. Units for which retrofit is not feasible must receive a documented alternative risk-mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document that the system is suitable for the unit’s foreseeable fire hazards and provide customers with instructions for its safe operation and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, before purchase and in accessible form, model-specific safety notices including current 55 °C sustained-heat test results, material safety limitations, and required installation, maintenance, and emergency instructions. They must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and provide updated safety advice. A manufacturer must initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, and must promptly notify the relevant regulator and affected customers, explain the risk, and provide a remedy or safe return process. Regulators may require a recall or other corrective action sooner where warranted by the risk.
