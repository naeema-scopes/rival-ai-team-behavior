# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K25 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or household, and promptly update those customers as material facts emerge. Reports must include known incidents and identify any gaps in incident data.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. A model with a failure rate above 7% may not be newly placed on the market. For a model above 5% but no more than 7%, the manufacturer must promptly provide results to regulators and customers purchasing that model, submit a regulator-approved remediation plan, and retest after corrective changes. Individual-company per-model failure rates will not be published to the general public. Results must be retained and updated when a material design change could affect performance.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on all new units placed on the market from 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit path for installed units and complete required retrofits within 6 years of adoption, subject to safe technical feasibility; customers must receive clear instructions on availability and operation. Shut-off must not replace local emergency protections or prevent emergency responders from making a unit safe.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be tested and maintained in accordance with regulator-approved requirements, and must not obstruct emergency response or create a separate undue safety hazard. Manufacturers must disclose the system’s capabilities, limitations, and maintenance requirements to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its per-model 55 °C sustained-heat test methods and results, known material limitations, and instructions for safe installation, operation, and emergency response. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of material new safety information and provide updates as investigations proceed. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents involving overheating or fire per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and submit the relevant incident data. The regulator must review the evidence and may require an immediate sales pause and interim protections. The manufacturer must submit and carry out a time-bound corrective-action plan under regulator oversight. A recall is mandatory if the regulator determines there is an imminent or unmitigated serious risk, or the manufacturer fails to carry out the required plan; affected customers must then be notified promptly and offered an effective remedy or safe decommissioning instructions.
