# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D42 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, with a follow-up report containing the investigation findings within 30 days. They must notify the affected customer within 24 hours of learning of an incident involving that customer’s unit, and promptly provide safety instructions. Incidents must not be omitted because the cause is still under investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with adequate sample sizes and repeat testing after material design changes. Before sale and after any material design change, manufacturers must provide regulators with the test method, sample size, and complete per-model results. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. A rate above 5% requires prompt corrective action, a regulator-reviewed remediation plan, and at least annual retesting. A model above 5% may remain on sale for up to 12 months under regulator-supervised interim safeguards while remediation is completed; it must demonstrate a rate of 5% or less by the end of that period. Regulators may require additional safeguards or earlier suspension where the evidence warrants it. Customers must receive the complete results for the model they buy, along with required corrective actions and safety precautions. Individual-company per-model failure rates are not to be publicly published.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete retrofits within six years of the standard’s effective date where technically feasible, prioritizing units with elevated safety risk. Customers must be informed about the capability, limitations, and any required connectivity.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide these to regulators and customers. Units already installed before the effective date are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale and throughout ownership, customers must receive clear safety information, including model-specific thermal-test results, known hazards, incident reporting and emergency instructions, and any remote shut-off limitations. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of a newly identified material safety risk and provide interim precautions. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; it must notify regulators and affected customers without delay, explain the risk and remedy, and report recall progress to regulators.
