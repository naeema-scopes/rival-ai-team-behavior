# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J25 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the regulator any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written incident report within 10 calendar days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 24 hours when an incident indicates an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within 5 business days, providing known hazards and interim safety instructions. Reports must include the model, incident date, known circumstances, and corrective actions; manufacturers must preserve relevant records and promptly update reports as facts develop.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the regulator and to customers who buy or own that model, in accessible form before purchase and upon request for installed units. Per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires a corrective-action plan submitted to the regulator within 30 days and follow-up testing within 12 months; regulators may require additional safeguards based on the evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit path for compatible installed units and complete required retrofits within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Shut-off capability must not prevent occupants or emergency responders from safely isolating a unit locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and installation conditions, and must not create an unreasonable risk to occupants or emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions and disclose any limitations of the system.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material thermal hazards and provide clear instructions for safe use, including any needed service or software update. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. The rate must be calculated using confirmed thermal incidents and the model’s installed-unit population during that period, with the underlying numerator and denominator documented and provided to the regulator. When the threshold is reached, the manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers promptly and explain the remedy and timeline. The recall must provide an effective safety remedy, such as repair, replacement, or refund as appropriate; reaching the trigger requires a recall but does not by itself automatically prohibit all sales of the model. Regulators retain authority to require additional restrictions based on the evidence.
