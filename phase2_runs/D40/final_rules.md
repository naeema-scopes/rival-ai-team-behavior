# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D40 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete investigation update within 30 days. The affected customer shall be notified within 24 hours of confirmation, or sooner if immediate action is needed to protect people or property. Reports shall include model, incident date and location, known injuries or damage, and any protective action taken. Manufacturers shall reconcile incident records against service and warranty data at least quarterly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a common regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers shall submit per-model sample sizes, methods, and results to regulators and provide the results for each model to customers who buy that model, before purchase where practicable and otherwise promptly after installation. Individual-company per-model results need not be publicly disclosed; any public reporting may be aggregated or anonymized. A model exceeding 5% shall promptly undergo corrective action and retesting, with the regulator determining any interim sales restrictions based on the evidence and risk; exceedance alone does not automatically require a market-wide ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery systems shall support remote emergency shut-off by installation. For already-installed units, manufacturers shall provide retrofit capability or an equivalent effective risk-reduction measure within six years, prioritizing units with elevated risk. Customers must be informed of any interim precautions and how to obtain assistance.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide regulators with implementation plans and may adopt the requirement earlier. Any delay or alternative measure shall not reduce other applicable safety obligations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At sale and after installation, customers shall receive clear safety instructions, emergency contact information, and steps to take for an alarm, overheating, smoke, or other hazard. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified safety risks and recommended actions, and provide updates as investigations develop. When a model’s thermal incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers, investigate, and propose corrective action. Regulators shall promptly assess the evidence and risk and may require a recall or other protective measures; a recall is required when the regulator determines it is necessary to protect customers or the public. Manufacturers shall report the scope and implementation of any recall without delay.
