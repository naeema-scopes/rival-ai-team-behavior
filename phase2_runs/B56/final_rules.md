# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B56 · variant B · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Halcyon': 45})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the regulator. Incidents involving fire, injury, or a credible risk of imminent harm must be reported within 24 hours of discovery; all other thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. Reports must identify the model, incident date and circumstances, and known injuries or damage, and be updated as facts become available. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours of discovering a material safety risk, or sooner where necessary to prevent imminent harm, and provide safety instructions and a contact for questions.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a documented, reproducible protocol and a representative sample. The protocol, sample size, pass/fail criteria, and per-model results must be recorded and provided to the regulator and standards team. Before purchase, customers must receive the actual per-model test results, including the failure rate, as well as known material safety risks and operating guidance. Results must be reviewed at least annually. A model with a failure rate above 5% may not be newly placed on the market; the manufacturer must take corrective action and retest before resuming market placement.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or emergency responders when needed to address an imminent safety risk. Manufacturers must provide a secure, documented activation process and notify affected customers when shut-off is activated. Units installed before this requirement takes effect must be upgraded or replaced to provide the capability within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible; any unit that cannot be upgraded must be disclosed to its owner and the regulator, with a safe alternative or replacement plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s design, maintenance requirements, and validation testing, and provide this information to the regulator and customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s actual 55 °C test results, including its failure rate, as well as its safety status, known material safety risks, and clear instructions for safe installation, operation, and emergency response. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of new material safety information and provide updated instructions. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit supporting incident data. The regulator must assess the evidence and require proportionate corrective action, including a recall where the evidence indicates serious or imminent safety risk. Regulators may also require a recall below this threshold where the evidence indicates serious or imminent safety risk.
