# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J27 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, explosion, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of learning of it. A monthly report shall include all other verified overheating or thermal incidents, including incidents initially attributed to another cause, and shall identify the model, event date, outcome, and installed-unit denominator. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours of learning of an incident that presents a credible ongoing safety risk, with immediate notice where urgent protective action is needed. Corrections and material updates shall be sent promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units before sale and after a material design or manufacturing change. A model passes only if no more than 7% of tested units fail. This interim limit reflects the currently reported model results and shall be reviewed at least every three years using evidence from a broader set of models, validated test protocols, and consultation; any revision shall apply prospectively. The manufacturer shall submit the protocol, sample size, failure rate, and failure modes to the regulator within 30 days of testing and retain supporting records for inspection. Per-model results shall be provided to regulators and to customers considering or owning that model; public reporting shall use aggregate or anonymized results and shall not identify an individual company's per-model failure rate.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market from 24 months after adoption shall support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with safeguards against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. For units already installed on that date, manufacturers shall provide a compatible retrofit or equivalent mitigation within six years, prioritizing units with known faults or elevated risk. If retrofit is infeasible, the manufacturer shall notify affected customers and the regulator and provide an approved alternative safety measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New models placed on the market from 48 months after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the model’s battery chemistry and enclosure. Before that date, manufacturers shall assess suppression and other engineering controls as part of model risk management and document the rationale for the controls selected. Manufacturers shall provide customers clear instructions on emergency response and safe operation. The regulator may approve an equivalent or more effective protective design in place of built-in suppression.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive the model’s latest 55 °C sustained-heat test result, a plain-language summary of material thermal hazards, installation and emergency-response instructions, and any applicable safety notices. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material new safety information and provide updated instructions; urgent protective actions require immediate notice through available channels. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly investigate and report findings and corrective actions to the regulator and affected customers. Exceeding that threshold triggers a mandatory recall unless the regulator determines, on the basis of documented evidence, that an alternative corrective action is equally protective, prompt, enforceable, and will eliminate the unreasonable risk; affected customers shall be notified of that action. The regulator may require a recall at any lower rate where severity or other evidence warrants.
