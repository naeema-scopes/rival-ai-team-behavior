# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D13 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, death, evacuation, or property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller investigation report within 10 business days. Incidents presenting an imminent risk of serious harm must be reported immediately by the fastest available means. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 24 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk to their model or requires customer action. Reports shall include known model, unit, and incident details; manufacturers shall not omit incidents because the cause is not yet confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, and after a material design change or regulator request. Manufacturers shall provide per-model results, including failure rates, to the relevant regulator and to customers buying that model; results may be withheld from the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a corrective-action plan with a defined timetable, and retesting. The regulator may require additional safeguards or restrict sales if risks are not adequately mitigated. A result above 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sales; regulators may consider test uncertainty, incident data, and effective corrective measures.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly manufactured units shall include remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 24 months after this standard takes effect. For units already installed on that date, manufacturers shall provide a compatible retrofit or replacement pathway and complete required retrofits within 6 years, prioritizing units with identified safety risks. If a retrofit is infeasible, the manufacturer shall notify the owner and regulator and provide an effective alternative risk-reduction measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression, or an independently verified equivalent built-in fire-mitigation system, for units manufactured 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers shall provide regulators with evidence of effectiveness and shall not represent a system as equivalent without supporting test results. Existing installed units are not subject to retrofit solely under this section; identified hazards remain subject to incident reporting, customer notification, and corrective action requirements.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At sale and whenever information materially changes, customers shall receive the model’s current 55 °C test status and per-model test results, incident and hazard information relevant to their unit, safe-use instructions, and a clear contact and reporting channel. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers and regulators of material hazards and provide instructions and remedies. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must notify the regulator promptly and initiate a recall or equivalent regulator-approved corrective action, including customer notification and effective risk mitigation. This trigger requires corrective action; it does not by itself impose a permanent market ban.
