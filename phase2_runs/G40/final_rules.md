# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G40 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, emergency response, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and submit a fuller report within 10 business days. The report must include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and initial corrective actions. Manufacturers must notify the affected customer within 24 hours when the incident involves their unit or presents an immediate safety risk; other customers of the same model must be notified within 5 business days when the incident indicates a material model-wide hazard. Reports and notices must not be delayed while the cause is investigated.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a documented, representative sample and a consistent test method. The maximum allowed failure rate per model is 5%. Any model exceeding 5% must be reported to the regulator within 10 business days with supporting data and a corrective-action plan; the regulator must review the data, risk, and available mitigations and may require proportionate corrective action and retesting. Exceeding 5% does not by itself require an automatic sales suspension. Per-model results, including failure rates, must be provided to the regulator and to customers considering or owning that model. They must not be published for the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models introduced 3 years after this standard takes effect must support remote emergency shut-off. For units already installed on that date, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade plan within 2 years and complete implementation within 6 years, prioritizing units with known safety risks. Shut-off capability must not replace required local safety protections or emergency response procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must document that the suppression system is appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation, and must provide installation and maintenance instructions. This requirement does not replace other required local safety protections or emergency response procedures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices describing material hazards, relevant protective actions, and any corrective action or recall, promptly after identifying the hazard. Notices must go to affected customers and owners of the same model when the hazard may affect them; notices must not be delayed while the cause is investigated. When a model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting, model-specific data and its counting method. The regulator must validate the rate and assess the risk and available protective measures. Following that assessment, the regulator may require a recall or other proportionate corrective action. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and provide instructions for any required protective action. Per-model failure rates need not be published for the general public.
