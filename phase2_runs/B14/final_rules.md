# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B14 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator and notify affected customers within 24 hours of learning of an incident involving fire, injury, or an immediate safety risk. Other thermal incidents shall be reported to the regulator and affected customers within five business days. Reports shall include known circumstances, model and serial number, actions taken, and any identified risks. Manufacturers shall promptly update reports when material information changes and retain records of all incidents.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A failure rate above 5% triggers a corrective-action plan submitted to the regulator and affected customers, followed by retesting after corrective action; it does not by itself automatically require market withdrawal. Pending retest, the regulator may impose proportionate interim risk controls, restrict sales where warranted by the evidence, or require additional testing. Manufacturers shall submit complete per-model results to the regulator and provide results, including the model’s failure rate and corrective actions, to customers who buy or own that model. Results shall not be required to be published as company-specific per-model failure rates to the general public. Regulators may require additional testing or risk controls where results indicate a material safety concern.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, when necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under documented procedures. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units with this capability, or provide an equally effective regulator-approved safety measure, within six years of adoption. Shut-off functionality shall not prevent safe local operation or emergency access.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers shall document that the suppression system is compatible with the battery and does not introduce additional safety risks.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear model-specific safety notices, including known material risks, safety implications of relevant test results, and actions to take. Notices shall be sent promptly when a new material risk is identified and updated when information materially changes. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on verified incident data, shall trigger prompt notification to the regulator and a documented safety review. The manufacturer shall submit the review and proposed corrective actions to the regulator and notify affected customers of material risks and recommended actions. A recall is mandatory when the regulator determines, based on the incident data and other relevant safety evidence, that continued use presents an unacceptable risk and lesser corrective measures are inadequate. Manufacturers shall promptly implement any required recall and provide instructions for safe shutdown, repair, or return.
