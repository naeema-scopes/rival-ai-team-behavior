# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D55 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency shutdown to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents learned of after initial sale. They must provide a verified follow-up report within 10 business days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk or requires customer action, and promptly update them as material information becomes available. Reports must include model, serial number where available, event date, and known consequences; suspected incidents must not be omitted pending confirmation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample before sale and after material design changes. The observed failure rate must not exceed 5% for any model. An independent qualified laboratory must conduct or verify testing. Manufacturers must submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, failure rates, and results to the relevant regulator and provide them to the joint standard oversight body. Before purchase, customers must be given the results for the model they are considering; existing customers must receive results for their model within 30 days of verification. If a model exceeds the limit, the manufacturer must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and retest schedule to the regulator. New marketing must pause until corrective action and a passing retest are verified, unless the regulator expressly authorizes a time-limited alternative plan after assessing the risk, requiring interim safeguards, and setting monitoring and retesting conditions. Regulators may require action on installed units based on risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear instructions for customers and emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for an authorized regulator or emergency responder to request shut-off when there is an imminent safety risk, and must log and report activations. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability, or provide an equally effective regulator-approved alternative, within six years after adoption. Customers must be informed of the capability, its limits, and how emergency shut-off requests are handled.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression or an independently verified equivalent protective system. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. The system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation environment, tested to a regulator-approved protocol, and maintained in accordance with clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must disclose the system’s capabilities and limitations and must not represent it as eliminating fire risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear written notice of the product’s thermal and fire risks, required installation and maintenance, emergency steps, available safety features, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers must promptly send affected customers safety notices describing the hazard, urgency, and specific actions to take, and provide updates when instructions change. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period; the regulator must be notified promptly and customers must receive direct notice and an effective remedy. This trigger does not limit a regulator’s authority or a manufacturer’s duty to recall at a lower rate where risk warrants.
