# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H28 · variant H · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 25})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete investigation update within 30 days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and promptly provide any protective instructions. Reports must include incident circumstances, model and lot, and known consequences; personal information must be protected.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least once every three years thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt notification to the relevant regulator, a corrective-action plan, and retesting on a regulator-approved timetable; interim safeguards may be required where warranted. Exceeding 5% does not by itself automatically bar sales. The regulator may require sales restrictions if corrective action or interim safeguards are inadequate. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model test results, including sample size, failure rate, protocol, and corrective actions, to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model. Results must be provided in clear, accessible form with the model’s safety notices; this does not require public publication of individual company per-model failure rates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within 72 months after adoption, where technically feasible, and provide owners with the retrofit schedule and any interim safety instructions. Any unit for which remote shut-off is not technically feasible must be disclosed to its owner and the regulator, with an alternative emergency response plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations to customers and provide installation, maintenance, and emergency-use instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear, timely safety notices describing relevant hazards, protective actions, and how to obtain updates. When a model’s verified rate exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units during a 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, investigate, and submit its findings and corrective-action plan to the regulator. The rate must be calculated using confirmed thermal incidents and the model’s installed-unit population for the same period, with underlying data and calculations provided to the regulator for verification. Once the regulator verifies that the threshold is exceeded, the manufacturer must immediately begin corrective action under a regulator-approved plan. If the regulator finds, after a corrective-action period it sets, that the safety risk remains inadequately controlled, a recall is mandatory; the regulator determines the recall’s scope and implementation. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of any recall, identify affected models and units, explain the hazard and remedy, and provide direct notice and updates.
