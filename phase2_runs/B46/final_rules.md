# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B46 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a documented investigation update within 30 days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers of incidents posing a potential safety risk within 72 hours of determining that their unit or model may be affected, and provide safety instructions and subsequent material updates promptly. Reports shall include available unit, model, location, and incident information; personal information shall be protected.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a common, regulator-approved protocol before sale and at least annually thereafter. For the first five years after adoption, no more than 7% of tested units may fail; thereafter, the maximum failure rate shall be 5%. A model above the applicable limit shall be subject to regulator-supervised corrective action and retesting. For a model already on the market when the 5% limit takes effect, sales may continue under a regulator-approved corrective-action plan while the manufacturer makes documented progress toward compliance, provided the model’s annual thermal-incident rate remains at or below 8 per 100,000 installed units and the regulator finds no imminent or serious risk. Continued sales under such a plan shall have no fixed termination date, subject to at least annual regulator review; the regulator may suspend sales if progress is inadequate or risk increases. Per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results shall be submitted to regulators and provided to customers who own or buy that model, without requiring a request; individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be publicly published.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units that lack this capability within six years of adoption, prioritizing units with elevated risk. The shut-off function shall be tested at installation and at least annually; customers shall receive clear instructions for its use. Shut-off capability shall not replace required physical safety protections or emergency response.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement shall apply to all newly manufactured units placed on the market after that date. Manufacturers shall document suppression-system performance under the common regulator-approved safety protocol and provide those results to regulators and customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers clear safety notices for hazards affecting their model, including the nature of the risk, affected units, immediate precautions, and how to obtain repair, replacement, or support. A manufacturer shall promptly notify affected customers and regulators when a safety defect or incident pattern warrants corrective action. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; the manufacturer shall notify the regulator and affected customers and initiate the recall without delay. Regulators may require a recall at any lower rate where the evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
