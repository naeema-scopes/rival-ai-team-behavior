# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E16 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal runaway, fire, explosion, or event involving emergency venting of a battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a written incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to other units of the same model, including interim precautions and how to obtain support. Reports must include model, serial number, location, event timeline, injuries or damage, and known contributing factors; manufacturers must promptly update reports as facts become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units before sale and after a material design change. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and failures to regulators, which may inspect underlying records. Before purchase, customers must receive the 55 °C test result and failure rate for the model they are considering, with the sample size and test date; results for other models need not be publicly disclosed. A model with a failure rate above 5% must submit a corrective-action plan and undergo retesting at least annually; regulators may restrict sales where the results or other evidence show an unacceptable safety risk. Manufacturers must retain test records.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery systems must support remote emergency shut-off, with secure access for the customer and authorized emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For installed units that lack this capability, manufacturers must complete a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation plan within 6 years of adoption, prioritizing units with known safety risks. Where retrofit is infeasible, manufacturers must notify affected customers and regulators and explain alternative protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must demonstrate through regulator-approved testing that the system is suitable for the battery chemistry and enclosure and does not create additional hazards. Before the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must provide customers with clear information on the fire-protection measures present in their system and any applicable limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific safety instructions, installation and maintenance requirements, emergency response guidance, the model’s 55 °C test result and failure rate, and a clear route to report incidents. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide interim precautions and repair or replacement information. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, provide the model-level data and a corrective-action plan, and initiate a recall. The recall must include prompt customer notice and an effective repair, replacement, or refund remedy. Regulators may require additional protective actions. Manufacturers must notify customers without delay and provide progress updates until the risk is addressed.
