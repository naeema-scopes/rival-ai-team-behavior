# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B50 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account of known facts. They shall submit a complete report within 10 calendar days and promptly update it as material facts emerge. Affected customers shall be notified within 24 hours when an incident presents an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within five calendar days, with clear protective steps and contact information. Reports shall include model, incident date and location, consequences, and known contributing factors.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and at least annually thereafter. A failure rate above 5% shall trigger prompt notice to the regulator and a regulator-supervised investigation and proportionate corrective-action plan, including retesting and a timetable for improvement; it shall not by itself require immediate market withdrawal. Manufacturers shall provide per-model test methods, sample sizes, and numerical results to the relevant regulator. Customers considering or purchasing a model shall receive a clear safety summary explaining the testing standard, whether the model met it, any applicable protective instructions, and any corrective actions. Individual-company per-model results need not be disclosed to the general public. Results and corrective actions shall be reviewed by the regulator.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide a plan to enable this capability on compatible installed systems, with completion no sooner than six years after this standard takes effect; units that cannot be retrofitted shall be identified to owners and the regulator, with safe alternatives specified.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Manufacturers shall assess fire-suppression measures as part of the safety design for new home battery models. Built-in fire suppression shall be required on new units manufactured beginning three years after this standard takes effect, subject to regulator-approved equivalent safety measures where appropriate.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety and emergency-response instructions at sale and prompt notice of material new hazards, safety updates, or corrective actions. Manufacturers shall notify regulators of thermal incidents and provide model-level incident data. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, provide protective instructions, and submit incident and remedy information for review. The regulator shall promptly assess the risk, considering incident severity and trend, and determine whether a recall or other corrective action is required; any required action shall include a timetable for remedy or replacement.
