# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J09 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency shut-down, within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller initial investigation report within 10 calendar days. Reports shall include available model, serial-number, installation, and event details; updates shall be provided as the investigation progresses. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers when an incident indicates a safety risk or requires inspection, changed use, or corrective action, and in all cases within 5 calendar days of determining that such action is warranted. Suspected serious or imminent risks require immediate regulator and customer notification. Reporting obligations apply even when cause is uncertain.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a statistically adequate sample. Manufacturers shall submit per-model failure rates, sample sizes and test methods to the regulator and provide the results to customers who buy or are considering that model. A model with a failure rate above 5% shall trigger regulator notification, a corrective-action plan and retesting within 12 months. Until it meets the limit, the regulator may require risk controls, sales restrictions or other proportionate measures; exceedance does not by itself require automatic permanent market exclusion. Regulators and affected customers shall receive any safety instructions needed to manage an identified risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units manufactured 18 months or more after this standard takes effect shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide a secure, documented shut-off capability and instructions for its use. For units already installed when the standard takes effect, manufacturers shall complete a technically feasible retrofit within 6 years; where retrofit is infeasible, they shall notify affected customers and the regulator, provide an alternative risk-control plan, and implement it promptly.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units manufactured 36 months or more after this standard takes effect shall include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and reliability protocol. Manufacturers shall disclose the system’s capabilities, limitations, maintenance requirements, and any identified risks to customers. Regulators may approve equivalent protective designs where they provide comparable fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive the model-specific 55 °C test results and clear safety, installation, maintenance, and emergency-response instructions. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified safety risks and provide updates on corrective actions. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 units of that model installed during the year. Manufacturers shall promptly report the rate, supporting incident counts, and installed-unit denominator to the regulator; the regulator shall verify the calculation. A recall plan shall be submitted promptly and specify customer notification and timely inspection, repair, replacement, or refund measures. Regulators may oversee implementation and require additional protections. The trigger requires effective corrective action and does not by itself constitute a permanent market ban.
