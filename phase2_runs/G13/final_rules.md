# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G13 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury associated with a covered battery, including incidents initially suspected to have another cause. Notify the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of the incident and provide a complete investigation update within 30 days. Notify the affected customer within 24 hours of learning of an incident involving their unit, with immediate notice when there is an active hazard. Submit quarterly incident totals and rates, including corrected or previously unreported cases, to regulators. Do not delay reporting while investigating.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A failure rate above 5% requires a corrective-action plan and retesting within 12 months; exceeding 5% alone does not prohibit continued sale. A model may not be newly introduced or materially changed above 7% without regulator authorization. Manufacturers must submit each model’s protocol, sample size, failure rate, and results to the regulator before sale, after material design changes, and annually thereafter. Notify existing customers of material changes or safety-relevant results, and provide relevant information to customers before purchase. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be publicly disclosed; aggregate, non-identifying industry statistics may be published. Regulators may require further corrective action or retesting.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery systems must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must provide a safe retrofit for compatible installed units and complete retrofit or an equivalent protective measure for all installed units within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Provide customers clear instructions and disclose any compatibility or safety limitations; remote shut-off must not substitute for other required protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must disclose whether a system includes built-in suppression and provide customers clear instructions on its operation and limitations. This requirement does not replace other safety protections or incident-reporting duties.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, provide customers with model-specific thermal-test results, incident and safety information, and clear instructions for reporting hazards. Notify affected customers promptly of safety-relevant incidents, newly identified risks, corrective actions, and recall instructions; use direct contact and an accessible public notice where a broader population may be affected. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 10 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Regulators may also require a recall or other protective action at any rate where evidence indicates an imminent or serious safety hazard.
