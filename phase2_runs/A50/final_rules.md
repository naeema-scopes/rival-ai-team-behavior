# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A50 · variant A · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 49})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents not resulting in injury or property damage. The report shall identify the model, incident date and location, known outcomes, and any immediate safety actions; missing information shall be supplemented as it becomes available. Within 72 hours, the manufacturer shall notify the affected unit’s owner of known hazards and interim precautions. Manufacturers shall provide regulators a quarterly summary of incidents, including incidents previously reported and any reporting delays.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-recognized protocol and a documented, representative sample. Manufacturers shall provide complete numeric per-model results, sample size, protocol, and corrective-action plans to the relevant regulator on a confidential basis. Before purchase, prospective customers shall receive material safety findings and precautions relevant to safe use, including notice when a model exceeds the applicable failure-rate limit. A failure rate above 5% shall prompt corrective action and confirmatory testing, but shall not by itself require a sales ban. Regulators may restrict sales where warranted by the evidence or an imminent safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months after this standard takes effect shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall retrofit already-installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within six years after the standard takes effect, where technically feasible, and shall notify owners of any units that cannot be retrofitted and provide safe-use guidance.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months after this standard takes effect shall include built-in fire suppression designed and tested to applicable safety requirements. Manufacturers shall provide customers clear instructions on its operation, limitations, and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive material safety findings, relevant incident-rate information, and clear instructions for recognizing and responding to thermal hazards. Manufacturers shall promptly notify owners of newly identified material safety risks, provide interim precautions, and notify customers directly when a recall is initiated. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, investigate, and submit a corrective-action plan. The regulator shall determine whether a recall is required based on the incident evidence and risk; any recall shall be communicated without delay and include a repair, replacement, or refund plan.
