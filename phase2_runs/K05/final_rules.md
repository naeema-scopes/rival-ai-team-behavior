# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K05 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any fire, thermal runaway, injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response involving a covered battery within 24 hours of becoming aware of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. Initial reports may be updated as facts are verified; a material update is due within 30 days. The manufacturer must notify the affected customer within 24 hours for an incident involving immediate danger or an evacuation, and within 5 business days for any other confirmed thermal incident. Reports must identify the model and provide the known date, circumstances, and consequences. Regulators may require follow-up information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry, at least once every 2 years, and after a material design or manufacturing change. The test plan must use a representative sample and disclose its size and uncertainty. Per-model results, including failure rates, must be submitted to the relevant regulator and disclosed to customers who buy or own that model in clear, accessible form; results may not be withheld from those customers as confidential. Regulators may audit test methods and underlying records. Results must also be made publicly available in aggregate. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator within 60 days and complete follow-up testing within 12 months. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically bar sale; the regulator may restrict or suspend sales if the manufacturer fails to implement an adequate plan or if the model presents an unreasonable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## S3_remote_shutoff
New home battery models placed on the market from 3 years after this standard takes effect must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible shut-off method. Manufacturers must provide instructions to customers and emergency responders. For units already installed when the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must offer a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within 6 years, where technically feasible, and notify owners of availability, limitations, and safe alternatives. No installed unit may be remotely shut off without an immediate safety need or the customer's informed authorization.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units placed on the market 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression designed for the battery’s foreseeable thermal hazards and validated under a regulator-approved test protocol. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers and emergency responders.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices about known hazards, required maintenance, emergency shut-off instructions, and any material safety updates affecting their model. Notices must be sent promptly when a new material risk is identified. Manufacturers must report model-level thermal incident counts and installed-unit denominators to the relevant regulator at least annually and describe their counting methods; regulators may audit underlying records. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when the regulator determines that a serious safety defect presents an unreasonable risk. The relevant regulator must verify the rate using reported data and may require an earlier recall where warranted. A recall under this section requires prompt notice to affected customers and the regulator, an explanation of the hazard and available remedy, and progress updates until completion. A recall does not by itself prohibit future sales; any sales restriction or suspension is subject to regulator determination.
