# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K15 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal runaway, fire, explosion, or injury involving a home battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide affected customers and occupants of the directly adjoining homes with an initial safety notice within 24 hours. A report must include known circumstances, injuries or property damage, model and serial number, and immediate safety guidance. Manufacturers must submit a complete investigation report to the regulator within 30 days, with updates as material facts become available. Near-misses without fire, thermal runaway, or injury must be reported to the regulator quarterly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and after any material design change. The compliance target is that no more than 5% of tested units fail. Test sample size and protocol must produce a reliable estimate, and results must include uncertainty and failure modes. A model exceeding 5% must promptly submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retest on a regulator-set schedule; exceeding the target alone does not automatically bar market entry or require market withdrawal while the plan is being implemented. Regulators may restrict sales or require other protective measures where the results or other evidence indicate serious risk. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results to the relevant regulator and customers purchasing that model, but need not publish individual-company per-model failure rates to the general public. Regulators may publish aggregated, non-identifying industry results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems placed on the market from 3 years after adoption must include a remote emergency shut-off capability accessible to the manufacturer and the customer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide safe updates and clear instructions for use. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must offer a compatible retrofit or replacement pathway and complete required retrofits within 6 years, where technically feasible; customers must be notified of any limitations and interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems placed on the market from 36 months after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed and tested to contain or mitigate battery fires under the regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific safety information, including the latest 55 °C sustained-heat test results, known relevant hazards, required maintenance, and emergency response and shut-off instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and occupants of directly adjoining homes of material safety risks and provide interim safety guidance. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, notify the regulator and affected customers, and provide a repair, replacement, or refund remedy. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action when warranted by evidence of serious risk, even if the numerical trigger is not met.
