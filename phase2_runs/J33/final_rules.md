# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J33 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal event involving fire, smoke, injury, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 30 days. A pattern suggesting an imminent serious hazard must be reported within 24 hours. Customers whose unit is affected by a confirmed safety issue must be notified directly within 5 days of confirmation, with immediate instructions where continued use may pose a serious risk. Reports must include known incident counts and be updated as facts are verified.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least every three years thereafter, using a regulator-recognized protocol and statistically representative samples. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7% per model. Manufacturers must provide full per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the relevant regulator and make those results available directly to customers who buy that model; results must not be published to the general public. A model exceeding the limit must undergo corrective action and a passing retest within a regulator-set period; regulators may restrict sales where the risk warrants it. Customers must receive prompt safety notices about confirmed material risks and required actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off no later than 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide compatible retrofit capability and complete retrofits of already-installed units within 6 years after adoption, prioritizing units subject to safety alerts. Shut-off must be fail-safe, accessible to authorized emergency responders under defined procedures, and must not disable essential customer safety alerts.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The system must be designed and validated to control or limit fire propagation without creating additional material hazards, and manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and safe-use instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about known hazards, affected models and serial numbers, recommended actions, and available support. Notices must be sent directly and promptly when a confirmed safety issue affects a unit; where continued use may pose a serious risk, customers must receive immediate instructions. A manufacturer must initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. At or above 8 incidents per 100,000 in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, provide model-level data, and submit a corrective-action and risk-mitigation plan for regulator review; this does not delay customer notices or other urgent protective measures. For a mandatory recall, the manufacturer must notify regulators and affected customers and provide a remedy or safe decommissioning instructions.
