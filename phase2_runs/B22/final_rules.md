# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B22 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of it. Incidents involving death, serious injury, active fire, or an ongoing hazard shall be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 5 calendar days when an incident indicates a material safety risk to their model, and promptly provide safety instructions and material updates. Reports shall include the date, model, known consequences, and available incident details; manufacturers shall update reports as facts are confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol. A model meets the standard only if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers shall submit per-model failure rates and test methods to regulators within 30 days of testing and provide results to customers of that model within the same period. Regulators may publish aggregate results; individual per-model results shall not be published to the general public. If a pre-market or in-market test shows a failure rate above 5%, the manufacturer shall notify the regulator within 30 days and submit a corrective-action plan within 60 days, including a retest schedule. Corrective actions and retesting shall be completed within 6 months. Pending retest, the regulator may authorize limited market entry or continued installations under an interim safety plan, impose additional protections, or suspend installations or sales where the risk warrants. A model that fails its retest shall not be installed unless the regulator authorizes it under an updated safety plan.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units manufactured 24 months or more after this section takes effect shall support remote emergency shut-off. For units already installed when the requirement takes effect, manufacturers shall provide a compliant retrofit or replacement within 6 years, prioritizing units presenting elevated safety risks. Shut-off capability shall not replace required local safety controls or emergency response procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after this standard takes effect shall include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance standard. Manufacturers shall document compliance and provide installation and maintenance instructions to customers. The requirement does not replace other required safety controls or emergency response procedures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of confirmed safety hazards and provide clear instructions for safe use, service, or cessation of use. Initial notice shall be sent within 5 calendar days after determining that a material hazard exists, with updates as material information becomes available. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, calculated using the model’s installed-unit population and confirmed thermal incidents, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers, submit a corrective-action plan and timeline, and initiate a mandatory recall. The recall shall include a remedy and timeline sufficient to protect households and the public. Individual per-model test failure rates need not be published to the general public, but must be provided to regulators and to customers of that model as required by S2; this limitation on general-public publication does not permit withholding results from those customers.
