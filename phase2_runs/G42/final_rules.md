# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G42 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported to the regulator within 5 business days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk or requires customer action, and provide updates as material information becomes available. Reports must include known incident counts and installed-unit denominators; corrected or previously omitted reports must be promptly submitted.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before initial certification and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must provide per-model sample sizes, failure rates, and supporting test results to the relevant regulator at certification and after each retest, and must provide the model’s failure rate and material supporting test results to prospective customers before purchase. A failure rate above 5% exceeds the performance criterion and requires prompt notice to regulators, a corrective-action plan, and retesting under the approved protocol. Regulators may require additional safeguards or restrict sales based on the risk and adequacy of remediation; exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit certification or continued sale. Per-model results must not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems placed on the market must include a remotely operable emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a documented means for customers or emergency responders to request activation. Manufacturers must provide software and communications support for the function. For units installed before this requirement takes effect, manufacturers must offer a compatible retrofit or equivalent mitigation within 6 years, prioritizing units with known safety concerns and notifying affected owners of available options.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of compliance as part of model certification.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its 55 °C sustained-heat test results and relevant incident and recall notices. Manufacturers must promptly notify owners of newly identified material safety risks, recommended precautions, and corrective actions, and provide updates as material information becomes available. A manufacturer must initiate a regulator-supervised recall when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. Manufacturers must report model-level incident counts and installed-unit denominators to regulators and affected customers, and notify customers promptly when the recall threshold is met.
