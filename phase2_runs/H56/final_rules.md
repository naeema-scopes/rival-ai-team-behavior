# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H56 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, thermal runaway, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a detailed report within 5 calendar days. Reports must include the incident date, model, location, known consequences, and corrective actions, and must be updated as facts become available. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a safety risk to their model or installed unit, and promptly provide any material updates. No incident may be omitted because it was not independently verified; reports may be marked preliminary.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the relevant regulator and provide those results to customers purchasing that model. Results must identify the test protocol and date; they are not required to be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% triggers a time-bound corrective-action plan and retesting on a regulator-set schedule. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sale or justify a sales restriction; any interim measures must be based on a separate, documented risk determination and be proportionate to the demonstrated risk. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected owners of resulting safety precautions or material hazards.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required for all models. New units must support it before sale. Manufacturers must enable the capability on already-installed units within six years of adoption, where technically feasible, and notify owners of units that cannot be updated. Shut-off commands must be authenticated, logged, and designed to prevent unauthorized activation; customers must receive clear instructions for emergency use and any limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be tested as part of the model’s safety evaluation, maintained according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and disclosed to customers, including its limitations and any required maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including applicable safety notices, incident-reporting and emergency instructions, unresolved hazards, required precautions, and that model’s thermal-test results. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected owners of material hazards, required precautions, repairs, and safety updates, using direct contact and other reasonable channels. A manufacturer must initiate a mandatory recall when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period. The rate must use the model’s installed-unit population for that period and include all known thermal incidents, whether or not independently verified or previously reported; manufacturers must promptly correct the rate as new information becomes available. The manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers within 48 hours of the threshold being met and provide a remedy at no cost. A recall is also required when the regulator determines a serious defect presents an unreasonable risk, regardless of rate.
