# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J41 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible product-specific safety risk, and promptly provide any updated instructions or corrective action. Reports must include known circumstances, model and unit identification, and actions taken; suspected incidents must not be omitted while investigation is pending.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a regulator-accepted sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a representative sample and a documented, reproducible protocol. New models must not exceed a 5% failure rate. A model already marketed when this standard takes effect that exceeds 5% may remain available under regulator-approved risk controls and enhanced incident monitoring while the regulator reviews its safety case. The manufacturer must submit an updated safety case and test and incident evidence at least annually. After the three-year evidence review, the regulator may permit continued sale of that existing model under appropriate controls if it determines the verified risk is adequately managed; it may instead require further corrective measures or restrict sale if the evidence shows unacceptable risk. The 5% limit applies to new models unless regulators revise it through a transparent standard-setting process. Regulators must review the limit after three years using accumulated test and incident evidence. Before placing a model on the market, and following a material design change, manufacturers must submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results to the regulator. Per-model results, including the failure rate and test method, must be made available to customers of that model before purchase; individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must support secure remote emergency shut-off by the date this standard takes effect, with a safe local shut-off available if communications fail. For already-installed systems, manufacturers must provide compatible remote shut-off capability or an equivalent safe corrective measure within six years of the effective date. Any remote function must be authenticated, logged, and designed to prevent unauthorized activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New models placed on the market 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression, validated for the battery chemistry and enclosure. The requirement applies to existing model lines when newly manufactured units are placed on the market after that date. Suppression systems must not create additional electrical, toxic-exposure, or maintenance hazards and must be covered by documented inspection and service instructions. Regulators may approve an equivalent protective design where the manufacturer demonstrates an equal or greater reduction in fire risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s safety-relevant instructions, emergency-response steps, known material hazards, and per-model sustained-heat test results, including the test method and failure rate. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material new safety findings and provide updated instructions or corrective actions. When a model’s confirmed thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly report it to regulators and notify affected customers. Regulators must promptly review the evidence and may require interim protective measures while the rate or cause is verified. Following review, regulators must require a recall or other effective corrective remedy proportionate to the verified risk and the demonstrated effectiveness of alternatives. Recall notices must explain the risk, affected units, remedy, and how to obtain it.
