# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H27 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of confirmation, including incidents initially reported by customers or service providers. Within 72 hours, manufacturers must notify affected customers whose units or model may be implicated, provide known safety precautions, and explain how to report further incidents. A fuller incident report, including known cause and corrective actions, is due to the regulator within 30 days; updates must be submitted promptly as material facts emerge. Reporting obligations apply regardless of injury or property damage.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample, with retesting after a material design or manufacturing change and at least every three years. Manufacturers must submit numerical per-model results and test methodology confidentially to the relevant regulator; individual-company rates must not be publicly published. At purchase, customers must receive the numerical per-model failure rate, test date, protocol summary, applicable safety precautions, and any corrective-action status. A model with a failure rate above 5% must have a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retest schedule; exceeding this level alone does not automatically prohibit sales. Manufacturers must update customers when material retesting changes the result or corrective-action status.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required for all new units installed 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must retrofit or otherwise enable remote shut-off for existing units within six years after adoption where technically feasible, and must provide customers with clear instructions for using it. Any infeasibility determination and mitigation plan must be documented for the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured for installation must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s design, maintenance requirements, and performance evidence and provide this information to the relevant regulator. Units manufactured before the effective date are not subject to this requirement.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase, each customer must receive the numerical per-model 55 °C test failure rate and associated information specified in S2; numerical per-model results are submitted confidentially to regulators and must not be publicly published as individual-company rates. Manufacturers must promptly provide affected customers with a safety notice when a regulator identifies a material model-specific risk, when the model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when a safety notice is otherwise required under S1. A notice must describe the risk, precautions, and available corrective actions. A model exceeding 8 incidents per 100,000 in a year must be reported to the regulator for review and corrective-action determination. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 10 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when the regulator determines that a defect presents an imminent risk of serious injury or fire. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and carry out the regulator-ordered recall.
