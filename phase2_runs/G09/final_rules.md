# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G09 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, smoke, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and submit a written incident report with known facts and corrective actions within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their product or requires customer action. Updates shall be provided as material facts become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a common, regulator-approved protocol, with a representative sample and documented results. Manufacturers shall provide per-model results, sample sizes, and test methods to the relevant regulator before market entry and at least annually thereafter. Customers who buy a model shall receive that model’s failure rate, sample size, and test method, and these results shall be updated when retesting materially changes them. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate shall undergo corrective action and retesting under regulator oversight; regulators may restrict sales if safety concerns remain unresolved.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide a safe, accessible means to activate shut-off and clear customer instructions. Existing installed units shall be upgraded or otherwise provided with an equivalent remote shut-off capability within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible; manufacturers shall document units for which this is not feasible and provide an alternative emergency isolation method.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document the suppression system’s effectiveness, maintenance requirements, and limitations, and provide this information to customers and regulators.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase and upon material safety updates, customers shall receive clear instructions on safe installation, operation, warning signs, emergency response, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of a confirmed product hazard and provide protective steps, repair, replacement, or refund as applicable. Manufacturers shall report model-level thermal-incident counts and installed-unit denominators to the regulator at least quarterly. The denominator shall be the model’s average number of installed units during the reporting period, with the calculation method documented; incident counts shall include verified overheating, fire, smoke, or thermal-runaway events attributable to that model. Regulators may audit records and require independent verification. If available data indicate that a model may exceed 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and provide interim protective measures. The regulator shall verify the counts and denominator within 10 business days of receiving the notice; if the verified rate exceeds 8 per 100,000, it shall require a recall or an equally protective corrective action with a specified timetable. Verification shall not delay interim protective measures where an imminent hazard is indicated. Manufacturers shall submit a corrective-action plan and provide regular progress updates to the regulator and affected customers.
