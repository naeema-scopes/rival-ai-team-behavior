# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D33 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, evacuation, or property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, followed by a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. Incidents involving death, serious injury, or an active risk to the public must also be reported immediately by telephone or other rapid channel. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours of identifying a credible product-related thermal hazard, and provide material updates promptly. Reports must include the model, incident date and location where known, effects, and actions taken. Suspected incidents must not be withheld pending confirmation; corrections may be submitted as facts are established.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo an independently witnessed sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. Manufacturers must submit per-model failure rates, sample sizes, and corrective-action plans to the relevant regulator, and provide complete per-model results to customers who buy that model, including updated results after each required test. Individual-company results need not be published to the general public. If a model exceeds 5%, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit a corrective-action and retesting plan. The regulator shall set a fixed retesting schedule and may require sales suspension, customer protections, or other restrictions based on the evidence. Continued sale or use before passing retest is permitted only if the regulator expressly approves a time-limited corrective-action plan with appropriate risk controls; approval does not change the 5% limit. Regulators may require additional testing or restrictions where evidence indicates an unacceptable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All newly manufactured home battery systems must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety hazard, by the manufacturer or emergency responders. The function must be secure, tested, and accompanied by clear customer instructions and a safe local means of shutdown. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit path for installed units and complete retrofits, or offer replacement where retrofitting is impracticable, within 6 years of adoption of this standard. Shut-off must not disable essential safety monitoring or prevent safe restart after the hazard is resolved.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and reliability protocol. This requirement applies to units manufactured 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must validate suppression performance for each model and provide the results to the relevant regulator and affected customers. Suppression must not substitute for thermal testing, incident reporting, or other required safeguards.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices about relevant hazards, safe operation, and required actions, and promptly notify affected customers of credible product-related thermal hazards and material updates. A manufacturer must initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must notify the relevant regulator and affected customers promptly, explain the risk and remedy, and report progress until the recall is complete. Regulators may require a recall sooner where the evidence indicates an imminent or serious safety risk.
