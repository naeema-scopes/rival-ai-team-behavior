# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D02 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their model, with immediate notice where continued use may present an imminent danger. Reports must include known model, serial number, date, and circumstances; manufacturers must update incomplete reports as facts become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. The benchmark maximum failure rate is 5%. A model above the benchmark must be reported to the regulator with its test results and a corrective-action plan; the regulator may permit continued sale subject to mitigation, monitoring, and a time-bound compliance plan where the full evidence does not indicate an unacceptable safety risk. Tests must be repeated after material design or manufacturing changes and at least annually. Before sale, manufacturers must provide per-model test results and methodology to the relevant regulator and disclose the numerical failure rate to customers considering that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. Existing models must submit results and any required corrective-action plan within 24 months of this standard taking effect.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can safely place the system in a non-operating state, with appropriate authentication and protections against accidental or unauthorized activation. This requirement applies to models first offered for sale 24 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for use and maintain a 24-hour process for regulators and emergency responders to request activation when there is an imminent safety risk. For units already installed when the standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or equivalent safe shutdown capability within 60 months, unless the regulator approves an alternative based on documented technical infeasibility.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression designed to limit fire propagation and reduce risks to occupants and neighbouring properties. The requirement applies to units manufactured 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must validate suppression performance under regulator-approved fire and thermal-abuse testing and provide the test results to the regulator and customers considering the model. Existing installed units are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety instructions, incident-reporting contact details, and prompt notice of any model-specific hazard or corrective action; urgent danger notices must be sent without delay. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a 12-month period, and promptly notify the regulator and affected customers. The regulator may require a recall sooner where evidence indicates an imminent or serious safety risk. Manufacturers must provide updated incident-rate data to the regulator at least quarterly.
