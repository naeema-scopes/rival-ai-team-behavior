# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G52 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, evacuation, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 business days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 48 hours when the incident indicates a credible safety risk requiring customer action; otherwise, include the information in the next routine safety communication. Reports must include known incident counts and installed-unit denominators, identify any reporting gaps, and be updated as facts are verified.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-recognized independent laboratory and a published test protocol. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, confidentially to regulators and to customers buying that model; individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. A model with a failure rate above 5% must undergo confirmatory testing and submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan, with progress reports. Regulators may require sales restrictions where results or incident evidence show an imminent safety risk; exceeding 5% alone does not automatically suspend sales. Halcyon reports a 3.9% 55 °C failure rate for its flagship model.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must include a remotely operable emergency shut-off that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety hazard, by the manufacturer or emergency services under regulator-defined safeguards. Manufacturers must provide secure, reliable operation and clear customer instructions. For installed units lacking this capability, manufacturers must offer a no-cost retrofit or equivalent safe replacement within 72 months of adoption, prioritizing units with elevated risk. Halcyon reports remote shut-off support in 81% of its installed units.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The system must be validated for the unit’s credible thermal-runaway scenarios and must not create additional hazards. Manufacturers must provide evidence of validation to regulators. Halcyon estimates the added cost at $67 per unit; Northwind estimates $42 per unit.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, symptoms, immediate precautions, and support or remedy options. Notify affected customers within 48 hours when action is needed; otherwise include material safety information in the next routine notice. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, using consistently attributed incidents and that model’s installed-unit denominator, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators, provide supporting data, and initiate a recall with a free remedy. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of required actions and report corrective-action and recall completion rates to regulators; regulators may oversee implementation.
