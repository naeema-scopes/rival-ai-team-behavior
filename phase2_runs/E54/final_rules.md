# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E54 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written incident report within 10 calendar days. They must promptly preserve and provide relevant unit and incident records. Affected customers must be notified within 72 hours when the incident presents a credible safety risk to them; nearby residents must be notified when the regulator determines there is a material risk to the public. Reports must identify known incidents, including incidents not previously reported, and may be corrected as investigations develop.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample. A model’s failure rate must not exceed 5%. Manufacturers must submit full per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the relevant regulator, and disclose the numerical per-model failure rate and test date to customers buying that model. A model exceeding the limit must be reported promptly to the regulator and customers, with clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, precautions, and corrective actions. A model exceeding the limit may not be newly sold until it passes retesting. The regulator may require remediation of installed units.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety hazard, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under regulator oversight. Manufacturers must provide a secure, reliable means to activate it and explain its operation to customers. For already-installed units lacking this capability, manufacturers must complete retrofit or an approved equivalent safety measure within six years of this standard’s effective date, prioritizing higher-risk units.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions for the system’s operation and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, required precautions, incident-reporting contacts, and any corrective actions. Notices must be sent promptly when a credible safety risk is identified and updated as material information becomes available. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers promptly and initiate a recall, including a remedy plan and schedule. The regulator may require a broader or faster recall where the risk warrants it. Manufacturers must report incident rates and recall progress to the regulator and keep customers informed until the remedy is complete.
