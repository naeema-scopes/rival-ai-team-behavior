# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D54 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, uncontrolled overheating, injury, death, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. Customers whose unit is involved, and customers in the affected model or batch where a safety risk is reasonably indicated, shall be notified within 72 hours of that determination. Reports shall include known incidents, including incidents initially reported through retailers or service providers. Manufacturers shall maintain records of thermal incidents and periodically reconcile internal records with information from retailers and service providers. Any previously unreported incident meeting these criteria shall be reported within 24 hours of discovery, with the reason for the delay documented.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market launch and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results and supporting methods to regulators and provide results to customers who buy or own that model in clear, accessible form. Individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public; regulators may publish aggregated or anonymized results. If a model exceeds a 5% failure rate, the manufacturer shall promptly notify affected customers, submit a corrective-action plan and retest schedule to regulators, and follow any required precautions. Regulators may impose proportionate interim safeguards or sales restrictions; exceeding 5% shall not by itself automatically prohibit sales.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off shall be included on all new units placed on the market beginning 24 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers shall provide a secure software or hardware retrofit for compatible installed units, and complete required retrofits within 6 years of the effective date. Where retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer shall notify affected owners and provide an equivalent risk-reduction measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The requirement shall apply to units manufactured on or after that date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers clear safety notices describing known hazards, precautions, incident-reporting channels, and any relevant model-level thermal test results. Notices shall be sent promptly when a material risk is identified and updated as new information emerges. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer shall promptly notify affected customers and regulators, stop sales of the affected model pending corrective action, and provide a repair, replacement, or refund.
