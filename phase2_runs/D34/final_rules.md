# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D34 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any fire, thermal runaway, or other overheating incident involving a home battery to the relevant regulator within 5 business days after learning of it. An incident involving death, injury, evacuation, or property damage shall be reported within 24 hours. Reports shall include known unit/model identifiers, circumstances, and corrective actions, with updates as investigations progress. Manufacturers shall notify the affected customer within 5 business days, or within 24 hours for an urgent hazard, and provide immediate safety instructions where needed. Reports to regulators shall not be delayed pending completion of an investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after material design changes. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results, sample sizes, and test methods confidentially to regulators before market entry and annually thereafter, and provide a model’s results directly to its customers and prospective purchasers. Exceeding 5% requires prompt regulator notification, investigation, and a corrective-action plan. The regulator shall assess the results and risk, may require interim protective measures, and shall determine whether sales must be suspended pending correction and retesting; continued sales may be allowed only under regulator-approved safeguards. Regulators may publish aggregated findings. Manufacturers shall also provide relevant hazards, precautions, and safety instructions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market 24 months after adoption shall support secure remote emergency shut-off, with customer safeguards and a local means of emergency operation. Manufacturers shall provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade for installed units within 6 years after adoption where technically feasible, prioritizing higher-risk units. Where a retrofit is not feasible, the manufacturer shall notify the owner and regulator and provide an effective alternative safety measure. Shut-off capability shall not replace other safety protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers shall provide regulators with evidence of compliance and disclose any material limitations of the suppression system to customers. Fire suppression does not replace other required safety protections.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices describing known hazards, required precautions, incident-reporting contacts, and any model-specific safety instructions relevant to the customer. Notices shall be delivered promptly when new material safety information becomes available, and before sale for prospective purchasers. Notices may describe test-related hazards and actions without disclosing an individual company's per-model test failure rate. A manufacturer shall initiate a regulator-supervised recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers without delay, provide safe-use or shutdown instructions, and report recall progress and corrective actions to regulators. The recall process shall allow documented corrective action, retesting, and regulator review; any return to sale requires regulator clearance. A recall is not, by itself, a permanent market ban. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action where evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk, regardless of the numerical trigger.
