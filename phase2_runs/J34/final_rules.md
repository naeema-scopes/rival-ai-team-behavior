# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J34 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving a battery, including overheating whether or not it results in fire, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage, to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days after learning of it, and notify affected customers within 10 calendar days. A fatality or incident presenting an imminent risk to other units must be reported to the regulator within 24 hours, with an initial customer safety notice within 48 hours where a hazard may remain. Reports must include known circumstances, affected models and serial ranges, and steps taken; manufacturers must promptly supplement incomplete reports. Aggregate incident counts and corrective actions must be updated to the regulator quarterly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers must provide test methods, sample sizes, and per-model results to the relevant regulator and the other participating company under appropriate confidentiality protections. Before purchase, each prospective customer must receive the model’s numerical 55 °C failure rate directly and privately, together with clear information about material hazards, safe operating instructions, and any regulator-required precautions. Individual per-model failure rates must not be published or otherwise disclosed to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a corrective-action plan, and follow-up testing. Exceeding 5% is not by itself an automatic prohibition on sale or service; the regulator shall determine proportionate additional measures based on the evidence, corrective actions, and risk to users and the public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months after this standard takes effect must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. For units already installed on the effective date, manufacturers must offer a compatible retrofit or replacement pathway and complete the program within 6 years, prioritizing higher-risk units. Shut-off capability must not prevent emergency services or customers from following approved safe operating procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression suitable for the unit’s battery chemistry and installation environment. Manufacturers must validate the suppression system and provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers. The regulator may approve an equivalent protective design where it provides comparable fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, each prospective customer must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its numerical per-model thermal-test failure rate provided directly and privately, known material hazards, safe installation and operation instructions, and how to report an incident. Individual per-model failure rates must not be published or otherwise disclosed to the general public. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material hazards and corrective actions, using direct contact where feasible and public notices where affected customers cannot be reached. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit the model for an independent risk review. The regulator must require a recall, including stopping sale and providing repair, replacement, or refund, when the review finds that a recall is necessary to protect users or the public; otherwise it must order proportionate corrective action and follow-up, with its decision and rationale made public.
