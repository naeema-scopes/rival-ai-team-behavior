# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K09 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a potential safety risk, including known hazards and immediate precautions. Reports must include the model, incident date and circumstances, and known outcomes; manufacturers must promptly update regulators and affected customers as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a consistent, independently verifiable protocol. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including failure rates, confidentially to the relevant regulator and disclose those results to customers who buy or own that model. Per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. A model exceeding 5% must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and undergo regulator-supervised retesting; exceeding 5% alone does not require market exclusion, and regulators may impose proportionate corrective measures, including sales restrictions, based on demonstrated safety risks. Models marketed before adoption must be allowed a six-year compliance period to meet the 5% limit, with interim milestones, monitoring, and risk-appropriate corrective actions. During that period, exceeding 5% alone does not require sales restrictions; regulators retain authority to act on demonstrated safety risks. The parties will review the protocol and compliance approach with regulators after two years of collected data.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery systems must include a remotely operable emergency shut-off that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under regulator-defined safeguards. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and maintain secure, reliable access to the function. Existing installed units that lack remote shut-off must be upgraded or replaced within six years of adoption where technically feasible; manufacturers must notify affected owners of any limitations and available interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All newly manufactured home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation, independently tested, and maintained in working order. Manufacturers must disclose the system's capabilities and limitations to customers and provide safe inspection and maintenance instructions. Regulators may require earlier corrective measures for a model presenting an imminent hazard.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices describing known hazards, precautions, and any corrective actions, promptly after a material safety risk is identified. Manufacturers must notify affected customers and regulators when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year. Exceeding that rate triggers a mandatory recall of the affected model; manufacturers must promptly stop sales, notify affected customers, and provide a repair, replacement, or refund plan, subject to regulator oversight.
