# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A04 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents for which the cause is not yet established. A preliminary report must identify the product/model, incident date and location, known injuries or property damage, and immediate safety actions; material updates and a final investigation report are due within 30 days. The manufacturer must notify the affected customer within 24 hours when the incident presents an ongoing safety risk, and otherwise within 5 business days, with instructions for safe handling and support. Reports must include annual incident counts and installed-unit denominators. Manufacturers must disclose and correct any previously omitted incidents within 30 days after this standard takes effect.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. Reports must state the protocol, sample size, failure count, and failure rate and be submitted to the regulator before market entry and annually thereafter. Before purchase, customers must receive the complete 55 °C test results for the specific model they are buying; customers of existing units must be notified of material new results. Per-model results must be provided to regulators and affected customers and must not be withheld from customers; public publication is not required. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. A model exceeding 5% must not be newly placed on the market. For models already on the market, the manufacturer must submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan within 90 days and pass a retest within 18 months; sales may continue during this period subject to the plan and any interim safety measures required by the regulator. If the model fails to meet the limit by the deadline, it must cease sales. Test records must be retained for at least 10 years.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market from 24 months after this standard takes effect must support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a documented means for the customer or emergency responders to request activation. For units already installed on the effective date, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation plan within 6 years. Retrofit work must be performed safely and without charge where technically feasible; where it is not feasible, the manufacturer must provide an equivalent mitigation and notify the customer.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market from 36 months after this standard is adopted must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the battery system’s foreseeable thermal events. Manufacturers must document the system’s performance, maintenance needs, and limitations and provide this information to customers and regulators. The requirement does not replace other required safeguards or incident reporting.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its 55 °C sustained-heat test results, known material hazards, safe installation and operation instructions, and emergency response steps. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety findings, changed risks, and corrective actions, using direct contact where practicable and a public notice when affected customers cannot reasonably be reached. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, calculated using complete incident data, including incidents reported or corrected under S1, and that model’s installed-unit denominator. Regulators must be notified of the trigger and recall plan; customers must receive prompt notice and instructions for return, repair, or other safe remedy.
