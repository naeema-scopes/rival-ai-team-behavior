# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A06 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident, including overheating or fire, to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a follow-up report within 30 days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or household, and provide updates as material information becomes available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter. Testing must follow a common protocol established by the relevant regulator, use a representative sample of at least 100 units per model, and be conducted or independently verified by a qualified laboratory. A model exceeds the allowable failure rate if more than 7% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results, including the protocol, sample size, and verification details, to the relevant regulator and to customers of that model before purchase and upon material updates, and retain results for audit. Regulators may require corrective action and retesting when results indicate a material safety concern. Public reporting may use aggregated results, but this does not limit disclosure of a model’s results to its customers.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models must include a remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units with this capability within 6 years of this section taking effect, where technically feasible, and must notify owners of any units that cannot be retrofitted.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this section takes effect. Manufacturers must disclose to customers whether a unit includes built-in fire suppression and provide any applicable safety instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, before purchase and upon material safety updates, clear notices describing relevant thermal hazards, safe installation and use, incident-reporting contacts, and applicable model-specific safety test results. A manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and the relevant regulator when it identifies a credible safety risk. When a model’s verified thermal incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, submit a corrective-action plan, and undergo independent review. A mandatory recall is required if the regulator confirms an ongoing serious risk or the manufacturer fails to implement effective corrective action. Recall instructions must be promptly provided to affected customers.
