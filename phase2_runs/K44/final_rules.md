# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K44 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
A manufacturer must report every battery-related thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or emergency thermal shutdown to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents for which the cause is not yet established. It must provide a follow-up with known causes and corrective actions within 30 days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 72 hours after determining that an incident presents a material safety risk, with practical safety instructions and a contact channel. Reports must identify the model and incident, while protecting customers’ personal information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market launch and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers must submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator. A model with a failure rate above 5% must submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator and undergo retesting within 90 days; exceeding 5% does not by itself prohibit sale or require a recall. The regulator may require additional proportionate measures based on the risk and corrective actions. Manufacturers must provide customers and prospective buyers with the model’s 55 °C test failure rate, test date, and any relevant precautions or corrective actions in plain language. Per-model test results must not be published for the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery models placed on the market after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support the function, or provide an equivalent safety mitigation approved by the regulator, within six years of adoption. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation; customers must be informed when it is used.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Regulators may approve an equivalent mitigation for a specific design where evidence demonstrates comparable protection for households and neighbours.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety instructions at purchase and promptly notify affected customers of material hazards, recommended precautions, and corrective actions. Customers and prospective buyers must be able to access relevant model-specific safety instructions, actions, and 55 °C test failure rates. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, as verified by the regulator, must be recalled promptly; the manufacturer must notify affected customers and provide the regulator with requested data and corrective-action details. Regulators may require interim warnings or repairs while a recall is implemented. Public notices must identify affected models and safety actions without disclosing customers’ personal information or publishing individual companies’ per-model test failure rates.
