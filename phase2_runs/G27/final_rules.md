# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G27 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. They shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety hazard, and promptly provide any updated instructions or warnings. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial number, and actions taken. Incidents may not be omitted because their cause is uncertain.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% for any model. Before sale, after any material design change, and at least annually, manufacturers shall submit per-model test protocols, sample sizes, results, and corrective actions to the responsible regulator. Per-model failure rates and test results shall not be published to the general public. Current and prospective customers for a model shall receive that model’s failure rate and material safety information, with precautions. If a model exceeds the limit, the manufacturer shall submit a corrective-action plan within 30 days and complete retesting within 180 days. Sales may continue during this period unless the regulator determines that continued sale presents an imminent safety risk; a model that fails the retest shall not be newly sold until it passes.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Each newly manufactured home battery system shall include a remotely accessible emergency shut-off function that can isolate the battery from charging and discharging while preserving functions needed for safe monitoring. The function shall be available to the customer and emergency responders through a secure, documented interface, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers shall provide clear instructions and disclose limitations, communications requirements, and fallback procedures. For systems already installed when this standard is adopted, manufacturers shall provide a feasible retrofit or equivalent safe isolation method within 6 years, where technically practicable, and inform owners of units that cannot be upgraded.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard shall include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to limit fire propagation and protect occupants and neighbouring properties. Manufacturers shall document the suppression system’s performance, maintenance requirements, and limitations, and provide this information to customers and the responsible regulator. Suppression components shall be evaluated as part of the system’s safety testing.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material battery safety hazards, provide clear safe-use and response instructions, and issue updates as new information becomes available. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly submit supporting incident and installed-unit data to the responsible regulator. The regulator may promptly verify the data, but verification shall not delay protective action. Exceeding this threshold triggers a mandatory, regulator-overseen recall of that model. The recall shall promptly provide an effective remedy, which may include repair, replacement, return, or safe-use restrictions, as appropriate to the hazard; it does not by itself require delisting if the regulator determines an effective remedy protects households and neighbours. Manufacturers shall promptly carry out the recall and notify affected customers with instructions for safe use, shutdown, return, repair, or replacement.
