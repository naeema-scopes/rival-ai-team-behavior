# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E26 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents that appear contained or involve a third-party system. They must provide an initial notice to affected customers within 48 hours when a product may present a safety risk, followed by material updates as facts are confirmed. A detailed report is due within 30 days. Records must be retained and made available to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a regulator-approved protocol. For the first 24 months after adoption, the maximum failure rate is 7%; thereafter it is 5%. A model exceeding the applicable limit must undergo corrective action and retesting under a regulator-approved compliance plan and may not be represented as compliant until it meets the applicable limit. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results, methods, and sample sizes to regulators and retain them for audit. Before purchase, customers must receive the complete 55 °C test results, including methods and sample sizes, along with the model’s compliance status and any required corrective action. Regulators may publish aggregated results, but must not publish individual-company per-model failure rates to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All newly manufactured home battery systems must include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to provide this capability, or provide an equivalent regulator-approved emergency control, within 6 years of adoption. The shut-off function must be accessible to the household and emergency responders, with clear instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression validated to a regulator-approved performance standard. Manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and safe-response instructions. Regulators may approve an equivalent protective design where testing demonstrates comparable or better fire-risk reduction. No built-in suppression requirement applies to units manufactured before the effective date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety, installation, monitoring, and emergency-response instructions; the model’s compliance status; any required corrective action; and complete per-model 55 °C sustained-heat test results, including methods and sample sizes. Manufacturers must provide these complete results to regulators and retain them for audit. Regulators may publish aggregated results, but must not publish individual-company per-model failure rates to the general public. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a newly identified material safety risk, provide interim protective steps, and issue material updates as facts are confirmed. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, and notify regulators and affected customers without delay; regulators may require a recall at a lower rate when evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk. A recall requires corrective action and customer remedy; it does not by itself prohibit future sales of a corrected, compliant model.
