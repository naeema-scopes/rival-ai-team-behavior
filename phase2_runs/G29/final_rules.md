# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G29 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every known thermal incident involving a battery, including overheating, thermal runaway, smoke, or fire, to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, regardless of whether injury or property damage occurred. They shall submit a follow-up report with available findings within 30 days and promptly supplement it as material information becomes available. Reports shall include the model, incident date and location, known impacts, and any immediate safety measures. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety risk to their model, and promptly update those customers as material information becomes available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model shall undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A failure rate of no more than 5% is the performance benchmark. Manufacturers shall submit full per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective-action plans to the relevant regulator, and disclose the per-model failure rate and test date to customers buying that model. A model exceeding the benchmark shall undergo corrective action and retesting. The regulator shall review the results and determine appropriate measures, including whether continued market access is safe; exceeding the benchmark alone does not automatically prohibit market access.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability within 12 months of adoption. Manufacturers shall provide a safe, secure method for authorized emergency responders or the relevant utility to activate shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall assess installed units lacking this capability and complete feasible retrofits within 6 years of adoption, prioritizing units presenting the greatest safety risk. Where retrofit is infeasible, manufacturers shall notify affected customers and the regulator, explain the limitation, and provide an alternative safety measure or operating guidance.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems shall include built-in fire suppression beginning no earlier than 36 months after adoption. The regulator shall establish performance and verification requirements and may approve equivalent protective measures where they provide comparable safety. Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear information about the system’s fire-safety features and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety and operating information at purchase and prompt notice of material safety risks affecting their model, including recommended precautions and any corrective action. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and provide the incident and installed-unit data for review. The regulator shall assess the evidence, including incident severity, trends, and corrective actions, and determine whether a recall or other protective measures are warranted. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers and regulators of any recall or other required safety action.
