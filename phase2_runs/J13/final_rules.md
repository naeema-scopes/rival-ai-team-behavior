# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J13 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator. Reports of fire, injury, or an active risk to people or property are due within 24 hours of discovery; other thermal-incident reports are due within 5 business days. Within 5 business days, manufacturers must notify affected customers when an incident may indicate a safety risk, and provide prompt updates on protective actions. Reports must include known circumstances, affected models and units, and actions taken; manufacturers must supplement them as facts develop. Records of all incidents, including incidents not reportable under other rules, must be retained for at least 10 years.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a documented, regulator-accepted protocol and a representative sample. The 5% failure rate is the regulatory performance threshold under discussion. A model exceeding it must be reported to the regulator within 5 business days and have a corrective-action plan submitted within 30 days, including risk controls, a retest schedule, and customer protections as warranted. The regulator must determine proportionate measures based on the level of risk and verified evidence; exceeding the threshold alone does not automatically require a permanent sales ban. Continued sales may be conditioned on effective interim safeguards and timely improvement, and regulators may require restrictions or a sales pause where needed to protect the public. Per-model results, including failure rates, must be submitted to regulators and provided to customers who buy that model, including prospective customers before purchase; individual company per-model failure rates will not be publicly disclosed. Regulators may revise the threshold as evidence develops.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on all newly manufactured units beginning 24 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide a secure, authenticated shut-off capability and clear instructions for its use. Units already installed must be retrofitted, where technically feasible, within 6 years; manufacturers must notify owners of retrofit availability, costs, and any safety limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and designed to limit fire propagation and protect occupants and nearby property. Manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and end-of-life instructions and disclose any relevant limitations to customers. Regulators may approve an equivalent protective design where the manufacturer demonstrates that it provides comparable or greater fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices promptly when credible information identifies a material risk affecting their model, including the nature of the risk, affected units, and steps customers should take; urgent risks require notice within 24 hours of discovery, and other material risks within 5 business days. Manufacturers must promptly update notices as facts and protective actions develop. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units during a rolling 12-month period triggers a mandatory recall. The rate must use the model’s installed-unit population during that period as the denominator and include all qualifying incidents, whether or not previously reported. Manufacturers must promptly report suspected threshold exceedances and provide supporting data; independent review must verify the rate without delaying urgent protections. Within 24 hours of verification, manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers and initiate the recall. Recall measures may include repair, replacement, or refund, as appropriate to the verified risk; a recall does not by itself require a permanent sales ban, though regulators may impose sales restrictions where needed to protect the public. Regulators may require immediate protective action, including an immediate recall, before verification where evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
