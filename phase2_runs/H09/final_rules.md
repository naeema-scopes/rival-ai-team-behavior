# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H09 · variant H · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 26})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any fire, thermal runaway, evacuation, injury, or property-damage event involving a home battery within 24 hours of becoming aware of it. Other overheating or safety incidents requiring emergency service or product repair must be reported within 5 business days. Within 30 days, the manufacturer must provide an updated incident record with known facts, unit/model identification, and corrective actions; material new information must be reported promptly. Affected customers must be notified within 5 business days of an incident involving their unit, with urgent hazards communicated immediately. Reports must include incidents regardless of suspected cause, and manufacturers must retain auditable incident records.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a validated 55 °C sustained-heat test before sale and at least annually, and after any material design or supplier change. Test methods, sample sizes, failures, numerical failure rates, and corrective actions must be documented. Complete per-model results, including numerical failure rates, must be provided confidentially to the relevant regulator and to customers considering or owning that model. Individual-company per-model test failure rates need not be published to the general public. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must promptly undergo corrective action and retesting; the regulator may require additional safeguards or restrict new installations based on the evidence. Exceeding the threshold alone does not automatically prohibit sale or installation.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on all new home battery units installed 12 months or more after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway for installed units that lack the capability and complete retrofits within 72 months after adoption, prioritizing units with elevated safety risks. Shut-off must be accessible to authorized emergency responders and the customer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and maintain a safe local means of operation if remote communications fail.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on all new home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard. The requirement applies to all models and must be verified through documented testing and certification. Manufacturers must provide customers and regulators with the suppression system’s operating instructions, limitations, and maintenance requirements.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers must receive clear, model-specific safety information, including the numerical 55 °C sustained-heat test failure rate, a plain-language summary of test findings and corrective actions, known material risks, installation and emergency guidance, and how to report an incident. Complete numerical per-model test results must also be provided confidentially to regulators; individual-company per-model test failure rates need not be published to the general public. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material new hazards and provide updated instructions and interim safe-use guidance. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting incident records, including the installed-unit denominator and incident dates. The regulator must verify the model-specific rate using those records and may require additional records or an audit. Once the threshold is verified, the manufacturer must initiate a recall without delay. The manufacturer must publicly announce the recall and provide affected customers with repair, replacement, and safe-use instructions. This trigger applies to model-specific incident rates, not company-wide rates.
