# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K53 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents initially suspected to involve another cause. They must notify affected customers within 24 hours when the incident involves their unit or presents a credible risk to their model, provide immediate safety instructions, and update them as material facts emerge. A detailed incident report must be submitted to the regulator within 10 calendar days, with a final investigation report within 30 days or an explanation and revised date. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and promptly correct incomplete or missed reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including sample size and failures, confidentially to the regulator and provide the model’s test date, sample size, number of failures, and failure rate to customers buying or owning it. Results must not be published to the general public by company and model. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% for any model. A model exceeding 5% must be promptly reported to the regulator and undergo a regulator-reviewed corrective-action plan and retesting on a regulator-set schedule. The regulator may require additional measures, including sales restrictions, where results indicate elevated or imminent serious risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must include remote emergency shut-off capability beginning 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to provide this capability within 6 years after adoption where technically feasible, and must notify customers of any units that cannot be retrofitted and provide safe shutdown instructions. Remote shut-off must not replace emergency response procedures or customer access to safe local controls. The regulator may require faster action for a model presenting an imminent serious risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system and its limitations in customer materials. The regulator may require earlier corrective measures for a model presenting an imminent serious risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and the regulator of a thermal incident involving their unit, provide safe-use and response instructions, and update customers as the investigation develops. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The rate must be calculated from documented incidents and installed-unit counts for the same model and period and reported to the regulator. A recall requires prompt customer notice and an effective remedy, which may include repair, replacement, or refund; a recall is not, by itself, a prohibition on future sales. The regulator may also require a recall sooner where the evidence indicates an imminent serious risk. Recall notices must explain the hazard, affected units, and remedy.
