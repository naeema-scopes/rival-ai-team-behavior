# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E23 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a material risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include known incident counts, affected models, and corrective actions; suspected under-reporting must be corrected promptly. Within 90 days of adoption, manufacturers must reconcile and report thermal incidents from the prior 12 months, including incidents not previously reported to regulators. Regulators may audit incident records and reporting controls.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers must provide per-model test results and methods to regulators before sale and after material design changes. They must provide the results and methods to prospective customers before purchase and to owners of that model, without requiring a request, and update them when results change. Individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. If a model exceeds the limit, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and submit a corrective-action and retesting plan. The regulator must assess the risk and remediation and determine any interim sales restrictions or other measures; exceeding the limit alone does not require an automatic market-wide ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation within six years of the standard taking effect, prioritizing units presenting the highest documented risk. Where retrofit is infeasible, the manufacturer must notify the owner and regulator and provide an effective alternative mitigation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Until that requirement takes effect, manufacturers must document the fire-risk controls used in each model and provide installation, maintenance, and emergency-response instructions to owners. Any alternative measure must not reduce the safety protections required by this standard.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, manufacturers must provide prospective customers with model-specific safety information, including thermal-test results and methods, known material risks, required maintenance, and emergency instructions. They must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and any corrective action. If a model’s confirmed thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, provide supporting data, and submit a corrective-action plan. The regulator must promptly assess the evidence and risk and determine whether a recall or other protective measures are required; exceeding the threshold alone does not automatically trigger a recall. Manufacturers must promptly implement any regulator-ordered recall, notify affected customers without delay of any recall or customer action, explain the risk and remedy, and report progress to the regulator.
