# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E45 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a follow-up report containing known causes and corrective actions within 30 days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include incidents regardless of whether injury or damage occurred; suspected incidents must be reported and may be updated as facts are confirmed. Records must be retained for at least 10 years.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Before sale and after any material design change, manufacturers must submit full results to the regulator, including protocol, sample size, failure count and rate, and testing date. Customers and prospective purchasers must be able to obtain the full test results for the model, including its failure rate; manufacturers are not required to publish individual-company per-model failure rates to the general public. Regulators may require retesting and audit supporting records. A model must not exceed a 5% failure rate; a model exceeding that limit must promptly stop sales pending regulator-approved corrective action and successful retesting.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit or otherwise enable this capability on installed units within 6 years of adoption, where technically feasible, and must notify customers of any units that cannot be upgraded and provide safe alternatives.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on all new home battery units manufactured for sale beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The requirement applies to new units only.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of safety risks, required precautions, and corrective actions, and provide updates as material information becomes available. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. The rate must use the model’s installed-unit count for that same period; incident classifications and unit counts must be supported by records available for regulator audit. Manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers without delay upon reaching the trigger, stop sales of the affected model, and implement regulator-approved corrective action or removal.
