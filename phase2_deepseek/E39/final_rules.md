# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E39 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving fire, smoke, or uncontrolled overheating to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a full incident report within 10 business days. They must notify the affected customer within 48 hours, provide immediate safety instructions, and notify customers of nearby units if the incident may indicate a hazard shared by those units. Reports must include known injuries, property damage, model and serial number, and corrective actions. Suspected incidents must be investigated and reported; uncertainty is not grounds to delay initial notice. Manufacturers must maintain records of thermal incidents and reconcile those records against service, warranty, and customer-support records at least annually; any newly identified reportable incident must be reported promptly under these deadlines.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved sample size and test protocol. No more than 5% of tested units of any model may fail. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to regulators, and provide the relevant model’s results directly to customers who buy or own that model. These customer disclosures do not require public publication of individual-company per-model failure rates. If a model exceeds the limit, the manufacturer must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and retest on a regulator-approved schedule. The regulator may restrict new sales if corrective action is inadequate, retesting fails, or continued sales pose an unacceptable risk; protective action must not be delayed where there is an immediate risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery systems must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 24 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must retrofit compatible capability to already-installed units within six years of the effective date, or document to the regulator why a unit cannot safely be retrofitted and provide an equivalent protective measure. Shut-off must be operable by the customer and accessible to emergency responders under defined emergency procedures, with safeguards against unauthorized activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard is adopted. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and installation, and must be tested to a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers. This requirement does not replace other required fire-safety measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must give customers clear safety notices upon learning of a credible model-specific hazard, including the hazard, affected models and serial ranges, immediate precautions, and where to obtain updates and assistance. Notices must be sent promptly and through direct channels where available; public notices must also be issued when affected units cannot be reliably identified or reached. A recall is mandatory when any model’s thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a 12-month period. Manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop sales, and provide an effective remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund. The regulator shall verify incident counts and installed-unit denominators; verification must not delay interim protective notices or action where there is an immediate risk.
