# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G25 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, or injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a fuller incident report within 10 calendar days. Other thermal incidents requiring emergency services or causing property damage must be reported within 5 calendar days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours of determining that their unit or model may present a material safety risk, with immediate instructions for urgent hazards. Reports must include known incident counts and rates, and must not omit incidents because they were not initially reported.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. Manufacturers must provide per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results to the responsible regulator and to customers who buy that model, in clear and accessible form; individual-company, per-model results need not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, investigation, and a corrective-action plan, including retesting under regulator oversight. The regulator may require additional risk controls or restrict a model where the evidence indicates unacceptable risk; exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sale. Manufacturers must retain underlying records and provide them to regulators on request.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery systems must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete retrofits, where technically feasible, within 6 years of the standard taking effect. If a retrofit is infeasible or would create a greater safety risk, the manufacturer must notify the regulator and provide an equivalent mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after the standard is adopted. Manufacturers must provide the regulator with evidence that the suppression system is appropriate for the battery design and does not introduce a greater safety risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about known material risks, safe operation, and required corrective action, and promptly update notices when material new information emerges. A manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours after determining that a unit or model may present a material safety risk, with immediate instructions for urgent hazards. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, investigate the causes, and submit a corrective-action plan. The regulator must promptly assess the incident severity and other available evidence and may require a recall, including an immediate recall where warranted. A manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and provide clear recall and corrective instructions when a recall is required.
