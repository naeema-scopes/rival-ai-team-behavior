# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A38 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, or hazardous overheating to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller investigation report within 10 business days. They shall notify customers whose units may be affected within 72 hours, including known hazards and immediate protective steps. Reports shall include incidents known to the manufacturer regardless of whether the unit is confirmed to be the cause.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers shall submit test methods, sample sizes, and complete per-model results to the responsible regulator before market entry and after material design changes. Manufacturers shall provide customers considering or owning each model access to that model’s complete per-model test results, including its failure rate; results need not be publicly published. Regulators shall determine any corrective action or sales restrictions based on the complete evidence and risk; no automatic numerical failure-rate ceiling applies.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 24 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall retrofit already-installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible, and shall provide customers with safe interim instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify customers whose units may be affected by a safety hazard, describing the hazard, affected models or serial ranges, and protective steps. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. For this standard, a recall requires customer notification and an appropriate corrective remedy; it does not automatically require a sales suspension or market ban. Regulators may determine whether sales suspension or other additional measures are warranted based on the evidence and risk. Regulators may also require a recall or other corrective action when evidence shows an imminent or material safety risk.
