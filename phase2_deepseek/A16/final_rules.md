# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A16 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. They must notify the affected customer within 48 hours when the incident involves that customer’s unit or presents a safety risk to that customer. Reports must include known circumstances and be updated as facts are confirmed; suspected incidents must not be withheld pending final investigation. Within 30 days after this standard takes effect, manufacturers must also report any known thermal incidents that occurred in the preceding 12 months but were not previously reported, and notify affected customers as required above.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after a material design change. Until regulators revise the threshold following review, a model with a failure rate above 8% must not be newly placed on the market until corrective action is accepted by the regulator and the model passes a retest. For any model above 5%, manufacturers must submit a corrective-action plan and retest schedule to regulators, and complete the corrective action and retesting under regulator oversight. Results, sample sizes, and corrective-action plans must be submitted to regulators; customers buying the model must receive its relevant test results. Individual company per-model failure rates are not to be published to the general public. Regulators should periodically review the 5% and 8% thresholds as evidence accumulates, considering incident data and feasible risk-reduction measures.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must provide a secure software or hardware retrofit for already-installed units that can practicably support the function, completing the retrofit within six years of this standard taking effect. Where retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must inform the owner and provide practical alternative emergency guidance.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard is adopted. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions on system operation and maintenance, and disclose any limitations. Until the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must provide customers with practical fire-response guidance and maintain appropriate safeguards against thermal incidents.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s relevant 55 °C sustained-heat test results, safety limitations, and emergency-response instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material new safety findings, corrective actions, and incidents involving their unit; notices must explain the hazard and steps to take. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a 12-month period. Manufacturers must notify the regulator and affected customers without delay, stop new sales pending regulator-approved corrective action, and complete the recall or other regulator-approved remedy.
