# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K22 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, or emergency thermal shutdown within 10 calendar days of learning of it. An incident involving death, serious injury, evacuation, or an active threat to people or property must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 calendar days after determining that a reported incident presents a material safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include the model, incident date and known circumstances; regulators may request follow-up information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market release, at least once every three years, and after a material design change. The regulator shall set and consistently apply the test protocol and sample-size requirements. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results and sample sizes to the regulator and provide the results, including the model’s failure rate, to customers who buy that model. A result above 5% requires prompt notification to the regulator and a corrective-action plan; it does not by itself bar marketing. The regulator may authorize continued or conditional marketing with proportionate safeguards while corrective actions are implemented, and may restrict sales where the evidence demonstrates an unacceptable safety risk. Customers must receive a safety notice when the regulator or manufacturer identifies a material risk requiring customer action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months after this standard takes effect must support secure remote emergency shut-off. For units already installed on that date, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation within 6 years where technically feasible; they must inform owners of limitations and interim safety steps. Shut-off must not disable necessary safety monitoring or prevent emergency services from acting.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured on or after 36 months following adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-set performance and safety requirements. Manufacturers shall disclose to customers whether a unit includes suppression and provide safe-use and maintenance instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with model-specific safety notices promptly when a regulator or manufacturer identifies a material risk, including the nature of the risk, affected units, recommended actions, and available support. For each model, if its verified thermal incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 units of that model installed during a rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit the underlying incident data, installed-unit denominator, verification method, and corrective-action plan. A verified rate above this threshold triggers a mandatory recall. The regulator shall oversee the recall’s scope and require clear customer instructions and an effective remedy, repair, or replacement at no cost.
