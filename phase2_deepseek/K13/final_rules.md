# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K13 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any fire, thermal runaway, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage involving a home battery within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, followed by a complete report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include known incident counts and rates, corrective actions, and whether any incidents were not previously reported; uncertainty must be identified and updates supplied as facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-recognized protocol before market release and after a material design or manufacturing change. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results, including test methods and failure rates, to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator review and a documented corrective-action plan; it is not, by itself, an automatic market ban. The regulator may require additional testing, risk controls, sales restrictions, or other proportionate measures based on the evidence. Manufacturers must clearly disclose each model’s results to prospective customers before purchase and to existing customers, alongside any required protective actions. Public reporting may include aggregate results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or emergency responders when necessary to address an imminent safety risk, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for customers and responders. For installed units that lack this capability, manufacturers must provide a compliant retrofit or replacement within 6 years of adoption, unless the regulator approves an alternative risk-reduction measure for units where retrofit is not technically feasible.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression designed for the hazards of the battery chemistry and installation environment. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must validate the system’s performance through testing and provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers and installers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase and installation, customers must receive clear written safety instructions, foreseeable hazards, emergency contacts and shut-off guidance, plus a way to register for safety updates. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and provide required protective actions, without waiting for a recall decision. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting data; the regulator must assess the evidence and may require a proportionate recall or other corrective action. A recall is required when the regulator determines that a serious defect creates an unacceptable risk. Recall notices must explain the hazard, affected units, and remedy; progress and completion rates must be reported to the regulator.
