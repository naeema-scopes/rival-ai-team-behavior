# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G50 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report every confirmed thermal incident involving fire, smoke, or uncontrolled overheating to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of it. An incident involving death, serious injury, evacuation, or material property damage must be reported within 24 hours. The initial report may be preliminary and must be updated as facts are confirmed. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 calendar days when an incident presents a reasonably foreseeable safety risk to them; urgent risks require notice within 24 hours. Reports must include the unit/model, event date and location, known consequences, and immediate safety actions. Regulators may require follow-up data.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-recognized protocol before sale, at least once every 3 years, and after a material design change. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt notice to the regulator, a corrective-action plan within 6 months, and retesting within 12 months; it does not by itself prohibit sales or require a recall. Manufacturers must submit the protocol, sample size, and per-model results to the relevant regulator and provide the applicable model’s results to customers who buy that model. The regulator may require sales suspension or other protective measures based on its assessment of demonstrated or imminent risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## S3_remote_shutoff
New home battery models placed on the market 3 years after this standard takes effect must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a local means of safe operation. For already-installed units, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation plan within 6 years of the effective date, prioritizing units with known hazards. If remote shut-off is technically infeasible for a unit, the manufacturer must notify the owner and regulator and provide an alternative risk-reduction measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system must be designed and validated to reduce the likelihood or consequences of thermal runaway and must not create an unreasonable additional safety hazard. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide clear safety notices, including relevant precautions and mitigation steps. Urgent risks require notice within 24 hours; other material risks require notice within 5 calendar days of confirmation. A model’s annual thermal-incident rate is the number of confirmed thermal incidents divided by the number of that model’s installed units, expressed per 100,000 units. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting incident and installed-unit data. The regulator must assess the evidence and may require a recall, sales restriction, or other corrective action based on the risk; exceedance alone does not automatically trigger a recall. Customer notices and any required recall instructions must be issued without delay, and within 24 hours when the risk is urgent. Model-specific test results and incident data must be provided to the regulator and relevant customers; individual-company per-model test failure rates need not be publicly disclosed.
