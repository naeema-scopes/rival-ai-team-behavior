# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H17 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, or fire to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of it. An incident involving injury, property damage, evacuation, or an active fire must be reported within 24 hours. Reports must include the unit model, incident date and location, known consequences, and any immediate safety actions. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours of identifying a credible serious hazard, and otherwise within 5 days of a regulator report, with clear instructions for safe use and how to report an incident.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after any material design change. A failure rate above 5% is a regulatory review threshold, not an automatic prohibition on sale. Manufacturers must submit per-model methods, sample sizes, and results, including failure rates, confidentially to the relevant regulator. Before purchase, each prospective customer must receive the model’s per-model test results, including its failure rate, along with relevant test limitations, any identified safety concerns, and corrective actions or use instructions. This disclosure is made directly to prospective purchasers and does not require publication of individual-company per-model failure rates to the general public. For results above 5%, the manufacturer must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and risk assessment; the regulator may require additional testing, customer notices, or proportionate corrective action based on the assessment.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability beginning 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within 6 years of adoption. Shut-off must be accessible to the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety hazard, to emergency responders or the manufacturer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Customers must receive clear instructions on how to use the feature and its limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and designed to limit fire propagation and protect occupants and neighboring property. Manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and operational instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety instructions and information on how to report incidents. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of material newly identified hazards, with instructions for safe use and any required corrective action. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when a model exceeds 8 validated thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when the regulator determines that a defect presents an imminent or otherwise unacceptable risk. Incident counts and installed-unit denominators must be documented and made available to the regulator for validation. A recall is a corrective safety action, not an automatic permanent sales ban; under regulator oversight, the remedy must be proportionate to the validated hazard and may include repair, replacement, or refund. Recall notices must identify affected models, explain the hazard, state the remedy and next steps, and be sent promptly to affected customers and made publicly available. Customer notices must communicate safety risks and actions without publishing individual-company per-model test failure rates.
