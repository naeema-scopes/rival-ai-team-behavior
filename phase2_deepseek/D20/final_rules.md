# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D20 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the appropriate regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. Affected customers must be notified within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their unit or requires an immediate safety action. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and actions taken; follow-up findings must be provided promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample before sale, at least annually, and after material design changes. A failure rate above 5% is an action threshold requiring a corrective-action plan and retesting on a regulator-set schedule; it is not, by itself, an automatic sales ban or market-access cutoff. Regulators may require additional measures, including sales restrictions, based on the results, the adequacy of remediation, and evidence of safety risk. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the regulator, and provide those results and corrective actions to customers who purchase that model. Customer disclosure does not require public disclosure of individual-company per-model failure rates. Customers must also receive relevant safety notices and protective instructions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include a remotely accessible emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer with prompt customer notification. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to add this capability within six years of this standard’s effective date, where technically feasible, and provide customers a safe interim procedure until retrofit is complete.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard’s adoption. Manufacturers must disclose whether each model includes this feature and provide customers with clear instructions on its operation and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s safety status, known material thermal hazards, and clear safety, installation, maintenance, and emergency instructions. Customers who purchase a model must also be given its per-model 55 °C test results and any required corrective actions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified safety risks and required protective actions. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 model-specific installed-unit exposure during a year. The denominator must reflect that model’s installed fleet at risk over the reporting year, using a documented, regulator-reviewable method; manufacturers must document incident counting and attribution. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, pause sales while the regulator-approved recall remedy is implemented, and provide a remedy or safe removal plan. A recall is not, by itself, a permanent market ban; regulators may impose further restrictions based on risk and remediation.
