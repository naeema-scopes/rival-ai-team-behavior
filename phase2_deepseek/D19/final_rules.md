# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D19 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every fire, thermal runaway, or other confirmed thermal incident involving a home battery to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents with no injury or property damage. A preliminary report must identify the product/model, installation location, known consequences, and immediate safety steps; an updated report is due within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk, and promptly provide any updated instructions or warnings. Reports must not be withheld because an investigation is ongoing.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with a statistically adequate sample and documented results. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to regulators, including updated results after retesting, and provide each model’s results to customers who buy that model before purchase. During the first 60 months after adoption, a failure rate above 5% requires a regulator-approved corrective-action plan, interim protections proportionate to risk, and retesting on a regulator-set schedule. After that transition period, each model must meet a maximum failure rate of 5%. A rate above 5% does not by itself require withdrawal or a sales restriction; regulators should determine any such action based on verified incident records, the adequacy of protections, and progress under corrective-action plans. Public reporting may include aggregated data.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units placed on the market 12 months or more after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to support remote shut-off within 72 months after adoption where technically feasible; where infeasible, they must notify affected customers and regulators, explain the limitation, and provide an appropriate alternative emergency protection plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed and tested for the unit’s foreseeable thermal-fire hazards. Manufacturers must document validation testing and provide installation, maintenance, and emergency-response instructions. Regulators may approve an equivalent protective design where it provides comparable or better safety performance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear installation, operating, emergency-response, and maintenance instructions at purchase and in accessible digital form. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide updated precautions and repair information. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, using verified incident and installation data. The regulator shall supervise the recall and may require repair, replacement, or another effective remedy; a recall does not by itself require a sales ban. Regulators may impose sales restrictions or other protective action where needed to control risk, including where the severity or pattern of incidents warrants action before the trigger is met. Recall notices must explain the hazard, affected units, immediate precautions, and remedy.
