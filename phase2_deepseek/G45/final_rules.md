# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G45 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the regulator. Incidents involving injury, fire, or an immediate serious risk must be reported within 24 hours of discovery; other thermal incidents must be reported within five calendar days. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and any action taken. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and provide periodic updates and a final investigation summary to the regulator.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo an independent sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and adequate sample size before sale and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, including failure rates and test dates, to the regulator and to customers who buy that model. The regulator may also publish aggregate results. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt corrective action, regulator-approved retesting, and safety measures proportionate to the evidence; exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sales. The regulator may restrict or suspend a model where evidence shows an unacceptable safety risk. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of test results indicating a material safety risk and provide relevant risk information and protective steps.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market 12 months or more after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within six years after adoption where technically feasible, with documented exceptions and interim risk-mitigation instructions for units that cannot be retrofitted. Shut-off systems must be secure, reliable, and usable by authorized emergency responders and customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance and reliability requirements. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations to customers. The regulator may approve equivalent protective designs where they provide comparable fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices promptly when a model presents a material thermal risk, including the nature of the risk, affected models and serial numbers, steps to reduce risk, and how to obtain support. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers without delay, provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions, and report recall progress. A recall does not replace incident reporting or customers’ access to safety notices and other safety-relevant information.
