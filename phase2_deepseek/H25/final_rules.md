# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H25 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account and available information. They shall provide a complete incident report within 10 business days and promptly update it as material facts emerge. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when the incident indicates a material safety risk or requires customer action; otherwise, include it in the next scheduled safety communication. Reporting obligations apply regardless of suspected cause.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a harmonized 55°C sustained-heat test before market introduction and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol specifying sample size, duration, operating conditions, and failure criteria. A failure rate above 5% is an action threshold requiring prompt submission of results and a corrective-action plan to regulators, followed by retesting within 12 months; it is not by itself a prohibition on sale. Regulators may require proportionate interim safeguards or suspend sales where the evidence demonstrates an unacceptable risk. Per-model results and methodology shall be submitted to regulators and provided to customers who buy or own that model. Per-model failure rates shall not be publicly disclosed; public reporting may use anonymized or aggregated results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models shall include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units with this capability, or provide an equivalent effective remote emergency isolation method, within 60 months after adoption. Where a retrofit is technically infeasible, the manufacturer shall notify the owner and regulator, provide a safe alternative, and explain the limitation. Shut-off capability shall not replace required local protections or emergency response procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption. The suppression system shall be appropriate to the battery chemistry and enclosure, and validated through the applicable safety testing. Manufacturers shall document its performance and maintenance requirements and provide these to customers and regulators. This requirement does not replace other fire-prevention, detection, or reporting obligations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices promptly when a material risk affects their model, including the nature of the risk, recommended actions, and available remedies; urgent action notices shall be sent within 48 hours of determining that action is needed. Manufacturers shall notify regulators and affected customers when a recall or safety remedy is initiated. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and provide incident data and a corrective-action proposal. The regulator shall assess the evidence, including incident severity, trend, and effective remedies, and determine whether a mandatory recall or other corrective action is warranted. Incident counting and installed-unit methods shall be consistent. Public communications shall not disclose individual-company per-model test failure rates.
