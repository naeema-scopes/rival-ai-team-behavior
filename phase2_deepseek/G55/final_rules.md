# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G55 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account of known facts. They must provide a complete investigation report within 30 days, or explain the delay and provide periodic updates. Affected customers must be notified within 48 hours of learning of an incident involving their unit or a material risk to their safety. Reports must not be withheld because an incident is still under investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market introduction, at least every two years thereafter, and after a material design change. The failure rate must not exceed 7% of tested units for any model. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and corrective-action plans to the relevant regulator and provide the per-model results to customers buying or considering that model in clear pre-purchase safety information; these results are not for general-public publication. Models exceeding the 7% limit must not be placed on the market or continue to be sold until corrective action is verified by retesting. For models exceeding 5% but not 7%, manufacturers must submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator and retest within 12 months.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market after adoption must include a remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must enable that capability for installed units that support it and provide a safe retrofit or equivalent mitigation plan for other installed units within six years after adoption. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and the customer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation; it must not prevent safe local operation when communications are unavailable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation. The suppression system must be designed and validated to reduce the risk of fire spread and must not create an undue hazard during activation or maintenance. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices promptly when a newly identified hazard may affect their unit, including the nature of the risk, recommended precautions, and how to obtain updates or support. Notify affected customers within 48 hours of learning of a material safety risk, and provide updates when material information changes. A manufacturer must initiate a mandatory recall when a model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units. Calculate rates using installed-unit exposure and all known thermal incidents during the year, with supporting data submitted to the regulator for verification. Upon a recall, promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions.
