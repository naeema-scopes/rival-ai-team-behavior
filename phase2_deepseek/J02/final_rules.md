# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J02 · variant J · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 44})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. A manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their units, and provide prompt updates if the risk assessment changes. Suspected incidents must be investigated; confirmed reporting omissions must be corrected promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with sample size and failure rate recorded. A model’s failure rate must not exceed 8%. Manufacturers must provide per-model results to the regulator and make them available to customers who buy that model; individual per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. A model above the limit requires a corrective-action plan, regulator review, and retesting on a defined schedule. Regulators may restrict production or installation where evidence indicates serious risk. For installed units, manufacturers must promptly assess risk and implement regulator-approved protective measures.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models must include remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a secure, authenticated shut-off capability for installed units that support it, and must bring all other installed units into compliance within 72 months of adoption of this standard, unless a regulator approves an alternative safety measure where retrofit is infeasible. Shut-off must not prevent emergency responders or occupants from safely isolating the system locally. Manufacturers must maintain and test the capability and provide clear instructions for its use.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document suppression performance and maintenance requirements, and provide those instructions to customers. The requirement does not replace other applicable fire-safety measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices covering safe installation, operation, emergency isolation, and how to report a suspected incident, and must update affected customers promptly when material safety information changes. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on regulator-verified incident and installed-unit data. The incident count must include all verified thermal incidents, including incidents reported late or corrected after investigation; reporting omissions do not exclude incidents from the calculation. The manufacturer must notify the regulator promptly upon reaching the trigger, notify affected customers within 72 hours, and provide a timely, effective remedy plan, which may include repair, replacement, or refund. A recall requires an effective customer remedy and does not by itself require a permanent market ban; regulators may separately require interim protective measures or restrict sales where evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk, and may require a recall sooner on that basis.
