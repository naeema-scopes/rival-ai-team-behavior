# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A03 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any confirmed fire, thermal runaway, or overheating event involving a battery, including events causing injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response. Initial reports are due within 24 hours of learning of the event; a fuller report, including known circumstances and corrective actions, is due within 10 business days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and promptly provide updated instructions as facts develop. Reports shall include model, serial number where available, and incident date and location.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample size. A failure rate above 5% shall trigger prompt regulator review, validation of the test results, and a corrective-action plan; it shall not by itself automatically bar a model from sale. The regulator may require market restrictions where it determines the risk cannot be adequately mitigated. Manufacturers shall provide per-model test results, failure rates, and test methods to the regulator and to customers buying that model, before sale. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers when new test results materially change the model’s safety information.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders. Manufacturers shall provide clear instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For installed units lacking the capability, manufacturers shall offer a retrofit or equivalent safe remediation within 72 months after adoption, prioritizing units subject to a safety notice.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression, or an independently verified equivalent built-in measure that limits fire propagation, beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement shall not prevent regulators from requiring earlier corrective measures for a model presenting an imminent safety risk. Manufacturers shall document the effectiveness of the selected measure using a regulator-approved test protocol.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers shall receive clear safety and installation instructions, emergency-response guidance, and notice of material model-specific safety risks. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers when new information indicates a material risk, with instructions for safe use or discontinuation as appropriate. When a model exceeds 8 verified thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and provide incident records and denominator data for review. Rates shall use verified incidents involving that model during the reporting year divided by its installed units during that year; the regulator shall validate the records and denominator and assess the risk and available mitigations. A verified exceedance shall trigger a mandatory recall only when the regulator determines the risk presents a material danger that cannot be adequately mitigated by other measures. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of any required recall and its remedy.
