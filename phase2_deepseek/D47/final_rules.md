# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D47 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency service response to the responsible regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of it, and notify affected customers within 10 calendar days when the incident presents a material safety risk or requires customer action. Imminent risks requiring stop-use or other immediate protective action must be reported to regulators and affected customers without delay. Reports must include known circumstances, impacts, and corrective actions; manufacturers must promptly update incomplete reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and adequate, representative sampling. A model must meet a maximum failure rate of 5%. If a model exceeds that limit, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, submit its results, methods, and sample sizes, and implement regulator-supervised corrective action and retesting. The regulator shall determine any interim sales restrictions or other protective measures based on model-specific safety evidence, with immediate restrictions where needed to address imminent risk. Manufacturers must provide per-model results and material updates to the responsible regulator and customers buying that model. Per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market from 3 years after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units installed before that date, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or replacement pathway and complete required retrofits within 6 years after adoption. Where retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must notify the customer and regulator, provide an effective alternative safety measure, and document the exception.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured for sale 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance and maintenance requirements. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations to customers. Regulators may approve equivalent alternative protection where built-in suppression is technically infeasible, provided the alternative offers comparable risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and the responsible regulator of material safety risks, including thermal incidents, corrective actions, and any stop-use or service instructions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly submit incident data, installed-unit exposure calculations, and supporting methodology to the regulator. The rate must be model-specific and include all known thermal incidents, whether or not previously reported, using installed-unit exposure over the same period. Exceeding this threshold requires expedited regulator-supervised review; the regulator shall determine whether a recall or other protective measure is required based on the evidence. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of any required recall and provide clear protective instructions and details of required corrective measures. This requirement must not delay immediate protective action where risk is imminent.
