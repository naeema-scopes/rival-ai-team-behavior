# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E49 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving fire, injury, property damage, emergency evacuation, or emergency intervention within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary incident report. Other confirmed thermal incidents shall be reported within 5 business days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers of a serious incident involving their model within 72 hours of confirming the incident, and provide material safety updates as they become available. Reports shall include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and immediate safety measures; a complete investigation report is due within 30 days. Manufacturers shall maintain incident records and correct any omissions promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo standardized sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a documented, reproducible method and a representative sample. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt notification to the regulator, a regulator-approved corrective-action plan, and retesting within a regulator-set timeframe. Exceeding 5% shall not by itself require an automatic sales ban; the regulator may impose proportionate interim safeguards or restrict sales where necessary to address a demonstrated safety risk. Manufacturers shall provide per-model methods, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator and directly to current and prospective customers for that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public; industry reporting may use aggregated, non-identifying results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability. For units already installed when this standard is adopted, manufacturers shall provide a retrofit or other approved means of remote emergency shut-off within six years of adoption. Where a unit cannot reasonably be retrofitted, the manufacturer shall notify its owner and provide an equivalent safety measure approved by the regulator. Shut-off systems shall be secure, available to authorized emergency responders or operators as appropriate, and shall not prevent safe local operation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system shall be evaluated for effectiveness and shall not create an additional hazard under foreseeable installation and operating conditions. Manufacturers shall provide customers with instructions for inspection, maintenance, and safe response to an incident.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive model-specific sustained-heat test results, known material thermal hazards, safe installation and operating instructions, and clear steps for reporting an incident. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of any new material safety risk and provide required precautions and repair or replacement information. If a model exceeds 8 confirmed thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and submit the incident data and a proposed corrective-action plan for review. The regulator shall assess incident severity, evidence of a common cause, exposure, and the effectiveness and timeliness of proposed measures, and may require a recall where necessary to address an unacceptable risk. The regulator may require an earlier recall where incident severity or other evidence shows an imminent risk. Any recall shall include direct notice to affected customers and free repair, replacement, or refund.
