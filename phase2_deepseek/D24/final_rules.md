# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D24 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the regulator any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, property damage, or emergency response within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller investigation report within 30 days. Manufacturers shall maintain records of all thermal incidents, including incidents not meeting immediate-reporting criteria, and provide an annual summary to the regulator. Affected customers must be notified promptly when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or requires action; notices must include practical steps to reduce risk and a contact for assistance.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must be tested under a sustained-heat test at 55 °C. The failure rate must not exceed 5% of tested units for any model. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test methods and complete results to the regulator for compliance review; regulators shall keep company-specific failure rates confidential and shall not publish them. Before purchase, customers shall receive the exact per-model 55 °C test failure rate, pass/fail status, material safety limitations, any required corrective actions, and an explanation of the applicable 5% limit. Regulators shall assess exceedances individually and may require corrective action, retesting, or risk-based sales restrictions; exceedance alone does not require a permanent market ban. Any restriction shall be proportionate to the risk and reviewed after corrective action or retesting. Retesting is required after a material design or manufacturing change and at least annually.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off shall be required on all new home battery units from 24 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide a secure, tested shut-off capability and clear instructions for its use. For units already installed, manufacturers shall offer a retrofit or equivalent safe solution and complete it within 6 years after adoption. Where retrofit is infeasible, the manufacturer shall notify the owner and provide an alternative risk-reduction plan for regulator review.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to customers and regulators. This requirement does not apply retroactively to units already installed.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information covering relevant installation, operating, maintenance, emergency, and incident-reporting instructions, plus any model-specific safety limitations or corrective actions. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected owners and, where a hazard may affect nearby people, provide appropriate notice to relevant local authorities and the public. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; regulators may also order a recall where evidence indicates an equivalent serious safety risk. Manufacturers shall promptly report the basis for a recall and provide customers with a remedy and instructions.
