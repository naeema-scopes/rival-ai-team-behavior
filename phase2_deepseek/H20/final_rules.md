# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H20 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety risk for their model, and promptly provide any protective instructions. Reports shall identify the model and known circumstances. Manufacturers shall also submit an annual reconciliation of incidents and explain any incidents not previously reported.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a standardized 55 °C sustained-heat test before sale and at least annually, using a statistically representative sample and a common protocol. The failure rate shall not exceed 7% for any model. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results and supporting data to regulators and provide the results to customers of that model; individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be publicly published. A model exceeding the limit must be reported promptly to the regulator with a corrective-action plan and retested before continued production.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market 12 months or more after adoption shall include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units to provide that capability, where technically feasible, within 6 years after adoption. The shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation; customers shall be informed how it works and how to request activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression designed to limit propagation of thermal events. The system shall be validated under the applicable standardized safety tests, maintained for the expected service life, and accompanied by customer instructions on its operation and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers clear safety notices for their model, including known hazards, protective steps, and material updates, within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety risk, and promptly update notices as facts change. A verified rate above 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units for any model in a year, calculated from complete incident and installed-unit data, shall trigger an expedited regulator review, completed within 30 days. The regulator shall assess incident severity, corrective measures, and available test results, and may require interim protections. The regulator shall order a recall when the evidence shows an unreasonable serious risk that cannot be adequately addressed by lesser measures. Manufacturers shall submit data needed for verification and cooperate. The regulator may also order a recall or other corrective measures where evidence shows an unreasonable serious risk. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of any recall and provide clear instructions.
