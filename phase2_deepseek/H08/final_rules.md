# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H08 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 business days. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their model, including interim precautions and available support. Reports shall identify the model and incident circumstances, while protecting personal information. Suspected incidents shall be reported; manufacturers shall not delay reporting pending confirmation of cause.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before initial market release and after a material thermal-design change. The maximum allowed failure rate is 8% per model. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model results, including the exact failure rate, test methods, and sample size, to the relevant regulator and to customers purchasing that model; exact rates shall not be publicly published. A failure rate above 5% triggers a corrective-action plan, interim safeguards as appropriate, and follow-up testing within a regulator-approved period. A model above 5% but at or below 8% shall not be subject to automatic sales suspension solely for exceeding 5% while complying with an approved corrective-action plan; the regulator may impose safeguards or restrict sales where evidence indicates an unreasonable safety risk. A model exceeding 8% shall not be marketed unless and until it meets the limit or the regulator authorizes otherwise based on evidence and adequate safeguards. The regulator shall review updated results and determine whether further action is warranted.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed home battery systems shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 24 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide a secure, reliable means for an authorized user or emergency responder to initiate shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers shall provide compatible remote shut-off capability within 72 months where technically feasible, and shall inform owners of any limitations and available alternatives. Shut-off shall not replace required local safety protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Suppression systems shall be designed and validated for the battery chemistry and credible failure modes, and shall not create additional hazards. Manufacturers shall provide installation, maintenance, and end-of-life instructions for the suppression system.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety and operating instructions at purchase and prompt notice of any confirmed safety risk that could affect their unit, including interim precautions and available corrective actions. Regulators shall be notified promptly of thermal incidents and material emerging hazards. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, submit model-specific incident data and a corrective-action plan, and provide interim precautions. The regulator shall promptly assess the model-specific evidence and the adequacy of the plan. A recall and remedy shall be mandatory if the plan is inadequate, is not implemented on schedule, or interim measures are insufficient to protect customers and the public. The trigger applies to model-specific rates, not company-wide rates.
