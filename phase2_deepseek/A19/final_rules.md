# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A19 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal runaway, fire, explosion, or injury involving a home battery to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it. They must notify affected customers within 5 calendar days, including known hazards, immediate precautions, and how to obtain support. A manufacturer must provide a complete incident record to the regulator within 30 days, including cases initially reported by customers or third parties.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market placement and after a material design or manufacturing change, using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample. Results and corrective-action plans must be submitted confidentially to the relevant regulator and provided to customers who buy that model, on request and before purchase. A failure rate above 5% triggers regulator review and a documented corrective-action plan; it does not by itself prohibit market placement. Regulators may require additional testing, corrective action, or restrictions based on test results and incident evidence, with restrictions proportionate to the risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market from 3 years after this standard takes effect must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer guidance. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete retrofits, where technically feasible, within 6 years after the effective date. Customers must be informed of any unit that cannot be retrofitted and of available safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units placed on the market from 36 months after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression designed and tested to contain or limit battery fires without creating additional hazards. Manufacturers must document the system’s performance and maintenance requirements, provide these to customers, and submit supporting evidence to the relevant regulator. The regulator may approve an equivalent protective design where it provides comparable fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its 55 °C sustained-heat test result, relevant incident history, emergency instructions, and any required maintenance or limitations. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of new material safety risks and provide practical steps to reduce risk. When a model’s thermal incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the relevant regulator and provide incident records and a corrective-action proposal. The regulator must review the evidence, including the completeness of incident reporting and the installed-unit denominator, and may require additional monitoring, corrective action, restrictions, or a recall proportionate to the risk. Exceeding the threshold requires review but does not by itself mandate a recall. Regulators may also require a recall or equivalent protective action at lower rates where the evidence warrants it. Any recall must include prompt customer notice, safe-use or shutdown instructions, and arrangements for repair, replacement, or refund.
