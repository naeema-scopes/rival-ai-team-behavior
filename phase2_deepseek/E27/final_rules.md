# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E27 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, or fire in a home battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents not yet confirmed to have caused a fire. Within 72 hours, notify customers whose units may present a related safety risk, with interim safety instructions where appropriate. Submit a complete incident report, including investigation findings and corrective actions, within 30 days; update it when material facts change.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a common, independently witnessed protocol. A failure rate of 5% is the safety benchmark for each model. Manufacturers must promptly submit test results and a corrective-action and monitoring plan to the regulator, investigate failures, and retest after corrective action. Exceeding the benchmark does not by itself automatically bar new placements; regulators may impose interim sales restrictions or other protective measures based on the test evidence and risk. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results, including failure rates and test conditions, to regulators and to customers who buy or own that model. Publish the protocol and aggregate industry results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off no later than 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within 6 years after adoption, prioritizing units with known safety risks. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation; customers must receive notice and instructions. Manufacturers must report retrofit progress to regulators every six months until complete.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations in customer safety materials.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Provide customers, before purchase and throughout ownership, accessible safety notices covering the model’s compliance status, known thermal risks, relevant incident trends, and safe-use and emergency instructions. Provide each model’s complete thermal-test results, including its failure rate and test conditions, to customers who buy or own that model, as required by S2; public notices may use aggregate results. Notify affected customers promptly when a newly identified risk may require protective action, and provide clear instructions and updates. A manufacturer must initiate a safety recall when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period; report the trigger and recall actions to regulators and notify affected customers without delay. A recall requires protective action and regulator-approved corrective measures; it does not by itself permanently prohibit the model from the market, subject to applicable regulatory decisions and verification of corrective action.
