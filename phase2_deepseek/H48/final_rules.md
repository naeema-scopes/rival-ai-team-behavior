# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H48 · variant H · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 47})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving a battery, including overheating, smoke, or fire, to the regulator. Incidents involving fire, injury, evacuation, emergency-service response, or material property damage must be reported within 24 hours of becoming aware of them; all other thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. A written follow-up is due within 10 business days for 24-hour reports. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 5 business days after determining that customer action is needed. Reports must include known facts, units and models affected, and actions taken; updates are required as information develops. Records of all incidents and reports must be retained for at least 10 years.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo an independently witnessed sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after a material design or manufacturing change. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results, including test method, sample size, and exact failure rate, to the regulator confidentially. Before purchase, customers considering that model must receive the test method, sample size, whether the model exceeds 5%, and any required safety actions or restrictions; exact per-model failure rates need not be disclosed to customers or published publicly. A model exceeding 5% must undergo corrective action and retesting; the regulator will review complete test and field evidence to determine whether further restrictions are warranted.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or replacement option and complete the retrofit within 6 years, where technically feasible. Customers must be notified of any limitations and safe interim procedures. Shut-off must not disable safety monitoring or prevent emergency services from following safe procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide evidence to the regulator that the suppression system is suitable for the battery chemistry and installation, and instructions for inspection and maintenance. Units installed before that date are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety instructions at purchase and promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks, incident patterns, updated safe-use guidance, and any action needed; notices must be accessible and identify affected models and serial ranges. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must notify the regulator and affected customers promptly when the trigger is met, explain the basis and scope, and provide a remedy and safe interim instructions. The regulator may require a recall below that threshold where the evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
