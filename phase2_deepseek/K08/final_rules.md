# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K08 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal runaway, fire, or event requiring emergency-service response to the regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 10 calendar days. Reports must identify the affected model, incident date and known circumstances, and be updated as material facts emerge. The manufacturer must notify the affected household within 24 hours for an active hazard or within 5 calendar days for other confirmed incidents, with practical safety instructions. Protect personal information; regulators may request supporting records.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a regulator-approved sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a statistically adequate, documented sample. Results and failure rates must be submitted to the regulator and disclosed to customers who buy that model. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and undergo a retest on a regulator-approved schedule; regulators may impose proportionate safeguards based on test results and other safety evidence. Public disclosure beyond affected customers and regulators is not required. Customer notices must include practical safety information and any corrective actions relevant to their units.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include a remote emergency shut-off function beginning 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a secure, tested means for authorized emergency shut-off and disclose its operation and limitations to customers. For installed units lacking the function, manufacturers must provide a feasible retrofit or equivalent risk-mitigation plan within 72 months after adoption, prioritizing units with known hazards. Shut-off must not prevent a household from using local controls or contacting emergency services.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must validate the system’s performance and compatibility with the battery, document maintenance requirements, and disclose limitations and emergency instructions to customers. Regulators may require earlier, targeted safeguards for a model presenting an immediate serious hazard.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, provide customers clear written information on thermal risks, installation and maintenance requirements, emergency actions, and how to report a safety concern. Promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material hazards and provide updated instructions and any required corrective action. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, or when the regulator determines that a serious unreasonable hazard otherwise warrants recall. Notify regulators and affected customers without delay and implement the recall under regulator oversight.
