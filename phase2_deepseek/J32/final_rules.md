# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J32 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal runaway, fire, explosion, or injury involving a covered battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 calendar days. They shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a material risk requiring an immediate safety action; otherwise, notify affected customers within 10 days. Reports must include known circumstances, unit/model identification, and any containment steps. Suspected incidents shall be reported even before the cause is confirmed; updates may follow as the investigation proceeds.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model shall undergo a standardized 55 °C sustained-heat test before sale and after a material design or manufacturing change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% of tested units. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test protocols and results to the relevant regulator and provide the results to customers considering or owning that model; per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. A model exceeding the limit shall promptly submit a corrective-action plan and retesting schedule to the regulator. The regulator shall determine proportionate interim safeguards and whether sales may continue during corrective action, considering the test findings and risks to users and the public. The manufacturer shall implement approved corrective actions and retest within the regulator-approved period; the regulator may restrict sales where necessary to address an imminent serious risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New covered battery models placed on the market 36 months or more after adoption of this standard shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide an over-the-air upgrade where feasible and safe. Existing installed units that support the function shall have it enabled by default, with clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall assess installed units that do not support remote shut-off and provide a safe retrofit or replacement plan within 36 months of adoption. Any required retrofit or replacement of an already-installed unit shall have a compliance deadline no earlier than six years after adoption; customers shall be informed if no safe retrofit is available. Shut-off shall not prevent emergency services from safely isolating a unit.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Covered battery units manufactured for sale 36 months or more after adoption of this standard shall include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to limit propagation and reduce hazards to occupants and neighbours. Manufacturers shall provide evidence of suppression-system testing to the relevant regulator and disclose its capabilities and limitations to customers. This requirement does not replace thermal-safety testing, incident reporting, or other required protective measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and throughout ownership, customers shall receive accessible safety notices describing material hazards, required installation and maintenance, emergency response steps, and any applicable software or shut-off features. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and provide updates as investigations proceed. A manufacturer shall initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when the regulator determines a defect presents an imminent serious risk; the manufacturer shall notify the regulator and affected customers without delay and provide a corrective-action plan. The regulator shall oversee the recall, determine its appropriate scope and safeguards, and specify the evidence and verification required before the recall may be ended or affected units returned to service. Per-model safety test results shall be provided to customers considering or owning that model, consistent with S2.
