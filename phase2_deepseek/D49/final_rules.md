# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D49 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving fire, smoke, overheating, injury, property damage, or emergency shutdown to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and submit a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. Notify affected customers within 24 hours when the incident indicates a material hazard to their unit or requires immediate protective action; otherwise notify them within 5 business days. Reports must include the model, event circumstances, known impacts, and actions taken. Maintain incident records and promptly update regulators and affected customers when material facts change.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must report results, sample size, and uncertainty to the responsible regulator and provide the per-model results to customers who buy that model; these results may not be withheld from those customers as confidential. Individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public; regulators may publish anonymized or aggregated findings. An interim maximum failure rate of 7% applies per model. A model exceeding the limit must promptly notify the regulator and submit a corrective-action plan, with regulator-approved restrictions or other protective measures where warranted. Review the limit as evidence accumulates, including whether test results predict real-world incidents.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must support remote emergency shut-off, subject to safeguards against unauthorized activation and a safe local shut-off option. Existing installed units must be retrofitted or replaced to support the function within six years of this standard taking effect, where technically feasible; document any infeasibility and interim risk controls for the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment, and must be designed and verified to reduce fire propagation without creating an unreasonable risk of injury or property damage. Manufacturers must document verification and maintenance requirements and provide them to installers and customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Provide customers clear installation and operating safety instructions, warning signs of overheating, emergency steps, and prompt notice of any newly identified material hazard affecting their unit. Notify affected customers and the regulator promptly when corrective action or a safety notice is warranted. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, calculated for that model. The recall must provide an effective protective remedy for affected units, subject to regulator oversight; it does not by itself require a sales ban. Also recall or take equivalent regulator-approved protective action when a serious hazard otherwise presents an unacceptable risk. Track incidents and notify customers of recall actions without delay.
