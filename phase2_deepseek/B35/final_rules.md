# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B35 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents initially reported by customers or installers. A preliminary report must identify the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and immediate safety actions; material updates are due within 30 days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 48 hours after determining that a product or condition presents a material safety risk. Records and final incident analyses must be retained and made available to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results to the regulator and disclose the results for a model to customers who buy or are offered that model. Regulators may publish aggregated or anonymized results. The parties recognize the currently discussed maximum failure rate of 5% per model; a model exceeding that limit must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and be retested within six months, with regulator-directed interim protections. Exceeding the limit alone does not automatically require market withdrawal.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models must include a remotely operable emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to support remote emergency shut-off, where technically feasible, within six years after adoption; where infeasible, they must notify affected customers and regulators, explain the limitation, and provide an equivalent protective measure. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must document suppression performance and provide the information to regulators and customers; regulators may grant narrowly tailored, time-limited exemptions where equivalent fire protection is demonstrated.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices for incidents, test failures, corrective actions, and any material change in risk, including instructions for safe use and support contacts. Notify affected customers within 48 hours after determining a material safety risk, and promptly update them as material facts change. A recall is mandatory when a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period; notify regulators and affected customers promptly, and no later than 48 hours after confirming the threshold is exceeded. Regulators may require an earlier recall or other protective action where evidence shows an imminent risk. Manufacturers must publish recall scope, remedy, and completion updates.
