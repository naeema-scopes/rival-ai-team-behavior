# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D03 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written incident report including known circumstances and affected model within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential material risk to their unit, and provide updates as material facts emerge. Reports shall not be withheld because an investigation is ongoing; manufacturers may supplement them as facts are confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for each model; a model above that limit shall not be newly marketed until corrective action and retesting demonstrate compliance. Manufacturers shall submit test methods, sample sizes, and results to the responsible regulator. Per-model results shall be provided directly to customers who buy that model and to prospective customers before purchase. Results shall not be published or otherwise made available for general public access; regulators may disclose aggregated or anonymized results. Regulators may require additional testing or safeguards where results indicate a material safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where an imminent safety risk warrants it, by the manufacturer with prompt customer notification. Manufacturers shall provide a secure, no-cost retrofit for installed units lacking this capability within 6 years of adoption. Shut-off shall not prevent local manual isolation or emergency responder access.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system shall be suitable for the battery chemistry and enclosure, and shall be tested under regulator-approved conditions to verify activation and effectiveness. Manufacturers shall provide installation and maintenance instructions to customers and make relevant test results available to the responsible regulator.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide affected customers timely safety notices identifying the model, known hazard, recommended precautions, and available corrective actions, and shall update notices as material facts emerge. A manufacturer shall promptly notify the responsible regulator and affected customers when test results or incident information indicate a material safety risk. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop sale of the affected model while the recall is in effect, and provide an effective remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund, subject to regulator oversight. A recall does not constitute a permanent market ban: sale may resume when the manufacturer demonstrates to the regulator that corrective action has addressed the risk and the model meets applicable safety requirements.
