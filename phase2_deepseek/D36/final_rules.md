# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D36 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with an initial account of the event and affected model. A complete investigation report, including known root cause and corrective actions, is due within 30 days; material new findings must be submitted promptly. Within 72 hours, manufacturers must notify affected customers of incidents presenting an ongoing safety risk and provide appropriate precautions. Reports must include unit counts and model identification, while protecting personal information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A failure rate above 5% is a regulatory action threshold, not an automatic sales ban. Manufacturers must provide per-model results and supporting test data to regulators before sale and after material design changes, and update results at least annually. Regulators must protect submitted results from public disclosure, subject to applicable law. Customers who buy or own a model must receive that model’s numerical failure rate and relevant test results, together with any applicable safety notices and precautions. A model exceeding 5% must be reported promptly and undergo corrective action and retesting within six months. Regulators may require proportionate interim risk controls, including restrictions on new sales, where the evidence demonstrates they are warranted.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market more than 12 months after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible shut-off option. Manufacturers must provide a secure software or hardware retrofit for installed units that can support the feature within 72 months after adoption, and must inform owners of compatible units how to enable it. Where remote shut-off is unavailable or unsafe for a unit, manufacturers must provide customers and regulators with the reason and an alternative emergency response procedure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation environment, validated under a regulator-approved test protocol. Manufacturers must document the system’s performance and maintenance requirements for regulators and provide those instructions to customers. Regulators may require an earlier safety measure for a specific model where evidence demonstrates an imminent risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety instructions, incident reporting contacts, and any model-specific safety notice or precaution within 72 hours after determining it is relevant to their unit. Notices must explain the hazard, affected models or serial ranges, steps customers should take, and how to obtain support; urgent risks require direct communication without delay. Verified data showing that any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling year must be promptly reported to regulators and affected customers and triggers a mandatory recall. The manufacturer must promptly implement the recall remedy required by the regulator. Regulators must set the recall scope and remedy to address the demonstrated risk, which may include repair, replacement, or refund, and may require interim sales restrictions or broader measures where warranted.
