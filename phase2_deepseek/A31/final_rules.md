# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A31 · variant A · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Halcyon': 42})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours, with known immediate precautions and a contact for further information. A follow-up report with the investigation findings and any corrective action is due within 30 days.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample before sale and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers must provide regulators with per-model results, including the measured failure rate and material corrective-action plans. Customers must receive the measured failure rate and relevant safety information for the model they buy. Regulators must keep individual-company per-model failure rates confidential and must not publish them or otherwise disclose them to the general public. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. A model exceeding 5% requires prompt corrective action and regulator-directed follow-up testing; exceedance is not an automatic ban. Regulators may restrict sale if required corrective action is not completed or the model presents an unacceptable risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must enable the function on compatible installed units within six years after adoption. For units that cannot be upgraded, manufacturers must notify affected customers and regulators within 12 months and provide a documented mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression meeting applicable regulator-approved performance requirements. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system and its limitations in customer materials.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices describing material hazards, required precautions, and available corrective actions, including promptly when new safety information could affect safe use. A model must be recalled when its thermal incidents exceed 8 per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period. Manufacturers must notify affected customers and regulators promptly, provide safe-use or shutdown instructions, and describe the remedy and recall progress.
