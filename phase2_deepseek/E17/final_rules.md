# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E17 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the appropriate regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must promptly notify the affected customer and, where there may be a hazard to nearby people or property, provide appropriate safety instructions to customers and relevant emergency responders. Reports must include known incident details and be updated as the investigation develops. Reporting obligations apply even when the cause is not yet established.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to regulators, and provide the results for a model to its current customers and prospective customers in a clear, accessible form. Results need not be released to the general public, except as required for enforcement or to protect against an imminent hazard; the regulator may publish anonymized or aggregated results. A model with a failure rate above 5% must be reported to the regulator and placed under a regulator-approved, time-bound corrective-action plan, including retesting and any interim safeguards the regulator determines necessary. Manufacturers must notify affected customers when a specific hazard or protective action warrants notice. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sale; the regulator may restrict or suspend sales if needed to address an unacceptable risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation plan; the completion deadline must be at least six years after this standard takes effect, with priority given to higher-risk units and progress reported annually to regulators.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard is adopted. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions on the system’s operation, limitations, and maintenance, and report any suppression-system failures associated with a thermal incident to the regulator.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of safety hazards and provide clear instructions on safe use, mitigation, and access to support. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, using regulator-validated incident and installation data, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and conduct a regulator-supervised risk assessment. The regulator must require a time-bound corrective-action plan and may require a recall when the assessment finds that a recall is necessary to address an unacceptable risk, including where corrective actions cannot adequately mitigate the hazard. Recall notices must explain the hazard, affected units, immediate precautions, and available repair, replacement, or refund. Manufacturers must also notify relevant emergency responders where a hazard may affect the public.
