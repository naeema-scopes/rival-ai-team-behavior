# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J38 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, evacuation, or material property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with an initial account of known facts. A complete investigation report, including root cause and corrective actions, is due within 30 days; material new findings must be submitted within 5 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk for their model, and provide updates when material information changes. All other thermal incidents must be reported to regulators within 5 business days and included in periodic customer safety reporting. Reports must not be delayed because the cause is uncertain.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample size. Full per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions must be provided confidentially to regulators. Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s latest numerical 55 °C failure rate, test date, and any unresolved corrective-action plan or material safety limitations. Individual-company per-model results must not be published to the general public; public reporting may include non-identifying aggregate results. A model with a failure rate above 5% must submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and undergo retesting on a regulator-approved schedule. A model above 8% may not be newly sold or installed unless and until the regulator approves corrective action and a passing retest.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed models must support remote emergency shut-off beginning 3 years after this standard takes effect, subject to regulator-approved cybersecurity and safe-operation requirements. For units already installed when the standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation plan, with completion no earlier than 6 years after the effective date. Regulators may approve alternatives where retrofit is impracticable or creates additional safety risks.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured for installation must include built-in fire suppression, or a regulator-approved equivalent protection system, beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The system must be validated for the battery chemistry and installation configuration and maintained according to manufacturer instructions. Regulators may approve an alternative where testing demonstrates equivalent or greater protection, or where built-in suppression creates additional safety risks. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive material safety limitations, installation and emergency instructions, the model’s latest numerical 55 °C test failure rate and test date, and any unresolved corrective-action plan. Manufacturers must promptly notify owners of material newly identified risks and provide clear instructions for safe use, service, or shutdown. Per-model test results must not be withheld from customers who buy that model. A model must be recalled when its thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units in a year, calculated using regulator-approved methods. The manufacturer must notify regulators and affected customers and promptly implement regulator-directed risk controls, which may include repair or replacement. A required recall is not, by itself, an automatic permanent market ban; continued sale is permitted only if the regulator determines it is safe. Regulators may require additional or broader corrective action where evidence indicates an imminent risk, regardless of the numerical trigger.
