# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H53 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary incident report within 5 business days. Affected customers must be notified within 48 hours when the incident indicates a material safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Manufacturers must submit a follow-up report with investigation findings and corrective actions within 30 days, or explain why more time is needed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. Per-model results, including failure rates and corrective actions, must be provided to the regulator and to customers who buy that model. Aggregate, non-identifying results may be published. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must submit a corrective-action plan and retest within 12 months. The regulator may allow up to 36 months from the initial test for a model to meet the 5% limit, with interim risk controls, monitoring, and sales restrictions as warranted by the risk. The regulator may require faster action where the results or incident history indicate an urgent safety risk. Manufacturers must notify affected customers of material safety risks and recommended actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway for already-installed units and complete required retrofits within 6 years of this standard taking effect, prioritizing units presenting the greatest safety risk. Any shut-off must preserve emergency responder access and notify the customer when practicable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide these to customers and regulators. The regulator may grant a narrowly tailored, time-limited extension where a manufacturer demonstrates that compliance is not yet feasible and equivalent risk controls are in place.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices promptly when a newly identified risk affects safe installation, operation, or maintenance, including recommended actions and updates as investigations progress. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and submit a model-specific risk assessment and corrective-action plan. The regulator must promptly review the evidence, including incident severity, test results, and corrective-action effectiveness, and require a recall when the model presents an unreasonable safety risk that cannot be adequately addressed through warnings, repairs, or other corrective measures. Regulators may require a recall below that rate where the same safety-risk standard is met. Manufacturers must report model-level incident counts and installed-unit denominators to regulators so rates can be assessed consistently. Regulators may also require additional reporting, sales restrictions, or other protective measures as warranted.
