# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D23 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every battery-related overheating, fire, or thermal runaway incident to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it, and immediately, within 24 hours, when the incident involves a fire, injury, death, evacuation, or material property damage. Reports shall include the unit/model identification, incident date and known consequences, and any immediate safety measures; unknown details may be supplemented as the investigation proceeds. Manufacturers shall notify the affected customer within 5 business days, or within 24 hours for the serious incidents listed above, and provide any immediate safe-use instructions. Suspected incidents shall be reported as such; reporting does not depend on confirmation of root cause.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A model passes if no more than 6.5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and failure rates confidentially to the regulator and provide the results to customers who buy that model, before purchase where practicable and promptly after testing or retesting. Individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public. The regulator may publish aggregated results that do not identify a company or model. Manufacturers shall retest after a material design or production change. A model exceeding the limit shall be subject to regulator-supervised corrective action and follow-up testing.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall submit a regulator-approved retrofit plan for installed units that do not support the function, prioritizing units with elevated safety risks, and complete retrofits within 72 months after adoption. Shut-off capability shall be tested and documented, and customers shall receive instructions for using it.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document compliance using a regulator-approved verification method.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices describing safe use, installation, maintenance, emergency response, and incident reporting, and promptly update notices when material safety information changes. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers, investigate, submit a corrective-action plan, and provide interim safety instructions. The regulator shall verify the rate. If the verified rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 in a year, the manufacturer shall initiate a recall of the affected model. The regulator shall specify the recall’s scope, remedy, and completion reporting; it may approve a remedy that permits continued sale only as part of the recall response, if the remedy effectively addresses the identified risk and affected customers are notified. Such approval does not waive the recall trigger.
