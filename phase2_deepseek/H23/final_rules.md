# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H23 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency shutdown to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with an initial account of known facts. They must provide affected customers with a safety notice within 48 hours when the incident presents a potential risk to their unit or household. A detailed incident report, including known cause and corrective actions, is due to the regulator within 30 days; material new findings must be reported promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least once every three years thereafter, using a documented and consistent sampling protocol. A model’s failure rate must not exceed 5% of tested units. A model exceeding this limit must undergo corrective action and retesting before further units are placed on the market. Manufacturers must submit identified per-model results, including failure rates and test dates, to the relevant regulator and provide those results to customers of that model. Regulators may publish aggregate results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must provide a secure retrofit or equivalent remote shut-off capability for units already installed within six years of adoption, where technically feasible, and notify customers of any limitations and available protective alternatives.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression for units manufactured on or after three years from adoption. Manufacturers must document that the suppression system is suitable for the unit and provide customers with instructions for its operation and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for their model, including applicable installation and maintenance requirements, known material hazards, and instructions for reporting incidents. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified safety risks and corrective actions, and provide updated notices when material information changes. A manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers when a model presents a material safety risk. Regulators shall determine whether a recall or other corrective action is required based on the totality of evidence, including incident rates, severity, testing, trends, and the adequacy of corrective actions. Regulators retain authority to require a recall or other corrective action whenever the evidence warrants it.
