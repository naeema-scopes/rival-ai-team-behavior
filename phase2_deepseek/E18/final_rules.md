# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E18 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a full incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when the incident presents a credible safety risk, and promptly provide safety instructions and material updates. Reports must include known product, model, installation, and incident details; manufacturers must update incomplete reports as facts become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt regulator notification, independent confirmatory testing, and a corrective-action plan on a regulator-set timeline; it is not, by itself, an automatic sales ban. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, sample sizes, and methods to the regulator and make them available to customers who purchase or own that model; public publication is not required. Pending review, the regulator may allow sales subject to interim safeguards and a time-bound remediation plan where it finds no imminent safety risk. The regulator may impose restrictions or other protective measures sooner if it identifies an imminent safety risk, and may act after review where confirmed results or the circumstances indicate an unacceptable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support this function within 6 years of adoption, prioritizing units with elevated risk and providing a documented retrofit schedule. Shut-off must be usable by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety hazard, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under safeguards against unauthorized use. Customers must receive clear instructions and notice before any remote shut-off.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation, independently validated, and maintained according to the manufacturer's instructions. Manufacturers must disclose its capabilities and limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety instructions, known material risks, installation and maintenance requirements, and emergency contact information. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material hazards and provide actionable safety guidance. When a model’s thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units during any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting data and methodology. The rate must include all known qualifying incidents, whether or not previously reported, and use the installed-unit denominator. The regulator must promptly verify the rate and assess the risk. Upon confirmation of an exceedance, the manufacturer must promptly notify current owners and provide actionable safety guidance while the regulator conducts a time-bound risk review and requires a corrective-action plan. A confirmed exceedance does not, by itself, automatically trigger a recall. The regulator must order a recall and customer notification, with a remedy and safety instructions, when its review finds the model presents an unacceptable safety risk. The regulator may require an immediate recall before verification where it identifies an imminent safety risk.
