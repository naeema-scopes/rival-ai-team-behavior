# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A35 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, evacuation, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. Other confirmed thermal incidents shall be reported within 10 business days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and promptly provide updated information as the investigation proceeds. Reports shall include available unit and model identification, incident circumstances, and known injuries or damage.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before initial market placement and after a material design or manufacturing change. A model passes only if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers shall provide full per-model results, test method, sample size, and test date to the regulator and directly to customers considering purchase of that model; individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public. Regulators may publish aggregated or anonymized results. A model exceeding 5% shall undergo corrective action and a passing retest before further market placement.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units to provide remote emergency shut-off, where technically feasible, within 6 years after adoption. Where retrofit is not feasible, the manufacturer shall notify affected customers and the regulator and provide an equivalent documented emergency response measure. Shut-off capability must not prevent emergency responders or users from safely isolating a unit locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any relevant limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive clear instructions for safe installation, operation, and emergency response, plus any model-specific safety precautions. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks, incidents, corrective actions, and recall instructions, using direct contact where practicable. For each model, the annual thermal-incident rate shall be calculated as confirmed thermal incidents during the preceding 12 months per 100,000 units of that model installed and in service during that period; manufacturers shall report the underlying incident and installed-unit counts to regulators. If a model’s rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers and submit a corrective-action plan for regulator review. A mandatory recall is triggered if the rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or if the regulator determines that a serious imminent risk requires recall. Recall remedies shall include repair, replacement, or refund.
