# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K52 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written incident report and available preliminary facts within 5 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a potential safety risk to their model or installation, and provide updates as material facts become available. Reports must include known model, serial number, location, incident circumstances, injuries or property damage, and any immediate safety actions; manufacturers must supplement incomplete reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must provide per-model test methods, sample sizes, and complete results, including failure rates, to the relevant regulator. Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s numerical failure rate, test method and sample size, and a clear explanation of material safety implications; updated results must be provided to current customers when materially changed. Results may be withheld from the general public except as required by regulators. A failure rate above 5% requires regulator review, a documented corrective-action plan, and retesting on a regulator-set schedule. A test result alone does not automatically prohibit sale; regulators shall consider test uncertainty, incident evidence, and feasible risk controls when determining compliance and any market restrictions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where an imminent hazard warrants it, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under documented safeguards. Manufacturers must provide a secure, accessible method for activation and clear customer instructions. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance, where technically feasible, within 6 years of adoption; if retrofitting is infeasible, the manufacturer must provide an equivalent documented emergency-safety plan and notify the affected customer.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Regulators may approve an equivalent or alternative protective design where evidence demonstrates comparable or better fire-risk reduction. Manufacturers must disclose the system’s suppression or approved alternative protection features to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, prospective customers must receive clear safety information for the model they are considering, covering its numerical 55 °C test failure rate, test method and sample size, material safety implications, incident-reporting contact details, emergency shut-off instructions, and any applicable safety notices. Current customers must receive materially updated test results and prompt notice of material safety risks, with updates and corrective instructions. Individual company per-model test failure rates must not be published to the general public; full results must be provided to regulators, and model-specific results to prospective purchasers and current customers of that model. Regulators shall review model-level thermal incident rates. A model with a verified rate exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period must be recalled. Regulators shall verify the data and methodology, and manufacturers must report the data and methodology used to calculate the rate. Pending verification and implementation of a recall, regulators may require interim protective measures; manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and provide necessary safety instructions.
