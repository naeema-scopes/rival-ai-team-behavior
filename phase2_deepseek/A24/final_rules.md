# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A24 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
A thermal incident means an unintended overheating event, fire, or thermal runaway involving a battery, whether or not it causes injury or property damage. Manufacturers shall report every such incident to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of it, and immediately (within 24 hours) where there is a fire, injury, evacuation, or credible imminent risk. They shall provide affected customers with a safety notice within 5 calendar days of determining that the incident may indicate a product hazard, and promptly update the regulator and customers as material facts emerge. Reports shall include known model, serial number, location, event description, and consequences; missing information shall be supplemented when available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo the specified sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers shall provide per-model results, including the failure rate and test protocol, to the relevant regulator and to customers purchasing that model before purchase, retain supporting records, and update results promptly after any retest. Per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt notice to the regulator and affected customers, a corrective-action plan, and a retest schedule; it does not by itself prohibit sale or require a market ban. Regulators may require proportionate further measures based on evidence and risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall include remote emergency shut-off capability from the date of adoption. Manufacturers shall enable and maintain the capability, provide customers with clear instructions for its use, and ensure that authorized emergency responders can request shut-off when there is a credible imminent safety risk. Existing installed units shall be brought into compliance within 6 years of adoption where technically feasible; manufacturers shall notify owners of units that cannot be upgraded and explain available risk-reduction measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system shall be designed and validated to reduce the risk of fire propagation and protect occupants and neighbouring members of the public, and shall be maintained in accordance with manufacturer instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices about known hazards, safe operation, required maintenance, and any material change in risk, and shall promptly update notices as material facts emerge. For calculating annual model-specific thermal incident rates, count thermal incidents as defined in S1 and use a defined 12-month period and verified installed-unit denominators; document the incident count, period, and denominator for regulators. A manufacturer shall initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers shall notify the relevant regulator and affected customers promptly upon meeting the recall trigger and provide instructions for remedy, replacement, or safe decommissioning. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action below the trigger where the evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
