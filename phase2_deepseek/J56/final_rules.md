# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J56 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential product safety risk, and promptly provide safety instructions. Reports must include known model and unit identifiers, circumstances, and corrective actions. Suspected incidents shall be reported as such; updates may follow as facts are confirmed. Manufacturers shall reconcile incident records against available customer complaints, service and warranty data, and other relevant sources at least quarterly, and promptly report any previously omitted incidents to the regulator and affected customers as applicable.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved, representative sampling and test protocol. A failure rate above 5% is an intervention threshold, not a permitted-maximum or pass/fail limit. If a model exceeds 5%, manufacturers shall promptly notify the regulator, submit a documented corrective-action plan, and retest within six months or on a regulator-approved schedule. Regulators shall review verified results and may require proportionate corrective action and safeguards based on the overall safety evidence; exceeding 5% alone does not make a model non-compliant or automatically require market withdrawal. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results, including sample size, failures, and protocol, to the relevant regulator and provide them to customers of that model before sale and after material design changes; results shall not be publicly disclosed by the standard. Manufacturers shall retain supporting records and provide them for regulator verification or audit.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems shall support a secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a local means of operation. For systems already installed when this requirement takes effect, manufacturers shall provide compliant retrofit capability or an equivalent regulator-approved safety measure within six years. Retrofit instructions and any associated customer costs must be clearly disclosed.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system shall be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation, and designed to limit fire propagation without creating undue risk to occupants or responders. Manufacturers shall provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers and make relevant design and performance evidence available to regulators.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, safe installation and operation, and steps to take if a warning or incident occurs. Notices shall be delivered promptly when a material safety risk is identified and updated as facts are confirmed. A manufacturer shall initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period. Rates shall use the model’s installed-unit denominator for that period and incidents identified through incident reports, customer complaints, service and warranty records, and other relevant sources. Manufacturers shall retain supporting records and provide them to the regulator for verification; the regulator may require an independent audit. If a reported rate may exceed the trigger but is not yet verified, the manufacturer shall promptly provide the underlying records and cooperate with verification. Once the trigger is verified, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers and provide instructions for repair, replacement, or safe shutdown. The recall shall be implemented through an effective regulator-approved corrective action; the trigger alone does not automatically prohibit further sales or require market withdrawal, without limiting regulators’ authority to impose additional safeguards for an imminent serious safety risk. Manufacturers shall report annual model-level incident rates and supporting data to the regulator.
