# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K33 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, evacuation, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers within 5 business days when the incident indicates a potential safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports shall include known circumstances and model/serial identification; suspected incidents shall be reported as such and updated when facts are confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market release and at least once every three years thereafter, using a regulator-approved sampling and test protocol. A failure rate above 5% requires a corrective-action plan and prompt retesting. A model with a failure rate above 7% shall not be placed on the market unless the regulator authorizes interim sales subject to safeguards; the regulator may also require safeguards or other measures for units already installed. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the regulator, and provide results to customers purchasing that model before sale. These results shall not be published for the general public. Results shall identify the test date and any corrective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market shall include remote emergency shut-off capability within 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units lacking the capability within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible, and shall provide customers with clear instructions for using the feature. If a unit cannot be retrofitted, the manufacturer shall notify its owner and the regulator, explain the limitation, and provide an equivalent emergency-response measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers shall document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide those details to customers and the regulator.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers shall receive clear written instructions on installation, safe operation, warning signs, emergency response, and how to report a safety concern. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material hazards and provide actionable steps. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period shall trigger prompt regulator review and a corrective-action plan. The regulator shall determine whether a mandatory recall is warranted based on verified incidents, installed-unit exposure, severity, and the effectiveness of corrective actions; a recall is mandatory when the regulator determines that a defect presents an imminent serious risk. Rates shall be calculated using verified incidents and installed-unit exposure, with reporting and corrective-action requirements set by the regulator.
