# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K11 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal runaway, fire, explosion, injury, evacuation, or material property damage involving a unit within 24 hours of learning of it, followed by a preliminary investigation report within 10 business days and a final report within 30 days or when the investigation concludes. Suspected thermal incidents without injury or damage must be reported within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 5 business days after determining that a safety notice or action is warranted. Reports must include model, serial number, location, and known circumstances, subject to applicable privacy protections.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative units. Before market entry and at least annually thereafter, manufacturers must test and submit per-model results and supporting methods to the regulator. Results and test methods must be provided to current owners and prospective customers before purchase; individual-unit identifying information may be withheld. A model with a failure rate above 5% must promptly notify the regulator and submit a corrective-action plan with milestones. The regulator may require further testing or risk controls based on the evidence. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sale or require withdrawal of existing units; restrictions must be based on an individualized safety assessment and proportionate corrective action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All newly installed systems must include a remotely operable emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible shut-off as backup. Manufacturers must provide owners clear instructions and test the function during commissioning. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance or replaced within 6 years of this standard taking effect; where retrofit is technically infeasible, the manufacturer must provide an equivalent safety measure approved by the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and installation, and must be tested under a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must provide owners with maintenance and inspection instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, before purchase and throughout ownership, clear safety notices describing material hazards, required precautions, emergency response and shut-off instructions, and any applicable corrective actions. Notices must be delivered promptly in accessible formats and languages appropriate to the customer base. A manufacturer must initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period. The manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers promptly, explain the risk and remedy, and provide the remedy at no cost. Regulators may require additional action where the risk warrants it.
