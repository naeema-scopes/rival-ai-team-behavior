# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H07 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, or fire to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents for which the cause is not yet known. A preliminary report must be followed by an investigation update within 30 days. Manufacturers must notify affected owners within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk or requires immediate precautions; otherwise, they must provide an incident-related safety notice within 30 days. Reports must include known incidents and identify any gaps in incident data.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a common, regulator-approved protocol. An accredited independent laboratory must test each model before market launch and after a material design or manufacturing change. Manufacturers must provide regulators the complete per-model results, including sample size and numerical failure rate, and provide those same complete results directly to customers considering or owning that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. Regulators may require further testing, corrective measures, market restrictions, or other protective action based on test results, incident history, uncertainty, and risk to customers and the public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery systems placed on the market from 24 months after this standard takes effect must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units installed before that date, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or an equivalent safe alternative within 6 years of the standard taking effect. Owners must be notified of the available means and limitations of emergency shut-off.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems placed on the market from 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression designed to limit the spread of a thermal event. Manufacturers must document the system’s performance under the regulator-approved test protocol and provide those results to the responsible regulator. The requirement does not replace other fire-safety obligations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of safety risks, required precautions, and corrective actions, and must provide an annual safety notice describing relevant incidents and available safety updates. Manufacturers must report model-specific thermal-incident data and the methodology used to calculate rates to the responsible regulator. When a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting data; the regulator must assess the evidence, incident severity, uncertainty, and available corrective measures and may require a recall or other protective action. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action at any rate where the evidence indicates an unacceptable risk to customers or the public.
