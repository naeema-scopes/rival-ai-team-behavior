# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G43 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency shut-off to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account and known impacts. Other thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. Affected customers must be notified directly within 24 hours for an incident presenting an imminent safety risk, and within 5 business days for other reportable incidents. Manufacturers must submit a complete incident investigation and corrective-action update to the regulator within 30 days, and promptly update customers if the assessment changes.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with a statistically adequate sample and results reported at least annually. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. A model exceeding that limit must be reported to the regulator within 30 days with a corrective-action plan and retested within 12 months. During that period, the regulator may permit continued sales subject to interim safeguards, enhanced monitoring, and customer notice; failure to meet the limit after retesting requires suspension of new sales until compliance is demonstrated. Full per-model results must be provided to the regulator and to customers who own or are considering that model; individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery models must include remote emergency shut-off capability for units manufactured 18 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide a safe, documented shut-off mechanism and instructions to customers and emergency responders. For units already installed, manufacturers must offer and complete a compatible retrofit, or an equivalent regulator-approved safety measure, within 6 years of adoption. Retrofit plans must prioritize higher-risk units and be reported to the regulator annually.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on new home battery units manufactured 3 years after adoption of this standard. The system must be validated for the battery chemistry and installation environment under a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions for safe operation and maintenance, and report any suppression-system failures to the regulator under S1.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s latest 55 °C sustained-heat test result, thermal-incident rate and reporting period, known material safety limitations, and clear instructions for safe installation, operation, and emergency response. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material new safety information and provide updated instructions and corrective actions. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; the manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers within 24 hours of confirming the threshold is exceeded and promptly implement the regulator-approved recall. Regulators must receive the incident and test data needed to assess compliance.
