# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D41 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. They must notify affected customers within 24 hours when an incident presents an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within 5 business days, with known hazards and protective steps. Reports must include available unit, model, location, and injury or damage details; corrected or newly discovered information must be sent promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and independent laboratory. Before sale and after any material design change, manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample size, and corrective actions to regulators. Complete per-model results, including failure rate, must be provided to customers considering that model and existing customers of that model; public reporting may be anonymized or aggregated. The benchmark failure rate is 5%. A result above 5% requires prompt corrective action and follow-up testing under regulator oversight. Regulators shall determine proportionate interim sales restrictions and protective measures based on the results, corrective actions, and demonstrated risk; exceeding 5% alone does not automatically require a stop-sale. Regulators may require a stop-sale where the risk warrants it.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must include remote emergency shut-off capability beginning 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a secure means to activate shut-off during an emergency and clear instructions for customers and emergency responders. For installed units, manufacturers must offer a retrofit or equivalent safe mitigation, prioritizing units with known hazards, and complete the program within 6 years after adoption. Regulators may approve an alternative where retrofit is infeasible and equivalent protection is demonstrated.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include a built-in fire-suppression system meeting a regulator-approved performance and maintenance standard, beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions on system status, maintenance, and emergency response. Regulators may approve an equivalent protective design where the manufacturer demonstrates comparable fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific test results and clear safety instructions, including installation, operation, emergency response, and known hazards. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide updated instructions and available remedies. A manufacturer must initiate a regulator-supervised recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; regulators may require an earlier recall where the risk warrants it. Recall notices must identify affected units, explain the hazard, and state the remedy and how to obtain it.
