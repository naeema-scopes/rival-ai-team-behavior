# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H43 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## Incident reporting
Manufacturers must report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of confirmation, including incidents involving injury or property damage. They must provide a complete incident report within 10 business days and promptly update it when material facts change. The affected customer must be notified within 24 hours of confirmation; where the incident may pose an immediate risk to other users or nearby people, the manufacturer must also promptly issue an appropriate safety alert.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
## Thermal testing
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved, statistically representative protocol. Manufacturers must report each model’s failure rate, test method, reporting period, sample size, and supporting data to the relevant regulator, and disclose the results to customers considering or owning that model. Per-model results may be withheld from the general public, but not from regulators or customers of that model. A model with a failure rate above 5% must promptly undergo regulator-supervised corrective action and retesting, with appropriate interim protections where warranted. Exceeding 5% alone does not require an automatic sales ban or recall; regulators may require further corrective action or market restrictions based on evidence of an unacceptable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## Remote emergency shut-off
All newly manufactured home battery systems must support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or relevant emergency responders when necessary to address an imminent safety risk. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for authorized emergency responders to request activation. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance, where technically feasible, within 6 years after adoption; manufacturers must notify owners of units that cannot be upgraded and provide applicable safe-use instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
## Built-in fire suppression
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance and safety requirements. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Regulators should establish test methods and installation requirements before the effective date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
## Customer disclosure and recalls
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information, including model-specific 55 °C test results, known material thermal risks, safe-use instructions, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and provide updated instructions or corrective measures. Regulators must investigate when a model has more than 8 confirmed thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units during any rolling 12-month period. After investigation, regulators must order a recall or equivalent market restriction when the evidence establishes that continued sale or use presents an unacceptable safety risk; they may require interim protective measures while investigating. Incident rates must be calculated using the model-specific installed-unit population and confirmed thermal incidents, and regulators may require manufacturers to provide supporting data. The 8-per-100,000 threshold triggers investigation, not an automatic recall.
