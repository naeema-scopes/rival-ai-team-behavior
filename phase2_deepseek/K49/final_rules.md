# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K49 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the regulator any thermal incident involving fire, injury, evacuation, emergency-service response, or material property damage within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written update within 10 days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 10 days. Affected customers must be notified within 72 hours when an incident indicates a material hazard or requires action to protect people or property. Reports must include incident circumstances, model, corrective actions, and relevant installed-unit data; regulators may request follow-up information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
All models must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. The 5% failure rate is a review benchmark, not an automatic market-exclusion threshold. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, the test protocol, and relevant limitations to the regulator, and disclose the per-model results and relevant limitations to customers before purchase. A result above the benchmark requires prompt regulator notification, investigation, and a time-bound corrective-action plan. Regulators may require retesting and impose proportionate restrictions where corrective action is inadequate. The benchmark should be reviewed periodically in light of validated safety evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a secure, documented means for authorized emergency personnel or the system operator to initiate shut-off, subject to safeguards against unauthorized access. Existing installed units that can be retrofitted must be brought into compliance within six years of this standard taking effect; units that cannot be safely retrofitted must be disclosed to their owners and addressed through an approved mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The requirement and approved suppression performance criteria should be reviewed periodically in light of validated safety evidence. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of compliance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety and emergency-response instructions at purchase and prompt notice of material safety updates, newly identified hazards, and corrective actions affecting their unit. A manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers promptly when a safety defect presents a material risk. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide the underlying incident and installed-unit data; the regulator must assess the evidence and may require corrective action or a recall where it establishes an unacceptable safety risk. Regulators may also require a recall where other evidence shows an unacceptable safety risk.
