# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J29 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any fire, thermal runaway, or other overheating event involving a battery, whether or not it caused injury or property damage, within 24 hours of becoming aware of it. A fuller report, including known circumstances and corrective actions, is due within 10 business days; material updates must be provided promptly. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a material risk requiring action, and immediately where an imminent hazard requires urgent action. Records of all incidents and reports must be retained for at least 10 years.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a statistically adequate, representative sample. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt notification to the regulator, a corrective-action plan, and retesting after changes. The regulator shall assess the results, corrective progress, and risk in determining whether further protective measures are warranted; exceeding 5% does not by itself prohibit sale or require a market ban. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model test results to the regulator and share them with the other participating manufacturer under confidentiality protections. Before purchase, customers must be given the model’s 55 °C test failure rate and relevant context, together with clear explanations of material risks and recommended actions. Individual per-model test failure rates need not be published to the general public. Manufacturers must retain test records for at least 10 years.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off by the effective date set by the regulator. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide remote shut-off capability through retrofit or replacement within 6 years of the standard taking effect, where technically feasible. Manufacturers must submit a deployment plan and provide customers with clear instructions for any interim safety measures. Shut-off systems must be secure, reliable, and designed to avoid unintended deactivation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard is adopted. Manufacturers must provide the regulator with an implementation plan and evidence that the suppression system is effective, reliable, and does not introduce other safety hazards.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices describing material hazards, required precautions, and any actions customers should take; urgent hazard notices must be issued immediately, and other material notices within 5 business days of identifying the risk. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit a corrective-action plan. The regulator shall promptly assess the risk and corrective progress and may require a recall, with implementation—including notification, repair, replacement, or refund—proportionate to the risk. Exceeding the threshold alone does not automatically require a recall or sales ban; regulators may order either where warranted. Manufacturers must retain records of notices and recall actions for at least 10 years.
