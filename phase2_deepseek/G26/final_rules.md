# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G26 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, followed by a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk, and promptly provide safety instructions and material updates. Suspected incidents must be logged and investigated; uncertainty about cause does not excuse timely initial reporting.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-recognized protocol before sale and after any material design change. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. A model exceeding 5% may not be sold unless the regulator, after reviewing the per-model results, field performance, and risk controls, grants a specific conditional authorization finding that material risk is adequately addressed. Any authorization must require defined corrective measures, a sales limit or pause where appropriate, and retesting by a regulator-set deadline; the regulator may suspend authorization or require further protections. Manufacturers must promptly provide per-model results and supporting risk information to the regulator. Manufacturers must provide per-model results to customers who own or are considering that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units placed on the market must include a remotely operable emergency shut-off that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety hazard, by the manufacturer or emergency responders. The function must be secure, tested before sale, and usable without reliance on a cloud service where practicable. Manufacturers must provide a plan to retrofit installed units lacking this capability, prioritizing units with known safety concerns, and complete retrofits within 72 months after adoption. Until retrofit, customers must receive clear instructions for local isolation and emergency response.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-recognized effectiveness and reliability test. This requirement applies to units manufactured 36 months after adoption of this standard or later. Manufacturers must validate suppression performance as part of model qualification and disclose relevant limitations and maintenance needs to customers. Existing installed units are not required to be retrofitted solely to add suppression, but manufacturers must provide updated safety guidance if testing or field incidents identify a material hazard.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, before purchase and throughout ownership, clear safety notices covering material hazards, safe installation and operation, emergency isolation, and required maintenance. When a newly identified hazard could materially affect safe use, manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours of determining that a credible safety risk exists, with instructions and updates as they become available. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, provide supporting data, and conduct a documented risk assessment. The regulator must promptly determine whether a recall or other corrective action is necessary, considering incident severity, data reliability, exposure, and effective remedies; it may require a recall where the evidence shows an imminent or material safety risk. Pending that determination, the regulator may require interim protective measures, including a sales pause. Per-model test results must be available to regulators and customers who own or are considering that model, but must not be published for the general public.
