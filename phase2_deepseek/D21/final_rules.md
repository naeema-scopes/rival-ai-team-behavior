# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D21 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account of known risks and response steps. They must submit an updated investigation and corrective-action report within 30 days. Within 72 hours of learning of an incident, manufacturers must notify the affected unit’s customer of known immediate safety steps and how to obtain updates. If an incident presents an imminent risk to other customers or neighbours, the manufacturer must promptly alert the regulator and issue an appropriate public safety notice.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample, with failures and sample size reported. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results to the relevant regulator and provide results to customers who request results for their own model; individual-company failure rates need not be published publicly. A model exceeding 5% must be retested and submitted with a corrective-action plan and timetable to the regulator. Exceeding 5% triggers regulatory review but does not by itself require an automatic ban; the regulator may require additional safeguards, restrict sales, or take other proportionate action based on the results and evidence of risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems must include a remotely operable emergency shut-off that can be activated by the customer and, where an imminent safety risk warrants it, by the manufacturer with prompt customer notification. Manufacturers must provide customers and emergency responders clear instructions for its use and disclose any limitations, including loss of connectivity. For units already installed when this standard is adopted, manufacturers must enable remote shut-off where technically supported within six years; where it is not supported, they must notify customers and provide an equivalent safe shut-off procedure. Regulators may approve an alternative or extension upon evidence that retrofit is infeasible or creates additional risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression appropriate to the system’s chemistry and installation, validated under a regulator-approved test protocol. Manufacturers must provide evidence of suppression performance and maintenance requirements to the regulator and customers. Regulators may approve an equivalent protective design where it provides comparable mitigation of fire spread and risk to occupants and neighbours.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At sale and after material safety updates, customers must receive plain-language information on safe operation, warning signs, emergency actions, incident-reporting contacts, and how to obtain the model’s per-model 55 °C test results. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of material newly identified risks and recommended actions. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period; the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop new sales pending regulator review, and implement the regulator-approved recall or corrective action. Regulators may also require a recall where other evidence shows an unacceptable safety risk.
