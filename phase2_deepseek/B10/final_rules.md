# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B10 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, evacuation, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a potential safety risk to their units, and promptly provide updates as material facts emerge. Near-misses involving a safety-system failure must be reported to the regulator within 5 business days.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least every three years, using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including sample size and failures, to the relevant regulator and make them available to current and prospective customers of that model; per-model failure rates need not be published for the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a corrective-action plan, and retesting within a regulator-set timeframe. For models already on sale when this standard is adopted, manufacturers must submit a corrective-action plan promptly and meet the 5% maximum within 12 months of adoption; any such model still above 5% after that period must stop sale until regulator-approved retesting shows a rate at or below 5%. New models must meet the 5% maximum before sale. Regulators may require earlier stop-sale or additional corrective action where warranted, including for an imminent hazard.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for its use and ensure the function can be activated by an authorized emergency responder or the customer when needed. For units installed before that deadline, manufacturers must provide a feasible upgrade or retrofit plan within 12 months after adoption and complete retrofits where technically feasible within six years, prioritizing units presenting elevated safety risks.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the battery chemistry and enclosure, and manufacturers must provide evidence of its effectiveness to the relevant regulator. Units already installed are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices for incidents or newly identified hazards that could affect their units within 48 hours of determining a potential safety risk, and provide updates as material facts emerge. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The rate must be calculated from incidents and installed-unit exposure over the same 12-month period, with the manufacturer promptly submitting supporting data to the regulator. The manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop sales of the affected model, and provide a remedy plan; regulators may require an immediate stop-sale or additional corrective action where an imminent hazard exists.
