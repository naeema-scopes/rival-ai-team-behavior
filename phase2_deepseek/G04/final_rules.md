# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G04 · variant G · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 40})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency responder attendance to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of it. Incidents involving death, serious injury, or an active hazard must be reported within 24 hours. The affected customer must be notified within 24 hours of a serious or active hazard, and within 5 calendar days for other reportable incidents. Reports must include known circumstances, affected model and serial number, and any immediate safety actions; manufacturers must update regulators and affected customers when material facts change.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a common regulator-approved protocol before sale and after a material design or manufacturing change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 8% of tested units per model. Manufacturers must submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and full results to regulators for confidential review. Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s per-model failure rate, material safety limitations, and any required corrective actions. Models exceeding a 5% failure rate must submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan within 90 days and provide progress reports at least every 6 months; regulators may require interim safeguards or suspend sales if safety risks warrant. Any model introduced after adoption must meet the 8% limit before sale.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 24 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions for using the feature and maintain secure procedures for authorized emergency activation. For units already installed when the standard is adopted, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within 6 years, where technically feasible, and report progress to regulators annually.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document suppression-system performance and maintenance requirements and provide those instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific 55 °C test results, material safety limitations, required installation and maintenance instructions, and instructions for responding to overheating or fire. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of newly identified material hazards and corrective actions. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year, the manufacturer must promptly report the exceedance and supporting data to the regulator, and a recall of that model is mandatory. The regulator must oversee the recall and may require interim safety measures. Affected customers must be notified without delay of the recall remedy and applicable safety instructions.
