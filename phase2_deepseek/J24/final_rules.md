# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J24 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide affected customers with a safety notice within 48 hours. Other overheating incidents requiring repair or causing a unit to shut down shall be reported to the regulator within 30 days and summarized to affected customers within 30 days. Reports shall include known circumstances, affected models, and corrective actions; manufacturers shall update regulators and customers promptly as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. Manufacturers shall submit each model’s numerical failure rate and supporting results to the relevant regulator, and shall provide the numerical per-model failure rate and relevant safety information to customers considering or owning that model. A failure rate above 5% triggers regulator notification, a corrective-action plan, and retesting within 12 months; it does not by itself prohibit sale. Regulators shall review the test protocol and threshold after two years using accumulated test and incident evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units manufactured 12 months or more after adoption of this standard shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete retrofit or an equivalent regulator-approved safety measure within six years of adoption. Regulators may approve technically equivalent measures where remote shut-off is infeasible.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard shall include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance test. Regulators shall publish the test method and may approve equivalent suppression technologies that provide comparable protection. Manufacturers shall document compliance for each model.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with model-specific safety notices promptly when a material thermal hazard or corrective action is identified, stating the affected models, known risks, recommended precautions, and available remedies. If a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer shall notify the regulator and affected customers within 48 hours and submit the supporting data and proposed corrective actions for expedited review. The manufacturer shall initiate a recall of that model; the regulator may approve an equally protective immediate measure instead where it finds that measure adequately protects customers and the public. Regulators may require additional immediate protective action where evidence indicates imminent risk of serious harm. Manufacturers shall promptly implement required measures and update customers as material facts or remedies change.
