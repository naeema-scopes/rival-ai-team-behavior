# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H46 · variant H · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 34})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving a home battery to the regulator within 5 calendar days of learning of it, and notify the affected customer within 24 hours. A report must include the incident date, model, known circumstances, and any immediate safety instructions. Manufacturers must update the regulator and affected customer within 10 calendar days when material new information becomes available. Suspected imminent serious risks must be reported to regulators and affected customers without delay.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C. A failure rate above 5% requires the manufacturer to submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan, provide appropriate interim risk controls and customer safety information, and retest within 12 months. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sales; sales may continue during corrective action unless regulators determine that restrictions are warranted by the risk. Retesting is required after a material design change and at least once every three years. Manufacturers must provide per-model results to regulators and customers purchasing that model; identifiable company-specific failure rates need not be published publicly.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market beginning 3 years after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units installed before that date, manufacturers must provide a compatible remote shut-off retrofit or equivalent safe upgrade within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible, and inform customers of its availability and any limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide customers and regulators with information on the suppression system’s operation, limitations, and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of a confirmed thermal incident involving their unit, and provide safety instructions without delay where continued use may pose a risk. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly investigate, notify regulators and affected customers, and provide findings and precautions. Annual per-model rates must use documented incident counts and installed-unit denominators, with calculation methods and supporting records available to regulators for verification. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Regulators may require an earlier recall when evidence shows an imminent serious safety risk. Recall notices must identify affected models, explain the hazard, and state the remedy and how customers can obtain it.
