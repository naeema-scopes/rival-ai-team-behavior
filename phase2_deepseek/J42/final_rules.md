# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J42 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a follow-up report with known cause and corrective actions within 30 days. The manufacturer must notify the affected customer within 24 hours when the incident presents an ongoing safety risk, and otherwise within 5 business days. Reports must include the model, unit identification, incident date and location, injuries or property damage, and known circumstances. Suspected incidents must be reported; investigations must not delay initial notification.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-accepted protocol, with sample size, test conditions, failures, and failure rate recorded. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results to regulators and provide the applicable model’s results to customers before purchase; individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator review, corrective-action plan, and any regulator-required safeguards. A model with a failure rate above 5% may remain on the market only with regulator authorization, subject to those safeguards and retesting at intervals set by the regulator. Regulators may suspend or prohibit a model if the evidence indicates an unacceptable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery systems must support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or an authorized emergency responder when continued operation presents an imminent safety risk. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units with this capability within 6 years of adoption, where technically feasible; for units that cannot be retrofitted, they must provide an equally effective mitigation or replacement at no cost to the customer. Shut-off must not prevent emergency services from safely isolating the system locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery systems manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to reduce the risk of fire spread. The manufacturer must document the system’s effectiveness and maintenance requirements and provide these to regulators and customers. This requirement does not replace other required fire-safety protections.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the applicable model’s thermal-test results and clear safety, installation, maintenance, emergency-response, and incident-reporting instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and recommended actions, and provide updates as investigations progress. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, or when regulators determine that a defect presents an equivalent serious risk. The manufacturer must notify regulators and affected customers without undue delay, explain the hazard and remedy, and provide the remedy at no cost.
