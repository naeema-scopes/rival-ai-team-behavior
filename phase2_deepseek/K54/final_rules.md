# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K54 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. A preliminary report must identify the product/model, incident date and known impacts; a follow-up with investigation findings is due within 30 days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours of learning of a credible safety hazard, including when to stop use and how to obtain assistance. Where a credible hazard may affect nearby residents or require urgent response, manufacturers must promptly notify relevant local emergency services and provide a public safety notice with appropriate protective guidance. Reports must include all known incidents; incomplete information is not a reason to delay an initial report.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. The maximum permitted failure rate for a model newly placed on the market is 5%. Before sale and at least annually, manufacturers must test and submit the protocol, sample size, results, and corrective actions to the responsible regulator. A model already on the market that exceeds 5% must promptly submit a regulator-supervised corrective-action plan and retest within a regulator-set period; the regulator may impose interim sales or use restrictions where needed to protect safety. Customers considering or owning that model must receive its test failure rate and a plain-language explanation of the result and any safety limitations. Individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market 24 months or more after adoption must support a secure remote emergency shut-off that can be activated by the manufacturer or responsible emergency services when a credible imminent thermal hazard exists. Manufacturers must provide affected customers with instructions and notice when shut-off is activated. For units already installed at adoption, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent risk-control plan within 12 months and complete implementation within 72 months, prioritizing higher-risk units. Shut-off must not prevent emergency services from making a unit safe locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and enclosure, or an equivalent built-in measure demonstrated to limit fire propagation. Manufacturers must submit supporting validation to the responsible regulator and disclose the measure and its limitations to customers. This requirement does not apply retroactively to installed units.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers must receive plain-language safety notices describing the model’s thermal-test results, known material thermal hazards, safe installation and use, and steps to take during an incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified credible hazards and provide updated instructions. If a model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units, the manufacturer must notify the regulator within 24 hours, provide the underlying incident and denominator data, and submit a corrective-action and risk assessment within 30 days. The regulator must promptly assess whether to require a recall or other protective measures; a recall is mandatory when the regulator determines it is necessary to address an unacceptable risk. Manufacturers must execute any ordered recall and notify affected customers.
