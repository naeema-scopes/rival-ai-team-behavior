# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H04 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, smoke, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide an investigation update within 30 days. Manufacturers shall notify affected owners within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk or requires inspection, changed use, or other protective action. Reports shall include available model, unit, and incident details; missing information shall be supplemented as the investigation proceeds.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Before sale and at least annually thereafter, manufacturers shall test each model under a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved, statistically representative protocol. Manufacturers shall provide per-model methods, sample sizes, failure rates, and corrective actions to the relevant regulator. Owners and prospective purchasers shall be provided the model’s test methods, sample sizes, failure rates, corrective actions, and protective instructions; individual-company per-model results shall not be published to the general public. A model with a failure rate greater than 5% shall require regulatory review, a corrective-action plan where warranted, and confirmatory testing within 12 months. Exceeding 5% alone shall not bar sale. A model shall not be newly offered for sale if its failure rate exceeds 10%, or if regulators determine that continued sale presents an imminent safety risk, until the issue is resolved.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units shall support secure remote emergency shut-off at installation. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units that lack this capability within six years after adoption of this standard, where technically feasible, and shall notify owners of the retrofit schedule and any interim safety measures. Shut-off systems shall fail safely, be protected against unauthorized activation, and not prevent local emergency responders or owners from isolating a unit.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system shall be validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and enclosure, and manufacturers shall provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to owners. Earlier compliance is encouraged.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices and protective instructions promptly when a model-specific risk requires inspection, changed use, repair, or other protective action, and shall keep affected customers updated as investigations proceed. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop sale of the affected model pending regulatory review, and provide a corrective action or remedy plan. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action below this threshold when the evidence indicates an imminent safety risk.
