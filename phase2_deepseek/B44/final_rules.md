# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B44 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a full incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk, and promptly provide safety instructions and material updates. Suspected reporting gaps must be disclosed and corrected.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. No more than 5% of tested units may fail. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the regulator and provide the model’s results to current and prospective customers. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be publicly disclosed; public reporting may provide aggregated results. If a model exceeds the 5% limit, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and submit a corrective-action plan with a retest timeline. The regulator may set deadlines, require interim safeguards, and authorize continued sales under conditions while remediation is underway if it determines this is safe. The manufacturer must retest after corrective action. The regulator must impose appropriate restrictions, including suspension of sales, if the model remains noncompliant by the deadline or presents an unacceptable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems must include a remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for the customer or authorized emergency responders to activate it, and must disclose its operation and limitations. For units already installed at adoption, manufacturers must offer a compatible retrofit where feasible and complete any required retrofit within 6 years; if a retrofit is not feasible, they must promptly notify the customer and provide alternative emergency-isolation instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be validated for the battery chemistry and installation configuration and maintained in accordance with manufacturer instructions. Manufacturers must disclose the system’s capabilities and limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific 55 °C sustained-heat test results, including the failure rate, sample size, test date, and any corrective actions, and a clear explanation of relevant safety limitations. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety findings, updated instructions, and corrective actions. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly report the rate to the regulator, notify affected customers, and initiate a recall, following regulator-supervised implementation. The regulator may require interim safeguards and verify recall effectiveness. Regulators may also require a recall where other evidence demonstrates an unacceptable safety risk.
