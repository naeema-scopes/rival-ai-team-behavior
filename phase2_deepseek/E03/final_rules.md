# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E03 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
A manufacturer must report any confirmed or reasonably suspected battery-related overheating, fire, thermal runaway, or other thermal safety incident to the competent regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a follow-up investigation report within 30 days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to them or nearby members of the public, and provide immediate instructions where urgent action is needed. Reports must include known incident details and be updated as the investigation proceeds.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and after a material design change. The model must have a failure rate no greater than 5% of tested units. The manufacturer must submit per-model results, sample size, and protocol to the competent regulator and provide those results to customers who buy that model, including prospective customers before purchase. Results must be presented with sufficient context to explain the test protocol and sample size. Where results exceed the limit, the regulator may require prompt confirmatory testing and a time-bound corrective-action plan under its oversight before imposing a market restriction, unless an imminent safety risk warrants immediate action. The regulator may require retesting or corrective action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must support secure remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit for installed units that lack this capability, completing the retrofit within six years of this standard taking effect. Shut-off functionality must be protected against unauthorized access, and customers must receive clear instructions on its use and limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard is adopted. Manufacturers must provide clear customer instructions on the system’s operation, limitations, and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with prompt, clear safety notices when a known hazard may affect their unit, including the nature of the risk, steps to reduce it, and available support or corrective action. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units during a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the competent regulator and affected customers, investigate the cause, and submit a corrective-action plan and timeline. The regulator must review the evidence, may require confirmatory testing and interim risk controls, and must require a recall when the evidence indicates a serious safety risk that cannot be adequately mitigated without recall. The regulator may also require a recall or other corrective action below the threshold where evidence indicates a serious safety risk. Manufacturers must explain any remedy and timeline to affected customers and provide periodic status updates until the matter is resolved.
