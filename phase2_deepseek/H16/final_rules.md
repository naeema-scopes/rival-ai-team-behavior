# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H16 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the regulator any thermal event involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or material property damage within 24 hours of learning of it. A preliminary report must identify the model, known circumstances, and any immediate protective steps; a completed investigation report is due within 30 days, with updates as material facts emerge. Manufacturers must notify affected customers of a hazard or protective action without delay and no later than 24 hours after notifying the regulator. Suspected incidents must be logged, investigated, and included in periodic regulator reports; reports must not be withheld solely because an investigation is ongoing.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with a statistically adequate sample. Manufacturers must provide per-model results to the regulator and to customers purchasing that model; results need not be published to the general public. A result above 5% failure triggers a regulator review and a manufacturer risk assessment. The regulator may require a corrective-action plan and retesting, taking account of the result, incident history, and proposed mitigations. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sale. Retest after a material design change and at least every three years.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible means of shutdown. Manufacturers must provide secure remote-shutoff capability for existing installed units where technically feasible within six years after adoption, prioritizing models with elevated safety risks. If a unit cannot be retrofitted, the manufacturer must document the reason to the regulator and provide customers with an effective alternative emergency procedure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no earlier than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The regulator may approve equivalent built-in mitigation measures that provide comparable protection. Manufacturers must disclose the presence and capabilities of the suppression system and its maintenance requirements to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety instructions, the model’s thermal-test result, known material risks, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a safety hazard and provide corrective instructions. Manufacturers must track and report model-specific thermal incidents and installed-unit counts to the regulator, using a rolling 12-month period and a denominator of installed units. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in that period, or when the regulator determines that a serious defect presents an imminent risk. Manufacturers must notify the regulator and affected customers without delay and provide the recall remedy and instructions.
