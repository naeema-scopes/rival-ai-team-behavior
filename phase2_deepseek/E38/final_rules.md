# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E38 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller investigation report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk, and promptly update them as material facts emerge. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial identifiers, consequences, and corrective actions. Manufacturers must correct omissions and report previously unreported incidents promptly upon discovery.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a documented, reproducible protocol and a representative sample. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model test results, including the measured failure rate and test method, to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy or are considering buying that model. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for each model, with compliance required within 36 months of adoption. Until then, a model exceeding 5% must be reported to the regulator with a corrective-action plan and retested on a defined schedule. The regulator will determine proportionate restrictions based on test results, incident history, and corrective actions; exceeding the limit does not itself require an automatic market ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units manufactured 24 months after adoption of this standard must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a secure, accessible shut-off capability and clear instructions for its use. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide compatible retrofit capability or an equivalent safe mitigation within 6 years, prioritizing units presenting elevated risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression appropriate to the unit’s design and installation environment. Manufacturers must document suppression effectiveness and maintenance requirements, and provide this information to customers and regulators. Units installed before that date are not required to be retrofitted under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety and emergency-use instructions, relevant model-specific test results, and information on known material safety risks. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks, incidents, corrective actions, and any regulator safety notice, and provide updates as facts emerge. A manufacturer must initiate a model-specific recall when verified data show that the model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year. It must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, explain the risk, and provide safe shutdown, repair, replacement, or refund instructions. A recall requires an effective remedy for affected units; it does not by itself require a blanket market ban.
