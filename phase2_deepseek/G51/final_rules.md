# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G51 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving a battery, including overheating, smoke, or fire, to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it, even if there is no injury or property damage. Incidents involving death, serious injury, active fire, evacuation, or an ongoing danger to the public must be reported within 24 hours. Reports must include known unit and model identifiers and circumstances, with updates as facts are confirmed. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident presents a credible safety risk requiring immediate precautions; otherwise, include relevant safety information in the next customer communication.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and after a material design or manufacturing change. Regulators must prescribe and consistently apply the protocol, sample size, and failure criteria. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and supporting data confidentially to the relevant regulator. Each customer who buys a model must receive that model’s precise per-model failure rate and test results, alongside safety explanations, precautions, and corrective actions. Identifiable per-model results must not be published to the general public. Regulators may require proportionate corrective action, retesting, or other measures based on results and the model’s overall safety risk; no single test failure-rate threshold automatically bars a model from market.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete retrofits within 6 years of this standard taking effect, prioritizing units presenting the greatest safety risk. Where retrofit is technically infeasible, the manufacturer must notify the owner and regulator and provide an alternative risk-reduction measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Regulators should specify performance and maintenance criteria and may approve an equivalent protective design where it provides comparable risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with model-specific safety notices, including relevant incident trends, test results, and any precautions or corrective actions, promptly when a credible safety risk is identified; notices must be updated as material facts change. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 10 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Below that threshold, regulators may require corrective action, customer notice, or a recall based on the nature and severity of the risk. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers of a recall and provide clear remedy instructions.
