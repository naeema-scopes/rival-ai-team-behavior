# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G44 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a written update within 30 days. Other confirmed thermal incidents shall be reported to the regulator in a monthly summary. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours of a reportable incident involving their unit or model, with known hazards and immediate safety instructions. Reports must include incident counts, model, and installed-unit denominator; company-specific information is submitted to regulators and affected customers, not published for the general public.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo an independently witnessed sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. A failure rate above 5% shall trigger prompt regulator notification, investigation, and regulator-directed corrective action. A model with a failure rate above 10% shall not be sold until corrective action is taken and it passes a retest. Manufacturers shall disclose per-model methods, sample sizes, results, and corrective actions to regulators and current and prospective customers of that model. Public reporting may provide aggregated results, but not company-specific per-model failure rates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units placed on the market after adoption shall support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units to provide this capability, where technically feasible, within 72 months of adoption; where retrofit is infeasible, they shall notify affected customers and provide an equivalent regulator-approved emergency isolation measure and a documented plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units placed on the market shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system shall be validated for the battery chemistry and installation environment, maintained over the unit’s service life, and accompanied by customer instructions. Manufacturers shall provide regulators with evidence of system effectiveness and disclose relevant limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide affected customers with prompt safety notices describing known hazards, protective actions, and corrective measures, and update notices when material information changes. Manufacturers shall report model-level thermal-incident rates and installed-unit denominators to regulators annually. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and initiate an investigation. The regulator shall promptly determine whether a mandatory recall or other equally protective corrective action is required, based on the evidence and risk. Company-specific per-model rates shall be shared with regulators and affected customers, not published for the general public.
