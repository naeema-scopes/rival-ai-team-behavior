# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D39 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, explosion, or injury requiring medical treatment to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents, including overheating without fire or injury, must be reported within 5 business days. Reports must identify the product model, incident date and location, known consequences, and any immediate safety action. The manufacturer must notify the affected customer within 24 hours for a fire, thermal runaway, explosion, or serious injury, and within 5 business days for other confirmed thermal incidents. Manufacturers must update regulators and affected customers when material information becomes available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
## S2_thermal_testing
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and at least annually thereafter, with a statistically adequate sample and documented results. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for any model. A model exceeding that limit may not be newly sold until it passes a confirmatory test and corrective action is accepted by the regulator. Full per-model test results, including sample size and failure rate, must be submitted to the regulator and provided to customers considering purchase of that model; individual-company per-model results are not publicly published.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## S3_remote_shutoff
New home battery units must support a secure remote emergency shut-off function at installation. Manufacturers must provide a safe, no-cost upgrade path for installed units that lack the function and complete retrofits within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and the customer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear instructions for use.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment, validated under a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any required inspection or replacement interval to customers. Existing installed units are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide purchasers, before sale, the model’s latest 55 °C sustained-heat test results, including sample size and failure rate, and promptly disclose material safety notices and corrective actions to affected customers. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 confirmed thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a 12-month period. The manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop sales, and initiate the recall without waiting for a further incident or test. Customer notices must explain the hazard, immediate precautions, and how to obtain repair, replacement, or refund at no cost. Manufacturers must report recall progress and completion to the regulator.
