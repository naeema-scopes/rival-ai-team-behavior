# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J40 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, or emergency protective shutdown to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, followed by a written report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when the incident presents a credible safety risk or requires inspection, service, or altered use. Reports must include incident circumstances, model, and known injuries or property damage. Suspected incidents may be reported as preliminary and updated as facts are confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least every two years thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. Results above a 5% failure rate trigger a documented corrective-action plan, regulator review, and follow-up testing; they do not by themselves prohibit sales or require withdrawal of units already on the market. Regulators may impose proportionate restrictions where evidence shows an unacceptable safety risk. Manufacturers shall provide per-model test results, sample size, and corrective actions to the relevant regulator, the other participating company, and customers who buy that model. Public reporting may use aggregate results and must not identify an individual company's per-model failure rates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market from three years after adoption must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with appropriate safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to support the function within six years after adoption where technically feasible, prioritizing units subject to a safety notice. If a safe retrofit is not feasible, the manufacturer must provide an equivalent risk-reduction measure and notify affected customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market from 36 months after adoption must include built-in fire suppression designed for the battery system and installed in accordance with the manufacturer's validated safety design. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions on its operation, limitations, and maintenance. Units placed on the market before that date are not subject to this requirement, but must receive any applicable safety notices or recall actions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase and whenever materially revised, customers must receive model-specific safety information, including applicable thermal-test results, safe installation and operating instructions, emergency steps, and how to report a safety concern. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of safety risks, inspection or service needs, and changes to safe use. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly report the rate and supporting data to regulators for review and provide affected customers with any resulting safety notice. A recall is required when a regulator determines that a serious defect creates an unacceptable risk, considering incident rates, severity, and other available evidence; manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers of the recall, its reason, and the remedy.
