# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A34 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, injury, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. Any other confirmed thermal incident must be reported to the regulator within 5 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 5 business days after confirming a material hazard, including immediate safety instructions and how to report related incidents.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo an independently witnessed 55 °C sustained-heat test using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units before sale and at least annually. Test results and sample sizes must be submitted confidentially to regulators and disclosed to customers buying that model in clear, accessible form; this section does not require publication of individual-company, per-model failure rates to the general public. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must undergo a corrective-action plan and retest within a regulator-set period, with interim risk controls as appropriate. Sales may continue during remediation unless the regulator determines that an imminent safety risk warrants restricting sales.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide customers clear instructions for using it and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe shut-off solution within 6 years after adoption, prioritizing units with known hazards and notifying affected customers of the schedule. Any inability to meet these deadlines must be reported to the regulator with a proposed risk-control plan. Regulators may require earlier action where an imminent safety risk exists.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance requirements. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of compliance before sale.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices for their model, including applicable per-model thermal-test results, known hazards, and practical safety precautions, before purchase and promptly when material new safety information emerges. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of corrective actions or recall decisions. When verified data show that any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting data. Exceeding this threshold requires a mandatory recall and prompt notification to affected customers with instructions. Per-model incident rates and test results must be provided to regulators and affected customers, not published to the general public.
