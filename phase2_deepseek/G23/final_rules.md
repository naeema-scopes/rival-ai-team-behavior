# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G23 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled heating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it. Any fatality, life-threatening injury, or active risk to the public must be reported within 24 hours. The manufacturer must notify the affected customer within 2 business days after learning of an incident involving their unit, and promptly provide any immediate safety instructions. Reports must include known facts and be updated as the investigation develops. Records of all thermal incidents, including those not reportable under these criteria, must be retained for at least 10 years.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a representative sample and independent laboratory verification. A model’s failure rate must not exceed 8%. Results, including sample size, failure rate, and corrective actions, must be submitted to the relevant regulator and shared with the other participating manufacturer under confidentiality protections. If a model exceeds the limit, the manufacturer must promptly submit a corrective-action plan for regulator review; the regulator may require additional testing or other proportionate measures. Manufacturers must provide customers of each model with that model’s test results, including its failure rate and any corrective actions. Public disclosures may present results in aggregate but must not identify an individual company’s per-model failure rate.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market from 2 years after adoption must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway for installed units and complete retrofit or an equivalent safety measure within 6 years after adoption, prioritizing units with known safety concerns. Customers must be notified of the retrofit option and any limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression meeting applicable performance and safety requirements. Manufacturers must provide customers with clear information on the system’s operation, limitations, and maintenance. This requirement does not prevent earlier adoption or other appropriate fire-risk mitigation.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices describing material thermal risks, relevant safety instructions, and any corrective actions affecting their model. Notices must be provided promptly when a material risk is identified and updated as new information becomes available. If a model’s thermal incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units in any year, the regulator must require a model-specific recall. The recall must include an effective remedy, which may be a regulator-approved corrective action such as repair, replacement, or a safety update, and appropriate customer notification. The regulator may approve corrective measures and conditions for return to market once the risk is adequately addressed. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and report the recall’s scope, remedy, and completion progress to the regulator.
