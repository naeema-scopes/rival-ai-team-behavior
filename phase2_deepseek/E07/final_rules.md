# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E07 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a detailed follow-up within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety risk for their model; when there is an immediate danger, they must issue an urgent customer warning without delay. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and steps taken. Manufacturers must maintain records and promptly correct incomplete reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the regulator and make those results available to current and prospective customers of that model. A model with a failure rate above 5% must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and undergo retesting; the regulator may impose proportionate restrictions or additional safeguards based on the results and other safety evidence. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit continued or new sales while the manufacturer implements an approved plan, unless the regulator determines that restriction is necessary to address an unreasonable safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support it, or provide an equivalent regulator-approved emergency isolation measure, within six years after adoption. The regulator may approve a documented, technically justified alternative where retrofit is infeasible, provided equivalent protection is maintained and affected customers are informed.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The regulator may approve an alternative design that provides equivalent or greater fire protection, based on documented testing. Manufacturers must inform customers of the protection provided and any relevant limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, precautions, and reporting channels, and must promptly notify affected customers of material new safety information. A recall is mandatory when a model’s verified annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units. Calculate the rate from distinct thermal incidents attributable to that model during a consistent annual reporting period, divided by that model’s installed units during the same period and expressed per 100,000 units. Include incidents whether or not they were initially reported to a regulator; maintain auditable incident and unit records, explain the calculation, and make supporting records available to the regulator for verification. Manufacturers must notify the regulator and affected customers without delay, stop sales and deployment of the affected model, and implement the regulator-approved recall and remedy. The regulator may require a recall or other protective action below this threshold when the evidence indicates an unreasonable safety risk.
