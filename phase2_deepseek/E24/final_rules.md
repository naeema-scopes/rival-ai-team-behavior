# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E24 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
A manufacturer must report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the competent regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary incident record. It must submit a follow-up report within 10 calendar days, including known circumstances, model and serial number, injuries or damage, and corrective actions. Incidents presenting an ongoing risk must be reported to affected customers and nearby occupants who may be exposed within 48 hours of awareness, with immediate protective instructions. Other affected customers must be notified within 5 calendar days. Reports must not be withheld while cause is under investigation; updates must follow as facts are established.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a regulator-approved 55 °C sustained-heat test before sale and at least annually, using a consistent protocol and independent accredited laboratory. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% of tested units per model. Manufacturers must submit per-model test protocols, sample sizes, failure rates, and corrective actions to the competent regulator and provide the results to the independent laboratory. Results for each model must also be provided to its customers, including prospective customers before purchase; regulators may publish aggregated results, but not company-specific per-model failure rates. If a model exceeds 5%, the regulator must require corrective action and a confirmatory test. Pending confirmation, the regulator may authorize continued or limited sales subject to proportionate safeguards, monitoring, and customer notice where warranted by the evidence; it may restrict sales when necessary to address a serious risk. Manufacturers must not withhold customer-relevant results on grounds of commercial confidentiality.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must include a remotely operable emergency shut-off, with secure operation available to the customer and authorized emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and maintain the capability throughout the unit's supported service life. For units already installed when this rule takes effect, manufacturers must provide a no-cost retrofit or equivalent compliant upgrade within six years. If a unit cannot be upgraded, the manufacturer must notify its owner and regulator and provide a safe replacement or other regulator-approved mitigation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and foreseeable operating conditions, and must not create an unreasonable risk of toxic exposure or impede emergency response. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to owners and emergency responders.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, before purchase, model-specific 55 °C sustained-heat test results and clear safety instructions, including emergency shut-off guidance and known material hazards. They must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks, with protective actions and updates as information develops. A manufacturer must initiate a regulator-supervised recall when a model’s thermal incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period. The manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop new sales, and provide repair, replacement, or refund options and safe handling instructions. The regulator may require a recall below this threshold where evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
