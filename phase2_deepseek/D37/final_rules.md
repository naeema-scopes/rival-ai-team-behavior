# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D37 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, property damage, or emergency response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary incident report. A complete investigation report is due within 30 days, or the manufacturer must provide updates on the investigation until complete. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk relevant to their unit, and promptly provide material updates. Reports must include known model and unit-identification information; reporting obligations apply whether or not the cause has been confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and a statistically meaningful sample. The maximum allowed failure rate under this standard is 7% of tested units. This is a negotiated standard limit, not a finding that results above the regulators’ proposed 5% benchmark meet that benchmark. Manufacturers must provide per-model test methods, sample sizes, results, and corrective actions to regulators, and provide the results to customers who purchase that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates are not to be published to the general public. A model exceeding the 7% limit must not be newly sold until it passes retesting after corrective action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## S3_remote_shutoff
All new home battery units placed on the market must support a secure remote emergency shut-off. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a compliant retrofit or replacement within six years, where technically feasible. The manufacturer must notify affected owners of the process and any interim safety instructions. Remote shut-off must not replace local emergency controls or emergency-response procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard, all newly manufactured home battery units offered for sale must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to limit propagation of a thermal event, in accordance with a regulator-approved test protocol. Manufacturers must provide the protocol, test results, and maintenance instructions to regulators and make relevant operating and maintenance information available to customers. This requirement does not apply retroactively to units manufactured before its effective date. Fire suppression does not replace other required safety controls or emergency procedures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide each purchaser, before sale, the current model-specific 55 °C sustained-heat test result, test date, sample size, and any applicable corrective actions. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours after learning of a credible safety risk relevant to their unit, and promptly provide material updates, including regulator safety notices and recall instructions. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model’s thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period. The manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop new sales of the model, and follow regulator-approved recall or corrective-action instructions. Sales may resume only after the regulator verifies the remedy and updated safety evidence. Rates and recall determinations must use documented incident and installed-unit counts, with methods and data supplied to the regulator.
