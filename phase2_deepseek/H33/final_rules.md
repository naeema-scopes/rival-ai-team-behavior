# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H33 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any fire, thermal runaway, or other thermal incident involving an installed battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 30 days. The manufacturer must notify the affected customer within 72 hours, with immediate notice where there is an urgent safety risk. Reports must include known injuries, property damage, and containment actions; suspected incidents must not be omitted pending confirmation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the regulator. Regulators must protect precise per-model results from general public disclosure. Before purchase, customers and prospective customers must receive the test date, sample size, and precise per-model failure rate, together with a clear explanation of material findings and safety implications. Beginning 24 months after this standard takes effect, a model’s failure rate must not exceed 7%. A model exceeding the limit must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and retest within 12 months; regulators may require interim safeguards, restrict sales, or take other protective measures based on the evidence. Manufacturers must promptly inform regulators and affected customers of material changes or newly identified safety risks.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within six years of the standard’s effective date, where technically feasible. Manufacturers must provide customers with clear instructions for activating the feature and disclose any units that cannot be retrofitted.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of compliance and disclose to customers whether the unit includes the required system.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about incidents affecting their model, known risks, and recommended precautions or corrective actions, promptly and no later than 72 hours after notifying regulators, with immediate notice for urgent risks. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting model-specific data, and the manufacturer must initiate a recall and promptly notify affected customers with instructions and remedies. The regulator may require additional protective measures. The trigger is model-specific; company-wide rates do not establish that a model has crossed it.
