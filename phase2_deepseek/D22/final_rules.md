# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D22 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller account within 10 business days. Other confirmed overheating incidents shall be reported within 10 business days. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and provide regulators with periodic incident summaries and corrective actions. Reports must include model, date, location, known consequences, and actions taken.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample. A failure rate above 5% shall be reported promptly to regulators, together with the test method, sample size, results, and a corrective-action plan; the model shall be retested on a defined schedule. Regulators may require proportionate interim safeguards or restrict sales where a documented risk assessment warrants it; exceeding the threshold alone does not automatically prohibit sales. Per-model test results, including failure rates, shall be provided to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model, and shall not be publicly disclosed as individual-company results. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of any material safety risk and relevant corrective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall provide regulators and customers with information on operation and limitations. For already-installed units, manufacturers shall provide compatible remote shut-off capability or an equivalent safety upgrade within six years after adoption, where technically feasible; regulators may approve alternative risk-reduction measures where a retrofit is infeasible.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured for sale shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to customers and regulators. Existing installed units are not subject to retrofit under this requirement.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices describing material hazards, safe operation, warning signs, reporting channels, and any required corrective actions, and shall promptly notify affected customers when a material safety risk is identified. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers of a recall, explain the remedy and timeline, and provide periodic completion updates. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action sooner where evidence shows an imminent or serious risk.
