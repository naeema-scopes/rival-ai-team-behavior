# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H38 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any fire, thermal runaway, or other thermal event causing injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of learning of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 10 business days. Reports must include known unit/model and event details, and be updated when material information becomes available. The manufacturer must notify affected customers promptly when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and in any event within 5 business days of that determination.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually, using a representative sample and a jointly agreed protocol. Manufacturers must submit per-model results to the relevant regulator within 10 business days of completion and provide the results to customers who buy that model through a readily accessible customer notice; results must identify the model, test date, sample size, and failure rate. Results are not required to be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% must be reported to the regulator within 24 hours, with a corrective-action plan submitted within 30 days. A model may continue to be sold while the plan is implemented unless the regulator determines, based on a documented risk assessment, that continued sales present an unacceptable risk. A failure rate above 10% requires suspension of sales until retesting demonstrates a rate of 10% or less, unless the regulator authorizes otherwise based on a documented risk assessment. The parties will review the protocol and thresholds against accumulated evidence after two years.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability starting 2 years after adoption, subject to safeguards against unauthorized activation and a means of local emergency operation. Manufacturers must provide an upgrade plan for installed units and complete feasible retrofits within 6 years after adoption. Where retrofit is technically infeasible, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and provide an approved alternative safety measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must meet a jointly agreed performance and maintenance standard and must not create unreasonable risks during normal use or emergency response. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions for inspection, maintenance, and emergency response.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide affected customers with prompt, plain-language safety notices when an incident, test result, or other new information indicates a material safety risk; notices must describe the risk, affected models and serial ranges, recommended precautions, and available remedy. Notices must be sent within 5 business days of determining the risk and updated as material information becomes available. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must notify the regulator within 24 hours and provide the data needed for a documented risk assessment. The regulator must promptly investigate. A recall is mandatory if the regulator determines that the model presents an unacceptable safety risk; the manufacturer must then promptly notify affected customers, stop distribution as directed, and submit a regulator-approved recall and remediation plan.
