# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K28 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify the affected household within 24 hours and provide safety instructions; where the incident could pose a continuing risk to nearby people or property, they must also promptly notify relevant emergency services and affected neighbours. Suspected incidents must be investigated and included in the report. No incident may be omitted because its cause is uncertain.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale, at least annually thereafter, and after a material safety-related design change. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification and a corrective-action plan with follow-up testing on a regulator-set schedule; it does not by itself require sales suspension. Regulators receive per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results. Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s test date, protocol, sample size, and per-model test results, including its failure rate. Manufacturers must promptly provide updated per-model results and recommended actions to existing customers after retesting or a material finding. Regulators may require additional protective measures where results indicate an unacceptable risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support the function, or provide an equivalent regulator-approved safety measure, within 6 years of adoption. Shut-off must be available to the customer and authorized emergency responders, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and installation conditions, and maintained in working order throughout the product’s service life. Manufacturers must provide customers with inspection and maintenance instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and promptly after a material safety finding, manufacturers must give customers clear model-specific safety information, including known hazards, required precautions, and material safety findings. Regulators must receive per-model test results and incident-rate data. Customer notices must explain relevant safety findings and recommended actions; they do not require publication of an individual company’s per-model test failure rates to the general public. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators when a safety defect or elevated risk is identified, and provide free repair, replacement, or refund as appropriate. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; the manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers without delay and begin the recall process.
