# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A56 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents involving injury or property damage. A detailed follow-up report, including known circumstances and corrective actions, is due within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk to their model or batch; urgent hazards require immediate notice. Regulators may require updates as investigations progress.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after a material design change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% of tested units per model. A model exceeding 5% may continue to be sold only under a time-limited corrective-action authorization from the regulator, based on a safety assessment, interim risk controls, and a defined retest plan; the regulator may suspend the authorization or require further action if risks are not adequately controlled. Manufacturers must provide regulators with per-model results, test methods, sample sizes, and corrective actions. Customers must receive the complete 55 °C test results, including the per-model failure rate and sample size, for the model they buy, together with applicable material safety risks, precautions, and corrective actions. Public reporting may include aggregate results. Regulators may require retesting where results indicate a material safety concern.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## S3_remote_shutoff
Remote emergency shut-off is required on all new units placed on the market beginning two years after adoption. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide a compatible shut-off retrofit or equivalent upgrade within six years, where technically feasible, and communicate safe interim measures to affected customers. Shut-off systems must be secure, fail-safe, and usable by authorized emergency responders without creating an unsafe loss of essential power.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on all new units placed on the market beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Systems must be designed and tested to operate safely with the battery and must not create additional hazards.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about known material risks, affected models or batches, safe use and interim precautions, and available corrective actions. Notices must be sent promptly when a material safety risk is identified, and immediately where urgent action is needed. When a model’s thermal-incident rate may exceed 8 per 100,000 installed units during a rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly report supporting incident counts, installed-unit denominator, and methodology to regulators. Regulators must verify the data. A verified model-specific rate exceeding 8 per 100,000 during that period triggers a mandatory recall of that model. Manufacturers must promptly carry out the recall and notify affected customers and regulators. Manufacturers must report model-specific incident rates and supporting information to regulators.
