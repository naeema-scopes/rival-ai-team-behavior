# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D25 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a detailed follow-up within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk, and promptly provide safety instructions and updates. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known consequences, and any immediate mitigation. Suspected incidents must not be omitted while an investigation is pending.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample. Before market entry and after material design changes, manufacturers must submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, failure rates, and results to regulators and provide the same per-model results to customers considering or owning that model in clear, accessible form. Results must identify the protocol and test date. A model exceeding the 5% failure-rate benchmark must submit a corrective-action plan and undergo retesting; regulators may restrict marketing or installation if the manufacturer fails to mitigate the risk or evidence indicates serious or imminent danger. Manufacturers must promptly provide customers relevant corrective actions and material updates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to provide this capability within six years of the standard’s effective date, prioritizing units subject to safety alerts or elevated risk. Where a safe retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers and provide an approved alternative mitigation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after the standard’s adoption. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment, and manufacturers must provide maintenance and replacement instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices for incidents, hazards, corrective actions, and safe-use instructions, and must promptly update affected customers when material information changes. For each model, the annual thermal-incident rate is the number of confirmed thermal incidents during the preceding 12 months divided by that model’s installed units, expressed per 100,000 units. Manufacturers must maintain auditable incident and installed-unit records and provide the calculation and supporting data to the regulator. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must notify the regulator and affected customers promptly upon determining that the trigger is exceeded, provide a corrective-action and recall plan, and report completion progress. Verification or investigation must not delay protective action where evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action earlier when the evidence indicates such a risk.
