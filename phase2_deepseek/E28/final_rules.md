# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E28 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal event involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, with a complete report within 30 days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within five days when an event or emerging hazard presents a credible safety risk, and promptly provide updates or protective instructions. Near-misses and other thermal events without a credible safety risk must be recorded and included in quarterly regulator summaries.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after a material design change. A failure rate above 5% triggers regulator review and a corrective-action plan, not automatic exclusion from sale. Regulators may require retesting, additional safeguards, or a time-limited sales restriction based on the evidence and adequacy of corrective measures. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and corrective-action plans to regulators and provide each model’s actual test results directly to prospective and current customers, along with relevant safety findings and protective instructions. Customer disclosure is by direct notice or on request and does not require public posting of individual-company per-model failure rates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a means to notify the household when shut-off occurs. Manufacturers must retrofit compatible installed units, or provide an equivalent safety upgrade, within six years of adoption. Regulators may approve a longer schedule where technical infeasibility is demonstrated and interim protections are provided.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers must demonstrate that the suppression system is appropriate for the battery chemistry and does not create an additional unacceptable hazard.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide prospective and current customers directly with model-specific safety notices, including known thermal hazards, protective instructions, material safety updates, and the model’s actual 55 °C sustained-heat test results. These notices do not require public posting of individual-company per-model failure rates. Notify affected customers within five days of identifying a credible safety risk and promptly provide updates and protective instructions. When verified thermal incidents for any model exceed 8 per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, begin a recall, and provide customers with protective instructions; the regulator may set the recall scope and oversee implementation. Manufacturers must submit supporting incident and exposure data. A recall is also required whenever the regulator determines that a serious safety hazard warrants one, regardless of the incident-rate threshold.
