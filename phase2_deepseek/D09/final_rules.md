# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D09 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report any death, serious injury, fire, thermal runaway, or event requiring emergency-service response involving a covered battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written incident report within 10 calendar days. Other overheating or safety incidents must be reported to the regulator within 10 calendar days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly, and no later than 24 hours after a reportable serious incident, when the event may indicate a hazard to their unit or household. Reports must include known information and be updated as investigations proceed; uncertainty does not excuse timely reporting.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples before sale and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers must submit full per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the regulator and an independent testing body. Regulators and independent testing bodies may share results with other authorities as needed for safety oversight. Customers purchasing a model must be given access to that model’s failure rate, test date, safety status, material risks, and required corrective actions. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a corrective-action plan, and retesting. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sale; regulators will determine proportionate interim measures based on verified risk, test uncertainty, and remediation, and may authorize continued sale subject to time-limited protective conditions and monitoring.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery systems must include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and ensure authorized emergency responders can request activation through a continuously available channel. For systems installed before adoption, manufacturers must provide shut-off capability through retrofit or equivalent safe upgrade within six years, unless the regulator approves an alternative for a specific system where retrofit is infeasible or creates greater safety risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems must include built-in fire suppression. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide those instructions to customers. The regulator may approve an equivalent protective measure where built-in suppression is technically infeasible or would create a greater safety risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase and in the product’s safety information, customers must receive clear instructions on safe installation and operation, warning signs, emergency response, and how to report a safety concern. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a material safety risk and provide updated instructions or corrective actions. A manufacturer must initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on the best available verified data; it must notify the regulator and affected customers without delay and provide a remedy plan. A mandatory recall requires an effective customer-protection remedy, but does not by itself impose a permanent market ban; the regulator determines any interim sales restrictions based on verified risk, available remedies, and protective conditions. The regulator may require a recall below that threshold where the risk warrants it.
