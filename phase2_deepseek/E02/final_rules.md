# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E02 · variant E · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 45})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, or emergency evacuation to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers and, where there is a credible risk to nearby people or property, relevant local emergency authorities within 72 hours. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial number, injuries or property damage, and immediate safety steps. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and report annually, including incidents initially determined not to be product-related.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, a representative sample, and documented results. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers must submit each model’s results, including sample size and exact failure rate, to the responsible regulator and independent conformity assessor. Before purchase, customers must receive a safety-relevant summary stating whether the model meets the limit and describing any required corrective actions; exact per-model failure rates and sample sizes are not customer-facing. A model exceeding the limit must be investigated, corrective action documented, and retested on a regulator-approved schedule. Exceeding the limit requires regulator oversight and proportionate interim safeguards, but does not by itself require market withdrawal or a ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer under regulator oversight. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and disclose any conditions that could prevent operation. Units already installed must be brought into compliance within six years of adoption, unless the regulator approves an equivalent safety measure or determines retrofit is impracticable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must disclose the system’s capabilities, limitations, inspection requirements, and maintenance instructions to customers. Regulators may approve an equivalent protective measure where built-in suppression is technically unsuitable, provided it delivers comparable safety protection.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear written safety information covering installation and ventilation requirements, warning signs, emergency response, incident-reporting contacts, and any remote shut-off or fire-suppression capabilities. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide updated instructions and available remedies. A mandatory recall or other regulator-directed protective action is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, investigate the cause, and implement the recall or other regulator-approved remedy without delay. The trigger requires protective action and regulator oversight; it does not by itself require automatic market exclusion.
