# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H03 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, thermal runaway, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller report within 10 calendar days. Other confirmed thermal incidents and material safety defects must be reported within 5 business days. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial number, injuries or damage, and actions taken; manufacturers must promptly supplement material new information. Affected customers must be notified directly within 24 hours when an incident presents an ongoing risk or requires immediate precautions, and otherwise within 5 business days, with safe-use and service instructions. Reports must not be delayed while facts are being verified.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol. A failure rate above 5% requires the manufacturer to submit a corrective-action plan and retest schedule to the regulator within 30 days. The regulator may approve continued sale of the model under a time-limited mitigation plan, with progress reports and retesting at intervals it sets; it may restrict or suspend sales if the plan is inadequate or the risk is unacceptable. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and corrective-action plans to regulators and provide results for the relevant model to customers considering or owning it. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market 24 months after adoption must support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a clear customer override or restoration process where safe. For installed units, manufacturers must provide the capability through a retrofit or compatible replacement within 72 months after adoption where technically feasible; where infeasible, they must notify customers and regulators, explain the limitation, and provide an equivalent risk-mitigation plan. Remote shut-off must not replace local protective controls or emergency instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression validated for the battery chemistry and enclosure, or an alternative built-in measure demonstrated through regulator-approved testing to provide equivalent mitigation of fire propagation. Manufacturers must provide validation evidence to regulators and customers. This requirement does not apply retroactively to units already installed.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a thermal incident or material safety risk, provide safe-use and service instructions, and notify customers directly when corrective action is needed. A model-level incident rate must be calculated using a documented method and reported to regulators with the underlying event count, installed-unit denominator, and relevant uncertainty. The regulator must promptly verify the rolling-year event count, installed-unit denominator, calculation, and resolve material data-quality disputes, taking documented uncertainty into account. Verification or dispute resolution must not delay interim risk-reduction measures directed by the regulator. When the regulator verifies that a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling year, a mandatory recall is triggered; uncertainty or data-quality review must not waive a verified exceedance. Manufacturers must promptly provide the regulator a recall plan and take any interim risk-reduction measures directed by the regulator; affected customers must be notified without delay. Regulators may publish recall notices and safety information.
