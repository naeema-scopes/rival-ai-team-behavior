# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B18 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a detailed follow-up within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk, and promptly provide updated instructions as facts develop. Reports must include known incident counts and installed-unit estimates; suspected under-reporting must be corrected promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers must provide per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model, and retain records for audit. Results above a 5% failure rate require a corrective-action plan and retesting; regulators may require evidence-based interim safeguards. A test result alone does not automatically prohibit continued sale. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers of material test findings.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support a secure remote emergency shut-off function no later than 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide customers clear instructions for its use and maintain a staffed process for regulator-directed emergency shutdowns. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance, through retrofit or replacement where technically feasible, within 72 months after adoption. A unit without the function must have an equivalent documented emergency isolation procedure until compliance.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment, and manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions. Regulators may approve an equivalent built-in mitigation design where evidence demonstrates comparable fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices describing known hazards, protective actions, and material changes to risk promptly as information becomes available. Manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers without undue delay when credible evidence indicates a systemic safety risk. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly submit its incident data and corrective-action plan to the regulator and initiate a recall. A manufacturer must also initiate a recall when regulators determine that a serious defect presents an imminent risk of death or significant injury. Incident-rate calculations must use documented incident counts and installed-unit estimates; regulators may require corrective action or a recall based on other compelling safety evidence.
