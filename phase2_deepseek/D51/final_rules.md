# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D51 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or material property damage to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written incident report within 10 calendar days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety risk, and promptly provide updates as facts are confirmed. Reports must include the unit and model identifiers, incident circumstances, and known harm. Suspected incidents must be reported; reporting does not depend on a completed investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results to the regulator before sale and after any material design change. Before purchase, customers must receive that model’s latest test protocol, sample size, and failure rate; these results may be provided to prospective customers for that model but must not be published for general public access. Retesting is required at least every two years and after a material design change. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt notice to the regulator and affected customers, a corrective-action plan, and retesting within six months. Regulators may impose proportionate restrictions, including sales restrictions, where evidence shows adequate safety cannot be demonstrated; exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sale.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Existing installed units that can be retrofitted must be upgraded within six years of adoption. Manufacturers must notify owners of units that cannot be upgraded and provide appropriate alternative emergency guidance.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and foreseeable residential installation conditions, and must be tested as part of the model’s safety evaluation. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions on system operation, inspection, and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase and at installation, customers must receive clear safety instructions, emergency contact details, and information on known hazards, required maintenance, and how to identify and report a safety incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and provide actionable safety instructions. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, using model-specific incident and installed-unit data verified by the regulator. The manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and implement the regulator-approved recall, which may use an appropriate corrective remedy; the threshold itself does not prescribe a particular remedy or automatically require a sales ban.
