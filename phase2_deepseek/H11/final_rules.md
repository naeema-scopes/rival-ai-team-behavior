# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H11 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 business days. Customers whose unit was involved must be notified within 48 hours, with known hazards and immediate safety instructions. Manufacturers must notify affected customers and regulators promptly if new information materially changes the risk assessment.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the regulator and disclose those results to customers who buy or are considering buying that model. A model with a failure rate above 7% may not be sold until it passes a retest. A model above 5% and at or below 7% must submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator and complete a retest within 12 months. Individual company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units manufactured 12 months or more after adoption must support secure remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a safe, accessible shut-off process for users who lack connectivity or choose not to enable remote access. Existing units must be covered by a retrofit or replacement plan submitted to the regulator within 12 months; feasible upgrades must be completed within 6 years after adoption. Remote shut-off must not prevent local emergency response or create a new material safety risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required for new units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption. The system must be validated for the battery chemistry and foreseeable installation conditions, and must not introduce a greater fire, toxic-exposure, or electrical hazard. Manufacturers must provide the regulator with the suppression-system validation results before the affected units are placed on the market.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, before purchase and through the product’s supported customer-information channels, clear safety notices covering known material hazards, required installation and maintenance precautions, and model-specific 55 °C test results. Customers must be notified promptly of newly identified material risks and provided with immediate safety instructions. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly investigate, submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator, and provide affected customers with a safety notice. A mandatory recall is required when a model exceeds 12 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when a regulator determines that a defect presents an imminent risk of serious injury or death. Rates and thresholds apply to individual models, not company-wide totals.
