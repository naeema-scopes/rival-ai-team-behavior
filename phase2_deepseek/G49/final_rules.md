# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G49 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every confirmed thermal incident involving a home battery—including overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway—to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 days. The initial report shall identify the model, known circumstances, injuries or property damage, and immediate safety actions. Manufacturers shall notify the affected customer within 24 hours, or as soon as contact is practicable, and provide known hazards, immediate safety steps, and a contact for assistance. Incidents presenting an imminent risk to other users or the public shall be reported to the regulator and affected customers immediately.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. A model’s failure rate shall not exceed 7%. Results, including per-model failure rates, shall be submitted to the regulator and provided to customers who buy that model; they shall not be publicly disclosed. A result above 5% shall trigger regulator review and a corrective-action plan within 30 days. The regulator shall assess test results, incident evidence, and remediation and may require sales restrictions or other corrective measures where warranted; exceeding 5% does not itself impose an automatic sales ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models introduced 24 months after this standard takes effect shall support remote emergency shut-off. For units already installed when the standard takes effect, manufacturers shall provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within 6 years where technically feasible, with clear instructions and no charge to the customer.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after this standard takes effect shall include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance protocol. Manufacturers shall provide the regulator with evidence of compliance before placing a covered model on the market. This requirement does not apply to units manufactured before that date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of a confirmed thermal incident involving their unit, including the known hazard, immediate safety steps, and a contact for assistance. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period shall trigger prompt regulator notification and a documented safety review. The regulator shall assess incident evidence and the manufacturer’s corrective-action plan and may require a recall, sales restrictions, or other protective measures where warranted. The manufacturer shall notify the regulator within 10 days of reaching the trigger. If a recall is ordered, the manufacturer shall promptly issue customer recall instructions and remedies. Public notices shall identify affected models and safety actions, but shall not disclose per-model test failure rates.
