# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B05 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include known facts, unit/model identification, immediate safety guidance, and updates as the investigation proceeds. Suspected incidents must be reported even before the cause is confirmed; manufacturers must correct or supplement reports when new information becomes available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a documented, regulator-accepted protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must test before market introduction, at least annually, and after material design or manufacturing changes. Within 30 days of each test, manufacturers must provide the full per-model results and protocol to the relevant regulator and to customers who own or are considering purchasing that model. Regulators must treat these results as confidential except where disclosure is required by law. If a model’s failure rate exceeds 5%, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, submit a corrective-action plan and retest schedule, and follow any risk-based protective measures the regulator orders. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically bar sale, suspend sales, or require recall; regulators may require additional testing or stronger action where evidence indicates a serious safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery models must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or emergency responders when a credible imminent safety risk exists, subject to safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide clear customer notice when shut-off is activated and instructions for safe follow-up. For installed units, manufacturers must provide the capability through retrofit or an equivalent effective safety measure within 6 years of adoption, where technically feasible; any infeasible units and interim protections must be reported to the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression meeting applicable performance and safety requirements beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s effectiveness and maintenance requirements and provide them to regulators and customers. Units manufactured before the effective date are not subject to this requirement.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear installation and operating safety instructions, known material risks, and instructions for reporting incidents. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified safety risks and provide interim precautions and updates. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, provide supporting data and a risk assessment, and initiate a recall. The manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers, explain the remedy and timeline, and report recall progress. Regulators may require interim protections during the recall and may require a recall or other protective action below the threshold when evidence indicates a serious safety risk.
