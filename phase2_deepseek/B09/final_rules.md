# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B09 · variant B · NOT adopted (signatures: {})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, with a follow-up report of the investigation and corrective actions within 30 days. Within 72 hours, manufacturers shall also notify affected owners and occupants whose units may present a related hazard, including immediate precautions and support contacts. Reports shall include known incident counts and installed-unit denominators; corrections and previously omitted incidents shall be reported promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a validated sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least every three years or after a material design change. The maximum allowed failure rate is 8% for each model. A model exceeding 8% shall not be placed on the market, and any affected marketed model shall undergo regulator-directed corrective action and retesting before continued sale. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test methods and complete results to regulators and disclose results to current and prospective customers of that model, including the test date, failure rate, and any required safety limitations or corrective actions. Results shall not be published in a manner identifying an individual company or model; regulators may publish aggregated, non-identifying results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new models placed on the market 24 months or more after adoption shall include remote emergency shut-off that remains operable during a thermal emergency where technically practicable, with clear instructions for customers and emergency responders. Manufacturers shall provide a plan to regulators for enabling this capability on compatible installed units and shall complete those upgrades within 6 years of adoption where feasible; any unit not upgraded shall receive clear notice of its limitation and safe shutdown instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to reduce the risk of fire spread. Manufacturers shall provide evidence of design validation to regulators and disclose the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive clear information on thermal risks, installation and maintenance requirements, emergency actions, and available safety updates. Manufacturers shall promptly notify owners and occupants of material newly identified hazards and provide interim precautions; notices of an urgent hazard shall be issued within 72 hours of identification. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and provide incident-rate calculations and supporting data. The regulator shall review the information and may require corrective action or a recall, taking account of incident severity, investigation evidence, and the model’s risk. Regulators may require a recall at any rate where a serious hazard warrants it. Within 72 hours of initiating a recall, the manufacturer shall notify affected customers and the regulator of the recall and its remedy.
