# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G10 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written incident report within 10 calendar days. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a safety risk relevant to their model or requires customer action. Reports shall include the model, event date and location, known consequences, and any immediate safety measures. Manufacturers shall keep incident records and disclose them to regulators on request.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C using a regulator-approved sampling protocol. A model’s failure rate shall not exceed 5%. Manufacturers shall promptly notify the regulator of any result above 5%, submit a corrective-action plan, and retest on a defined schedule. A model exceeding 5% shall not be newly sold until it passes a retest or the regulator approves an alternative risk-control plan. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results, including failure rates and test protocols, to the responsible regulator. Before purchase, customers shall receive the model’s full per-model test results, including its failure rate and test protocol, along with compliance status and applicable safety measures. Individual companies’ per-model failure rates shall not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems shall include remote emergency shut-off capability for installations beginning 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall provide a secure means for the customer or an authorized emergency responder to initiate shut-off, with clear instructions and safeguards against unauthorized access. For units already installed at adoption, manufacturers shall provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within 72 months, where technically feasible; any unit not upgraded shall receive clear customer instructions for local emergency isolation. Shut-off capability shall be tested during installation and maintained in accordance with manufacturer instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems manufactured for sale or installation shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The system shall be designed to control or contain a battery fire and shall be validated through a regulator-accepted test protocol. Manufacturers shall provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers and retain evidence of design validation for regulator review. Units placed on the market before the effective date are not required to be retrofitted under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive the model’s per-model 55°C sustained-heat test results, incident-rate information, compliance status, and applicable safety instructions. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers and the responsible regulator of a newly identified model-specific safety risk, explaining the risk and any action customers should take. When a model’s thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit a documented corrective-action plan. A mandatory recall is required when a model’s rate exceeds 10 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, or whenever the regulator determines that an imminent serious hazard exists, regardless of the rate. For rates above 8 through 10, the regulator may require a recall or approve an alternative risk-control plan only if it adequately protects customers and the public. Rates shall be calculated from incidents and installed-unit exposure for that model, with the method and supporting records provided to the regulator. Notices shall state the hazard, affected units, immediate precautions, and remedy; manufacturers shall provide progress updates to the regulator and affected customers.
