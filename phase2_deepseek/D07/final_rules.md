# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D07 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway, including incidents that cause injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response. Report a death, serious injury, fire, or suspected thermal runaway within 24 hours of becoming aware; report other thermal incidents within 5 business days. Submit an initial account of known facts and provide material updates and a final investigation report as they become available. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly when an incident indicates a hazard that could affect their unit, with immediate notice for an urgent risk and otherwise within 5 business days. Reports must include model, incident date and location, consequences, and known corrective actions. Preserve incident records and provide them to regulators on request.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved, statistically representative protocol. Manufacturers must submit full per-model results and corrective-action plans confidentially to regulators. A result above the 5% failure-rate benchmark triggers regulator review and a time-bound corrective-action plan, but does not automatically prohibit sales. Regulators may require additional testing, mitigation, sales restrictions, or other proportionate measures based on the evidence and risk. Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s actual 55 °C test failure rate, along with relevant context, safety precautions, and any model-specific hazards or corrective actions. Manufacturers must promptly provide updated safety information to existing customers if new or corrected results materially affect their model. Regulators may publish aggregated or anonymized findings.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to provide this capability within 6 years of the standard’s effective date, prioritizing units with identified safety risks. A regulator may approve an alternative mitigation plan where a retrofit is infeasible, provided it offers equivalent protection.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to reduce the risk or consequences of thermal runaway. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of the standard. Regulators may approve an equivalent built-in suppression solution supported by evidence of comparable protection. Manufacturers must provide compliance evidence to regulators before placing covered units on the market.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive prompt, plain-language notice of safety incidents or hazards that could affect their unit, including recommended precautions and how to obtain support. Manufacturers must notify affected customers and regulators when a safety defect is identified and provide updates as corrective actions proceed. A recall is mandatory when any individual model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units for that model in a year. Calculate and report each model’s rate separately; do not substitute a company-wide rate. The manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers and provide safe repair, replacement, or refund options.
