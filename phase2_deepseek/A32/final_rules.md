# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A32 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete follow-up report within 10 business days. Customers with affected units shall receive a prompt safety notice within 72 hours of confirmed material risk, including immediate precautions and support contacts. Manufacturers shall keep incident records and report aggregate annual incident counts to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and an independent laboratory, across a statistically adequate sample. Manufacturers shall provide complete per-model results to the relevant regulator and retain results for audit. Results above the 5% benchmark shall trigger regulator review and a time-bound corrective-action plan proportionate to risk; they shall not by themselves automatically bar further sales. Owners of each model shall receive the complete per-model test results, as well as material safety risks, precautions, and corrective actions. Material safety risks and precautions shall be promptly communicated to affected customers. Complete results need not be disclosed to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall provide a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with a safe local shut-off option that remains available if communications fail. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers shall provide a retrofit or equivalent remedy within six years, prioritizing units with elevated risk and notifying affected customers of interim precautions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units manufactured 36 months or more after this standard is adopted shall include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance requirements. Manufacturers shall disclose suppression capabilities and limitations to customers. The regulator shall establish test and verification procedures before the requirement takes effect.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with model-specific safety notices, including known hazards, incident trends, safe-use and emergency instructions, and material changes to safety information. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the regulator shall promptly verify the model-specific rate using manufacturer records. Once verified, the manufacturer shall initiate a recall of that model and promptly notify affected customers of the risk, required precautions, and recall remedy. Company-wide aggregate rates shall not by themselves trigger a model recall. Manufacturers shall maintain supporting records and provide them to the regulator for verification, and shall provide customers with updates through completion of the recall remedy.
