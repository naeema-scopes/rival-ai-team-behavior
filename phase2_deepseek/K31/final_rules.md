# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K31 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency intervention to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when the incident indicates a credible safety risk, and promptly provide safety instructions. Reports must include model, incident date and location, and known outcomes. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and report confirmed omissions or corrections to regulators without delay.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually, using a representative sample and a disclosed test protocol. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, methods, and corrective actions to regulators. Customers considering or owning a model must receive its actual per-model test results, including the failure rate, sample size, test method, and material corrective actions, directly through customer-facing channels; a general safety summary alone is insufficient. These results are not required to be posted publicly. A result above 5% requires prompt notice to regulators and affected customers, corrective action, and a regulator-verified retest within 180 days. During remediation, continued sales may proceed subject to regulator-required interim safeguards. If a regulator-verified retest still exceeds 5%, the regulator must determine whether new sales should be suspended, taking account of the test results, incident record, corrective actions, and interim safeguards; it may permit continued sales under enforceable conditions where the evidence does not show serious risk. Regulators may restrict existing use where evidence shows a serious risk. Manufacturers must disclose results and corrective actions to customers.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models must include remote emergency shut-off capability. For units already installed when this standard is adopted, manufacturers must provide a compatible retrofit or other effective remote shut-off solution within 6 years. They must publish annual progress and offer the solution in stages, prioritizing units without shut-off capability and installations presenting elevated risk. A manufacturer seeking an extension for a specific unit or installation must document technical infeasibility to the regulator, which may approve only a limited, reasoned exception and require interim risk controls. Manufacturers must notify customers of availability, timing, approved exceptions, and limitations. They must provide clear instructions for using the feature and must not represent it as a substitute for emergency services or safe isolation procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide evidence that the suppression system is suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment, and disclose its maintenance requirements and limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear installation, operation, emergency-response, and maintenance safety instructions at purchase, plus prompt notice of newly identified material risks and any required corrective action. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Regulators may also require a recall or other corrective action when evidence shows an imminent or serious risk, regardless of the numerical trigger. Manufacturers must notify affected customers directly and provide instructions and remedies.
