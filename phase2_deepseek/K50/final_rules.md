# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K50 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents causing no injury or property damage. A complete report, including known cause, model, serial/batch identifiers, and corrective actions, is due within 10 calendar days; updates are due as facts change. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to units of the same model or batch, and promptly provide interim safety instructions. Reports must not be withheld because an incident is under investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. The failure rate shall not exceed 5% for any model. Before placing a model on the market and at least annually thereafter, the manufacturer shall submit the per-model test protocol, sample size, results, and failure rate to the regulator. The manufacturer shall provide the model’s results and failure rate to customers considering purchase and to owners of that model; individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be publicly published. A model exceeding the limit shall promptly be reported to the regulator and may be marketed only under an expressly approved, time-limited corrective-action plan that includes customer protections, monitoring, and retesting on a regulator-set schedule. The regulator shall suspend new sales if the plan or protections are inadequate, or if retesting does not demonstrate compliance by the approved deadline.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## S3_remote_shutoff
New home battery systems shall include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers shall provide a retrofit for already-installed units, or an equally effective safety remedy where retrofit is infeasible, within six years of this standard taking effect. Shut-off must be available to authorized emergency responders and the customer, operate safely during loss of ordinary connectivity where practicable, and include protections against unauthorized access. Manufacturers shall provide clear instructions and disclose any limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers shall provide clear information about the suppression system’s capabilities, maintenance, and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety instructions at purchase and promptly notify affected owners of material safety risks, required precautions, and corrective actions, including interim instructions while a remedy is pending. Manufacturers shall notify regulators and affected customers when evidence indicates a model-specific safety risk. A mandatory recall is required when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; manufacturers shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers and implement the regulator-approved recall remedy. The regulator shall determine a proportionate remedy, which may include repair, replacement, refund, or other protective action; a recall does not by itself require permanent market exclusion. Any continued sales during corrective action must be expressly authorized by the regulator and must not expose customers or the public to unacceptable risk. Incident rates and recall determinations shall use documented, model-specific data and be updated as verified information becomes available.
