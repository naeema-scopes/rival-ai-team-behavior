# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K23 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents involving injury or property damage. They shall provide a verified incident report, including known circumstances and corrective actions, within 10 calendar days, and update it when material information becomes available. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety risk to their model or batch, and promptly provide protective instructions. Reports shall include incidents regardless of whether injury or damage occurred.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample before sale and after material design changes. Manufacturers shall submit test methods, sample sizes, and per-model results to the regulator and disclose the per-model results to customers who buy that model, including the test date and protocol. Regulators may publish aggregate results. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5%. A model exceeding this limit shall enter a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retest within 24 months. Exceeding the limit alone shall not bar sales during that period; the regulator may require interim safeguards or restrict sales where evidence indicates an imminent safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market after this standard takes effect shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall retrofit already-installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within six years, where technically feasible, and shall provide customers clear instructions on how to use the feature. Where retrofit is not technically feasible, manufacturers shall notify affected customers and the regulator and provide an alternative risk-mitigation plan for regulator approval.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression shall be required on all new units manufactured 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall disclose whether each unit includes built-in suppression and provide customers with applicable safety and maintenance instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers clear safety notices promptly when new evidence indicates a material risk affecting their model or batch, including protective instructions and any corrective action. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, based on regulator-validated data. Manufacturers shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop distribution of the affected model, and carry out the regulator-approved recall. Regulators may require a recall or other protective measures below this threshold when the evidence warrants.
