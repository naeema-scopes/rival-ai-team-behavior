# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H02 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency intervention to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a follow-up investigation report within 30 days. Affected customers shall be notified within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their model or requires action; notices shall describe the hazard and steps to take. Manufacturers shall provide annual model-level incident counts and installed-unit denominators to regulators. Reports shall not be withheld because an incident is under investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after any material design change. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% of tested units. Manufacturers shall submit exact per-model results to the relevant regulator and provide them to customers who own or are considering that model; company-specific per-model results shall not be published to the general public. If a model exceeds 5%, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and submit a corrective-action plan and retest schedule. The regulator shall determine whether sales may continue under interim safeguards while corrective action and retesting proceed, based on the level of risk; exceeding 5% alone shall not automatically require a sales ban. Before unrestricted sales resume, the model must meet the limit on retest or receive regulator approval of an alternative risk-control plan.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally operable alternative. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units that do not support remote shut-off within 6 years of adoption. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers how the feature works, its limitations, and how to obtain assistance; shut-off capability shall not replace required physical safety protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Suppression systems shall be validated for the battery chemistry and installation environment, maintained over the unit’s service life, and accompanied by customer instructions and servicing information. This requirement does not replace thermal safety testing or incident reporting.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, warning signs, and steps to take, and promptly update notices when new material safety information becomes available. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, provide the regulator with model-level incident counts and installed-unit denominators, and initiate a mandatory recall. The manufacturer shall explain the hazard and available remedy to customers and report recall progress to the regulator.
