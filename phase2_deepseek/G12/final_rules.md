# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G12 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it, including incidents with no injury or property damage. Incidents involving injury, evacuation, fire-service response, or material property damage shall be reported within 24 hours. Reports shall include model, location, known consequences, and corrective actions. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a product-specific safety risk, and promptly provide material safety updates.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol that specifies sample size and failure-rate calculation. Manufacturers shall submit complete per-model results, including failure rates and test methods, confidentially to regulators. Manufacturers shall provide the complete results for a model to current and prospective customers of that model, in an accessible form, before or at purchase. Results need not be published to the general public as individual-company per-model failure rates. The maximum allowed failure rate is 7%. A model exceeding 7% shall be subject to a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retesting; it shall not be newly placed on the market until it meets the limit. Regulators may restrict sales of models already on the market if corrective actions do not adequately address the risk. Regulators shall review the threshold after three years using incident and test evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery models placed on the market from 24 months after adoption shall support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade for compatible already-installed units within 6 years of adoption, prioritizing units with known safety risks. Where retrofit is technically infeasible, the manufacturer shall notify the owner and provide a safe alternative.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured for sale or installed from 36 months after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance and reliability requirements. Manufacturers shall provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose the suppression system’s limitations to customers. Regulators shall review the requirements periodically as technology and safety evidence develop.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks, incidents, corrective actions, and safety updates, using direct, accessible communications and providing instructions for safe use. Manufacturers shall report each model’s annual thermal-incident rate to regulators and disclose it to customers who have purchased or are offered that model. A manufacturer shall initiate a regulator-supervised recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The recall notice shall identify affected units, explain the risk and remedy, and provide customers with timely repair, replacement, or refund instructions.
