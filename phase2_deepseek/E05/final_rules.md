# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E05 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any fire, thermal runaway, or overheating event that causes injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response within 24 hours of learning of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 10 business days. Reports must include the model, serial number, date, location, known consequences, and any corrective action, with updates as facts are confirmed. Manufacturers must notify the affected customer within 24 hours for events involving injury, fire, evacuation, or an immediate safety risk, and within 10 business days for other confirmed thermal incidents. Preserve incident records and provide requested records to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Before sale and at least annually thereafter, manufacturers must test each model and submit results, protocol, sample size, and corrective actions to the regulator. Results and corrective actions must be provided to customers who buy that model and made available to prospective customers before sale. Per-model results need not be publicly disclosed, but may be shared with the other participating company under appropriate confidentiality protections. A model with a failure rate above 5% must promptly implement corrective action and retest. A model may not be sold if its failure rate exceeds 7%; sales may resume after a retest demonstrates a rate at or below 7%. Regulators may require additional measures based on the risk, including measures for units already installed.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent hazard, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under regulator-approved safeguards. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and maintain secure, reliable access to the function. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent compliant upgrade within six years of this standard taking effect; where a retrofit is infeasible, the regulator may approve an alternative safety measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must document that the suppression system is suitable for the battery chemistry and installation and provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions. Regulators may approve an equivalent protective measure where built-in suppression is technically infeasible, provided the measure offers comparable protection.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices for confirmed thermal incidents involving their unit, including known hazards, immediate precautions, and contact information, within the timelines in S1. They must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of material new risks or corrective actions and provide updated instructions. For each model, calculate the thermal-incident rate for each rolling 12-month period as confirmed thermal incidents divided by the number of that model’s installed units, expressed per 100,000 units. Manufacturers must report supporting incident and installed-unit data to regulators, who may audit the calculation. If a model’s verified rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, investigate, and initiate a recall. Recall measures must be proportionate to the hazard and may include repair, replacement, or refund; they must not delay urgent protective action. Regulators retain authority to require earlier protective measures where the evidence indicates urgent risk. Manufacturers must provide regulators supporting evidence, investigation findings, and recall instructions, and keep affected customers informed of remedies and timing.
