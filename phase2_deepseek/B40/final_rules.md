# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B40 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it, and notify affected customers within 10 business days when the incident may indicate a safety risk. Imminent hazards must be reported to regulators and affected customers without delay, and no later than 24 hours after discovery. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial number, and any corrective action; manufacturers must promptly supplement incomplete reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least every 3 years thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit full per-model results, including failure rates, to regulators. Before purchase, purchasers must receive the full test results, including the failure rate, for the model they are considering. A failure rate above 5% triggers regulator notification, a corrective-action plan, and retesting on a regulator-approved schedule; it does not by itself prohibit continued production. Regulators may require stronger measures based on the totality of test and incident evidence. Review the threshold after 3 years using incident and test data.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market 2 years after this standard takes effect must support secure remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a practical retrofit for installed units that can support the function, completing the retrofit within 6 years after the effective date. Shut-off must be designed to avoid unsafe operation, require authenticated authorization, and preserve local emergency protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved safety and reliability protocol. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of compliance before sale. The requirement does not replace other fire-safety protections or incident-reporting duties.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must give purchasers clear safety information at sale, including known material hazards, safe-use and emergency instructions, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a material safety risk and provide updated instructions or corrective-action information; imminent hazards require notice without delay and no later than 24 hours after discovery. When a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and provide incident and corrective-action data. Regulators shall assess the evidence, including incident severity, test results, and corrective actions, and may require a recall or other protective action where warranted. Any recall must include notice to affected customers, an explanation of the hazard and remedy, and a remedy without charge.
