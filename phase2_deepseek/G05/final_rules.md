# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G05 · variant G · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 38})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it. An incident involving death, serious injury, or an ongoing hazard must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a material safety risk for their unit or model, and promptly provide safety instructions. Reports must include available incident details and be updated as investigations develop; suspected incidents must not be omitted merely because the cause is not yet confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually, using a representative sample and a common test protocol agreed by the parties. A model with a failure rate above 10% must not be newly marketed. For a model with a failure rate above 5% and up to 10%, the manufacturer must implement corrective action and enhanced monitoring, and provide its corrective-action plan to regulators. Manufacturers must provide each model’s test results and corrective-action plans directly to regulators and to customers who own or are considering that model, in time for customers to make informed safety decisions. Individual per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public, without limiting disclosure to those customers or regulators.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery models must provide a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, when necessary to address an imminent safety hazard, by the manufacturer or emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed when this standard is adopted, manufacturers must enable remote shut-off where supported and offer a retrofit or equivalent risk-reduction measure for other units within 6 years. Any shut-off action must be accompanied by prompt notice to affected customers and instructions for safe follow-up.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and enclosure. Manufacturers must provide test evidence demonstrating that the system operates as intended and does not introduce additional hazards. Until the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must disclose whether a unit has built-in fire suppression and provide customers with applicable fire-safety instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its 55 °C sustained-heat test failure rate, thermal-incident information, safety features, and applicable operating and emergency instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks, corrective actions, and any change to safe-use instructions; notices must be direct, accessible, and not limited to online postings. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers and initiate a recall, including communicating recall instructions and remedies directly to affected customers. The trigger is assessed separately for each model. Manufacturers must provide regulators with incident data and proposed corrective measures.
