# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E42 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, evacuation, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a full incident report within 10 calendar days. They must notify the affected household within 24 hours when safe contact details are available, and promptly notify nearby residents when the incident presents an ongoing hazard to them. Reports must include known unit and model identifiers, incident circumstances, and corrective actions. Suspected under-reporting must be disclosed and corrected promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and failures to regulators and provide results to customers who buy that model and prospective customers before purchase. Results must also be provided to the other party’s designated safety authority under confidentiality protections; they must not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt notice to the regulator, a corrective-action plan, and confirmatory testing on a regulator-approved schedule. A model may remain on sale while following that plan unless the regulator determines a restriction is necessary based on the evidence and safety risk. A failure rate above 7% requires a regulator-directed risk assessment and may warrant a stop-sale or other protective measures.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a secure, tested shut-off capability and instructions for its use. Existing installed systems lacking this capability must be retrofitted or replaced within six years of the standard’s effective date, where technically feasible; the manufacturer must notify affected owners of the schedule and any interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and reliability standard. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of the standard. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system’s operating limits and maintenance requirements to customers. Regulators may approve equivalent protective technology where it provides comparable fire-risk mitigation.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of a safety hazard, required precautions, corrective actions, and how to obtain assistance; notices must be clear, accessible, and updated as material facts change. For each model, manufacturers must calculate and report its annual thermal-incident rate per 100,000 installed units to the regulator, using a documented method and promptly correcting material errors. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers and initiate a recall with an effective remedy, such as repair, replacement, or refund, subject to regulator oversight. A recall requires an effective remedy; it does not by itself impose a market ban, though regulators may order a stop-sale or other measures when necessary to protect safety. The manufacturer must also notify the public when a hazard may affect neighbours or others beyond the affected household. Recall notices must explain the risk, identify affected models and units, state the remedy and timeline, and provide contact details.
