# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H10 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account of known facts. They shall provide an updated investigation report within 30 days. Affected customers shall be notified promptly, and no later than 72 hours after the manufacturer determines their unit or safety may be affected. Reports shall include incidents regardless of whether injury or fire occurred.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a regulator-approved, statistically representative sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers shall submit each model’s failure rate, sample size, test date, and corrective-action information to the regulator, and shall disclose those per-model results to customers who buy that model. Public disclosure may be aggregated and non-identifying. A model with a failure rate above 5% shall submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator within 60 days and be retested within 12 months. Regulators may require additional measures based on test results and evidence of risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units placed on the market shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide a secure, accessible method for authorized emergency shut-off and clear customer instructions. Units already installed when the standard is adopted shall be upgraded to support remote shut-off, or replaced with compliant units, within six years, where technically feasible. Manufacturers shall report infeasibility and interim risk-reduction measures to the regulator and affected customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units placed on the market shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide evidence of suppression-system effectiveness and maintenance requirements to the regulator and customers. Any identified limitation or hazard associated with the suppression system shall be disclosed promptly to affected customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety and installation instructions at purchase and prompt notices of newly identified material hazards, recommended actions, and service updates. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator, investigate the cause, and submit its findings and proposed corrective actions. A recall shall be initiated when the regulator determines that a serious defect presents an unreasonable risk, or when the manufacturer’s investigation establishes that a recall is necessary to address a material safety risk. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers and regulators without delay and provide a corrective-action plan.
