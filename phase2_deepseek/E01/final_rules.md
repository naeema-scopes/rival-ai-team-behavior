# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E01 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, evacuation, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a follow-up report within 30 days. Other confirmed thermal incidents shall be reported within 10 business days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 48 hours of a credible serious hazard, and within 10 business days for other incidents presenting a material safety concern. Reports shall include known unit and model identifiers, incident circumstances, and corrective actions; updates shall be provided as facts are confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a standardized 55 °C sustained-heat test before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved sample size and protocol. Manufacturers shall submit complete per-model results, including sample size and failures, to the relevant regulator and provide the complete results for that model to customers before purchase. Results shall include the model’s failure rate, test date, and protocol. If a model exceeds the 5% failure-rate benchmark, the manufacturer shall submit a corrective-action plan within 30 days and complete retesting within 90 days. Exceeding the benchmark alone shall not automatically prohibit sale; the regulator may impose proportionate restrictions where warranted by the evidence, including an imminent serious risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide a secure, accessible shut-off capability and clear instructions for its use. Existing installed units shall be brought into compliance, through retrofit or replacement where technically feasible, within six years after adoption. Shut-off capability shall not prevent emergency responders or occupants from using safe local controls.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression designed for the hazards associated with the battery chemistry and installation. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall provide installation and maintenance instructions and evidence of suppression-system performance to the relevant regulator.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive clear information on installation, safe operation, emergency response, and how to report a suspected defect. Manufacturers shall promptly notify owners and relevant authorities of newly identified material hazards and provide updated safety instructions and available remedies. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 verified thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when the regulator determines that a defect poses an imminent serious risk; the regulator may require a broader recall where warranted by the evidence. For this threshold, manufacturers shall count confirmed incidents involving fire, smoke, or uncontrolled overheating and use the model’s installed-unit population during the same year as the denominator. They shall maintain auditable incident and unit-count records and provide them to the regulator on request.
