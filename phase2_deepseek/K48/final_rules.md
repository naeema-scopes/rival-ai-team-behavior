# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K48 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a material safety risk to their units or requires customer action. Reports shall include known circumstances, units and models involved, injuries or damage, and immediate mitigation. Manufacturers shall maintain incident records and report aggregate annual incident counts by model and installed-unit denominator to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C under a common regulator-approved protocol before sale and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers shall submit exact per-model protocols and results to the relevant regulator and provide the exact failure rate and test date to customers considering purchase of that model. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% for any model. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a corrective-action plan, and retesting on a regulator-approved schedule. The regulator shall determine proportionate interim measures based on risk; an exceedance alone does not automatically require permanent market withdrawal. Manufacturers shall maintain records of test results and corrective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability. For units already installed, manufacturers shall provide the capability through retrofit or another regulator-approved equivalent within 6 years of adoption where technically feasible. Manufacturers shall publish a retrofit plan within 12 months and notify affected customers of safe interim steps. The regulator may approve an alternative schedule or equivalent protective measure where retrofit is infeasible.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression, or a regulator-approved equivalent fire-mitigation system, beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall submit an implementation plan within 12 months and provide customers with interim fire-safety instructions until the requirement takes effect.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive the model’s exact failure rate and test date for its latest 55°C sustained-heat test, material safety warnings, and instructions for emergency response and reporting. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified hazards and provide updated safety instructions. A manufacturer shall initiate a regulator-supervised recall when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, or when the regulator determines a defect presents an imminent risk of serious injury or fire. The regulator shall determine risk-appropriate customer remedies, which may include repair, replacement, or refund; a recall under this section does not by itself require a permanent market ban. Recall notices shall explain the hazard, affected units, actions customers must take, and available remedies.
