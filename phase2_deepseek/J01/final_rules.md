# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J01 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every confirmed thermal incident involving a home battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents involving fire, injury, evacuation, or emergency-service response. Reports shall identify the affected model and include available incident and installed-base information; manufacturers shall provide a written update within 10 business days and supplement the report as further facts become available. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers when an incident indicates a material safety risk or requires customer action. Manufacturers shall submit any other suspected thermal incidents in a quarterly report, clearly identified as unconfirmed, and preserve incident records for regulator review.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry, at least annually thereafter, and after material design changes. The maximum allowed failure rate is 7% for any model. Manufacturers shall submit complete per-model results to the relevant regulator and provide them to customers buying that model; results need not be published to the general public. Manufacturers shall retain supporting records for regulator review. If a model exceeds 5%, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and enhanced monitoring measures, and conduct a confirmatory test within 12 months. During that period, continued sales may occur only with regulator approval, required customer safety warnings, and any interim safeguards the regulator specifies. Where the manufacturer demonstrates measurable progress and the regulator finds continued supervised sales safe, the regulator may extend the corrective-action period once, for up to a further 12 months, with enhanced monitoring and any additional safeguards it requires. If the model fails to meet the 7% limit by the applicable deadline, sales shall cease until it passes a confirmatory test and the regulator approves corrective action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery models placed on the market 24 months after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a means for customers or emergency responders to verify shut-off status. Manufacturers shall provide clear instructions for use. For installed units, manufacturers shall provide a feasible retrofit or replacement plan for units that can support the feature within 72 months of adoption; where retrofitting is not technically feasible, they shall disclose that limitation and provide alternative emergency-response instructions within 72 months of adoption.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Home battery units manufactured for sale 36 months or more after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance test. The requirement applies to new units and does not require retrofitting units already installed. Manufacturers shall document test results and provide them to regulators on request. Regulators may approve a narrowly scoped, time-limited exemption where a manufacturer demonstrates that compliant suppression is not technically feasible and equivalent risk controls are in place.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with model-specific 55 °C test results, clear installation and emergency-response instructions, and prompt safety notices when an incident or new evidence indicates a material risk or customer action is needed. Manufacturers shall maintain a public recall and safety-notice page and notify affected customers directly. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on validated incident and installed-base data; manufacturers shall promptly report the exceedance to regulators and customers and initiate the recall without delay. The recall shall provide an effective remedy, such as repair, replacement, or safe decommissioning, as appropriate to the risk. The threshold triggers a recall and does not by itself require a permanent market ban; regulators may require sales suspension or other restrictions while corrective action is assessed. Regulators may require an earlier recall where the evidence indicates an imminent serious risk.
