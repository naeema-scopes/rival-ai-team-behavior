# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B52 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal runaway, fire, overheating event, or injury/property-damage event involving a home battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware, with a preliminary incident report within 5 business days and a final investigation report within 30 days. Affected customers must be notified within 48 hours when the event may indicate a hazard in their unit or model. Reports must include known circumstances and corrective actions; updates are required as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry and at least every 3 years or after a material design change. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt reporting of the results to the regulator and a regulator-approved corrective-action and retesting plan; it is not by itself an automatic market ban. The regulator may impose proportionate market restrictions based on the full safety evidence and progress on corrective actions. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, including the failure rate and test date, to regulators and to customers who buy or own that model. Results need not be published publicly. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety findings and provide relevant precautions or corrective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized access and clear instructions for customers and emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit program for installed units, prioritizing higher-risk units, and complete it within 6 years of the standard taking effect. Where retrofit is impracticable, the manufacturer must provide an effective alternative safety measure and notify affected customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide clear information on the system’s operation, limitations, and maintenance. Until the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must document their fire-mitigation measures and provide customers with relevant safety instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, at purchase and upon material safety updates, clear safety instructions, emergency contact details, model-specific safety warnings and recommended precautions. Customers who buy or own a model must receive that model’s 55 °C test failure rate and test date. Per-model test results must also be provided to regulators; they need not be published publicly. When a credible hazard may affect a unit or model, affected customers must be notified promptly with the hazard, recommended precautions, and any corrective action. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year triggers a mandatory, proportionate recall. The regulator shall determine the appropriate remedy—repair, replacement, or removal—and timeline based on verified model-specific evidence, while ensuring affected customers are protected. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of the recall, explain the remedy and timeline, and provide updates until it is complete.
