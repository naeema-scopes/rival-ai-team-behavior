# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J47 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller investigation report within 30 days. They must notify affected customers and, where an incident presents a credible risk to nearby people or property, provide appropriate safety instructions as soon as practicable and no later than 72 hours after determining the risk. Reports must include known incidents even if the cause is not yet established; manufacturers must promptly correct incomplete reports. At least annually, manufacturers must reconcile incident reports against service, warranty, and customer-contact records, disclose any previously unreported incidents to the regulator, and explain the reporting gap and corrective steps.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a documented sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the relevant regulator. Results must be disclosed to customers who buy that model; regulators may publish aggregate, non-identifying results but not individual-company per-model failure rates. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retest schedule. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7% per model. Regulators may restrict sales if a manufacturer fails to meet its plan or if the evidence indicates unacceptable risk. Regulators shall periodically review the protocol and limits in light of implementation and safety evidence.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## S3_remote_shutoff
New units placed on the market must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, under defined emergency conditions, by the manufacturer or emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation. Existing installed units must be retrofitted or replaced with compatible remote shut-off capability within six years of the standard taking effect, where technically feasible; manufacturers must notify owners of the schedule and any interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that date. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any applicable limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety instructions at purchase and prompt notice of material safety risks, updated operating guidance, and any corrective action affecting their unit. Manufacturers must notify affected customers and regulators promptly when credible evidence indicates a model-specific safety risk. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly report the rate and supporting data to the regulator and submit a risk assessment and proposed corrective action. The regulator shall determine whether a recall or other protective action is required based on the evidence and risk to customers and the public.
