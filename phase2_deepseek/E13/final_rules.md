# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E13 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving fire, smoke, overheating, or emergency shut-off to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. They must notify the affected customer within 24 hours when the incident involves their unit, and promptly provide safety instructions. Reports must include model, incident date, known circumstances, injuries or property damage, and corrective actions. Manufacturers must reconcile incident records with regulator submissions at least annually and promptly report any previously unreported incidents identified through that reconciliation, explaining the delay and corrective steps taken to prevent recurrence.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt regulator notification, a corrective-action plan, and retesting on a regulator-approved schedule; it is not, by itself, a prohibition on sale. Manufacturers must submit per-model protocols and results to the regulator before sale and after any material design change, and retain supporting records. They must provide per-model results, including the failure rate, to prospective customers before purchase and existing customers on request; individual-company per-model failure rates must not be publicly published. Regulators may require proportionate safeguards or restrict sales based on evidence of unacceptable safety risk, considering validated test results and corrective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent compliant solution for already-installed units within six years of this standard taking effect, prioritizing units presenting the greatest risk. Shut-off systems must include safeguards against unauthorized activation, and customers must be informed of operation, limitations, and any required connectivity.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard is adopted. Suppression systems must be suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment, and designed and tested to limit fire propagation and protect occupants and neighbouring properties. Manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and end-of-life instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers must receive clear safety and emergency instructions, model-specific 55 °C test results, known material limitations, and contact details for reporting incidents. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of newly identified material hazards, provide interim safety instructions, and report corrective actions. Annual thermal incident rates must be calculated per model using a consistent method and installed-unit denominator, with supporting records made available to regulators for validation. A manufacturer must initiate a mandatory recall of any model whose validated thermal incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. It must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, pause further sales pending regulator review, and provide a repair, replacement, or refund plan under regulator oversight. A recall requires corrective action; it is not, by itself, a permanent market ban. Sales may resume only after the regulator determines that corrective actions adequately address the hazard. Incident rates and recall status must be disclosed to affected customers and the public.
