# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H55 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the regulator within 5 business days of learning of it. Incidents involving death, serious injury, fire spread beyond the battery, or an ongoing imminent hazard must be reported within 24 hours. Reports must include the model, incident date and known circumstances, and any response taken; submit material updates promptly. Notify affected customers within 24 hours when an incident indicates an imminent hazard, and otherwise within 5 business days when a model-specific safety risk or corrective action is identified.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C before initial certification and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A failure rate above 5% triggers regulatory notification, corrective action, and retesting; it is not an automatic market exclusion. The manufacturer must notify the regulator within 5 business days, submit a corrective-action plan within 30 days, and retest within 90 days. The regulator may require additional measures based on the results and risk. Provide per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the regulator and joint standards body confidentially, and directly to customers and owners of that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery models placed on the market must provide a secure remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units with this capability, where technically feasible, within 6 years after adoption; where a retrofit is not feasible, they must provide an equally effective emergency isolation method and notify affected customers of its use.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must disclose whether the unit includes built-in suppression and provide customers with applicable fire-response instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive, at sale and after any material safety update, clear instructions on safe operation, warning signs, emergency response, and how to report an incident. Affected customers must be notified promptly of a relevant hazard and corrective action. If a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide data needed to verify the rate. The regulator must promptly investigate and determine appropriate corrective action, including recall where the evidence establishes an unacceptable safety risk or imminent serious hazard. Company-wide rates alone do not trigger a model-specific recall.
