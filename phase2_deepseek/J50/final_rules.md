# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J50 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or suspected thermal runaway within 5 calendar days of learning of it. An incident presenting an imminent risk of injury or spread of fire must be reported within 24 hours. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, known injuries or property damage, and any immediate safety actions. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 calendar days when an incident indicates a material safety risk for their unit or model; urgent risks require notice within 24 hours. Updates must be provided when material facts change.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, a representative sample, and documented failure criteria. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including protocol, sample size, and failure rate, to the regulator at certification and after a material design change. Results must be provided to customers considering or owning the model, but individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires a regulator-reviewed corrective-action plan and follow-up testing; it does not by itself bar certification or sale. Regulators may require immediate restrictions where evidence indicates imminent danger.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off at installation. Manufacturers must provide a safe, secure shut-off capability for installed units that lack it within 6 years of adoption, where technically feasible; if it is not feasible for a unit, the manufacturer must notify its owner and regulator and provide an equivalent safety measure or replacement plan. Shut-off must be used only for a credible safety emergency, with prompt notice to affected customers when practicable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The requirement must be verified through testing under a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must disclose to customers whether a unit meets this requirement.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices about hazards relevant to their model, recommended precautions, and any corrective action, promptly and within 24 hours for an urgent risk or 5 calendar days for other material safety risks. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year triggers prompt regulator verification, investigation, and a manufacturer corrective-action plan with a defined timetable. Following verification, the regulator must require a recall when the evidence indicates an unacceptable safety risk, or when the manufacturer fails to complete adequate corrective action on time. Regulators may order an earlier recall where evidence indicates imminent danger. For any required recall, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and regulators without delay and provide a free remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund.
