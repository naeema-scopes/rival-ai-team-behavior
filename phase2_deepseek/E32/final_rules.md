# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E32 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or material property damage to the relevant safety regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. A preliminary report must identify the product model, incident date and location, known impacts, and immediate safety actions; a fuller investigation update is due within 30 days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a product-specific safety risk, and promptly provide any recommended precautions. Reports must not be delayed while the cause is being determined.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo 55 °C sustained-heat testing using a regulator-approved, statistically adequate sampling and test protocol. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5%. Before sale and upon retesting, manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and test methods to the relevant safety regulator and make the complete per-model results available to prospective and current customers of that model. Manufacturers must retain supporting records. Results above 5% require a corrective-action plan and retesting within a regulator-set period; regulators may require further corrective action or restrict sales where results indicate an unreasonable safety risk. Retesting is required after material design or manufacturing changes. Customers must also receive clear safety instructions and recommended precautions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized access and a means of local shut-off. Manufacturers must provide owners clear instructions and maintain the service needed for emergency use. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a compliant retrofit or replacement within six years, prioritizing units presenting the greatest safety risk. Any shut-off action must be communicated to the customer as soon as practicable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard is adopted. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions on the system’s operation and limitations and maintain it in accordance with the product’s service schedule.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety instructions, known material risks, and guidance on incident reporting and emergency actions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified product-specific safety risks and provide recommended precautions. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, notify the relevant regulator and affected customers, and provide a free repair, replacement, or refund. Regulators may require additional corrective action where warranted.
