# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B28 · variant B · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Halcyon': 70})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers and, where a credible risk extends beyond the affected customer, provide a safety notice to customers and relevant emergency responders within 72 hours. Reports must include known incident circumstances and corrective actions; updates are required as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before initial market placement and after a material design or manufacturing change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% for any model. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model test results, including failure rates, to the relevant regulator and, before purchase, to customers considering that model. A model exceeding the limit must not be newly placed on the market until corrective action and successful retesting.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly manufactured units must support remote emergency shut-off where technically feasible, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. For already-installed units, manufacturers must provide compatible retrofit or replacement options and complete required upgrades within six years of the rule’s effective date. Any unit posing an imminent risk must be addressed sooner through a targeted safety action.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide customers clear instructions on the system’s operation, limitations, and maintenance. Earlier implementation is encouraged but not required.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s compliance status, complete per-model thermal-test results including failure rates, safety warnings, installation and maintenance requirements, and emergency-response and incident-reporting instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and corrective actions, and issue safety notices when a credible risk may affect other customers or the public. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, provide interim protective instructions, and initiate a recall.
