# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H30 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident record and any known contributing factors within 30 days. A manufacturer must notify affected customers within 7 days when an incident indicates a material safety risk or requires a customer action; urgent hazards require notice without delay. Reports must include incidents initially missed or not reported, once identified.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, with a representative sample and repeat testing after material design changes. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5%. A result above 5% requires prompt regulatory oversight and corrective action, but is not by itself an automatic prohibition on sale. Manufacturers must provide per-model failure rates and test methods to the relevant regulator and provide results to customers and prospective customers of that model in clear, accessible form; individual-company per-model rates need not be published generally. If a model exceeds 5%, the manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers within 7 days, submit a corrective-action plan within 30 days, implement interim risk controls, and complete remediation and retesting within 6 months. Continued sale during remediation is permitted only while effective interim controls are in place, the regulator has not ordered suspension, and the manufacturer meets the applicable deadlines. A model still above 5% after the remediation period must not be sold unless the regulator authorizes continued sale based on adequate risk controls. The regulator may require earlier suspension if the model presents an imminent safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway for installed units and complete retrofits within six years of this standard taking effect, where technically feasible. Customers must receive clear instructions on shut-off functionality and any limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard is adopted. Manufacturers must provide customers with information on the suppression system, its limitations, and any required inspection or maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks, corrective actions, and safe-use instructions; urgent hazards require notice without delay. Manufacturers must publish accessible safety notices for each affected model. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must immediately notify the relevant regulator and affected customers, provide interim risk-mitigation instructions, and submit incident and population data for prompt regulator review. A recall is mandatory if the regulator confirms the threshold is exceeded and determines that the risk cannot be adequately controlled through corrective action; an imminent safety risk requires immediate recall or other protective action.
