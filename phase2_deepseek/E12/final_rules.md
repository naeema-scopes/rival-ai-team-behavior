# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E12 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a follow-up report containing known cause and corrective actions within 10 business days. The manufacturer must notify the affected customer within 24 hours where the incident involves an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within 5 business days. Reports must include all known incidents; manufacturers must not omit incidents because the cause is still under investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a consistent regulator-approved protocol. The maximum failure rate is 5%. Manufacturers must submit exact per-model results to the relevant regulator before sale and after material design changes or required retesting. Before purchase, prospective purchasers must be given the model’s exact per-model failure rate and test results, pass/fail status, and a plain-language safety summary explaining material risks and protective steps. Existing owners must be given access to the exact per-model failure rate and test results, pass/fail status, and safety summary. These model-specific results must be provided directly to prospective purchasers and existing owners and need not be disclosed to the general public. A model exceeding the 5% maximum must be reported promptly to the regulator and undergo corrective action and retesting. The regulator shall determine proportionate interim measures, including any sales restriction, based on the results and associated risk. Results must be retained and made available to the regulator on request.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off beginning 12 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within six years after adoption, where technically feasible; any units not retrofitted must be clearly identified to their owners, who must receive emergency shut-off instructions and available alternatives. Shut-off capability must be tested at installation and at regular maintenance intervals. Remote shut-off must be used only for an emergency or safety-related response, with prompt notice to the affected customer when practicable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document compliance and provide the documentation to the relevant regulator before sale.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety instructions, the model’s pass/fail status and a plain-language safety summary, incident-reporting contacts, and information on available emergency shut-off features. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and corrective actions, and provide updated instructions when needed. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers without delay, stop sales, and implement the recall and remedy directed by the regulator.
