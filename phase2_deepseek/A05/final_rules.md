# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A05 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account of known facts and affected models. They must provide an updated investigation report within 10 business days, or explain the delay and provide periodic updates. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and promptly provide relevant precautions and any regulator-required actions. Reports must include incidents regardless of whether injury or property damage occurred.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must submit per-model failure rates and test-method details to the responsible regulator and provide the exact per-model failure rate and test date to customers before sale. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt notice to the regulator, a corrective-action plan, and regulator-directed retesting; it does not by itself require market withdrawal if the regulator determines continued sale is safe subject to controls.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All newly manufactured home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off no later than 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support remote shut-off, or provide an equivalent regulator-approved emergency control, within 72 months after adoption. The control must be accessible to authorized emergency responders and customers and must not create an additional safety hazard.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on all new home battery units manufactured from 36 months after adoption. The requirement must be met by a regulator-approved suppression system appropriate to the unit’s chemistry and installation environment.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers must receive clear installation and safe-use instructions, known material hazards, and instructions for reporting incidents. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected owners of material new safety information and provide periodic status updates during investigations or corrective actions. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected owners, stop sales, and provide an effective remedy under the regulator’s direction.
