# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E11 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, or event requiring emergency-service response to the market regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported to the regulator within 5 business days. Manufacturers must notify the affected customer within 24 hours for a serious event and within 5 business days for other confirmed thermal incidents, including immediate safety steps and how to contact the manufacturer. Reports must include model, date, location, known injuries or damage, and corrective actions; personal information must be protected.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample before sale and after a material design change. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% for any model. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, failure rates, and supporting methodology to the regulator and to customers who buy that model; this requirement does not require public publication of individual-company per-model failure rates. A model exceeding 5% does not meet this standard. The regulator must require corrective action and retesting, and may impose sales restrictions or other measures based on its assessment of the risk and the corrective action needed to protect affected households. The regulator may require additional action based on evidence of serious risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must provide a remote emergency shut-off function that can be initiated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety hazard, by the manufacturer under a documented safety protocol. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and maintain secure, reliable access to the function. Existing installed systems lacking this capability must be retrofitted or otherwise brought into compliance within 6 years of this standard taking effect; where retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must provide a regulator-approved equivalent safety measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery storage units manufactured 36 months or more after this standard is adopted must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and reliability protocol. Manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and operating instructions. The regulator may approve an equivalent protective design where evidence demonstrates comparable fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices for confirmed thermal incidents involving their model, including known risks, recommended immediate steps, and how to obtain assistance. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly when a safety defect or emerging pattern may affect safe operation, and provide updates as information develops. A model must be recalled when its thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. The rate must count all known thermal incidents, including those initially not reported to a regulator, against a consistent installed-unit denominator for the same period. Manufacturers must maintain records supporting the calculation and provide them to the regulator for verification. Verification must not delay customer notice, stopping sales, or recall action once the threshold is met. The manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers, stop sales of the model, and implement a regulator-approved remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund as appropriate. The regulator may require additional action based on evidence of serious risk.
