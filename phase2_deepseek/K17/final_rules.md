# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K17 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account of known circumstances and affected units. They must provide a full investigation report within 30 days, updating it as material facts emerge. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety risk to their model or installed units, and promptly provide updates and recommended protective steps. Reports and notices must not be withheld because an investigation is ongoing.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model test results, including the measured failure rate and test date, confidentially to the responsible regulator and disclose results directly to customers who purchase that model; individual company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7% per model. A model exceeding 5% must submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator and complete retesting within 12 months; regulators may require interim protective measures based on risk. A model exceeding 7% may not be newly marketed until corrective action and retesting demonstrate compliance. Installed units remain subject to customer notification and the recall criteria in S5.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a secure, tested shut-off capability and instructions for its use. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade within six years, prioritizing systems with known safety issues. Shut-off must not replace required emergency response or customer safety procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression designed and tested for the system’s battery chemistry and enclosure. Manufacturers must document performance under the regulator-approved fire-suppression test protocol and provide results to the regulator and customers. The requirement does not apply before that effective date; interim systems must meet applicable fire-safety requirements and provide customers with clear emergency instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of thermal incidents, material safety risks, and corrective actions, using direct notices and accessible public safety information. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers without delay, stop new sales of the model pending regulator review, and provide a remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund, as directed by the regulator. Notices must explain immediate safety steps and how customers can obtain assistance.
