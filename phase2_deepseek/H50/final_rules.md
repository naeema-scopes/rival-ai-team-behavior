# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H50 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every battery-related fire, thermal runaway, or other thermal incident involving injury, property damage, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 5 calendar days of becoming aware of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 30 calendar days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 calendar days when an incident presents a material safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include known circumstances and corrective actions; manufacturers must promptly update incomplete reports as facts are confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample, with testing repeated after material design changes. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results and test methods confidentially to the relevant regulator and provide complete per-model results, including failure-rate data, to customers considering or owning that model. A failure rate above 5% triggers regulator review, including consideration of test evidence, uncertainty, and the model’s real-world safety record; the regulator may require additional testing, corrective action, or other proportionate safeguards. Regulators may publish aggregated or anonymized findings.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required for all newly manufactured home battery units beginning 24 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible. Manufacturers must inform customers of units that cannot be retrofitted, explain available risk-reduction measures, and provide regulators with a remediation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All newly manufactured home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and enclosure, and maintained in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices promptly when a newly identified hazard may affect their unit, including the nature of the hazard, signs to watch for, and any actions to reduce risk. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the relevant regulator and provide the supporting data and proposed corrective-action plan. The regulator must assess the evidence, including incident severity, trend, and effective corrective actions, and may require a recall or other proportionate safeguards. Manufacturers must notify affected customers without delay when a recall is ordered, explain the remedy and timeline, and provide progress updates until the remedy is completed.
