# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D15 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## Incident reporting
Manufacturers must report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, explosion, or injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary account of known facts. Other confirmed overheating events must be reported within 5 business days. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers—within 24 hours for fires, thermal runaway, or injury, and within 5 business days for other confirmed overheating events—and provide any immediate safety steps. A fuller investigation report is due to the regulator within 30 days, with material updates provided as they become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
## Thermal testing
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and an adequately sized representative sample before sale, at least every two years thereafter, and after a material design change. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and test methods to the relevant regulator and provide complete results, including the failure rate, to customers who buy or own that model and to the other participating manufacturer for standards review; individual company per-model failure rates must not be publicly published. A model exceeding the limit must be reported to the regulator and affected customers within 5 business days, with a corrective-action plan and retest schedule. Exceeding the limit requires corrective action and regulator review, but does not automatically require withdrawal of units already in service, suspend new sales, or impose a permanent market ban. Any restriction on continued sale must be based on a regulator’s model-specific assessment of imminent risk, considering available incident data and proposed mitigation. Regulators may require additional measures where necessary to address an imminent risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## Remote emergency shut-off
Newly installed systems must support a secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear instructions for customers and emergency responders. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must offer an upgrade or other technically feasible means of remote shut-off within 6 years. Where retrofitting is infeasible, the manufacturer must document the reason and provide an equivalent risk-reduction measure and customer guidance.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
## Built-in fire suppression
New battery systems must include built-in fire suppression. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of conformity before placing covered new units on the market.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
## Customer disclosure and recalls
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety instructions, emergency contact and shut-off guidance, and the model’s latest 55 °C sustained-heat test results, including its failure rate. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks, incidents, and corrective actions, using the timelines in S1. A manufacturer must initiate a recall of any model whose thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units in a year, notify the relevant regulator and affected customers, and provide safe-use or shutdown instructions while the recall is implemented. A recall under this section is not, by itself, a permanent market ban; any return to sale must follow verified corrective action and applicable regulator review. Regulators may require additional corrective action where evidence indicates an imminent risk.
