# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G41 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with an initial account of known impacts and response actions. A complete report, including investigation findings and any corrective measures, is due within 30 days; material new findings must be provided promptly. Affected customers must be notified within 24 hours when an incident presents an ongoing or serious safety risk, and otherwise within 5 business days. Reports must identify the model and installed-unit population and may not omit incidents because their cause is still under investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and adequate representative sampling. Manufacturers shall provide per-model test results, including failure rates, to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model, and retain results for audit. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must submit a corrective-action plan and be retested within 12 months. Regulators may require interim safeguards based on results; exceeding the threshold alone does not automatically require a market ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed home battery systems shall support remote emergency shut-off beginning 24 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall provide a safe retrofit path for installed units where technically feasible. No installed unit shall be required to receive a retrofit sooner than six years after adoption; manufacturers shall prioritize units with elevated risk and provide customers with interim safety instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document suppression performance and provide installation and maintenance instructions to customers. Regulators may approve an equivalent fire-mitigation design where it provides comparable protection.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, affected models or serial ranges, immediate precautions, and how to obtain support. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers promptly when a serious hazard is identified and provide updates as investigations or remedies progress. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Calculate the rate by model and calendar year, using the model’s average number of installed units during that year as the denominator and the number of distinct thermal incidents involving that model as the numerator. Count incidents regardless of whether investigation is complete; incidents provisionally attributed to a model shall be included and updated if later findings change the attribution. Manufacturers shall document the calculation and supporting records for regulator verification. When the trigger is met, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop distribution of the affected model, and provide a regulator-approved remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund.
