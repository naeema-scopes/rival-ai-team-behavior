# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A36 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 48 hours of learning of it, including incidents initially reported by customers or installers. A preliminary report is due within 24 hours where an incident involves a fatality, serious injury, or fire spreading beyond the battery installation. Manufacturers shall provide affected customers with a safety notice within 5 calendar days of identifying a credible product-related hazard, and promptly update the notice as material facts become known. Reports shall include model, incident date and location, known consequences, and actions taken. Manufacturers shall reconcile regulator reports against service, warranty, and customer-support records at least annually and promptly report any discovered omissions. Regulators may require follow-up and an annual incident summary.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and after a material design or manufacturing change. The maximum allowed failure rate is 7% for any model. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, failure rates, and corrective actions to regulators, and provide results for the relevant model to its customers at no charge and without requiring a request. Results need not be published to the general public. If a model exceeds 7%, the manufacturer shall notify the regulator and submit a corrective-action plan within 30 days. The regulator shall set a retest deadline appropriate to the risk, not exceeding 12 months, and may require interim safeguards or restrict sales based on test results, incident data, or other evidence of unacceptable safety risk. Continued sale after the retest deadline requires regulator review of corrective actions and retest results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market 24 months or more after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear instructions for customers and emergency responders. Manufacturers shall provide a secure software update enabling this function for installed units designed to support it within 6 years of adoption. For installed units that cannot support the function, manufacturers shall notify affected customers and provide safe shutdown instructions and an upgrade or replacement pathway within 6 years.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and reliability protocol. Manufacturers shall document suppression performance and maintenance requirements in customer materials. Units manufactured before that date are not required to be retrofitted solely to meet this provision.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices describing known hazards, affected models, steps to reduce risk, and where to obtain support. Notices shall be sent within 5 calendar days of identifying a credible product-related hazard, or sooner where urgency warrants, and updated as material facts emerge. A manufacturer shall notify regulators within 48 hours when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, investigate the cause, and submit a corrective-action plan. For each model, the annual rate shall be calculated using that model’s thermal incidents and installed-unit count over the same annual period; manufacturers shall submit the incident count, denominator, and calculation to regulators. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 12 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Regulators may require a recall below that threshold where evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk. Recall notices shall explain the remedy, timelines, and safe interim use or shutdown instructions. Company-specific per-model test failure rates need not be publicly disclosed; per-model test results shall still be provided to customers who buy or own that model.
