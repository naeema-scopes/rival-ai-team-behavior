# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E50 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it. Incidents involving death, serious injury, or an active risk to others must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and promptly provide safety instructions. Reports must include known circumstances, affected models and unit counts, and corrective actions; updates are due as material facts emerge. Suspected underreporting must be disclosed and corrected.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the regulator and the joint standards oversight body, and must provide the model’s results to customers before purchase. Results need not be published to the general public. The regulator will assess results against a 5% benchmark and require a documented corrective-action and retesting plan for models above it. Exceeding the benchmark alone does not automatically prohibit sales; the regulator may impose proportionate interim measures or restrict sales where the evidence indicates an unacceptable risk. Regulators may set risk-based transition plans for installed units.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off by 1 January 2028, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must provide compatible retrofit capability where technically feasible for installed units, and complete required retrofits within 6 years of the effective date. Regulators may approve documented exceptions where retrofit is infeasible, provided an equivalent risk-reduction measure and customer notice are in place.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and designed to reduce fire propagation and risks to occupants and neighbours. Manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and safe-use instructions. Regulators may approve a documented, equivalent protective measure where built-in suppression is technically infeasible.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its 55 °C sustained-heat test results and known material risks. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide practical safety instructions; notices must be updated as material facts emerge. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, submit the underlying data and a risk assessment, and implement a regulator-approved corrective-action plan. Regulators must promptly review the evidence and may require a recall, sales restriction, or other protective measures where the risk warrants it; they may require earlier action when warranted by the evidence.
