# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A42 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, evacuation, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a preliminary incident report within 5 business days. Other overheating or thermal-fault incidents shall be reported to the regulator in a quarterly aggregate report, including model, unit population, and known outcomes. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers when an incident indicates a credible safety risk, and provide follow-up information as it becomes available. Reports must not omit incidents because their cause is still under investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a harmonized 55 °C sustained-heat test before market entry and after a material design change. The regulator shall receive per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, and may require retesting or other corrective action. Customers considering or purchasing a model shall receive that model’s per-model test results, including its failure rate; these results need not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires a documented corrective-action plan and retesting within 12 months. Continued sale and further regulatory action shall be determined by the regulator based on test results, incident evidence, and adequacy of corrective action; exceeding the threshold alone does not automatically require market withdrawal.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market shall support a secure remote emergency shut-off function, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall provide a retrofit plan for installed units that do not support the function, prioritizing units with elevated safety risk and completing feasible retrofits within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Where retrofit is technically infeasible, the manufacturer shall provide an equivalent safety measure and notify affected customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard is adopted. The suppression system shall be appropriate to the battery chemistry and enclosure, and shall be designed and tested to limit fire propagation without creating undue risks to occupants or responders. Manufacturers shall provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose any known limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices about incidents or hazards relevant to their model, including recommended precautions and any actions customers should take. Notices shall be sent promptly when a credible safety risk is identified, and updated as material information becomes available. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year shall trigger prompt regulator review, including assessment of incident severity, trend, exposure, and corrective measures. The regulator shall require a recall when the evidence indicates an unreasonable safety risk; manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers and provide remedy instructions when a recall is ordered.
