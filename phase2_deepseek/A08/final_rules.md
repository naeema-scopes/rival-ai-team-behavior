# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A08 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents without injury or property damage. They shall provide an initial customer notice to affected owners within 72 hours when the incident indicates a potential safety risk, and promptly update regulators and customers as material facts emerge. Reports shall include available incident and product-identification details; a manufacturer may not omit an incident solely because its cause is not yet confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample before sale and after material design changes. Manufacturers shall submit the protocol, sample size, and per-model results to the relevant regulator; those individual-company results shall not be published to the general public. At purchase, customers shall receive the model’s actual test result. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt notice to the regulator and affected customers, a corrective-action plan, and retesting on a regulator-approved schedule. A model may remain on sale while following an approved corrective-action plan unless the regulator determines that an immediate restriction is needed to address an imminent safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide a secure retrofit pathway for installed systems and complete retrofits within six years of this standard taking effect, where technically feasible. Shut-off capability shall not replace required local safety protections or emergency response procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system shall be designed and tested for the battery’s foreseeable thermal-fire hazards and maintained in accordance with manufacturer instructions. Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear information on its operation, limitations, and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase, customers shall receive clear safety information for their model, including applicable warnings, emergency actions, maintenance requirements, and the model’s 55 °C sustained-heat test result. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide updated instructions as facts emerge. When a model’s thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, based on a documented, consistently applied method for identifying confirmed incidents and installed units, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and provide the underlying data for review. The manufacturer shall submit and implement a corrective-action plan on a regulator-approved schedule. A recall is required when the regulator determines that a material safety risk cannot be adequately addressed through other corrective action. Any recall shall include prompt notice to affected customers, an explanation of the hazard, and an effective remedy, including repair, replacement, or refund.
