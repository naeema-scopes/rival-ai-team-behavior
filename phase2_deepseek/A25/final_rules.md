# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A25 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with an initial account and known unit/model details. Other thermal incidents must be reported within 30 days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly—within 24 hours for an urgent risk requiring immediate action, and within 5 business days for other reportable incidents—using direct contact where reasonably available. Corrected or additional information must be supplied as it becomes available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Exceeding this limit requires prompt regulator notification, investigation, a documented corrective-action plan, and retesting on a defined schedule. Continued sale pending corrective action and retesting is permitted only with regulator-approved safeguards and a documented corrective plan. Full per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, must be submitted confidentially to regulators and provided to customers purchasing that model. Results must not be published to the general public as company-specific per-model failure rates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models must include a remotely accessible emergency shut-off function before market entry. For units already installed when this standard is adopted, manufacturers must provide a compatible shut-off capability or an equivalent effective emergency isolation measure within 72 months, where technically feasible. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for authorized use and maintain secure access and availability; shut-off must not replace emergency services or required local safety protections. Any infeasibility or equivalent measure must be documented and reported to the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the battery system. This requirement applies to units manufactured 36 months after adoption of this standard or later. Manufacturers must test and document suppression performance under relevant thermal runaway conditions and provide the results to regulators. Suppression must not substitute for other required safety measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must give purchasers model-specific safety notices before sale and promptly when material new information emerges. Notices must explain any action customers should take and provide a contact for questions. Per-model test results and material incident or corrective-action information must be provided to affected customers and regulators, but must not be published to the general public as company-specific per-model failure rates. The 8-incident-per-100,000 threshold must be calculated separately for each model, using that model’s annual thermal incidents and installed-unit population. When any model exceeds that threshold in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and initiate a recall, with regulator oversight of recall implementation and interim protections. Manufacturers must also initiate a recall when otherwise necessary to address an imminent or serious safety risk.
