# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E46 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, property damage, or a unit safety shutdown to the regulator within 5 business days after learning of it. Any fatality, serious injury, or continuing imminent hazard must be reported to the regulator within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers promptly when an incident indicates a material risk to their units, and provide interim safe-use or isolation instructions where appropriate. Reports must include known facts and be updated as investigations develop.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including failure rates, to the regulator and disclose those results to current and prospective customers of that model in a clear, accessible format. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification and a corrective-action plan within 60 days; it does not by itself prohibit sale. The regulator may require additional testing, corrective measures, or restrict sales if evidence establishes an imminent serious safety risk. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and available corrective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months after this standard takes effect must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible alternative. For units already installed on that date, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent safe remedy within 6 years where technically feasible, and give owners clear instructions for emergency isolation in the interim. Manufacturers must report retrofit progress to the regulator annually.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and reliability protocol. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and disclose the system’s capabilities and limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear installation, safe-use, emergency-response, and incident-reporting instructions at sale, and notify registered owners promptly of material newly identified risks and corrective actions. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed unit-years over a rolling 12-month period, or when the regulator determines a defect presents an imminent serious safety risk. Rates must be calculated by model using incidents attributed to that model and the corresponding installed-unit exposure during the period; manufacturers must document the method and submit incident counts, exposure data, and calculations to the regulator for review. Manufacturers must notify the regulator and affected customers without delay, explain the risk and remedy, and provide status updates until the remedy is complete. Per-model test failure rates need not be disclosed publicly, but must be disclosed to current and prospective customers of the model as required by S2.
