# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D29 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, injury, evacuation, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days of learning of a serious incident involving their model, and promptly provide safety instructions. Reports must include the model, incident date, known consequences, and any immediate precautions; submit updates as facts are confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-accepted protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must provide per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator and retain them for audit. Customers must receive the per-model failure rate and compliance status at purchase and after any material safety update. Per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public; this does not limit disclosure to regulators or customers as provided in this section. A model with a failure rate above 5% must promptly submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan, including interim risk controls and a retest schedule. The regulator may restrict or suspend sales if the evidence indicates an unacceptable risk or the manufacturer fails to meet the plan; exceeding 5% alone does not automatically require a sales ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with clear customer instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For systems already installed, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent upgrade plan and complete implementation within 6 years of this standard taking effect. Where remote shut-off is not yet available, manufacturers must provide customers with interim emergency guidance.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New battery systems manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the battery’s chemistry and enclosure, and maintained according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions on its operation and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At purchase and after any material safety update, customers must receive the model’s thermal-test compliance status and per-model 55 °C test failure rate, relevant safety instructions, and a clear way to report incidents. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and provide protective steps. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide the underlying model-specific data and a corrective-action plan. The regulator must promptly investigate and determine appropriate protective measures, including recall where warranted by verified incident data, severity, trends, exposure, and corrective actions. Regulators retain authority to require a recall below this rate where evidence warrants it.
