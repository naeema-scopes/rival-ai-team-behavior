# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K46 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents involving injury, property damage, or a safety-related shutdown. A preliminary report is due within 24 hours even if the cause is not yet known; manufacturers must provide material updates and a final investigation report promptly as information becomes available. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours of learning of an incident involving their unit, and promptly notify other customers of the same model when the incident indicates a model-level safety risk. Reports must include known circumstances, injuries or damage, actions taken, and the model and unit identifiers.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a statistically representative sample. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results to the relevant regulator and provide results to customers on request; individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be published publicly. A result above 5% requires prompt regulator notification and a corrective-action and retesting plan, but is not by itself grounds for an automatic sales ban. Any restriction should follow a regulator’s model-specific safety assessment, considering incident data and corrective actions, with an opportunity to retest.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a means for the customer to verify shut-off status. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support this function within 6 years after adoption of this standard. Shut-off capability must not replace required physical safety protections or incident reporting.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression designed to control or contain a battery fire without creating an additional hazard. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document suppression-system design, maintenance needs, and performance in the applicable safety information for the unit.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices at purchase and promptly notify affected customers of newly identified hazards, safe-use instructions, and corrective actions; urgent notices must be sent without delay and through direct channels where available. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year triggers prompt regulator notification, a public customer notice, and an expedited model-specific safety assessment. The exceedance alone does not automatically require a recall: the regulator shall determine whether a recall or other corrective action is necessary based on incident severity, exposure, test results, and the effectiveness of alternatives. A recall is mandatory when the regulator determines that a model presents an unacceptable safety risk that cannot be adequately mitigated by less restrictive action.
