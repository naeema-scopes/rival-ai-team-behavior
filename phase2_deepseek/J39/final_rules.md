# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J39 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with an initial account of known facts. A fuller report, including investigation findings and any known similar incidents, is due within 30 days and must be updated as material facts emerge. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their unit or requires customer action; notifications must state the hazard, recommended action, and how to obtain assistance.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. A failure rate of 5% is a corrective-action benchmark, not an automatic bar to sale. A model exceeding 5% must promptly undergo a documented risk assessment and corrective-action plan, submitted to the regulator; the regulator may require additional testing or measures proportionate to the risk. Manufacturers must provide each model’s results, including test date, sample size, and failure rate, to the relevant regulator and to customers considering or owning that model, in clear, accessible form. Customers must also receive material safety risks and recommended precautions. Updated results and material risks or actions must be communicated to existing customers promptly. These results may be reported publicly in aggregate, but must not be withheld from customers buying or using the model.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must support secure remote emergency shut-off within 12 months of adoption. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for its use and ensure that remote shut-off does not prevent emergency services from taking appropriate action. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance within 6 years of adoption where technically feasible; otherwise, the manufacturer must provide the regulator with a risk-mitigation plan and notify affected customers of any limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance and reliability protocol. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide evidence of compliance to the regulator before sale; customers must receive clear information about the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear written safety information covering thermal hazards, installation and operating limits, warning signs, emergency actions, and support contact details. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material risks and provide updated instructions. A model-specific rolling 12-month rate exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units must be promptly reported to the regulator for validation. Once validated, exceeding this threshold triggers a mandatory recall. The regulator may also require a recall below the threshold where a defect presents an imminent risk of serious injury or fire. A recall requires prompt customer protection through safe shutdown, repair, or replacement as directed by the regulator; it does not by itself require permanent withdrawal from sale. Return to sale may be permitted after regulator-verified corrective action. Following a recall decision, the manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours, with instructions for safe shutdown, repair, or replacement.
