# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H44 · variant H · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 81})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, or emergency intervention to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. Affected customers must be notified within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or requires inspection, service, or precautions. Reports must include known facts and be updated as investigations progress; uncertainty does not delay initial reporting.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry, at least every three years, and after a material design change. A model passes if no more than 10% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must provide test methods, sample sizes, and full per-model results, including failure rates, to the relevant regulator. Customers who buy a model receive its pass/fail status, but not its per-model failure rate. Regulators may require corrective action or retesting where results indicate a safety concern.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on all newly manufactured units no later than 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a plan for enabling remote shut-off on installed units where technically feasible, and complete upgrades or provide an equally effective safety mitigation within 72 months after adoption. Customers must be informed of the availability and operation of the shut-off and any limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on new units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption. Manufacturers must disclose to customers whether a unit includes built-in suppression and provide clear instructions for any alternative fire-safety measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers when a model-specific safety risk is identified or when inspection, service, or precautions are recommended. Notices must explain the known risk, steps customers should take, and how to obtain assistance; they must be updated as material facts emerge. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit the relevant incident data. This threshold triggers expedited regulator review and a documented corrective-action assessment; the regulator will determine whether a recall is necessary based on the risk and evidence.
