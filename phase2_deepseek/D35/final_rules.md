# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D35 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a written incident report within 10 business days. They shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when the incident presents a potential safety risk, and promptly update them as material facts emerge. Reports must include known product/model and incident details; manufacturers shall maintain records and report previously unreported incidents upon discovery.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C using a documented, consistent protocol. A failure rate above 5% is an action threshold requiring the manufacturer to submit a corrective-action plan and retest on a regulator-approved schedule; it is not, by itself, an automatic sales ban. Manufacturers shall provide exact per-model results confidentially to regulators and disclose the exact results for each model to its owners and prospective customers. Individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be published or otherwise disclosed to the general public. Regulators may restrict sales or require other protective measures where results or other evidence indicate an unacceptable safety risk. Manufacturers shall promptly provide updates to owners and prospective customers when results or related material safety findings change.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers shall retrofit installed units that lack this capability, where technically feasible, within six years of adoption of this standard. They shall notify owners of the feature, its limitations, and safe activation procedures. Shut-off systems must be secure against unauthorized access and shall not replace required physical safety protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on all new units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall disclose the suppression system and its limitations to customers. The requirement does not replace applicable testing, incident reporting, or other fire-safety protections.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall promptly notify owners of any incident or safety finding that could affect safe use, including recommended precautions and material updates. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer shall notify the regulator within 24 hours and provide supporting records and calculations. Exceeding this threshold triggers a mandatory recall; the manufacturer shall promptly notify affected customers, provide recall instructions and an effective remedy, and comply with any regulator-directed sales suspension. Regulators may require a recall below this threshold where evidence indicates an unacceptable safety risk.
