# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B19 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving fire, injury, evacuation, emergency-service response, or material property damage within 24 hours of learning of it, and submit a complete incident report within 10 calendar days. Other overheating incidents requiring service or safety-related shutdown shall be reported within 30 days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 48 hours of learning of an incident that presents a credible ongoing safety risk, and promptly provide any regulator-required safety instructions. Reports shall identify the model and include incident counts and available circumstances.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved sampling protocol. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results, including sample size and failure rate, to the relevant regulator. The manufacturer shall also provide those results to customers who buy that model, in a clear and accessible format. A result above 5% shall require a corrective-action plan and follow-up testing within a regulator-set period; it shall not by itself prohibit sale. Regulators may publish aggregate results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models shall include a remote emergency shut-off capability beginning 24 months after adoption of this standard. Existing installed units shall be upgraded where technically feasible within 72 months after adoption; manufacturers shall inform regulators of the number and share of units upgraded and any technical barriers. The shut-off system shall be secure, available to authorized emergency responders and operators, and shall not prevent safe local operation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The requirement applies to units manufactured on or after that effective date; manufacturers shall provide regulators with documentation demonstrating compliance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide each purchaser, at sale, clear safety instructions, including emergency shut-off guidance where applicable. Model-specific 55 °C test results shall be provided directly to purchasers of that model in a clear, accessible format, but shall not be posted in a publicly accessible location; regulators may publish aggregate results. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 48 hours of learning of a credible ongoing safety risk, and promptly issue updated instructions or notices required by a regulator. A model-specific thermal incident rate exceeding 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, calculated from validated incident and exposure data, shall trigger prompt regulator review and a mandatory customer-facing recall or other regulator-approved safety remedy. The manufacturer shall notify affected customers and submit its remedy plan within 10 calendar days of confirmation; the regulator may require faster action where risk warrants. A rate exceedance alone does not require an automatic sales prohibition; regulators may order one where necessary to protect public safety.
