# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J37 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents initially suspected to involve a product defect. They must provide a follow-up report with known cause and corrective actions within 30 days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to them, and promptly provide safety instructions. Reports must distinguish confirmed incidents from suspected ones; aggregation must not delay reporting.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7% for any model. A model exceeding that limit must be reported to the regulator with its test results and a corrective-action plan; the regulator may impose proportionate restrictions pending demonstrated compliance. A result above 5% but at or below 7% requires a corrective-action plan and follow-up testing, but does not by itself require a market ban. Per-model results must be provided confidentially to the relevant regulator. The regulator may require tailored customer safety communications where results indicate a material risk. Individual-company per-model results must not be published to the general public. Results may be shared confidentially with the other standard-setting company for evidence review.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units with this capability within six years of the standard’s effective date, where technically feasible, and must inform customers of units awaiting retrofit and available interim safety steps.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after the standard’s adoption must include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance standard. Units manufactured before that date are not subject to this requirement. Manufacturers must disclose whether each unit includes built-in suppression and provide customers with applicable safe-use and emergency instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, before purchase and in durable form, model-specific safety information including thermal-test results, known material hazards, safe installation and operating instructions, emergency steps, and how to report an incident. They must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material safety risks and provide updated instructions and corrective-action information. When any model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, provide supporting data, and notify affected customers of the risk and interim safety steps. Exceeding this threshold requires a recall of that model. A recall must provide an effective remedy to affected customers, which may include repair, replacement, or refund; it does not by itself require a permanent market ban if the safety issue can be effectively remedied. The regulator must be notified of the recall and may require earlier protective action where evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
