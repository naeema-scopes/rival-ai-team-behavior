# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K04 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete investigation and final incident report submitted within 30 days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their model or product batch, and provide interim protective instructions where appropriate. Reports must include model, serial number, installation date and location where known, incident circumstances, injuries or damage, and corrective actions. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and reconcile them against service and customer-support records at least annually.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a validated sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a representative sample and a documented, reproducible protocol. Manufacturers must submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, failure rates, and corrective actions confidentially to the relevant regulator. A model with a failure rate above 5% must submit a corrective-action plan and implement interim safety measures as directed by the regulator; the regulator may require retesting and further mitigation based on the results. Manufacturers must disclose the model’s test method, sample size, and failure rate to customers and prospective purchasers of that model, and promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks with protective instructions and corrective actions. Disclosure of a model’s results to its customers does not require public release of individual-company results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must support remote emergency shut-off at the time of sale. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support remote shut-off within six years after adoption, prioritizing units presenting higher assessed risk. Shut-off controls must be secure, reliable, and accompanied by clear instructions; remote shut-off must not prevent users or emergency responders from safely isolating a unit locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must validate suppression effectiveness for the unit design and provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions. The requirement does not replace applicable testing, incident reporting, or other safety protections.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of safety risks and provide protective instructions and corrective actions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, submit a model-specific investigation and corrective-action plan, and notify affected customers of the risk and protective steps. The regulator must promptly assess the evidence and may require a mandatory recall where the risk warrants it; a recall is mandatory when the regulator determines that continued use presents an imminent serious safety risk. Annual model-level incident rates, customer notices, and corrective actions must be disclosed to customers.
