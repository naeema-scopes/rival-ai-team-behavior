# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J04 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. Affected customers must be notified within 48 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include known unit/model and incident details, actions taken, and any uncertainty; manufacturers must promptly supplement reports as facts become available. Incidents must not be excluded because their cause is still under investigation.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually, using a regulator-approved protocol and a representative sample. Manufacturers must promptly provide complete per-model results, sample sizes, test dates, and protocol details to the relevant regulator and to customers considering or purchasing that model. Customer disclosures must clearly identify the model’s failure rate and explain its safety significance. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, investigation, and a corrective-action plan with milestones and retesting. Crossing this threshold alone does not prohibit sales or require a recall; regulators may impose proportionate protective measures, including sales restrictions, where evidence shows an unmitigated safety risk. Material changes to the model require retesting.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed home battery systems must provide a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be initiated by the manufacturer or emergency responders when a credible imminent fire or thermal-runaway risk exists. The capability must safely isolate the battery without preventing locally available emergency response. Manufacturers must retrofit existing units to meet this requirement within 6 years of adoption of this standard, prioritizing units with known safety risks, and provide customers clear instructions for local shut-off if remote communication is unavailable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to limit propagation of a thermal incident, using a regulator-approved test protocol. Manufacturers must provide the regulator evidence of validation and make a clear summary available to customers. The requirement does not replace thermal testing, incident reporting, or other protective measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices describing material risks, affected models or units, recommended actions, and available remedies; urgent notices must be sent promptly and no later than 48 hours after determining that customer action is needed. A recall is mandatory when a model has more than 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, based on verified incidents and a documented installed-unit denominator. Manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, pause sale and distribution while the regulator reviews the risk, and provide a regulator-approved recall and remedy plan. The regulator may authorize sales to resume after corrective actions are verified and the model meets applicable safety requirements; crossing the trigger alone does not permanently prohibit the model. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action below this threshold when warranted by the risk. Manufacturers must publish status updates until the recall is complete.
