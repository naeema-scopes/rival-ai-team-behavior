# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D18 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written incident report within 10 calendar days. Suspected serious or fatal incidents must be reported immediately and no later than 4 hours. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 5 calendar days when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and update them promptly as findings develop. Reports shall include known product, event, and corrective-action details; supplemental information may follow as it becomes available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-accepted protocol and representative samples. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. A model exceeding that limit requires prompt regulator notification, a time-bound corrective-action plan, and retesting. The regulator shall determine whether interim sales restrictions are necessary based on the risk and adequacy of corrective measures; further sales shall be restricted where the regulator finds an imminent or unresolved serious risk. Per-model results, including failure rates and test protocols, shall be provided to regulators and to customers who buy that model in a clear safety summary. Results shall not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market from 24 months after adoption shall support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers shall provide compatible retrofit capability or an equivalent safe mitigation within 6 years, prioritizing units with known safety concerns. Customers shall receive advance notice and installation instructions; any shut-off must not compromise essential emergency functions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market from 36 months after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression meeting applicable safety requirements. Manufacturers shall provide customers with instructions on system operation, inspection, and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers clear safety notices about material risks, relevant test results for their model, recommended precautions, and corrective actions, promptly when a material safety risk is identified and whenever findings materially change. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and cooperate in a regulator-supervised recall assessment. The regulator shall require a recall when the threshold is exceeded, unless it determines that a time-bound corrective action provides an equally protective remedy; an equivalent serious risk independently warrants a recall. A recall means prompt customer notification and an effective repair, replacement, refund, or safe-use/shutdown remedy, and does not by itself require a permanent sales ban. Regulators shall be notified of the trigger, assessment, and recall or corrective actions.
