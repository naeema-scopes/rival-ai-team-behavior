# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K12 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the regulator any fire, thermal runaway, or overheating incident involving injury, property damage, evacuation, emergency-service response, or suspected product defect within 24 hours of learning of it. Other thermal incidents shall be reported within 10 business days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and provide updates as the investigation proceeds. Reports shall include known circumstances, model and serial number, and corrective actions; manufacturers shall promptly supplement incomplete reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after any material design change, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers shall submit full per-model results, methods, and sample sizes confidentially to the regulator. Results shall be made available only to customers considering or owning that model and shall not be published or otherwise disclosed to the general public in a way that identifies the model or manufacturer. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide relevant safety instructions. A model exceeding the limit shall submit a corrective-action plan and undergo retesting on a regulator-set timetable; exceeding the limit alone shall not automatically prohibit sales, though the regulator may restrict new sales if the manufacturer fails to meet the plan.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models shall support remote emergency shut-off no later than 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall retrofit already-installed units to support remote emergency shut-off within six years after adoption, where technically feasible. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers of any limitations and provide safe alternative emergency procedures where retrofit is not feasible.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall document compliance for each model before placing compliant units on the market.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive relevant safety instructions, emergency procedures, and any current safety notices. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide clear instructions for safe use, service, or cessation of use. A manufacturer shall initiate a recall when a model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units. The rate shall be calculated using verified incident and installed-unit data and reported to the regulator; recall notices shall be sent promptly to affected customers and include return, repair, or replacement instructions.
