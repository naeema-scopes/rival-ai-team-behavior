# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E19 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every fire, thermal runaway, or other incident involving uncontrolled overheating to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents with no injury or property damage. They must provide a complete investigation and corrective-action update within 30 days, and promptly supplement it if material facts change. The manufacturer must notify affected owners within 72 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk requiring customer action; notices must explain the risk and immediate precautions. Reports must not omit incidents because they were not independently verified.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, and after a material design or manufacturing change. Testing must use a regulator-approved representative sample and documented protocol. Manufacturers must submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results to regulators and provide the model’s most recent results directly to customers before purchase. These per-model results are not required to be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt investigation, a corrective-action plan, and follow-up testing; it does not by itself prohibit sale. Regulators may require proportionate protective measures, including restrictions or recall, where the evidence warrants them. Applicable customer-protection and recall requirements remain in force.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly installed systems must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed compatible systems, or provide an equivalent safety measure where retrofit is infeasible, within six years of this standard taking effect. A retrofit must not impair essential safety functions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after this standard is adopted must include built-in fire suppression appropriate to the battery chemistry and system design, validated through documented testing. The manufacturer must provide owners with clear instructions on system operation and limitations. The requirement does not replace incident reporting, thermal testing, or other applicable safety measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s most recent 55 °C sustained-heat test result, a plain-language description of material thermal hazards and required precautions, and instructions for reporting incidents. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified material safety risks and provide clear instructions for protective action, repair, or replacement. A recall is mandatory when a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. Manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and submit a corrective-action plan. Regulators shall verify the rate and specify proportionate customer-protective recall measures, which may include repair or replacement; a recall does not by itself impose an automatic market ban. Regulators may require a recall or other protective action below this threshold where the evidence warrants it.
