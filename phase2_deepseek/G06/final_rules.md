# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G06 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account and known consequences. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 30 days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within 30 days. Reports must include model, incident date, known circumstances, and corrective actions; regulators may request updates.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample. Manufacturers must submit full per-model results, including numerical failure rates, confidentially to regulators. A failure rate above 5% triggers prompt regulator notification, a regulator-approved corrective-action plan, and retesting; it does not by itself require market exclusion. Regulators may impose sales restrictions where warranted by the evidence. Customers who purchase or are considering purchasing a model must receive that model’s numerical test result directly and confidentially, along with relevant safety status, precautions, and corrective actions. Results must not be publicly posted or otherwise made generally available. Regulators may require additional testing after a material design change or incident trend.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All newly installed home battery systems must support a remote emergency shut-off function no later than 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units lacking this function so that they support remote emergency shut-off within 72 months after adoption. The function must be available to authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide customers with clear instructions for using the function and explain when shut-off is appropriate.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All newly manufactured home battery systems must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and designed to reduce the risk or consequences of thermal runaway and fire. Manufacturers must provide regulators with evidence of the system’s effectiveness and disclose its operation and limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks, incidents, and corrective actions, and provide model-specific safety status, precautions, and corrective actions to current and prospective customers. Full numerical per-model thermal-test results must be provided confidentially to regulators and directly and confidentially to customers who purchase or are considering purchasing the relevant model; results must not be publicly posted or otherwise made generally available. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers without delay, provide safe-use or shutdown instructions as appropriate, and report recall progress. Regulators may require a recall at a lower rate when the evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk.
