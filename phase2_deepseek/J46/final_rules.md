# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J46 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 30 days. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and promptly report any previously unreported incident upon discovery, explaining the delay and corrective steps. They must notify affected customers within 5 days when an incident indicates a material safety risk, and promptly provide safety instructions. Reports to regulators and affected customers are not general-public disclosure.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a representative sample and a common test protocol. The maximum permitted failure rate is 7% per model. Manufacturers must provide per-model results, sample sizes, and corrective-action plans to regulators and make the results available to customers of that model in clear, accessible form. Results need not be released to the general public. A model exceeding the limit must be investigated and retested; regulators may require proportionate corrective action and a time-limited compliance plan only where supported by evidence and protective of safety. Exceeding the limit does not by itself mandate a market-wide ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to enable remote emergency shut-off within 6 years of this standard taking effect, prioritizing units with elevated risk. Shut-off must be secure, reliable, and accompanied by customer instructions; emergency shut-off must not prevent safe local operation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 48 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system must be designed and tested to reduce the risk of fire propagation without creating additional hazards, and manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and safe-operation instructions. Units installed before the effective date are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices and instructions promptly when new information indicates a material risk, and notify affected customers of corrective actions. Thermal-incident rates must be calculated and reported by model using verified incidents and installed-unit exposure over the same 12-month period; regulators may audit the underlying records. If a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and submit the model for review. The regulator must assess the evidence and require proportionate protective action, which may include recall, safe shutdown, repair, replacement, or refund, with prompt notice and clear instructions to affected customers. A time-limited corrective-action plan may be permitted instead of recall only where the regulator determines, based on verified evidence, that continued use does not present an unacceptable risk and the plan will promptly reduce the risk below the threshold.
