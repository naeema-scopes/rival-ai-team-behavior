# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H18 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents involving injury, property damage, or suspected product malfunction. The manufacturer must provide an initial incident report even when the cause is not yet established, and update it as material facts emerge. Within 72 hours, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and provide immediate safety instructions when continued use may pose a risk. Reports must identify the affected model and batch to regulators; public notices must not disclose individual customers’ personal information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-recognized representative protocol and sample. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, including sample size, failure count, test date, and protocol, to the relevant regulator and provide those results to customers of that model. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must be retested and the manufacturer must submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator; customers of that model must be promptly notified of the results and relevant safety precautions. Material model changes require retesting before sale.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support a secure remote emergency shut-off that can place the battery in a safe state without creating additional hazards. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for using it and maintain an emergency response process. Existing installed units must be upgraded to support remote shut-off, or an equivalent safe emergency-isolation capability, within six years after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must notify owners of the capability, limitations, and any required upgrade.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document that suppression systems are tested for compatibility with the battery and do not create additional hazards. Units manufactured before the effective date are not subject to this requirement.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices promptly when a material model-specific safety risk is identified, including relevant test findings, affected units, and recommended precautions. Manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers of corrective actions and recall decisions. If a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the regulator must promptly review the evidence and require appropriate corrective action; a recall is required when the regulator determines the risk warrants removal from service, considering incident severity, validated rates, and available mitigations. Manufacturers must promptly notify customers if a recall is ordered and provide instructions and remedies.
