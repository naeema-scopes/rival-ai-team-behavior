# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H41 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account and known impacts. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 24 hours when an incident presents an immediate safety risk, and otherwise within 5 business days of confirming a relevant product hazard. Reports must include model, incident date, known circumstances, and follow-up updates as facts become available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a representative sample and a documented common protocol. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% for any model. Manufacturers must provide the regulator and customers who buy the model with its numerical failure rate, test method, and sample size; customers must also receive plain-language safety guidance and relevant protective actions. For a model above 5%, the manufacturer must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and complete corrective action and compliant retesting within 24 months. During that period, the regulator may require interim protections or restrict sales where needed to address a serious hazard. After 24 months, a model still above 5% may not be newly sold until compliant retesting demonstrates a rate of 5% or less. Models at or below 5% must be retested after a material design change or adverse safety trend.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must include a remotely accessible emergency shut-off capability no later than 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide a safe retrofit or equivalent shut-off capability for installed units within 6 years after adoption, prioritizing units with known safety concerns. Shut-off systems must fail safely, preserve local emergency access, and include customer instructions on operation and limitations.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and enclosure, independently validated, and designed to limit fire spread and protect occupants and nearby properties. Manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and end-of-life instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, affected models or serial ranges, protective actions, and support contacts. Notices presenting an immediate safety risk must be sent within 24 hours of confirmation; other material safety notices must be sent within 5 business days. Manufacturers must promptly investigate concerning thermal-incident trends and report supporting incident-rate data and investigation findings to the regulator. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide supporting data and investigation findings. The regulator must promptly assess the evidence and may require additional protective measures or a recall where it establishes an unacceptable serious product hazard. Recall notices must promptly explain the remedy and how customers can obtain it.
