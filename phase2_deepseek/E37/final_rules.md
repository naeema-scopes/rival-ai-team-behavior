# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E37 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, property damage, emergency-service response, or a safety-related shutdown to the regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. Affected customers must be notified within 24 hours for urgent hazards requiring immediate action, and within 5 business days for other reportable incidents, with known risks and recommended actions. Submit periodic aggregate incident counts and promptly correct incomplete reports.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model results and sample sizes to the regulator for oversight and provide the results, including the failure rate and sample size, to customers purchasing that model in a clear, accessible format. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must submit a corrective-action plan and complete retesting within 12 months. Regulators may require interim safeguards or other proportionate measures based on risk. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers when test findings identify a material hazard or action they should take, describing the risk and required actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off no later than 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for its use and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For installed units, manufacturers must assess compatibility and prioritize retrofit or other effective risk mitigation for units presenting elevated risk; provide customers with the assessment and any available upgrade options within 24 months.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document compliance and provide evidence to the regulator. Until the requirement takes effect, manufacturers should assess and mitigate fire risks using appropriate design and installation safeguards.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices describing material risks, affected models or units, recommended actions, and available remedies. Notify customers promptly when a newly identified hazard may require action, and issue updated notices as material information changes. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, submit the model’s incident data and a risk-mitigation plan, and initiate a recall. Pending completion of the recall, the regulator may require interim protective measures. Per-model incident data submitted for oversight must be treated as confidential except as necessary to inform affected customers of risks and required actions.
