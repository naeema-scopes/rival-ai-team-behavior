# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J36 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a follow-up report containing known causes and corrective actions within 10 business days. They shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a material risk to their model or requires customer action. Reports shall include the model, incident circumstances, and known outcomes; manufacturers shall update regulators and affected customers as material facts emerge.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. Manufacturers shall submit per-model numerical results to the relevant regulator and provide those results to customers of that model in clear, accessible form, including with purchase information and after each annual test. A failure rate above 5% shall trigger prompt regulator notification and review, including assessment of field incident data and a corrective-action plan where warranted; it shall not automatically prohibit sale. Regulators may require additional testing, corrective action, or other proportionate measures based on the combined evidence. Regulators may publish appropriately aggregated findings, but this does not limit customers’ access to results for the model they own or are considering purchasing.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall include remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers shall provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete retrofits within six years of this standard taking effect. The shut-off function shall be tested at installation and annually, with clear instructions for customers and emergency responders.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers shall provide customers clear instructions on the system’s operation, limitations, and maintenance. Regulators may approve an equivalent protective measure where evidence shows it provides at least equivalent risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive, at purchase and after any material safety update, clear notices describing relevant hazards, safe operation, and how to report an incident. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers and regulators of a serious safety defect and provide corrective instructions. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; regulators may also require a recall where other evidence indicates an unreasonable risk.
