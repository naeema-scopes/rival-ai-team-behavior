# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G53 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any battery-related fire, thermal runaway, or overheating event requiring emergency-service response, causing injury or property damage, or requiring evacuation, within 24 hours of learning of it. Other confirmed thermal incidents shall be reported within 5 business days. Reports shall include known circumstances, affected model and serial/batch information, and actions taken; updates shall be provided as material facts emerge. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 5 business days of confirming a safety risk that could require a change in use or service, and immediately where continued use presents an imminent serious risk. Regulators may require prompt customer notice where needed to protect the public.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55°C before initial market placement and after a material design or manufacturing change, using a statistically adequate sample and a published test protocol. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results, including failure rates and sample sizes, and corrective-action plans to the regulator confidentially. Customers who own or are considering a model shall receive its per-model test results, including failure rates and sample sizes, along with safety-relevant findings, risk information, and any required corrective actions. A result above the 5% benchmark triggers regulator review and a corrective-action plan with retesting on a regulator-approved schedule. The benchmark is a safety trigger for review and remediation, not by itself an automatic market ban; regulators may impose proportionate measures based on evidence and risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units shall support a remotely initiated emergency shut-off no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. For units already installed, manufacturers shall provide remote shut-off capability through retrofit or replacement within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible. Manufacturers shall submit implementation plans and progress reports to regulators. Regulators may require earlier action for a specific model where evidence indicates an imminent serious risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers shall disclose compliance status and any interim risk-reduction measures to regulators and customers. The transition period does not limit regulators’ authority to require earlier measures for a specific model where evidence indicates an imminent serious risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers shall receive clear safety notices promptly when a manufacturer or regulator identifies a safety risk that could affect use, maintenance, or continued operation; notices shall explain the risk and required actions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a 12-month period, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and customers who own or are considering that model, and submit the incident data and a corrective-action plan for review. The threshold triggers a regulator risk assessment, not an automatic recall or stop-sale. Regulators may require a recall, stop-sale, or other proportionate measures based on evidence of risk, including below the threshold.
