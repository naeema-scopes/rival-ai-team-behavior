# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E51 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete investigation report within 30 days. Incidents presenting an ongoing risk of injury or fire must be reported to regulators immediately and affected customers promptly, no later than 24 hours after identification. Other reportable incidents must be communicated to affected customers within 5 business days. Reports must include known circumstances, affected models and serial-number ranges, and any recommended precautions; manufacturers must update regulators and customers when material facts change. At least annually, manufacturers must reconcile incident records against available service, warranty, and customer-support records, correct identified reporting gaps, and submit the reconciliation and any newly identified reportable incidents to the regulator.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. No more than 5% of tested units may fail. Testing must be repeated after material design or manufacturing changes and at least annually. Before sale and after each retest, manufacturers must submit per-model methods, sample sizes, and identifiable results to the relevant regulator and provide those same model-specific results to customers buying that model. Regulators may publish non-identifying aggregate results. A model exceeding the limit must promptly undergo corrective action and retesting; it may not be newly sold until it passes retesting and regulators approve the corrective action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must support a secure remote emergency shut-off function, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a local means of operation. Manufacturers must provide owners clear instructions and a reliable method to request shut-off. For units already installed when this standard takes effect, manufacturers must offer a compatible retrofit or replacement plan and complete implementation within 6 years, prioritizing units with elevated risk. Shut-off must not disable safety monitoring or emergency communications.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and installation environment, and must not create additional hazards or impede safe emergency response. Manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and end-of-life instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices describing material hazards, warning signs, emergency actions, and available support, and must promptly update notices when material safety information changes. A manufacturer must notify regulators and affected customers promptly when a model presents a material safety risk, including recommended precautions and corrective actions. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The annual rate must be calculated using incidents involving that model and its installed-unit population for the same period, with incident records and denominators documented and available to regulators for verification. Manufacturers must not delay reporting or recall action while disputing or validating the calculation. Manufacturers must promptly notify regulators and affected customers of the recall, explain the reason and risks, and provide a no-cost remedy such as repair, replacement, or refund. Regulators may require a recall at a lower incident rate when the evidence indicates an imminent or otherwise unacceptable risk.
