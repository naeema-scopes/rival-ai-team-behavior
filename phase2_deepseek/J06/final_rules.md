# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J06 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
## S1_incident_reporting
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of becoming aware of it. A death, serious injury, fire spreading beyond the battery enclosure, or evacuation must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days of identifying a safety-relevant incident pattern, and within 24 hours where immediate precautions are needed. Reports must include incident counts, installed-unit denominators, known outcomes, and corrective actions. Regulators may require prompt updates as an investigation develops.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and after a material design or manufacturing change, using a regulator-approved sampling and test protocol. Manufacturers must submit test methods, sample sizes, per-model failure rates, and corrective actions to the regulator. Regulators may audit tests and require retesting. Results must be provided to owners and prospective customers of that model directly on request, but individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. A model exceeding the 5% failure-rate benchmark must undergo regulator-supervised corrective action and retesting; the regulator will determine proportionate interim safeguards based on the evidence, rather than imposing an automatic sales ban.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
## S3_remote_shutoff
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off beginning 2 years after this standard takes effect, with a safe local shut-off available if communications fail. Manufacturers must provide software and installation support to retrofit already-installed units where technically feasible, and complete required retrofits within 6 years after the effective date. A regulator may approve a documented alternative where a retrofit is infeasible or would create a greater safety risk. Shut-off capability must not prevent emergency responders or occupants from isolating the system locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation, and must be designed and tested to reduce the likelihood of fire propagation. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions. A regulator may approve a documented alternative where built-in suppression is infeasible or would create a greater safety risk, provided equivalent protection is demonstrated.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, at purchase and through a readily accessible online notice, model-specific safety information, including known hazards, safe installation and use instructions, and how to report an incident. Detailed per-model 55 °C test failure rates must be submitted to regulators and provided directly to owners and prospective customers of that model on request, but must not be published to the general public. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of newly identified hazards and precautions; notice is due within 24 hours when immediate action is needed and otherwise within 5 business days after identifying a safety-relevant pattern. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any year, the manufacturer must promptly provide the regulator with supporting incident counts and installed-unit denominators. The regulator must review the evidence and may require interim safeguards, corrective action, or a mandatory recall proportionate to the confirmed risk. Manufacturers must issue interim customer safety instructions while the regulator verifies the rate. Any recall must include customer notification, safe shutdown or other protective steps, and repair, replacement, or refund.
