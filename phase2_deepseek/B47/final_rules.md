# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B47 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery thermal runaway, fire, smoke event requiring emergency response, injury, evacuation, or material property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware. A fuller incident report, including known circumstances and affected model/serial range, is due within 10 calendar days; updates must be provided as material facts emerge. Manufacturers must notify owners of affected units within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety risk requiring precautions, inspection, or service, and promptly provide updated instructions. Reports and notices must not delay urgent protective action.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units before sale and at least annually thereafter. The 5% failure rate is the presumptive maximum performance benchmark. A model exceeding 5% must be reported promptly to the relevant regulator with its results and a corrective-action plan. The regulator may allow continued sale under time-limited conditions, including enhanced monitoring, customer precautions, and retesting, where the manufacturer demonstrates that the conditions adequately control risk; otherwise, the regulator may restrict or suspend sales. New models must meet the 5% benchmark before sale. Manufacturers must submit each model’s results, sample size, protocol, and corrective-action plan confidentially to the relevant regulator. Complete per-model results must be provided to customers who own that model and to the relevant regulator; results need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market must provide a remotely accessible emergency shut-off function within 12 months of adoption. Manufacturers must provide a secure, reliable means for an owner or authorized emergency responder to activate shut-off, with clear instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For installed units, manufacturers must provide and maintain a remote shut-off capability where supported; units without that capability must be upgraded or replaced within 72 months of adoption where technically feasible. If an upgrade is not feasible, the manufacturer must notify the owner, explain the limitation, and provide appropriate alternative emergency procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must document that the suppression system is compatible with the battery and does not introduce an unreasonable risk, and must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions to customers. This requirement does not reduce manufacturers’ existing obligations to address hazards or provide appropriate safety measures before its effective date.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about incidents affecting their model, including known risks, precautions, and service or inspection instructions, promptly when a potential safety risk is identified; updates must follow as material facts emerge. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. Manufacturers must notify affected customers and the relevant regulator promptly, provide a safe remedy, and report completion and remaining affected units. Recalls and notices must not be delayed pending completion of an investigation.
