# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B51 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, emergency-service response, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days after becoming aware of it, including incidents that do not meet a separate injury or damage threshold. Any incident involving death, serious injury, or an ongoing threat to occupants or neighbours must be reported immediately and no later than 24 hours. The affected customer must be notified within 24 hours for an ongoing hazard, and within 5 business days for other thermal incidents. Reports must include known circumstances and be updated as the investigation proceeds.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a documented sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must provide per-model results and supporting data to regulators, and provide clear results to customers who buy the model and existing owners of that model. Results must not be withheld from those customers as confidential. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a corrective-action plan, and regulator review of appropriate risk controls and retesting; it does not by itself automatically prohibit sale. Regulators may require additional protective measures based on test results, incident history, and risk-mitigation plans.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability, with appropriate safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide a plan to retrofit compatible already-installed units within 6 years of this standard taking effect; where a unit cannot be safely retrofitted, the customer must be informed and offered an equivalent safety measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard is adopted. Manufacturers must provide customers with clear instructions on the system’s operation, limitations, and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of reportable thermal incidents, material safety findings, corrective actions, and relevant per-model test results. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The rate must be calculated for each consecutive 12-month period using thermal incidents attributable to that model and the model’s average number of installed units in service during that period, based on records available to the regulator. Manufacturers must provide the supporting incident counts and installed-unit data to the regulator; verification must not delay protective action where available information indicates the threshold may have been exceeded. When triggered, manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers without delay, stop placing the affected model on the market, and provide a regulator-approved recall and remedy plan.
