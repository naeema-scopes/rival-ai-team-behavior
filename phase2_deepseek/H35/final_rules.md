# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H35 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
A thermal incident is any battery event involving unintended overheating, smoke, fire, venting, or thermal runaway, whether or not injury or property damage occurs. Manufacturers must report each incident to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it; an incident involving fire, injury, evacuation, or emergency-service response must be reported immediately and no later than 4 hours after awareness. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours, and immediately when continued use presents an urgent hazard. Reports must include the model, serial number where available, event date and location, known consequences, and actions taken. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and provide updates when material facts change.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a validated, reproducible protocol and a representative sample of production units. Testing must be completed before initial sale and at least annually, and repeated after a material design or manufacturing change. A failure rate exceeding the 5% benchmark requires a documented corrective-action plan and evidence-based regulator review; regulators may impose proportionate safeguards based on risk. Manufacturers must provide per-model protocols, sample sizes, test dates, and actual results to regulators and share them reciprocally with the other participating manufacturer under confidentiality. Before purchase and within 30 days after each retest, customers must receive the test date, sample size, protocol, the model’s actual 55 °C failure rate, whether it exceeds the 5% benchmark, and any applicable corrective actions or safeguards.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models must include a remotely operable emergency shut-off that can place the unit in a safe state, subject to safeguards against unauthorized access and loss of essential local safety functions. Manufacturers must provide existing installed units with this capability, through retrofit or an equivalent safe solution, within 72 months of adoption. The manufacturer must notify affected customers of any retrofit and provide clear instructions; shut-off must not replace required local protections or emergency response.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the unit’s foreseeable battery-fire hazards. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must document performance through testing and provide the results to regulators and customers. Any design must not create additional hazards or be represented as eliminating the need for applicable installation, detection, or emergency-response measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, before purchase and after material safety updates, clear notices describing model-specific safety performance, known hazards, safe use, and actions to take after an incident; affected customers must be notified promptly of newly identified material risks. Manufacturers must report model-level thermal incident rates to regulators annually and promptly notify regulators when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when the regulator determines that continued use presents an imminent serious risk. Manufacturers must notify affected customers without delay and provide safe repair, replacement, or refund instructions.
