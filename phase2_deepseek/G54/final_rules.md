# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G54 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any battery thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. The manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and any identifiable people facing an immediate hazard, and in all cases within 24 hours when a product presents an ongoing safety risk. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial identifiers, actions taken, and later material findings. Suspected incidents must be reported; uncertainty is not a basis for delay.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a common regulator-established protocol specifying sample size, test conditions, and failure criteria. Manufacturers must submit per-model failure rates and supporting test records to the regulator. A rate above 5% triggers prompt regulator notification, documented corrective-action review, and retesting; it is not by itself an automatic bar on production or sale. Regulators may require additional safeguards or restrict new placements based on the evidence and risk. Before purchase and when material information changes, manufacturers must provide customers with that model’s 55 °C failure rate and a clear summary of its test results, material risks, precautions, and corrective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must include a remote emergency shut-off function that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under documented safeguards. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and maintain secure, reliable access to the function. Existing installed units must be upgraded to support remote emergency shut-off, where technically feasible, within 6 years after adoption. If an upgrade is not feasible, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and the regulator and provide an equivalent documented risk-mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and enclosure, and must not create an additional material hazard. Manufacturers must provide regulators with test evidence demonstrating performance under the applicable fire and thermal-runaway scenarios.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety instructions at purchase and promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks, recommended precautions, and corrective actions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, investigate the cause, and provide affected customers with a safety notice describing the risk and any interim precautions. The regulator must promptly review the evidence and may require corrective action, restrictions on new placements, or a recall. A recall is mandatory when the regulator determines that the model presents an unacceptable risk, or that required corrective action has not adequately addressed the risk. Regulators may also require a recall where incident severity or other evidence demonstrates an imminent or unacceptable risk. Recall notices must identify affected units, explain the hazard and remedy, and be sent promptly through direct customer contact and other appropriate channels. Manufacturers must report recall progress and completion to regulators.
