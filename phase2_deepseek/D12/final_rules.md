# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D12 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours, with known hazards and immediate safety steps; urgent risks require immediate notice. Reports must include known incident circumstances and follow-up findings, and must not omit incidents because they were not independently verified.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo sustained-heat testing at 55 °C using a representative sample and a documented, consistently applied protocol. No more than 5% of tested units for any model may fail. Before sale and after material design changes, manufacturers must provide per-model test results and the test protocol to the relevant regulator and to customers who buy that model. Results must identify the model, test date, sample size, and failure rate; customer disclosures may explain technical findings in plain language. A model that exceeds the 5% failure limit must not be sold until corrective action is taken and a subsequent test demonstrates compliance.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All newly installed home battery systems must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for authorized emergency responders or the system owner to initiate shut-off. Existing installed units that lack this capability must be upgraded or replaced within six years of this standard taking effect, where technically feasible; customers must be informed of interim safe-use instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must document that the suppression system is suitable for the unit and provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions. Until the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must disclose whether a unit includes built-in suppression and provide appropriate fire-safety instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the model’s 55 °C sustained-heat test results, known material thermal hazards, required installation and maintenance instructions, and clear emergency steps. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of new material safety findings and provide updated instructions. A model with more than 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period must be recalled; the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, stop sales, and provide a remedy and safe-use or removal instructions. Regulators may require additional protective measures.
