# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A53 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator. Incidents involving fire, injury, evacuation, or property damage must be reported within 24 hours of discovery; other thermal incidents must be reported within five business days. Affected customers must be notified within 48 hours for urgent incidents and within five business days for other reportable incidents. Reports must include known safety risks and relevant model and lot identification.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results to the relevant regulator and retain them for audit. Customers who buy a model must receive its per-model test results and any relevant safety information in an accessible form; this does not require general-public publication of individual-company results. A failure rate above 5% requires a regulator-reviewed corrective-action plan and retesting; it does not by itself prohibit sale or require withdrawal. Regulators may impose additional measures based on the results and demonstrated risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 12 months after adoption. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within six years after adoption, where technically feasible, and must notify affected customers of the capability and how to use it. Any unit that cannot be safely retrofitted must be identified to its owner and the relevant regulator, with an alternative safe decommissioning or replacement plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and designed to reduce the risk of fire spread and harm to occupants and neighbours. Manufacturers must provide evidence of its effectiveness through regulator-approved testing.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices promptly when a product presents a credible thermal hazard, including the affected model or lot, the nature of the risk, and steps to reduce exposure or obtain assistance. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the relevant regulator and provide the underlying incident-rate data and a corrective-action plan. The regulator must review the evidence and determine whether a recall is required, taking account of the severity and circumstances of incidents, data quality, and the effectiveness of corrective actions. A recall is mandatory when the regulator determines that a continuing unacceptable thermal risk cannot be adequately controlled by other measures. The manufacturer must notify affected customers without delay and provide a remedy plan whenever a recall is required.
