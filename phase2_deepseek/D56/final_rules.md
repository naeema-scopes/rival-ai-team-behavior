# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D56 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents involving installed units and regardless of suspected cause. They must provide a follow-up investigation report within 30 days, or explain why it is delayed. Within 48 hours of becoming aware of a material safety risk, manufacturers must notify affected customers and provide immediate safety instructions; notices must not wait for completion of an investigation. Manufacturers must retain incident records and report previously unreported incidents promptly after discovery.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a representative sample and a consistent, documented method. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers must submit the test method, sample size, and per-model results confidentially to the regulator. Individual per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public; manufacturers must provide applicable results to customers and prospective buyers of that model. A model exceeding the limit must receive a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and be retested on a defined schedule. The regulator may restrict sales where necessary to address an identified safety risk. Manufacturers must notify affected customers of any required safety action.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that do not support this function within 6 years after adoption, where technically feasible, and must provide an alternative immediate emergency isolation method where retrofit is not feasible. Shut-off controls must be secure, tested, and accessible to the customer and emergency responders; remote shut-off must not prevent safe manual isolation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry, independently tested, and maintained in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. Manufacturers must disclose its capabilities and limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the applicable model’s latest thermal-test results, incident-rate information, installation and operating safety instructions, and clear emergency guidance. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks, corrective actions, and changes to safe-use instructions, using direct channels where available and accessible public notices otherwise. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, stop sales, and implement the recall and remedy directed by the regulator. This trigger does not limit the regulator’s authority to order a recall sooner when warranted by the risk.
