# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H42 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, emergency intervention, injury, or property damage within 24 hours of learning of it. They must provide a completed incident report, including known circumstances and affected model and batch, within 10 calendar days, and promptly update it as material facts become known. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours of learning of an incident that presents a potential safety risk to them, with interim protective instructions where appropriate. Records of all thermal incidents, including those not reportable under these criteria, must be retained and made available to regulators on request.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before market entry, at least annually thereafter, and after a material design change. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% of tested units per model. Manufacturers must submit test protocols, sample sizes, per-model failure rates, and corrective actions to the relevant regulator in confidence, and must provide the per-model numerical failure rate and test results to customers of that model in accessible form; those results need not be published publicly. Customers must also receive compliance status, material safety risks, corrective actions, and clear protective instructions. A model exceeding the benchmark may not be newly placed on the market, except under a regulator-approved, time-limited corrective-action plan with interim risk controls and customer notice. For models already on the market when this requirement takes effect, the regulator must allow a reasonable corrective-action period before restricting sales, based on risk and remediation feasibility; the plan must specify a retest deadline and does not waive the benchmark. Regulators may publish aggregate, non-identifying results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent safety risk, by the manufacturer or emergency responders under documented safeguards. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions, protect access against unauthorized activation, and maintain the capability throughout the unit’s service life. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance within 72 months of adoption where technically feasible; otherwise, the manufacturer must notify the owner and regulator, explain the limitation, and provide an equivalent risk-mitigation plan.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be suitable for the battery chemistry and enclosure, and its performance must be validated under the applicable regulator-approved fire and thermal test protocol. Manufacturers must disclose the system’s capabilities and limitations to customers and provide maintenance instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety and emergency-use instructions at purchase and be promptly notified of material new safety risks affecting their model, including recommended protective actions and how to obtain support. Manufacturers must calculate each model’s annual thermal-incident rate using documented incident and installed-unit data and report it to the regulator. If a model exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, provide supporting data and proposed corrective actions, and notify affected customers of the risk and protective instructions. The regulator must promptly verify the reported rate and assess incident severity, data quality, and the adequacy of corrective actions. Following verification, the regulator must promptly require a recall or other effective protective measures proportionate to the risk, and may require a recall or other protective measures below the threshold whenever warranted. Any recall must include customer notice and instructions for safe shutdown, repair, replacement, or refund.
