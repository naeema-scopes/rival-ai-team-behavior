# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D32 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents that caused no injury or property damage. Within 10 calendar days, they must provide known details, corrective actions, and the number of installed units used to calculate any incident rate; they must promptly update the report as facts are confirmed. If an incident presents a credible safety risk to users or nearby people, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and relevant emergency services within 72 hours of becoming aware of that risk. Reports must identify the affected model and distinguish confirmed incidents from suspected ones.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C under a regulator-approved common protocol specifying sample selection and minimum sample size, test duration, failure criteria, and treatment of retests. Manufacturers must submit each model’s results, sample size, protocol, and failure rate to the relevant regulator and provide them to customers considering or owning that model. A verified failure rate above 5% requires prompt corrective action and a regulator-approved plan with a deadline for retesting. While implementing that plan, sales may continue only under regulator oversight and any safeguards the regulator requires. If the model fails to meet the 5% maximum by the plan’s deadline, sales must be suspended until a compliant retest verifies a rate at or below 5%. Regulators may require additional safeguards or earlier action where an imminent safety risk exists. Results need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required on all new home battery units manufactured 12 months or more after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for authorized emergency responders or the manufacturer to initiate shut-off, with appropriate safeguards against unauthorized activation. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must enable remote shut-off within 72 months where technically feasible, with a regulator-reviewed retrofit plan, progress updates at least annually, and customer notice of the capability and its use. Where retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and the regulator and provide an alternative emergency isolation procedure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression designed to mitigate thermal-fire propagation. The manufacturer must document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide them to customers and the relevant regulator. Units manufactured before that date are not subject to this requirement, but manufacturers must provide customers with applicable safety guidance and any recommended retrofit information.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive the applicable model’s 55 °C sustained-heat test result and failure rate, plus clear instructions for safe installation, operation, and emergency response. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified safety risks and provide updated safety instructions and corrective actions. Incident rates must be calculated for each model over a stated 12-month period using the number of installed units of that model as the denominator, and must include reported and otherwise known incidents. A common regulator-approved counting and verification method must be used. When a regulator verifies that any model’s rate exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must initiate a recall and promptly notify the regulator and affected customers, explain the remedy, and report recall progress. Regulators may require earlier corrective action or recall where an imminent hazard exists.
