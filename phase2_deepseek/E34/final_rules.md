# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E34 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete investigation report within 30 days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a plausible continuing safety risk, and promptly provide updates or corrective instructions. Reports must include model, incident date and location, outcome, and known contributing factors. A missed or late report must be disclosed and corrected promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before initial sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. A model exceeding that limit must promptly notify the regulator and submit a corrective-action plan and independently validated retest schedule. The regulator may require a sales pause where the results or other evidence indicate an imminent safety risk; otherwise, the manufacturer may continue sales during a time-limited remediation period under regulator oversight. Manufacturers must provide full per-model results to regulators and directly to customers who own or are considering that model; results must not be withheld from those customers. Regulators may verify results through independent audits.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include a remotely operable emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for authorized emergency use and communicate its operation to customers and emergency responders. For units already installed, manufacturers must enable remote shut-off where supported and complete any necessary retrofit or equivalent effective mitigation within six years of adoption. If a unit cannot be retrofitted, the manufacturer must notify its owner and provide a safe alternative.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the unit’s battery chemistry and enclosure. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must document suppression performance and maintenance needs, and provide those instructions to customers. The requirement does not replace applicable fire-safety installation, spacing, or detection requirements.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices about incidents, known risks, corrective actions, and safe operation. Notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a plausible continuing safety risk, and promptly provide updates or corrective instructions. A mandatory recall is triggered when an independently validated model-level rate exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The recall must include prompt regulator and customer notice and repair, replacement, or refund instructions. A recall requires an effective remedy; it does not by itself permanently prohibit sales of a model once the regulator verifies corrective action and safe resumption.
