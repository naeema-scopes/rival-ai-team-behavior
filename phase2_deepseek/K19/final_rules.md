# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K19 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled heating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. The affected customer must be notified within 24 hours when the incident involves their unit or presents an immediate safety risk; otherwise, notify affected customers within 5 business days. Reports must include known incidents and disclose any identified reporting gaps. Regulators may request supporting records.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo an accredited 55 °C sustained-heat test before sale and at least annually thereafter. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5%. A result above 5% requires prompt submission of a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retesting on a defined schedule; exceeding the limit alone does not automatically require a sales ban. Manufacturers must submit per-model results to regulators and provide results to customers who own or are considering that model; individual per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public. The regulator may restrict sales for failure to meet corrective-action or retesting requirements, or where evidence indicates an imminent risk. Results and corrective actions remain subject to regulator review.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new units must support remote emergency shut-off by the effective date of this standard. For already-installed units, manufacturers must provide retrofit capability or an equivalent safe remedy within 6 years, prioritizing units subject to safety alerts or elevated risk. Customers must be notified of shut-off availability and instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression designed to limit propagation of thermal events, validated through independent testing and maintained in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Manufacturers must disclose suppression capabilities and limitations to customers. Units manufactured before the effective date are not subject to retrofit solely to meet this requirement; any safety notice or recall obligation remains applicable.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific safety information, including the latest 55 °C sustained-heat test failure rate, incident history, relevant operating limits, and emergency actions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material new safety findings, hazards, corrective actions, and regulator notices, and provide updates as investigations proceed. When a model’s verified thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, undergo an immediate regulator review, and submit and implement a corrective-action plan on the regulator’s schedule. A recall is required when the regulator confirms a serious safety defect or determines that the corrective action is inadequate to address the risk. Rate calculations, supporting data, and corrective actions must be provided to the regulator; customers must receive clear instructions and available remedies.
