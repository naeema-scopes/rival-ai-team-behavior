# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A37 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, evacuation, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a follow-up investigation report within 30 days. Other confirmed thermal incidents must be reported to the regulator within 5 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk, and promptly provide any updated safety instructions.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol, a statistically representative sample, and documented results. Manufacturers must submit each model’s results, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the regulator. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate must not be newly placed on the market unless the regulator has reviewed the results and approved a corrective-action plan and any conditions needed to protect safety. Retesting is required after material design changes or corrective actions. Before purchase, manufacturers must provide prospective buyers—and must provide current owners on request—with that specific model’s 55 °C failure rate, relevant safety findings, required precautions, and corrective actions. The regulator may also disclose these model-specific results to affected customers. Identifiable per-model failure rates must not be withheld from customers who buy or own that model; they need not be published to the general public. Regulators may publish non-identifying aggregate results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability, or provide an equally effective regulator-approved safety measure, within 6 years of adoption. Shut-off must be usable by authorized emergency responders and the customer, with clear instructions and safeguards against unauthorized activation.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and installation environment and validated under a regulator-approved test protocol. Manufacturers must provide installation, inspection, and maintenance instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety instructions, installation and maintenance requirements, any applicable corrective actions and safety precautions, and contact details for reporting safety concerns. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and provide practical mitigation instructions. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period, calculated from confirmed incidents and installed-unit estimates, or when the regulator determines that a defect presents an equivalent serious risk. The manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers within 24 hours of determining that the trigger is met, and provide a timely repair, replacement, or refund remedy. The regulator shall determine whether to suspend sales pending review based on the risk and necessary protective measures. Updates on the investigation and remedy must be provided to affected customers.
