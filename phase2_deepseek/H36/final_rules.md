# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H36 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator every thermal incident involving overheating, thermal runaway, fire, or explosion, including events without injury or property damage, within 5 business days of learning of it. A death, serious injury, or event presenting an imminent risk to the public must be reported within 24 hours. Within 30 days, the manufacturer must provide a follow-up with known causes and corrective actions. Affected customers must be notified within 5 business days of identifying a material risk to their unit, and immediately when urgent action is needed. Reports must include model, event date, location, known circumstances, and any injury or damage; regulators may request further information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Before sale, after material design changes, and annually thereafter, manufacturers must submit per-model results, test methods, sample sizes, and corrective actions to the relevant regulator, and make the results available to customers who buy that model. Results need not be disclosed to the general public. A result above a 5% failure rate requires a corrective-action plan and regulator review, but does not automatically prohibit sale; regulators may require further testing or impose restrictions where results indicate an unreasonable safety risk. Regulators should review the threshold as evidence and testing methods develop.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market from 3 years after this standard takes effect must support a secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. For installed units, manufacturers must provide a feasible retrofit or equivalent mitigation plan within 2 years, and complete required retrofits within 6 years of the standard taking effect. Where a retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must notify affected customers and provide an alternative safety measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units placed on the market beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the unit’s intended installation and use. Manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and limitations information to customers. Regulators may approve equivalent protective designs where they provide comparable risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers, at purchase and in accessible online materials, model-specific safety information, incident and emergency-response instructions, required maintenance, relevant test methods, and a summary of applicable safety testing. Per-model test results, including failure rates, must be made available to customers who buy that model and submitted to regulators; they need not be disclosed to the general public. When a manufacturer identifies a material safety risk, it must promptly notify affected customers with the nature of the risk, actions to take, and support contacts; urgent risks require immediate notice. Manufacturers must notify regulators of corrective-action plans. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, using a consistent installed-unit denominator and regulator-verified data, regulators must promptly review the evidence and may require a recall or other corrective action based on incident severity, test results, and available mitigations. Regulators may order a recall sooner when they determine that a model presents an unreasonable safety risk.
