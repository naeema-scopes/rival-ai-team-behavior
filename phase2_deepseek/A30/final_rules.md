# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A30 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, injury, emergency-service response, evacuation, or suspected safety-related property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a follow-up investigation report within 10 business days. All other suspected thermal incidents must be reported within 5 business days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 72 hours of learning of a material safety risk, and promptly provide updates as facts develop. Reports must include known incident circumstances, model and serial/batch identifiers, and actions taken; uncertainty does not excuse timely initial reporting.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and statistically adequate sample size. A model meets the performance limit only if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Before sale and after any material design change, manufacturers must submit per-model results, test methods, and sample sizes to the relevant regulator and provide the results to customers who buy that model. Regulators may publish aggregated or anonymized results, but not individual company per-model failure rates. A model exceeding the limit must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and complete retesting on a regulator-set timetable. The regulator may impose interim safeguards based on risk; exceeding the limit alone does not automatically prohibit marketing.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units manufactured 24 months after this standard takes effect must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a means for the customer or emergency responders to verify shut-off. For units already installed when the standard takes effect, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent compliant upgrade within 6 years, prioritizing units subject to a safety notice or elevated risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units manufactured 36 months after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated for the unit’s foreseeable thermal events. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide those details to regulators and customers. The requirement does not apply retroactively to units already manufactured.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices for incidents or emerging risks that could affect safe use, including affected models, practical precautions, and how updates will be delivered. Notify affected customers within 72 hours of learning of a material safety risk and promptly update them as facts develop. Manufacturers must track thermal incidents by model and report annual incident rates to the regulator using a consistent incident definition and an audited installed-unit denominator. The regulator must promptly verify a reported rate above 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; verification must not delay interim protective measures where warranted. Once verified, the regulator must require a recall of that model, and the manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and provide recall instructions. Regulators may also require a recall where the severity or circumstances of a safety risk warrant it, regardless of the numerical threshold.
