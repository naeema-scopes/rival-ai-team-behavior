# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G32 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written incident report and known causes or corrective actions within 10 calendar days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk for their model, providing immediate precautions and a contact for updates. Reports must include model, serial number where available, incident date, and outcome; manufacturers must correct missed or incomplete reports promptly.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and adequate representative sampling. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. Manufacturers shall submit per-model test results and supporting methods to the regulator and provide the complete results to customers of that model automatically, including with purchase information and updates after retesting. If a model exceeds the limit, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator, submit a corrective-action plan and retest on a regulator-approved schedule. Regulators may require interim safeguards or restrict sales where warranted by the risk; exceeding the limit alone does not automatically require a market ban. Regulators should review the limit and evidence after two years. Individual-company per-model failure rates need not be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models placed on the market 24 months or more after adoption of this standard must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must provide a secure, documented shut-off capability and clear instructions for its use. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must enable remote shut-off, where technically feasible, within six years; where it is not feasible, they must notify owners and provide safe-use guidance and any available mitigation. Regulators may approve extensions for documented technical or safety reasons.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Suppression systems must be suitable for the battery chemistry and enclosure, and validated under a regulator-approved fire-safety protocol. Manufacturers must document the system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide these to regulators and customers. Existing installed units are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section, but manufacturers must provide owners with any applicable safe-use guidance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with model-specific safety notices describing material hazards, immediate precautions, and how to obtain updates; notices must be sent promptly when a credible safety risk is identified. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, based on regulator-reviewed incident and installed-unit data, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers. The regulator must review the evidence and require appropriate corrective action; a mandatory recall is required where the regulator determines that the evidence indicates a serious or imminent risk. Regulators may also require a recall below the threshold where the evidence indicates a serious or imminent risk. Manufacturers must explain any required remedy and timelines to affected customers and report completion progress.
