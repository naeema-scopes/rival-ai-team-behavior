# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K03 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator any battery thermal incident involving overheating, fire, smoke, or thermal runaway within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify the affected customer within 24 hours when an incident involves their unit, and promptly provide safety instructions and any known risk to nearby occupants. Manufacturers must maintain incident logs and periodically reconcile them against customer complaints, warranty claims, and service records; identified reporting gaps must be disclosed to the regulator with the quarterly report. Aggregate incident counts and corrective actions must be reported to regulators quarterly; reports must include installed-unit denominators and model identification.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A failure rate above 5% requires the manufacturer to submit a corrective-action plan and retest schedule to the relevant regulator. Regulators may require corrective action, retesting, or sales restrictions based on the test results and associated safety evidence. Test results, sample sizes, and corrective actions must be provided to the relevant regulator before sale and after material design changes, and made available to customers considering that model on request. Individual-company per-model failure rates are not required to be published to the general public.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off by 1 January 2028, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must provide a secure means for authorized emergency services or the customer to request shut-off. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance, where technically feasible, within six years of this standard taking effect; manufacturers must provide customers with a retrofit plan and disclose units that cannot be retrofitted.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no earlier than 36 months after adoption of this standard. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the battery chemistry and enclosure, and manufacturers must provide installation, maintenance, and emergency-use instructions to customers. Until the requirement takes effect, manufacturers must disclose whether each model includes built-in suppression and describe other fire-mitigation measures.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale and after any material safety change, customers must receive model-specific safety information, including the 55 °C sustained-heat test failure rate and sample size, known thermal incident rate and its period and installed-unit denominator, required maintenance, warning signs, emergency instructions, and available shut-off and suppression features. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and corrective actions. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators, investigate and submit verified incident data and a corrective-action plan, and notify affected customers. A recall is mandatory when a model exceeds that annual threshold. Regulators may require an earlier recall where evidence indicates an imminent serious risk. Recall notices must provide clear instructions for stopping use, obtaining assistance, and receiving repair or replacement.
