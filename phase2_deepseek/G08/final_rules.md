# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G08 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every known battery overheating or fire event to the regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including events that cause no injury or property damage, and must report any other thermal event that presents a credible risk of serious harm. A fuller investigation report is due within 30 days, with updates as new information becomes available. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an event indicates a material safety risk, and provide interim precautions and updates on corrective action. Reports must include known incidents and the steps taken to identify and address any reporting gaps.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least every three years thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results, including failure rates, to the regulator and disclose those results to customers buying that model before sale, in clear context alongside relevant safety information. They must promptly report any material safety concern revealed by testing. The regulator may require an individualized, risk-based review and corrective action or retesting, taking account of test results, failure modes, and other relevant safety evidence. No result alone, including a failure rate above 5%, automatically bars a model from sale; any sales restriction or other enforcement measure must be based on the regulator’s assessment of that model’s risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems placed on the market from 1 January 2028 must support a secure remote emergency shut-off accessible to the manufacturer and authorized emergency responders. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for its use and safeguards against unauthorized activation. For systems already installed on that date, manufacturers must provide a remote shut-off retrofit or equivalent safety upgrade within six years, where technically feasible, and notify owners of the schedule and any interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems placed on the market must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. The regulator may approve equivalent protection where a manufacturer demonstrates that suppression is technically unsuitable or that an alternative provides equivalent or greater protection. Manufacturers must disclose any approved alternative and applicable safety limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers must receive clear safety instructions, relevant model-specific safety information, installation and maintenance requirements, emergency precautions, and information on available shut-off features. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and regulators of newly identified material safety risks and provide interim precautions and corrective-action updates. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide the underlying data. The regulator must promptly investigate and determine whether a recall or other corrective action is required based on the model’s risk and all relevant safety evidence. If a recall is ordered, the manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and provide corrective-action instructions. Incident rates must be assessed per model, not company-wide.
