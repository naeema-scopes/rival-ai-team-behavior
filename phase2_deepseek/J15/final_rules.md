# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J15 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
**Incident reporting.** Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled heating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written incident report within 5 business days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports must include known incident counts and installed-unit denominators; suspected under-reporting must be investigated and corrected.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
**Thermal testing.** Each model must undergo a standardized sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a representative sample. Manufacturers must report the sample size and failure rate to regulators and customers purchasing that model. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public. A model with a failure rate above 5% must have a regulator-reviewed corrective-action plan, enhanced testing, and retesting at least annually; it must achieve a failure rate of 5% or less within 36 months. If it has not met that limit by the deadline, new sales and installations must pause until it does. Regulators may require an earlier pause if the rate exceeds 8% or they determine there is an imminent unreasonable safety risk. Regulators should review the thresholds against accumulated test and incident evidence within 3 years.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
**Remote emergency shut-off.** New units must support remote emergency shut-off beginning 2 years after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide a safe, no-cost retrofit pathway for compatible installed units and complete required retrofits within 6 years, prioritizing units with elevated risk. Shut-off must not prevent local emergency operation or create an unsafe loss of essential functions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
**Built-in fire suppression.** New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must disclose the suppression system’s capabilities and limitations to customers. The requirement does not replace other required safeguards or emergency-response instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
**Customer disclosure and recalls.** Before sale, customers must receive the model’s latest 55 °C sustained-heat test results, relevant safety limitations, emergency instructions, and a clear process for reporting safety concerns. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and provide action instructions. Calculate each model’s annual thermal-incident rate separately using that model’s installed-unit denominator; report company-wide rates separately. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators and affected customers, provide a safe remedy or other protective action as directed, and undergo regulator-led safety review. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Regulators may require an earlier recall at any rate where evidence indicates an unreasonable safety risk.
