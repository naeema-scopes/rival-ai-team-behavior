# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B25 · variant B · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Northwind': 71})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator every battery-related overheating, fire, smoke, thermal runaway, or emergency shutdown, whether or not injury or property damage occurred. An initial report is due within 72 hours of learning of the incident, followed by a complete investigation report within 30 days. Manufacturers must promptly preserve relevant device and incident data. Customers whose unit was involved must be notified within 5 business days, with known hazards and immediate safety steps; material updates must be sent as they become available. Reports should include model, installation and event details, and suspected cause.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a regulator-approved sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a representative sample and standardized protocol. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5% per model. A model exceeding 5% is non-compliant; the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and submit a corrective-action plan. The regulator shall set proportionate safeguards and a retesting timeline based on risk; sales may continue subject to those safeguards unless the regulator determines a pause is necessary. Full per-model results, including failure rates, must be provided confidentially to the regulator. Customers must receive safety notices describing known hazards and recommended protective actions, but not individual-company per-model failure rates. Regulators should review the protocol and threshold as evidence accumulates.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New battery systems must provide a remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must enable the capability on compatible installed units and provide a safe retrofit or replacement plan for units that lack it, completing the program within 6 years of adoption. The shut-off must be accessible to authorized emergency responders and the customer, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must disclose any limitations and the procedure for use.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on new battery systems manufactured 36 months or more after adoption. The system must be designed and validated to reduce the risk of fire spread and must not create an additional material hazard. Manufacturers must provide test evidence to the regulator and disclose the system’s operation and limitations to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive safety notices describing known hazards and recommended immediate actions, and material updates as they become available. Full model-specific thermal-test results, including failure rates, must be provided confidentially to the regulator; customer notices must not publish individual-company per-model failure rates. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period triggers prompt regulator notification and a regulator-led risk review, initiated within 30 days. The regulator must require a recall when the review determines that the model presents an unacceptable risk. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of any recall and provide instructions for safe shutdown, removal, or other protective action.
