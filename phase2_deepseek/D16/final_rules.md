# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D16 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal runaway, fire, smoke event, or overheating incident involving a unit to the responsible regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents involving injury, property damage, or no apparent harm. They must provide a complete investigation and corrective-action update within 30 days, or explain why more time is needed. The manufacturer must notify the affected customer within 24 hours when the event involves their unit, and promptly provide safety instructions. If a hazard may affect other customers or the public, notify regulators and potentially affected customers within 24 hours and issue interim precautions. Keep an auditable incident log and report all incidents; suspected under-reporting must be disclosed and corrected.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. The target maximum failure rate is 5% for any model. Before sale and at least annually thereafter, manufacturers must test and submit per-model sample sizes, methods, exact failure rates, and corrective actions to the responsible regulator. Customers considering or owning a model must receive its exact per-model failure rate, test date and method, and any safety limitations, precautions, or corrective actions in plain language. Individual-company per-model failure rates must not be published to the general public; regulators may publish anonymized or aggregated results. A model exceeding 5% must be promptly reported to the regulator, undergo confirmatory testing and corrective action, and must not be represented as meeting the 5% criterion until it does. The regulator shall promptly assess evidence and risk and may authorize continued or conditional sales during corrective action, with customer disclosures and safeguards, or impose interim sales restrictions where warranted. An exceedance alone does not automatically require sales suspension.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery systems must provide a secure remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where an imminent safety hazard warrants it, by the manufacturer or responsible emergency authority under documented procedures. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions and maintain safeguards against unauthorized activation. For installed units, manufacturers must enable or retrofit remote shut-off within 72 months of adoption where technically feasible; where it is not feasible, they must notify the owner, explain the limitation, and provide an alternative emergency isolation method and instructions. Remote shut-off must not replace required physical safety protections.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery systems manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression conforming to a regulator-approved effectiveness and safety test. This requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Before that date, manufacturers must provide customers with clear information on any built-in suppression features and safe emergency response instructions. Regulators may approve equivalent protective technology where it provides at least comparable fire-risk reduction.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear written information on installation, safe operation, emergency response, maintenance, and how to report an incident. Notify affected customers promptly of material newly identified safety risks and provide practical precautions and remedies. For each model, thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units must be calculated using a consistent incident basis and installed-unit denominator over a rolling 12-month period. If any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in that period, the manufacturer must promptly report the exceedance to the regulator, initiate corrective action, and conduct a mandatory recall of that model. Notices must promptly reach known owners and be accessible to neighbours and the public, and explain the hazard, affected units, immediate precautions, and remedy.
