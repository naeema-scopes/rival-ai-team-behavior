# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H31 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or emergency shut-off to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents involving installed units. A detailed report, including known circumstances and corrective actions, must follow within 10 business days. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours of identifying a material hazard, describing precautions and available remedies. Reports must use consistent incident and installed-unit definitions and be retained for regulator review.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample sizes, and any corrective-action plans to regulators. Customers purchasing or using a model must receive its actual per-model failure rate and sample size, along with clear information about material safety risks, recommended precautions, and available remedies. Results need not be published to the general public. A failure rate above 5% requires a regulator-reviewed corrective-action plan and retesting within a regulator-set period. Exceeding 5% does not by itself require suspension of sales while the plan is implemented; regulators may require additional protective measures, including a sales pause, where the evidence warrants it.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units manufactured 24 months after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off. For units already installed on the adoption date, manufacturers must provide a remote shut-off retrofit or equivalent capability within six years, where technically feasible, and report progress annually to regulators. Customers must be notified of any limitations and interim safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured on or after 36 months following adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance requirements. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions on the system’s operation, limitations, and maintenance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety instructions at purchase and promptly be notified of any newly identified material hazard affecting their unit, including recommended precautions and available remedies. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and submit an independently auditable corrective-action plan, including a root-cause analysis and timeline for mitigation. The manufacturer must initiate a recall of that model when it exceeds this threshold. Incident and installed-unit counts must use a consistent, regulator-approved method. Public recall notices must identify affected models and explain customer actions. Customer notices must include material safety risks, precautions, remedies, and the affected model’s per-model test failure rate and sample size.
