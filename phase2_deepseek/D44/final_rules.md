# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D44 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 5 business days after learning of it. A fatality, serious injury, or ongoing fire hazard must be reported within 24 hours. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 5 business days when an incident indicates a safety risk relevant to their units, and promptly provide any necessary safety instructions. Reports must include known incident details and be updated as the investigation develops.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a documented, representative sample and a consistent protocol. Manufacturers must submit the protocol, sample size, and per-model results to the relevant regulator before sale and after material design changes. Before purchase, customers must receive that model’s 55 °C test result, sample size, applicable limitations, and protective instructions; these results may not be withheld from customers, though they need not be published to the general public. A result above 5% requires a regulator-reviewed corrective-action plan, a defined retesting schedule, and any proportionate interim safeguards needed to protect the public. Exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sale; regulators may require restrictions or other protective measures based on the risk and corrective-action progress. Regulators may publish aggregate, non-identifying industry results.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery units must support remote emergency shut-off at installation. Manufacturers must provide a secure, tested means for authorized emergency responders and the manufacturer to initiate shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized use and clear customer instructions. For units already installed when this standard is adopted, manufacturers must provide remote shut-off capability or an equivalent effective emergency isolation method within 6 years, prioritizing units with known safety risks and informing affected customers of the schedule and any interim precautions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document and validate the suppression system’s performance and provide that information to the relevant regulator. Units manufactured before the effective date are not subject to this requirement.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before sale, customers must receive clear safety information, including relevant per-model 55 °C test results, incident-reporting and emergency guidance, and any applicable limitations. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide instructions for safe use, shut-off, or other protective action. The annual thermal-incident rate for a model is the number of verified thermal incidents involving that model during the year per 100,000 units of that model installed during the same period; manufacturers must report known incidents and installation counts to the relevant regulator, which may verify or require correction of the calculation. A manufacturer must initiate a recall when the verified rate for any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Verification must not delay prompt regulator and customer notification or necessary protective action. Recall notices must explain the hazard and provide remediation instructions.
