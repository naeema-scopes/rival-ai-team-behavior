# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A52 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents initially reported by customers or third parties. Within 72 hours, manufacturers must notify the affected customer and provide any immediate safety instructions. Updates on injuries, material property damage, or changes to the hazard assessment must be sent promptly to the regulator and affected customers. Manufacturers must retain incident records and provide them to regulators on request.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each battery model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol before sale and at least annually thereafter. Manufacturers must submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results to the relevant regulator and provide results to customers of that model; individual-company per-model results need not be published to the general public. The maximum allowed failure rate for any model is 5%. A model exceeding 5% must promptly report the exceedance to the regulator and must not be offered for new sale until a compliant retest demonstrates a failure rate at or below 5%. The manufacturer must submit a corrective-action plan, including a retest schedule and measures addressing risks to installed units. The regulator must review the test results, incident data, and other relevant safety evidence and may require proportionate mitigation or additional testing for installed units. Updated results and required corrective measures must be provided to the regulator and affected customers.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery units must include a remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 24 months after adoption. Manufacturers must provide clear instructions for customers and emergency responders on how to activate it, and ensure activation does not prevent safe access to necessary emergency services. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide a safe retrofit or equivalent mitigation plan within 24 months and complete implementation within 72 months after adoption, prioritizing units with elevated risk. Any remote shut-off must be secure against unauthorized access and must not be used to disable a unit except for an emergency or safety-related purpose.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be designed and validated for the battery’s foreseeable thermal hazards, and must not create additional material risks to occupants or responders. Manufacturers must provide customers and emergency responders with instructions on its operation and limitations. Existing installed units are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide prospective and current customers with model-specific safety notices describing known thermal hazards and recommended precautions. Provide urgent safety instructions promptly when a hazard requiring customer action is identified, and update customers when material information changes. Per-model test results must be provided to customers of that model and the relevant regulator, but need not be published to the general public. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, calculated using verified incidents and that model’s installed base, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and provide the underlying data and a corrective-action plan. The manufacturer must initiate a recall of that model; the regulator may direct the scope and implementation measures. The manufacturer must promptly notify affected customers and provide repair, replacement, or refund instructions.
