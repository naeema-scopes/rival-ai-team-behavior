# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J18 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, including incidents still under investigation. A corrected, complete incident report is due within 10 business days, with material updates provided promptly. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident indicates a potential safety risk to their model or requires customer action. Reports must include the model, serial number where available, incident date and location, and known consequences. Manufacturers must retain incident records and provide them to the regulator on request.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a documented, repeatable protocol and a representative sample. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% per model. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model results to the regulator and to customers who purchase or are considering that model; results must not be withheld from those customers. A model above the limit requires a regulator-approved corrective plan and retesting within 24 months. Exceeding the limit alone does not automatically prohibit sales; the regulator may impose proportionate interim restrictions where necessary to protect safety, including restricting sales if the plan is not met.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market must support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to provide this capability within six years of this standard taking effect, where technically feasible, and report progress annually to the regulator. Remote shut-off must not replace local manual controls or emergency response procedures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured 36 months or more after this standard takes effect must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to mitigate thermal runaway and prevent fire spread. Manufacturers must document the system’s performance and maintenance requirements and provide these to the regulator and customers. Existing installed units are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the specific model, including its per-model sustained-heat test results, incident history, emergency procedures, and any known limitations. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material new safety risks and provide instructions for protective action. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; the manufacturer must notify the regulator and affected customers promptly and carry out a regulator-approved recall and remedy. The regulator shall determine whether distribution must stop during the remedy, based on the assessed risk and adequacy of interim protections. A recall requires appropriate corrective action and does not by itself permanently prohibit future sales; distribution may resume or continue subject to conditions accepted by the regulator, including any required retesting. The regulator may also require a recall when other evidence establishes an unacceptable safety risk.
