# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G37 · variant G · NOT adopted (signatures: {'Halcyon': 73})

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, or suspected thermal runaway to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, including incidents that caused no injury or property damage. Within 48 hours, they must notify affected customers and, where there is a credible risk to nearby people or property, provide a safety notice to relevant local authorities and people at risk. Reports and notices must state known hazards, immediate precautions, and how to obtain further information. Manufacturers must promptly update reports as material facts become known.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol. Manufacturers must submit documented per-model results, including model identification, test date, sample size, and failure rate, to the regulator and provide those results to customers of that model. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5%. Any model exceeding 5% must submit a corrective-action plan and be retested at least annually until its rate is 5% or lower. Regulators may require additional testing or protective measures where results indicate a safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new models must include a remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units that lack this capability within 6 years of adoption, prioritizing units with known safety risks. The shut-off must be usable by authorized emergency personnel and the manufacturer, with appropriate safeguards against accidental activation. Customers must be told how shut-off may affect their unit and what to do in an emergency.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and must not create additional hazards. Manufacturers must provide evidence of effectiveness through regulator-approved testing and disclose relevant limitations and maintenance requirements to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety notices describing known hazards, precautions, and corrective actions promptly after a material safety risk is identified; notices must be updated as facts change. Manufacturers must notify affected customers directly and provide public notice where affected owners cannot reasonably be identified. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly report the rate and underlying data to the regulator and submit a risk assessment and corrective-action plan. Exceeding this threshold triggers expedited regulator review, which may require a recall or other proportionate protective action. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers and comply with regulator requirements for protective measures.
