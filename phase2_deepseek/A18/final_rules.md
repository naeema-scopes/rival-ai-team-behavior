# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A18 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report to the relevant regulator every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal runaway, including incidents resulting in injury, property damage, emergency response, or a safety-related shutdown. The initial report must be made within 24 hours of learning of the incident, with available details; a follow-up report containing investigation findings and corrective actions is due within 30 days, and material new information must be reported promptly. Manufacturers must notify affected customers within 72 hours of learning of a credible material safety risk, including relevant precautions and available support. Reports must protect personal information and be updated as facts are confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter. Complete per-model results, including the numbers of units tested and failures and the resulting failure rate, must be provided to the relevant regulator and to current and prospective customers considering that model. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate among tested units must promptly submit a corrective-action plan and retest on a regulator-approved schedule. Exceeding 5% does not by itself prohibit sales; the regulator may require interim safeguards or restrict sales based on demonstrated risk and the adequacy of the corrective plan.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support a secure remote emergency shut-off capability no later than 24 months after this standard takes effect. For units already installed, manufacturers must provide the capability through retrofit or an equivalent approved measure within 6 years, where technically feasible, and must notify customers of the schedule and any limitations. Shut-off must not prevent emergency responders from safely isolating the system locally.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The regulator may approve a time-limited alternative safety measure where a manufacturer demonstrates that suppression is technically infeasible for a unit design and that the alternative provides equivalent protection. Manufacturers must disclose any approved alternative and its limitations to affected customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety instructions at purchase and prompt notice of any material safety risk affecting their model, including recommended precautions and available corrective actions. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any rolling 12-month period, the manufacturer must promptly notify the regulator and affected customers and provide the underlying rate and available incident information. Exceeding this threshold triggers a mandatory recall. Recall notices must explain the remedy, timing, and customer support process. The regulator may require earlier corrective action, including a recall, where an imminent hazard is identified.
