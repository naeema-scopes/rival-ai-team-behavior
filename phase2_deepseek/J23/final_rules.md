# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J23 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal runaway, fire, or injury involving a home battery to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a fuller incident report within 10 business days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a material safety risk, including immediate protective steps and contact information. Reports must include the incident date, model, and known circumstances; suspected incidents must be updated as facts are confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a prescribed representative sample. Manufacturers must provide per-model test methods and results to regulators and relevant conformity-assessment bodies, and disclose the results to customers purchasing that model. A failure rate above 5% requires prompt regulator notification, a time-bound corrective-action plan, and retesting on a regulator-approved timetable. Exceeding 5% does not by itself automatically prohibit sales; regulators may impose proportionate restrictions or other measures based on the evidence, severity, and effectiveness of corrective actions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks and provide safe-use instructions and corrective actions.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New home battery models must include a remote emergency shut-off capability. Manufacturers must provide an update or retrofit enabling this capability for installed units within 6 years of adoption, where technically feasible, and provide customers with clear instructions for its use. Any unit not yet equipped must retain an accessible local emergency shut-off and clear shutdown instructions; remote shut-off must not replace required physical safety measures.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must disclose whether a unit includes suppression and provide customers with installation, maintenance, and emergency-use instructions. Regulators may require earlier corrective measures for a product presenting an imminent serious risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information on installation, ventilation and placement, warning signs, emergency steps, and how to contact the manufacturer. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks and provide updated safe-use instructions. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 validated thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. The rate must use a consistent model-specific incident-counting method and installed-unit denominator, with supporting data provided promptly to regulators. The manufacturer must submit a time-bound recall and corrective-action plan for regulatory oversight; this process does not waive or delay the mandatory recall. Regulators may also require corrective action or recall where the severity or pattern of incidents presents an imminent serious risk.
