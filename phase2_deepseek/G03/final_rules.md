# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G03 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any death, serious injury, fire, thermal runaway, or other incident presenting a credible risk of fire or injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, and provide a completed incident report within 10 calendar days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a material safety risk, including immediate precautions and support contact information. Reports must include known facts and be updated as investigations establish more. Manufacturers must maintain records of all thermal incidents and report annually to the regulator the number of incidents, including those determined not to meet the reporting criteria, and the basis for that determination.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo an independently witnessed sustained-heat test at 55 °C before initial certification and at least once every three years thereafter, using a regulator-prescribed protocol and sample size. The maximum permitted failure rate is 5% from the effective date of this standard. A model exceeding the limit may not be newly certified or placed on the market until corrective action is completed and a passing retest is verified. Manufacturers must submit complete per-model results, including numerical failure rates, to the regulator and provide them to customers who buy that model. Numerical per-model failure rates must not be published publicly; public reporting may state compliance status and use aggregate data.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Newly certified home battery models must include a remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or an authorized emergency responder when necessary to address an imminent risk of fire or injury. The system must be designed to fail safely, maintain local emergency shut-off capability, and provide customers clear instructions on its operation. Manufacturers must retrofit installed units to provide remote emergency shut-off within six years after this standard takes effect, where technically feasible; any units not retrofitted must be disclosed to their owners and the relevant local fire authority.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units manufactured for sale must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-prescribed performance and reliability requirements, beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide customers with instructions on system limitations and maintenance. The regulator may grant a time-limited, publicly documented exemption for a specific design only upon evidence that an alternative built-in mitigation provides equivalent or greater protection.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers clear safety notices describing relevant hazards, safe operation, maintenance, and any corrective actions. Customers must be notified within 48 hours after the manufacturer determines there is a material safety risk, with practical precautions and support information; notices must be updated as material facts change. A manufacturer must initiate a mandatory recall when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Incident rates must be calculated using regulator-prescribed methods and submitted to the regulator; public reporting may use aggregate data and model-level recall status. A recall requires appropriate corrective action and verified retesting before affected products may return to sale; a recall trigger alone does not permanently bar a model from the market.
