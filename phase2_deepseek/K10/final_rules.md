# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K10 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written incident report within five business days. They must notify the affected unit’s customer within 48 hours, or sooner when needed to reduce an immediate safety risk, and provide known hazards, safe-use or isolation instructions, and available corrective actions. Reports must include the model, incident date and location, and known impacts; manufacturers must update regulators and affected customers as material information becomes available. Incidents must not be omitted because they were not independently verified.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before initial market placement and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit per-model test methods, sample sizes, and results to regulators. Results must not be published in a way that identifies an individual company's per-model failure rate. Results and relevant safety guidance must be provided to customers of the tested model. A failure rate above 5% requires a documented corrective-action plan submitted to regulators within 60 days and retesting within six months; it does not by itself prohibit continued sale while the plan is being implemented. Regulators may require additional measures where the results indicate an imminent safety risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
Remote emergency shut-off is required for all new units placed on the market beginning 24 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit pathway for installed units and complete retrofits within six years of the effective date, where technically feasible. Any unit that cannot be retrofitted must be disclosed to its owner with safe isolation instructions.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
Built-in fire suppression is required on all new units placed on the market beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. Manufacturers must document compliance using a regulator-approved method and provide compliance information to regulators and customers. This requirement does not replace incident reporting, safe-use instructions, or applicable corrective actions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety and incident notices promptly, including known hazards, safe-use instructions, and any required corrective action. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer must notify regulators, submit supporting data and a corrective-action plan within 30 days, and contact affected customers without undue delay with appropriate safety guidance. Exceeding this threshold triggers mandatory regulatory review and corrective action; it does not by itself require a recall. Regulators may order a recall or other protective measures based on the evidence and risk. Public notices must identify affected products and explain any recall and actions customers should take.
