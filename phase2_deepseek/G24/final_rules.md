# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run G24 · variant G · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a follow-up investigation report within 30 days. They must notify affected owners within 48 hours when an incident indicates a potential product hazard, and promptly update that notice as material facts become known. Reports must include model, incident date and location, known impacts, and actions taken. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and report aggregate counts quarterly, including incidents initially reported by customers or third parties.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo an independently witnessed sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least every three years thereafter, using a repeatable protocol and representative sample. Manufacturers must provide per-model results and sample details to the regulator; results must also be provided to customers who buy that model, but not published to the general public. The maximum allowed failure rate is 5%. A model exceeding 5% must submit a corrective-action plan within 90 days and pass a follow-up test within 12 months. For models already on the market when this standard takes effect, the manufacturer must meet the limit within 36 months; during that period, sales may continue subject to regulator oversight and any interim safety measures the regulator requires. The regulator may restrict sales sooner if it finds an imminent serious risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models introduced from 24 months after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, subject to safeguards against unauthorized activation and procedures for loss of connectivity. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to support remote shut-off within six years of adoption where technically feasible, and must inform owners of any units that cannot be retrofitted and provide safe alternatives.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New models introduced 36 months after adoption must include built-in fire suppression. Manufacturers must validate suppression performance under the prescribed independent safety test protocol and provide results to the regulator. Existing models are not subject to a retrofit requirement, but manufacturers must disclose whether suppression is included and provide owners with applicable safe-use guidance.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific thermal-test results, incident-rate information for the preceding year, and clear instructions for reporting incidents and responding to safety notices. Manufacturers must notify affected owners within 48 hours when a potential hazard is identified and update notices as material facts emerge. A regulator must review any model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. A mandatory recall is triggered when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, or when the regulator finds an imminent serious risk based on incident severity or other evidence. Manufacturers must promptly notify customers and implement any ordered recall.
