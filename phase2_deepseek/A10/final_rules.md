# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A10 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report to the relevant regulator any confirmed thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a written update within 10 days. Manufacturers shall notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a credible safety risk to their unit or requires customer action. Reports shall include known circumstances and corrective actions; manufacturers shall promptly supplement incomplete information.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative samples. Manufacturers shall submit test methods, sample sizes, and per-model results to the regulator and provide the results for each model to customers purchasing that model. Per-model results shall not be published for general public access. A model exceeding a 5% failure rate shall submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator within 60 days and be retested within 12 months. The regulator may require additional safeguards or restrict sales if the manufacturer fails to implement an adequate plan or the model presents an unacceptable safety risk. Retesting is required after a material design or manufacturing change and at least once every two years.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New models placed on the market 24 months or more after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off. Manufacturers shall provide a plan for enabling remote shut-off on compatible installed units, prioritizing units with identified safety risks. Any regulator-required retrofit deadline for already-installed units shall be no earlier than six years after adoption, with requirements based on technical feasibility and risk. Shut-off shall not disable safety monitoring or prevent emergency services from addressing an active hazard.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption shall include built-in fire suppression meeting a regulator-approved performance standard. The regulator may grant a time-limited, model-specific extension where a manufacturer demonstrates that compliant suppression is not technically feasible, provided equivalent risk controls are in place. Manufacturers shall disclose whether each model includes built-in suppression.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices describing known hazards, affected models or serial ranges, and recommended actions. Notices shall be sent promptly when a credible safety risk requires customer action, and updated as material information becomes available. A model exceeding 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year shall be reported promptly to the regulator and shall trigger a mandatory recall. The regulator may approve a narrowly tailored corrective-action pathway instead of immediate recall only upon a documented finding that it will reduce risk at least as quickly and effectively; this shall not delay customer warnings or protective actions. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of any recall or required corrective action. Per-model incident rates shall be reported to the regulator; the threshold applies to individual models, not company-wide rates.
