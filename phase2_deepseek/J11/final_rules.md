# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J11 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, thermal runaway, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with an initial account of known facts. They must submit a fuller incident report within 10 calendar days and update it as material facts become available. Affected customers must be notified within 48 hours when the incident indicates a potential safety risk to their unit or household. Reports must include known unit and model identifiers, circumstances, injuries or damage, and corrective actions. Suspected incidents must be logged and investigated; uncertainty about cause does not defer the initial report.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a harmonized sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market introduction, at least annually, and after a material design or manufacturing change, using a representative sample. Manufacturers must provide protocols, sample sizes, and per-model results to the regulator and retain them for inspection. Buyers and current owners must be able to obtain results for the model they own or are considering purchasing; these results are provided directly to those customers and are not required to be published to the general public. A model with a failure rate above 5% must undergo a documented risk assessment and corrective-action plan with regulator oversight; exceeding 5% alone does not automatically prohibit sale. Material safety findings must be communicated directly to affected customers.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery models must include remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the manufacturer or an authorized emergency responder to address an imminent safety risk. Manufacturers must explain activation and limitations to customers and responders. Units already installed when this standard is adopted must be brought into compliance within 6 years, where technically feasible. Manufacturers must document infeasibility and provide regulators an alternative risk-mitigation plan and schedule.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
All new home battery units must include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption of this standard. Manufacturers must document the suppression system's performance and maintenance requirements and provide this information to customers. Regulators may approve an equivalent built-in mitigation technology where evidence demonstrates comparable protection against fire propagation and risk to occupants and neighbours.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Customers must receive clear safety information at purchase and prompt, direct notice of any newly identified material risk affecting their model, including symptoms, immediate precautions, support contacts, and available corrective action. Manufacturers must notify affected customers and regulators without undue delay when an investigation identifies a credible safety concern, and provide updates as the investigation proceeds. When any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units of that model in a year, the regulator must promptly review the evidence and require appropriate interim protections. Within 30 days, the regulator must document its determination, including the risk assessment and whether effective corrective measures can adequately address the risk. The regulator must order a recall when it determines that a material safety risk cannot be adequately addressed by less disruptive corrective measures; exceeding the threshold alone does not automatically require a recall. Recall remedies and other corrective actions must be communicated directly to affected customers.
