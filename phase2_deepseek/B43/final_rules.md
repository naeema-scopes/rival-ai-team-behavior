# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run B43 · variant B · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a preliminary account of known facts. A complete investigation report is due within 30 days, with updates as material facts emerge. Affected customers must be notified within 72 hours of learning that an incident involving their unit may present a safety risk. Reports must identify the model and include known incident counts; manufacturers must not omit incidents because they were not initially reported.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved, statistically adequate protocol before sale, at least annually thereafter, and after a material design or component change. Manufacturers must provide complete per-model methods and results to regulators and to customers who buy or own that model. A model with a failure rate above 5% must promptly submit a corrective-action plan to the regulator and notify affected customers of the results, safety status, and relevant precautions. Regulators may require additional testing or corrective measures based on results and risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
All new home battery systems must support a secure remote emergency shut-off accessible to the customer and authorized emergency responders, with safeguards against unauthorized activation. Manufacturers must provide a retrofit path for installed units and equip at least 90% of technically compatible installed units within 72 months of adoption; any unit not retrofitted must be disclosed to its owner and documented with the regulator. Shut-off must not prevent safe local operation or emergency response when communications are unavailable.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery systems manufactured 36 months or more after adoption of this standard must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to reduce the risk of thermal propagation and fire. Manufacturers must document the suppression system’s performance and maintenance requirements for regulators and disclose them to customers. Systems manufactured before that date are not subject to this requirement, but manufacturers must provide owners with applicable fire-safety instructions.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive model-specific 55 °C test results, incident and recall history, remote shut-off capabilities, and clear safety instructions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material newly identified risks, incidents involving their unit that may present a safety risk, and corrective actions. A recall is mandatory when any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year; manufacturers must notify regulators and affected customers promptly and provide instructions for safe shutdown, repair, or return.
