# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run J44 · variant J · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report any thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or material property damage to the relevant regulator within 5 business days of learning of it, and immediately (within 24 hours) where there is a death, serious injury, or ongoing risk to the public. They shall notify affected owners within 10 business days, or within 24 hours for an urgent hazard. Reports must include known incident details and be updated as investigations progress. Manufacturers shall maintain records of all known thermal incidents, including incidents not initially reported, and submit an annual incident summary to regulators. The summary shall identify the models and installed-unit denominators used to calculate incident rates, reconcile incident records against available service, warranty, and customer-complaint records, and explain any exclusions or corrections. Manufacturers shall retain supporting records and provide them to regulators for verification on request.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before market entry and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. A result above 5% but not above 8% shall trigger regulator review and, where warranted, a corrective-action plan and retesting; it shall not by itself require market exclusion. A model fails the test if more than 8% of tested units exhibit a defined safety failure. Manufacturers shall provide per-model results, test protocol, sample size, and corrective-action plans to the relevant regulator. Customers shall receive a clear model-level safety summary, including applicable test results; individual-company per-model failure rates need not be publicly published. Regulators may require retesting after a material design change or adverse trend.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units placed on the market shall support secure remote emergency shut-off by 24 months after this standard takes effect, subject to safeguards against unauthorized access and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall provide a retrofit plan for installed units and complete feasible retrofits within 6 years after the effective date. Where retrofit is technically infeasible, the manufacturer shall notify the owner and regulator and provide an equivalent risk-reduction measure.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after this standard takes effect. The suppression system shall be validated for the battery chemistry and enclosure, designed to limit fire propagation and protect occupants and nearby persons, and maintained in accordance with manufacturer instructions. Until the requirement takes effect, manufacturers shall document fire-risk controls and provide installation and emergency-response instructions to customers.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
At sale and after any material safety update, customers shall receive clear model-specific safety information, including applicable 55 °C test results, incident and emergency-response instructions, and contact details for reporting safety concerns. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers and regulators of a confirmed hazard and provide instructions to reduce risk. A mandatory recall response is triggered when a model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units, calculated from all known thermal incidents and a documented, regulator-verifiable installed-unit denominator. The manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers and implement the regulator-directed protective response. The regulator shall determine whether distribution must stop and may require a targeted recall remedy, repair, replacement, refund, or other effective risk controls based on the hazard and affected units. Corrective action alone does not remove the response obligation unless the regulator approves it as an adequate remedy.
