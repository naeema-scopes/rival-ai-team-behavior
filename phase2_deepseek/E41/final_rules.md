# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run E41 · variant E · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every confirmed thermal event involving overheating, smoke, fire, or thermal injury to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of confirmation, and provide a complete incident report within 10 business days. They shall notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible product-specific safety risk or requires customer action. Reports must identify the model, event date, and known consequences; manufacturers shall correct incomplete reports as new information becomes available.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model shall be tested under a sustained-heat protocol at 55 °C before market placement and at least annually thereafter, using a statistically representative sample and a common test method. Manufacturers shall submit per-model results and sample sizes to the regulator; these individual-company results shall not be publicly disclosed. A failure rate above 5% requires a corrective-action plan submitted to the regulator and follow-up testing within 12 months; it does not by itself require market withdrawal. Regulators may require earlier corrective action or other measures where evidence indicates an imminent or serious risk. Requirements apply 24 months after adoption.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units shall support secure remote emergency shut-off by 24 months after adoption, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a clear customer notification and manual override procedure. Manufacturers shall retrofit already-installed units to support remote shut-off within 6 years after adoption where technically feasible; where retrofitting is infeasible, they shall provide an equivalent documented risk-reduction measure and notify affected customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New home battery units shall include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers shall provide regulators with evidence that the suppression system is effective and does not introduce new hazards. Units installed before that date are not subject to a retrofit requirement under this section.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers shall provide customers with clear safety notices describing material product-specific risks, applicable precautions, and any required customer action, and promptly update notices when new safety information emerges. Notices shall include the model’s latest 55 °C sustained-heat test result and sample size. A manufacturer shall notify affected customers within 48 hours after identifying a credible product-specific safety risk requiring customer action. When a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year, the manufacturer shall promptly notify regulators and affected customers. Regulators shall assess the evidence and may require corrective action, suspension, or recall, including a recall where warranted by the risk. Customer instructions shall be proportionate and explain safe shutdown and any return or remediation. Manufacturers shall report progress on required actions to regulators and affected customers until completion.
