# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run H51 · variant H · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers shall report every thermal incident involving fire, smoke, injury, property damage, or an emergency shutdown to the regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and provide a full incident report within 10 calendar days. Incidents involving death, serious injury, or fire spreading beyond the battery shall also be reported to affected customers and households within 24 hours; other affected customers shall be notified within 5 business days. Reports shall include known circumstances, affected models and serial-number ranges, and immediate safety measures. Manufacturers shall maintain incident records and disclose any previously unreported incidents to the regulator within 30 days of adoption.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55°C using a representative sample and a documented, repeatable protocol. The maximum allowed failure rate is 7% for any model. Manufacturers shall submit per-model protocols, sample sizes, and results to the regulator, and provide the applicable model’s results to prospective purchasers before purchase. Individual-company per-model failure rates shall not be publicly published; regulators may publish anonymized or aggregated results. When a model exceeds the limit, the regulator shall require a corrective-action plan and retesting, and may restrict sales if the manufacturer fails to implement an adequate plan.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months or more after adoption shall support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers shall retrofit already-installed units with remote shut-off capability within 6 years of adoption where technically feasible, prioritizing units with known safety risks. Where retrofit is not technically feasible, manufacturers shall provide an alternative safety measure and notify affected customers and the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units shall include built-in fire suppression beginning 36 months after adoption. The system shall be designed to mitigate thermal runaway and limit fire spread, and manufacturers shall provide installation, maintenance, and emergency-response instructions to customers. Manufacturers shall submit evidence of the suppression system’s safety and effectiveness to the regulator.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers shall receive the applicable model’s 55°C test result and clear instructions for safe installation, operation, emergency response, and reporting a suspected safety issue. Manufacturers shall promptly notify affected customers of material safety risks, incidents indicating a potential defect, and corrective actions, using direct contact where reasonably available and public notices otherwise. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model’s independently validated annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 incidents per 100,000 installed units. Validation shall use documented incident counts and installed-unit denominators for the same period, with regulator access to supporting records. The manufacturer shall notify the regulator and affected customers promptly, stop sale of the affected model, and provide a no-cost repair, replacement, or refund, subject to regulator oversight.
