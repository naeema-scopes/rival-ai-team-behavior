# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run D10 · variant D · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, smoke, fire, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, with a complete incident report within 10 business days. The manufacturer must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident presents a credible ongoing safety risk, and otherwise within 10 business days. Reports must include known circumstances, model and unit identifiers, injuries or damage, and any immediate safety action. Suspected under-reporting must be corrected promptly; reporting obligations apply regardless of whether the cause is confirmed.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved protocol and representative sample before sale and at least annually thereafter, including after material design changes. Per-model test results and supporting data must be submitted confidentially to the regulator and disclosed to customers who buy or are considering that model; public reporting must be limited to aggregated industry data that does not identify a company or model. A failure rate above 5% is a safety action level, not an automatic sales ban: the manufacturer must report it to the regulator within 5 business days and submit a regulator-approved corrective-action plan and retesting schedule. The regulator must assess the results and may require customer precautions, additional testing, or proportionate sales restrictions based on the risk assessment. Continued sale during remediation may be allowed with regulator approval and adequate interim protections; permanent market exclusion requires a separate determination that corrective action cannot adequately control the risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must support secure remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and clear customer instructions. Manufacturers must retrofit already-installed units to support the function within 6 years of this standard taking effect, prioritizing units subject to an active safety notice or elevated risk. Where a retrofit is not technically feasible, the manufacturer must provide an equivalent risk-reduction measure and notify the regulator and affected customers.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression no later than 36 months after this standard is adopted. The suppression system must be appropriate to the battery chemistry and designed to reduce fire spread and protect occupants and nearby properties. Manufacturers must provide installation and maintenance instructions and report any material suppression-system failure to the regulator. Before the effective date, manufacturers must disclose to customers whether a unit includes built-in suppression and provide clear guidance on fire response.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Manufacturers must provide customers with clear safety notices promptly when credible information indicates a risk involving their model, including the nature of the risk, affected units, actions customers should take, and available support. Notices must be sent directly to affected customers and made accessible to the public. A mandatory recall is triggered when a model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in any 12-month period. Manufacturers must notify the regulator within 5 business days of identifying that threshold has been exceeded and promptly notify affected customers; the regulator may require immediate protective measures pending completion of the recall.
