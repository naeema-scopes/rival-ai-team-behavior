# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run A15 · variant A · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving overheating, fire, injury, property damage, or emergency-service response to the relevant regulator within 24 hours of learning of it, with a written incident report within 10 days. They must notify affected customers within 48 hours when an incident indicates a credible safety risk to their model, and promptly provide material updates and protective instructions. Manufacturers must keep incident records and submit an annual model-level incident summary to regulators; they must not omit incidents because the cause is uncertain.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C before sale and at least annually thereafter, using a regulator-approved protocol and representative production units. Manufacturers must submit model-specific results, sample sizes, and corrective-action plans to the relevant regulator, and disclose the model’s test results and failure rate to customers who buy that model. A failure rate above 5% requires a corrective-action plan and follow-up test within 90 days; it does not by itself bar sale. Regulators may require additional safeguards where results or field incidents indicate a safety risk. Review the threshold against field and test data after two years.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units manufactured 24 months or more after adoption must support remote emergency shut-off, with safeguards against unauthorized activation and a locally accessible shut-off option. For already-installed units, manufacturers must provide a retrofit or equivalent risk-control plan within 6 years of adoption, prioritizing models or locations presenting the greatest risk. Where remote shut-off cannot be safely retrofitted, the manufacturer must provide an alternative mitigation and notify affected customers. Regulators may require earlier action for a specific model presenting an imminent risk.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units manufactured 36 months or more after adoption must include built-in fire suppression meeting regulator-approved performance and maintenance requirements. Before that date, manufacturers must assess fire risk for each model and document the mitigation measures used. Regulators may require earlier model-specific safeguards where evidence indicates an imminent or unacceptable risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Before purchase, customers must receive clear safety information for the model, including safety instructions, material incident trends, the model’s per-model 55 °C test results and failure rate, and any applicable corrective actions. Manufacturers must promptly notify affected customers of material new risks and provide protective actions and updates. If a model’s annual thermal-incident rate exceeds 8 per 100,000 installed units, the manufacturer must promptly notify regulators, investigate and validate the rate, and submit a corrective-action plan. Regulators must assess the validated rate and other evidence and determine proportionate protective action, which may include a recall. A recall is required when regulators determine that the model presents an imminent serious risk and no equally protective measure is available.
