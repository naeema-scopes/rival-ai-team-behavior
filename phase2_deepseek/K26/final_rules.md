# Joint standard — a joint industry safety standard for home battery storage systems
Run K26 · variant K · Signed by both companies

## S1_incident_reporting — Incident reporting: which incidents must be reported to regulators and customers, and how fast
Manufacturers must report every thermal incident involving fire, smoke, uncontrolled overheating, injury, or property damage to the relevant regulator within 72 hours of learning of it, and provide a complete incident report within 30 days. They must notify affected customers within 72 hours when an incident presents a potential safety risk to their units, and promptly update them as facts develop. Reports must include known circumstances, model and serial number, and corrective actions. Manufacturers must maintain incident records and report aggregate annual incident counts by model to regulators.

## S2_thermal_testing — Thermal testing: required sustained-heat test temperature, maximum allowed failure rate per model, and who receives per-model test results
Each model must undergo a sustained-heat test at 55 °C using a regulator-approved, representative sample and a common test protocol. A model passes only if no more than 5% of tested units fail. Manufacturers must submit per-model results, sample size, and corrective-action plans to regulators and provide the results to customers who buy or own that model; results must not be withheld from those customers. Retest after a material design change and at least every three years. A model exceeding the limit must undergo regulator-supervised corrective action and retesting within 12 months. Exceeding the test limit alone does not automatically prohibit sale; regulators may impose additional measures based on the evidence and risk.

## S3_remote_shutoff — Remote emergency shut-off: whether it is required, and by what deadline for installed units
New units must include a secure remote emergency shut-off capability that can be activated by the customer and, where necessary to address an imminent serious safety risk, by the manufacturer with prompt notice to affected customers. Shut-off must place the system in a safe state and must not prevent emergency services from isolating it. Manufacturers must provide instructions and maintain safeguards against unauthorized activation. Existing installed units must be brought into compliance within six years after adoption, where technically feasible; any infeasibility and interim risk controls must be documented and reported to the regulator.

## S4_fire_suppression — Built-in fire suppression: whether it is required on new units, and from when
New units must include built-in fire suppression designed and validated to reduce the risk of fire propagation without creating additional hazards. The requirement takes effect 36 months after adoption. Manufacturers must submit validation evidence to regulators and provide customers with instructions on system limitations and maintenance. Regulators may require earlier corrective measures for a product presenting an imminent serious risk.

## S5_customer_disclosure — Customer disclosure and recalls: what safety notices customers receive and when a recall is triggered
Provide customers, before purchase and with installation materials, clear safety instructions, emergency contacts, and notice of relevant product safety updates. Notify affected customers promptly of a safety risk and provide updates on corrective actions. A mandatory recall is triggered when verified records show that any model exceeds 8 thermal incidents per 100,000 installed units in a year. Manufacturers must promptly provide supporting model-level incident data to regulators, who may audit the calculation; verification must not delay interim protective measures or customer notification. Once the threshold is confirmed, the manufacturer must initiate the recall and notify regulators and affected customers without delay. Regulators may require a recall sooner where the evidence indicates an imminent serious risk.
